10worth readingDUNE Sterile Neutrinos

Sensitivity to Sterile Neutrinos with Elastic Neutrino-Electron and Highly Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering in the DUNE Near Detector

The DUNE Collaboration

hep-exarXiv:2611.10271

DUNE Near Detector sensitivity to 3+1 sterile neutrinos is evaluated using low-hadronic-energy CC interactions and neutrino-electron elastic scattering flux constraints.

Novel idea

The paper combines highly elastic ($ u^{\mathrm{reco}} < 0.3\text{ GeV}$) $\nu_\mu$-CC and $\nu_e$-CC interaction samples with $\nu+e^-$ elastic scattering to perform a short-baseline sterile neutrino search exclusively within the DUNE Near Detector. Restricting the hadronic energy transfer suppresses nuclear and cross-section model dependencies and tightly couples the reconstructed lepton energy to the incident neutrino energy, while $ u+e^-$ scattering provides a precise electroweak constraint on the flux.

Method

Simulated forward horn current ($1.1\times 10^{21}$ POT) events in ND-LAr and the Temporary Muon Spectrometer (TMS) are analyzed across 102 reconstructed lepton energy bins. Systematic uncertainties spanning the DUNE flux model, 68 GENIE interaction parameters, detector response, and a 0.5% radiative correction error on $\nu-e$ scattering are formatted into a fixed fractional covariance matrix. A 4D parameter fit ($|U_{e4}|^2, |U_{\mu 4}|^2, |U_{\tau 4}|^2, \Delta m^2$) is executed with a Feldman-Cousins profile likelihood construction.

Key results

  • Peak 90% C.L. sensitivity reaches $|U_{\mu 4}|^2 \approx 0.01$ and $|U_{e4}|^2 \approx 0.015$ for $\Delta m^2 \sim 4-10 \text{ eV}^2$.
  • At $\Delta m^2 \lesssim 1 \text{ eV}^2$, sensitivity is limited by baseline length, whereas at $\Delta m^2 > 10 \text{ eV}^2$, spectral oscillations average out and sensitivity is driven by overall rate changes constrained by $\nu+e^-$ scattering.
  • The 90% C.L. Feldman-Cousins critical value is $\Delta\chi^2_{\mathrm{FC}} = 10.06$ (vs. Wilks expectation of 7.78 for 4 d.o.f.), with flux uncertainties causing the largest broadening of the test statistic distribution.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to DUNE Near Detector physics and oscillation systematics. It demonstrates how low-energy-transfer CC events, when paired with $\nu-e$ elastic scattering flux normalization, can isolate short-baseline BSM signatures while controlling nuclear model dependence.

Caveats

The analysis uses a fixed fractional covariance matrix evaluated at the null hypothesis and relies on GENIE 2.12.10 systematic variations; residual model dependence in the mapping between true and reconstructed low energy transfer could affect sample selection efficiency and bin migration.

Abstract

The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) is a next-generation neutrino experiment designed for precision measurements of long-baseline three-flavor neutrino oscillations. The DUNE Near Detector (ND) is also sensitive to short-baseline oscillations arising from mixing with an eV-scale sterile neutrino. In this paper, we present a sensitivity study for sterile-neutrino oscillations in the $3+1$ framework using three complementary event samples in the simulated DUNE ND: highly elastic $\nu_\mu$ charged-current (CC), highly elastic $\nu_e$ CC, and neutrino-electron elastic-scattering ($\nu+e$) events. These highly exclusive samples are chosen to reduce neutrino-interaction and flux uncertainties: the low-hadronic-energy CC selections enhance the correlation between reconstructed lepton energy and neutrino energy, while the precisely calculable $\nu+e$ cross section provides an independent constraint on the neutrino flux. Residual interaction-model dependence remains in the CC samples. The statistical analysis considers $|U_{e4}|^2$, $|U_{\mu4}|^2$, $|U_{\tau4}|^2$, and $\Delta m^2$, with sensitivity results presented as fixed-$\Delta m^2$ slices for $|U_{\tau4}|^2=0$. The strongest sensitivity occurs for $4 \lesssim \Delta m^2 \lesssim 10~\mathrm{eV}^2$, where oscillations produce resolvable distortions of the reconstructed-energy spectra. For one year of on-axis forward-horn-current running, the 90\% confidence-level sensitivity reaches approximately $|U_{\mu4}|^2 \simeq 0.01$ and $|U_{e4}|^2 \simeq 0.015$, depending on the value of the other mixing parameter.

NuSTEC Review: Current Experimental and Theoretical Challenges of Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering

A. P. Furmanski, J. G. Morfín, L. Alvarez-Ruso, A. M. Ankowski, A. Ashkenazi, M. Sajjad Athar + 27 more

hep-ex hep-ph nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2611.10465

The NuSTEC collaboration reviews the current state, open challenges, and strategic priorities in neutrino-nucleus interaction modeling for precision GeV-scale oscillation experiments.

Novel idea

This comprehensive review acts as an updated global roadmap for the neutrino-scattering community following a decade of progress since the 2014 NuSTEC White Paper. It details the open theoretical and experimental issues across all kinematic regimes (QE, 2p2h/MEC, RES, SIS/DIS, FSI, CEvNS, coherent pion) and maps their direct impact on CP-violation and mass-ordering measurements at DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande, T2K, and NOvA.

Method

A synthesis of nuclear theory, experimental data, and event generator developments. The review evaluates theoretical models (ab initio, spectral functions, relativistic Fermi gas, GiBUU), generator implementations (GENIE, NuWro, NEUT, Achilles, GiBUU), electron-nucleus scattering constraints ($e,e'$ and $e,e'p$), hadron-nucleus transport data, and flux determination techniques.

Key results

  • Identifies 3-5% interaction systematic uncertainties in current oscillation analyses as the primary bottleneck to reaching the 1-2% statistical precision targets of DUNE and Hyper-K.
  • Establishes 6 critical priorities: funding theoretical nuclear models for relativistic dynamics and FSI; modular generator architectures and standard interfaces; detailed measurements of exclusive hadronic final states (neutrons, low-threshold protons, multi-pions); expanded electron/hadron scattering campaigns; standardized model-independent cross-section reporting; and dedicated hadron production experiments.
  • Highlights the lack of final-state neutron data and low-momentum hadron/pion transport constraints as major vulnerabilities for calorimetric and tracking detectors.

Why it matters to you

Must-read benchmark document for theoretical neutrino-nucleus physicists and generator developers. Co-authored by key experts in the field, it provides a comprehensive overview of the open problems in electroweak nuclear response, axial form factors, and FSI modeling.

Caveats

As a review and strategy document, it does not present new original calculations or fresh experimental data releases, but summarizes the current consensus and open issues across the field.

Abstract

The precise measurement of neutrino properties is among the highest priorities in fundamental particle physics, involving many experimental and theoretical efforts worldwide. Since the experiments rely on the interactions of neutrinos in the nuclear environment, the planned advances in the scope and precision of these experiments require a commensurate effort in understanding and modeling the hadronic and nuclear physics of these interactions, as incorporated in neutrino event generators. This modeling is essential to every phase of the experimental analyses, and the theoretical uncertainties play an important role in interpreting experimental results. In this Review of current challenges, we summarize the open questions in neutrino-nucleus interactions at GeV-scale energies, and discuss in detail the potential impacts of these unknowns on the measurement of neutrino properties, using the determination of oscillation parameters as a central example. After an Executive Summary and a concise Overview of the issues, we address how the neutrino event generators treat these challenges, how electron-nucleus interactions can help understanding these challenges and the important unanswered questions for each underlying physics process---from quasi-elastic to deep-inelastic scattering. Across every topic, we find that current challenges can be met only through active support and collaboration among specialists in strong interactions and electroweak physics, including theorists and experimentalists from both the nuclear and high-energy physics communities.

Machine learning assisted $\nu/\bar{\nu}$ discrimination in atmospheric neutrino events for CPV sensitivity in a 20-kton liquid scintillator detector

Dek Hao Liew, Mohammad Nizam, Shu Tian Eu, Siew Yan Hoh

hep-ex hep-pharXiv:2611.11474

Machine learning trained on inelasticity, neutron multiplicity, and lepton energy enables statistical $\nu/\bar{\nu}$ separation in unmagnetized liquid scintillator detectors, boosting atmospheric CPV sensitivity.

Novel idea

Demonstrates that unmagnetized 20-kton liquid scintillator detectors (such as JUNO) can partially overcome lepton charge-blindness in atmospheric neutrino interactions by using machine learning classifiers on hadronic observables ($y$, $n_{\mathrm{fn}}$, $E_\ell$) to statistically separate $\nu$ and $\bar{\nu}$ events.

Method

Atmospheric $\nu_\mu$-CC and $\nu_e$-CC events are generated in GENIE (Honda flux) and reweighted with 3-flavor oscillation probabilities including Earth matter effects. Reconstructed lepton/hadronic energies, directions, and neutron multiplicities (90% tagging efficiency) are generated via parameterized smearing. Logistic regression, XGBoost, and MLP models are trained; XGBoost is selected to evaluate CPV sensitivity for a 200 kton-year exposure via a Poisson likelihood incorporating 7 systematic parameters and marginalization over 5 oscillation parameters.

Key results

  • XGBoost achieves an AUC of ~0.83 for both $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_e$ CC event separation.
  • Neutron multiplicity is the single most dominant feature for classification (~77-79% relative importance).
  • Peak CPV sensitivity for a 200 kton-year exposure improves from $1.49\sigma$ (charge-blind) to $1.96\sigma$ (ML-assisted) under Normal Ordering, and from $1.14\sigma$ to $1.61\sigma$ under Inverted Ordering.

Why it matters to you

Relevant for atmospheric neutrino phenomenology and ML applications in detector physics; underscores the physical importance of final-state neutron multiplicity in distinguishing neutrino vs antineutrino interactions in dense detector media.

Caveats

Uses fast parameterized Gaussian/binomial smearing rather than full detector optical simulation and particle reconstruction; assumes a fixed 90% neutron tagging efficiency; profiles oscillation parameters within $3\sigma$ while fixing the $\theta_{23}$ octant at best fit.

Abstract

Atmospheric neutrinos span a broad range of energies and baselines, offering a complementary probe of leptonic CP violation. However, a JUNO-like liquid scintillator detector without a magnetic field cannot directly separate neutrino and antineutrino interactions, reducing CP violation sensitivity. This study investigates whether machine learning based statistical separation can improve CP violation sensitivity in a 20-kton detector. $\nu_{\mu}$ and $\nu_e$ charged current events are generated with GENIE (using Honda flux) in a JUNO-like detector geometry and are reweighted using OscProb three flavor oscillation probabilities incorporating Earth matter effects and NuFIT parameters. Detector response is approximated by smearing lepton and hadronic energies, neutrino direction, and neutron multiplicity. Logistic regression, XGBoost, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) classifiers are trained on inelasticity, neutron multiplicity, and charged lepton energy, with XGBoost selected for the analysis. CP violation sensitivity is evaluated for a 200 kton-year exposure using a Poisson likelihood with systematic nuisance parameters and marginalization over five oscillation parameters within their respective $3\sigma$ ranges. XGBoost achieves an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of approximately 0.83 for both flavor samples. Under the square root approximation, peak CP violation sensitivity improves from 1.49 $\sigma$ to 1.96 $\sigma$ for normal mass ordering and from 1.14 $\sigma$ to 1.61 $\sigma$ for inverted mass ordering. Machine learning based statistical neutrino-antineutrino separation can recover part of the CP violation sensitivity lost in charge blind detectors. These preliminary results motivate further studies incorporating validated detector response and systematic uncertainties.

Electroweak Parton Distribution Functions for Future Lepton Colliders

Tao Han, Wolfgang Kilian, Yang Ma, Krzysztof M\keka\la, Jürgen Reuter, Keping Xie

hep-pharXiv:2611.12793

A fully resummed Electroweak Parton Distribution Function (EW PDF) framework is implemented in the Whizard event generator for multi-TeV lepton colliders.

Novel idea

Integrates the complete SM Electroweak PDF (EW PDF) framework—which resums collinear electroweak gauge boson and fermion splittings across all helicity states—into the generic automated Monte Carlo generator Whizard, enabling partonic physics studies at high-energy lepton and muon colliders.

Method

Solves generalized DGLAP evolution equations in the unbroken $SU(3)_c \times SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ SM gauge theory including chiral couplings, gauge boson polarization states, and mass threshold matching. Numerical grids are integrated into Whizard. Detailed benchmark comparisons are performed for a 10 TeV $\mu^+\mu^-$ collider against full fixed-order diagrammatic calculations and the Effective Weak Boson Approximation (EWA) for $HH$, $t\bar{t}$, $\nu_\tau\bar{\nu}_\tau$, $WW$, and heavy scalar $XX$ production, alongside QED/QCD mini-jets below the EW scale.

Key results

  • Low-energy QCD mini-jet production ($gg \to jj$) via QED splittings is shown to be a massive background at a 10 TeV muon collider, with cross sections reaching $\mathcal{O}(10-100\text{ pb})$.
  • EW PDFs accurately match fixed-order matrix elements for longitudinal VBF processes ($HH, t\bar{t}$) at high invariant masses, exhibiting a 5-20% resummation suppression relative to leading-order EWA.
  • Demonstrates that the transverse mass scale $2H_T$ is significantly superior to $\sqrt{\hat{s}}/2$ for managing scale uncertainties and describing transverse kinematics in gauge-boson-initiated scattering.

Why it matters to you

Relevant to theoretical interest in electroweak resummation, QED/EW parton showers, and multi-TeV muon collider phenomenology. Provides a Monte Carlo tool to compute processes where electroweak gauge symmetry is manifest in initial-state splittings.

Caveats

The current framework does not yet link electroweak beam remnants to standard QCD hadronization/parton showers; collinear factorized approximations break down near kinematic endpoints ($x \to 1$) and forward low-$p_T$ gauge boson scattering where gauge cancellations dominate.

Abstract

At future high-energy colliders, such as a multi-TeV muon collider or the high-energy stages of an $e^+e^-$ linear collider, collinear radiation of electroweak gauge bosons becomes a dominant phenomenon. In this regime, many key processes are primarily initiated by photons and gauge bosons originating from lepton splittings. Electroweak Parton Distribution Functions (EW PDFs) provide an appropriate framework to describe these interactions by resumming large collinear logarithms arising from all possible Standard Model splittings, thereby enabling a partonic interpretation of the hard scattering. The formalism is particularly suitable for studying new physics beyond the Standard Model in the electroweak sector at higher scales. In this paper, we present the implementation of the EW PDF formalism within the Monte Carlo event generator \texttt{Whizard}. We demonstrate several applications of this framework and analyze its interplay with full fixed-order calculations as well as the purely kinematic Effective Weak Boson Approximation. We highlight scenarios where the EW PDF approach is especially useful for phenomenology and physics-potential studies for future lepton colliders. The EW PDF formalism is a vital step towards the ``electroweak jet regime'' where electroweak particles behave similarly to QCD jets.

Domain Adaptive Graph Neural Networks for Neutrino Experiments

Hemalata Nayak, Giuseppe Cerati, Aleksandra Ćiprijanović, Tammy Walton

physics.data-an cs.LG hep-exarXiv:2611.10057 · cross-list

Domain-adversarial training integrated into Graph Neural Networks (NuGraph3) mitigates performance degradation caused by simulation-to-data domain shifts in LArTPC event reconstruction.

Novel idea

Applies Domain-Adversarial Neural Networks (DANN) to Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for LArTPC hit filtering and semantic segmentation, enforcing domain-invariant latent space representations to prevent ML reconstruction model performance from degrading when moving from simulation to target experimental data.

Method

Evaluates the hierarchical GNN NuGraph3 using MicroBooNE open simulation data as the labeled source domain and 4 synthetic target domains representing common detector domain shifts: fixed scale variation, moderate Gaussian noise, severe Gaussian noise, and drift-time-dependent electron diffusion (RMS) shift. Adversarial training is enforced using a domain classifier and Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL) applied to the latent node embeddings.

Key results

  • Adversarial domain adaptation recovers target-domain precision losses by up to 0.19 and recall losses by up to 0.37 across perturbed target datasets.
  • In the severe Gaussian noise scenario, target semantic precision and recall rebound from 0.67 (without DA) to 0.86 (with DA).
  • Under fixed scaling, target filter recall is restored from 0.56 to 0.93 while maintaining source domain performance within 1% of the baseline model.

Why it matters to you

Addresses a key challenge in ML applications for LArTPC experiments (MicroBooNE, SBN, DUNE): the risk that subtle discrepancies between Monte Carlo simulations and detector data create domain shifts that degrade reconstruction efficiency or induce systematic bias.

Caveats

Evaluated on synthetic perturbations of the MicroBooNE open dataset rather than unblinded real experimental data; adversarial DA training introduces a ~5% computational time overhead per epoch.

Abstract

Weakly interacting particles such as neutrinos require highly efficient and precise detection methods to enable searches for new physics. Deep learning, and particularly Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), has shown promise for reconstruction tasks in liquid argon time projection chamber (LArTPC) neutrino experiments. However, differences between simulations and experimental data introduce domain shifts that can degrade model performance and potentially mimic or obscure new-physics signals. Here, we demonstrate that robust learning across datasets can still be achieved by using domain adaptation during training. We introduce, to our knowledge for the first time, domain-adaptation-enabled GNNs for robust LArTPC reconstruction tasks. We use MicroBooNE open dataset as our labeled source domain, and introduce four perturbed datasets as our unlabeled target domains-- including a fixed scale variation, moderate and severe gaussian noise, and a signal variation as a function of the position in the detector. The inclusion of adversarial-based domain adaptation during training improves performance for all four target domains, without a substantial drop in performance in the source domain. Precision in the target domain was improved up to 0.19 and recall up to 0.37, depending on the variation and the network task. These results demonstrate a potential of domain adaptation to bridge the gap between simulations and experiments, and build more trustworthy models for current and future LArTPC neutrino experiments.

Novel idea

Extends collision-balanced stationary solutions to arbitrary discrete or continuous energy spectra and energy-dependent collision rates, and demonstrates that these stationary populations accurately estimate time-averaged flavor populations even inside chaotic regimes.

Method

Formulates self-consistency equations for isotropic collective-precession states in quantum kinetic equations incorporating flavor self-interactions and collisional relaxation. Abstract

Core-collapse supernovae and neutron star mergers are among the most energetic astrophysical events and promising sources of multimessenger signals. Novel idea

Combines Coulomb-gauge-fixed quark bilinears (avoiding linear Wilson-line divergences and renormalons) with kinematically enhanced interpolating operators and hybrid/SRI'-MOM renormalization at physical quark masses to benchmark light-cone PDFs.

Method

Calculated on a $64^3 \times 64$ HISQ $(2+1)$-flavor ensemble at $a = 0.076$ fm with proton momenta $P_z = 1.8$ and $2.3$ GeV. Key results

  • Renormalized coordinate-space matrix elements show close agreement with NNPDF4.0-based NLO reconstructions, notably observing close agreement in the imaginary part for the first time.
  • The resulting $\overline{\text{MS}}$ light-cone PDFs agree with global phenomenological fits in the moderate-to-large longitudinal momentum fraction range $0.25 \lesssim |x| \lesssim 0.75$.

Why it matters to you

Provides a rigorous first-principles lattice benchmark for the unpolarized isovector PDF ($u-d$) using modern LaMET and Coulomb-gauge techniques, strengthening theoretical control for DUNE, JLab, and EIC cross-section analyses.

Caveats

Calculations are performed at a single lattice spacing, precluding a direct continuum limit, and rely on NLO matching and NLA asymptotic extrapolations to stabilize the Fourier transform.

We present a lattice quantum chromodynamics (QCD) calculation of the proton isovector ($u-d$) unpolarized parton distribution function (PDF) at physical sea and valence quark masses, using Coulomb-gauge-fixed quark bilinears within the large-momentum effective theory framework. This agreement is further supported by a comparison in coordinate space, where our short-distance lattice matrix elements are consistent with those obtained from the phenomenological PDF through NLO matching. In particular, we observe close agreement in the imaginary part for the first time. Together, these results represent an encouraging step toward precision benchmarking of lattice QCD calculations of PDFs.
