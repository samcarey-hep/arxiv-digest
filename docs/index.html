10must-readVud from superallowed beta decays

Determination of $V_{ud}$ from superallowed $\beta$ decays using effective field theory

Samuel Novario, Graham Chambers-Wall, Vincenzo Cirigliano, Wouter Dekens, Jordy de Vries, Stefano Gandolfi + 2 more

nucl-th hep-ex hep-lat hep-pharXiv:2611.18830

This paper presents the first EFT-based calculation of nuclear-structure-dependent radiative corrections for 12 superallowed beta decays, providing a more robust uncertainty quantification for the CKM unitarity test.

Novel idea

The authors introduce an EFT framework to systematically evaluate the $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$ correction, moving away from traditional nuclear models. They develop a density-based formalism to estimate three-body matrix elements at $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$, which are shown to be significant due to coherent scaling with $Z^2$.

Method

The study employs ab initio coupled cluster calculations for NMEs across 12 transitions, benchmarked against Quantum Monte Carlo for light nuclei. A global fit to superallowed decay rates is performed, treating short-distance two-nucleon contact interactions as free parameters.

Key results

  • Extracted $V_{ud} = 0.97373(60)$ from a global fit.
  • Identified a strong degeneracy between one LEC and $V_{ud}$, limiting current precision to $\delta V_{ud} \simeq 6 \times 10^{-4}$.
  • The unitarity deficit is reduced to $1.8\sigma$ due to a more realistic assessment of nuclear uncertainties.

Why it matters to you

This is highly relevant to your interest in ab initio electroweak responses and precision tests of CKM unitarity. It provides a rigorous, model-independent path to improving $V_{ud}$ precision, directly impacting your work on nuclear structure and electroweak currents.

Caveats

The precision is currently limited by the lack of independent constraints on the two-nucleon LECs, requiring future lattice QCD or dispersive inputs.

Abstract

A precision determination of $V_{ud}$ from superallowed $\beta$ decays requires exquisite control over radiative corrections, including nuclear-structure-dependent effects whose evaluation has traditionally relied on nuclear models. To assess the uncertainties inherent in the nuclear matrix elements, an alternative strategy based on effective field theory (EFT) has been established in recent years. Here, we present material progress in this program, in terms of a first EFT calculation of the crucial correction $\bar\delta_\text{NS}$ for 12 superallowed transitions ranging from $^{10}$C $\rightarrow$ $^{10}$B to $^{54}$Co $\rightarrow$ $^{54}$Fe. These main results rely on nuclear ab initio calculations using the coupled cluster method, benchmarked against Quantum Monte Carlo evaluations for light nuclei. The global fit to superallowed decay rates reveals that the precision in $V_{ud}$ is currently limited by short-distance contributions that in the EFT are parameterized as two-nucleon contact interactions, leading to an uncertainty $\delta V_{ud}\simeq 6\times 10^{-4}$. We discuss the consequences for the status of the first-row unitarity test of the CKM matrix as well as a roadmap how to reduce the uncertainty in $V_{ud}$ in the future.

10must-readEFT for Vud and superallowed decays

Ab initio calculations of ${\bar\delta_\text{NS}}$ and extraction of ${V_{ud}}$ using effective field theory

Samuel Novario, Graham Chambers-Wall, Vincenzo Cirigliano, Wouter Dekens, Jordy de Vries, Stefano Gandolfi + 2 more

nucl-th hep-ex hep-lat hep-pharXiv:2611.18843

This companion paper provides the detailed technical account of the ab initio EFT calculation of $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$ and the extraction of $V_{ud}$ for superallowed beta decays.

Novel idea

It details the consistent removal of corrections from the traditional phase-space factor that are already captured by the EFT matrix elements, ensuring mutual consistency across different isotopes. It also introduces a density-based method to estimate three-body $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ NMEs.

Method

The authors use coupled cluster theory with chiral interactions (1.8/2.0 EM and $\text{NNLO}_{\text{sat}}$) to compute NMEs. They perform a detailed uncertainty analysis, including CC truncation and Hamiltonian dependence, and align the phase-space factors with the EFT master formula.

Key results

  • Provides the first global ab initio evaluation of $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$ for 12 transitions.
  • Demonstrates that scale uncertainty in the EFT is now negligible, with the error budget dominated by contact-term renormalization.
  • Shows that removing double-counted phase-space corrections renders individual $V_{ud}$ extractions mutually consistent.

Why it matters to you

This paper is essential for understanding the technical implementation of the EFT-based nuclear corrections. It directly informs your work on ab initio electroweak responses and the systematic treatment of nuclear structure in precision electroweak physics.

Caveats

Requires further ab initio work on $\bar{\delta}_{C}$ to achieve a fully consistent EFT determination.

Abstract

Radiative corrections limit the precision of $V_{ud}$ extractions from superallowed $\beta$ decays. In particular, the model dependence in the nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) is difficult to assess without a clear organizing principle, which is provided by effective field theory (EFT). We report substantial progress in this program based on ab initio nuclear calculations using the coupled cluster method. Benchmarking results against Quantum Monte Carlo calculations for light nuclei, we evaluate all two-body NMEs that enter the correction $\bar\delta_\text{NS}$ across 12 superallowed transitions relevant for $V_{ud}$, including an estimate of truncation and Hamiltonian uncertainties. We find that certain three-body NMEs at ${\mathcal O}(\alpha^2)$ play an important role, due to a combination of large numerical prefactors and coherent scaling with $Z^2$. We develop a formalism to estimate such NMEs based on nuclear densities to complete the EFT evaluation of $\bar\delta_\text{NS}$, validating the corresponding two-body NMEs against ab initio calculations. We further identify the corrections in the traditional phase-space factor that are captured by the EFT and remove them consistently, which renders the individual transitions mutually consistent. Profiting from a recent calculation of ${\mathcal O}(\alpha^2 Z)$ ultrasoft photons, we find that the scale uncertainty in the EFT is now negligible, while the remaining uncertainty is dominated by contact terms required to renormalize the magnetic Gamow-Teller NMEs. One combination of low-energy constants can be determined from a global fit to the superallowed data, but the second one proves strongly correlated with $V_{ud}$, necessitating additional input to resolve the nuclear-structure effects. We argue that such short-distance contributions currently imply an uncertainty in $V_{ud}$ at the level of $\delta V_{ud}\simeq 6\times 10^{-4}$.

High-Statistics Study of Antiproton Annihilation at Rest on Argon in a LArTPC

M. Tanaka, K. Yorita, S. Arai, K. Ishikawa, Y. Utsumi, C. Watanabe + 23 more

hep-exarXiv:2611.19401

This paper reports a high-statistics study of antiproton-argon annihilation at rest in a LArTPC, providing a critical benchmark for hadronic interaction models.

Novel idea

It utilizes a high-purity antiproton beam at J-PARC to collect $>10^5$ annihilation events, allowing for a detailed comparison of vertex-adjacent energy deposition and spatial cluster multiplicities against Fritiof, INCL++, and GiBUU models.

Method

The authors developed a Geant4-based simulation pipeline, calibrated with cosmic muons, to model the full chain from ionization to electronics response. They compare experimental data with hadronic generators using radial energy profiles and cluster counts.

Key results

  • GiBUU provides the most consistent description of localized energy deposition near the annihilation vertex.
  • Fritiof overestimates cluster counts at large radii, while GiBUU and INCL++ better account for pion absorption.
  • The calorimetric energy scale is calibrated to within $3\%$ using cosmic muons.

Why it matters to you

This is highly relevant to your interest in neutrino-nucleus interactions and event generators. The data provide a crucial benchmark for FSI and intranuclear cascade models in argon, which are central to DUNE's interaction modeling and rare-event searches.

Caveats

The study focuses on antiproton annihilation; while informative for FSI, the specific annihilation dynamics differ from neutrino-induced DIS.

Abstract

This paper reports a high-statistics study of antiproton--argon ($\bar{p}\text{--}\text{Ar}$) annihilation at rest in the J-PARC T98 experiment using a single-phase Liquid Argon Time Projection Chamber (LArTPC). A dataset comprising over $10^{5}$ annihilation events was acquired together with comprehensive calibration data. In parallel, a dedicated Geant4-based simulation framework was developed to model the secondary beam transport and detector response. Together, these experimental and computational resources provide a foundation for benchmarking hadronic interaction models and for future LArTPC-based antimatter searches, such as GRAMS. As an initial application of this framework, we compare the measured annihilation topologies, localized energy profiles, and spatial cluster multiplicities with predictions from three hadronic interaction generators: Fritiof, INCL++, and GiBUU. While all models reproduce the overall features of the total energy deposition, GiBUU provides the most consistent empirical description of localized energy deposition near the annihilation vertex and spatial cluster multiplicities at larger radii, reflecting the key roles of secondary nucleon knockout and in-medium pion absorption. These results provide a high-statistics experimental benchmark for modeling low-energy antiproton interactions in liquid argon.

Inferring the neutral-current neutrino--nucleon cross section from IceCube data

Reinaldo Francener, Victor P. Goncalves

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2611.20887

This study investigates the sensitivity of 12 years of IceCube data to the neutral-current neutrino-nucleon cross section, finding that current data only allow for an upper limit.

Novel idea

The authors explore two distinct strategies—Earth absorption/regeneration and the track-to-cascade event ratio—to statistically infer the NC contribution, which is otherwise indistinguishable from CC backgrounds on an event-by-event basis.

Method

The analysis uses the IceCube HESE sample, solving the coupled neutrino-transport equations in the Earth (PREM profile) and performing a likelihood analysis on track and cascade event rates.

Key results

  • Current IceCube data set an upper limit of $\sim 1.16$ times the SM NC cross section.
  • The absorption-based method is limited by CC cross-section uncertainties, while the cascade-based method is sensitive to neutrino flavor composition.
  • IceCube-Gen2 is projected to robustly constrain the NC cross section, potentially establishing a lower limit.

Why it matters to you

This is relevant to your interest in neutrino-nucleus interactions at high energies. It highlights the experimental challenges of isolating NC interactions and the potential for future observatories to constrain these cross sections.

Caveats

The results are sensitive to the assumed astrophysical neutrino flavor composition and the normalization of the astrophysical flux.

Abstract

Neutrinos interact only via the weak force, through the exchange of $W^{\pm}$ and $Z^{0}$ bosons in charged-current and neutral-current interactions, respectively. Recent experiments have measured the charged-current cross section at high energies, whereas neutral-current interactions remain largely unexplored experimentally. In this work, we investigate the sensitivity of the IceCube Neutrino Observatory to the neutral-current neutrino--nucleon cross section using 12 years of data. We consider two strategies to constrain this quantity: $(i)$ leveraging neutrino absorption and regeneration as neutrinos traverse the Earth; and $(ii)$ using the track-to-cascade event ratio. We find that current IceCube data can set only an upper limit on the neutral-current neutrino--nucleon cross section. We further show that IceCube-Gen2 should be able to constrain the neutral-current cross section with both methods considered here.

10worth readingNeutrino mass ordering

Phenomenology of neutrino mass ordering synergies in reactor, accelerator, and atmospheric neutrino experiments, and the role of the detector response

Vanessa Cerrone, Marco Grassi

hep-pharXiv:2611.21252

This paper introduces a weighted bin average method to describe the wrong-ordering solution of any neutrino oscillation experiment, clarifying the synergy between reactor, accelerator, and atmospheric experiments.

Novel idea

The authors show that the wrong-ordering solution is a weighted average of bin-level preferences, where weights depend on statistical precision and detector response. This recovers known closed forms and extends the mass ordering sum rule to atmospheric neutrinos in Earth matter.

Method

The approach uses a Gauss-Newton iteration to find the wrong-ordering solution for various experiments (JUNO, DeepCore, ORCA6, T2K, NOvA). It models the detector response and matter effects to compute the 'intercept' of each experiment in the $\Delta m^2_{31}$ plane.

Key results

  • The wrong-ordering solution for any experiment is a line of slope $\approx -1$ in the $\Delta m^2_{31}$ plane.
  • Atmospheric neutrino solutions depend on detector resolution and energy window, differing between DeepCore and ORCA6.
  • The synergy of JUNO with accelerators depends strongly on $\delta_{CP}$, while with DeepCore it is nearly independent of $\delta_{CP}$ and $\theta_{23}$.

Why it matters to you

This is highly relevant to your interest in oscillation-analysis systematics and neutrino phenomenology. The method provides a powerful tool for understanding how different experiments contribute to NMO sensitivity.

Caveats

The analysis assumes specific detector configurations and oscillation parameter priors; results may vary with updated experimental data.

Abstract

The neutrino mass ordering is one of the main open questions of neutrino physics, addressed by reactor, accelerator, and atmospheric experiments and by their combination. When the data are fitted under the wrong ordering, each experiment returns its own value of $\Delta m^2_{31}$, its wrong-ordering solution. We show that this solution is a weighted average of the values preferred by the individual bins of the measured event spectra, in reconstructed energy and, for atmospheric neutrinos, also zenith angle, with weights set by their statistical precision. Both the values and the weights depend on the detector response. In the plane of $\Delta m^2_{31}$ fitted under the two orderings, the solutions lie on lines of slope close to $-1$, so that each experiment is described by one intercept. The weighted bin average extends the mass ordering sum rule to any oscillation experiment, including atmospheric neutrinos in Earth matter. It recovers the known closed forms for short-baseline reactors and for accelerators in vacuum, and for JUNO it equals a closed form evaluated at one effective energy, which depends on the energy resolution. Above the MSW resonance the frequency of atmospheric $\nu_\mu$ disappearance becomes nearly independent of $\delta_{CP}$, and the $\nu_\mu$ disappearance solution moves mainly with $\theta_{23}$. The atmospheric intercept depends on the energy and direction resolutions, the energy window, and the event classes, and differs between detectors. The synergy of two experiments is determined by the separation of their intercepts relative to their precisions on $|\Delta m^2_{31}|$. With JUNO, this separation depends strongly on $\delta_{CP}$ for an accelerator and is nearly independent of $\delta_{CP}$ and $\theta_{23}$ for IceCube DeepCore using all channels.

The authors perform a near/far disappearance analysis using neutrino interactions generated with GENIE 3.6.2 and transported through a Geant4 simulation. Method

The analysis utilizes random-triggered data from ProtoDUNE-SP (Dec 2018–July 2020), applying track selection criteria to isolate a high-purity cosmic muon sample. The rate is modeled with a sinusoidal function, and systematic uncertainties from argon purity and space-charge effects are evaluated.

Why it matters to you

Abstract

We present the measurement of the cosmic ray muon seasonal variation using single-muon events with the ProtoDUNE single-phase detector located at the CERN Neutrino Platform, using data collected from December 2018 to July 2020. Cosmic ray muon runs recorded during this period are analyzed to determine the muon rate as a function of time. The results show a seasonal modulation of the muon rate, with a significance of 4 {\sigma} relative to the null hypothesis of no seasonal variation. Nuclear Mott transition with neural quantum states

Zi-Xiao Zhang, Xiao-Lu Qian, Wan-Bing He, Gerd Röpke, Kai-Jia Sun, Yu-Gang Ma

nucl-tharXiv:2611.20160

MottNet, a momentum-space neural quantum state framework, reveals that alpha-particle Mott suppression in dense nuclear matter is significantly weaker than previously estimated.

Novel idea

The authors introduce MottNet, a self-attention-based neural quantum state framework that solves the A-body in-medium Schrodinger equation in momentum space, bypassing the limitations of conventional angle-averaging and perturbative approximations.

Method

The framework uses a self-attention mechanism to learn many-body correlations in momentum space, optimized via Variational Monte Carlo. It directly incorporates the in-medium Hamiltonian derived from pionless EFT.

Key results

  • Alpha-particle abundance is enhanced by orders of magnitude at high baryon densities compared to conventional models.
  • Provides a microscopic explanation for the large alpha-particle fraction observed in FOPI heavy-ion collision data.
  • MottNet Mott density thresholds for alpha particles are significantly lower than those from standard quantum statistical parametrizations.

Why it matters to you

This directly addresses the reader's interest in ab initio electroweak responses and nuclear structure, providing a novel ML-based tool for solving few-body problems in nuclear matter.

Caveats

The current implementation uses leading-order pionless EFT; extension to realistic nonlocal chiral interactions is planned.

Abstract

Bound clusters can dissolve in nuclear matter due to Pauli blocking, a phenomenon known as the Mott transition that is a key determinant of the nuclear abundances in heavy-ion collisions. Microscopic descriptions of this behavior require solving the in-medium Schr\"odinger equation. However, this remains a major computational challenge because the nuclear medium breaks Galilean invariance and couples angular-momentum channels at finite center-of-mass momentum. The emergence of weakly bound states near dissociation further places stringent demands on conventional methods. Here, we address these challenges by introducing MottNet, a momentum-space neural quantum state framework based on self-attention that directly solves the $A$-body in-medium Schr\"{o}dinger equation. Benchmarked against exact diagonalization of the in-medium deuteron, MottNet reveals substantially weaker Mott suppression for $\alpha$ particles in hot and dense nuclear matter than do conventional calculations based on combined approximations. At high baryon densities, the predicted $\alpha$-particle abundance is enhanced by orders of magnitude, even after continuum-edge subtraction. This finding offers a microscopic explanation for the unexpectedly large $\alpha$-particle fraction inferred from a combined analysis of FOPI heavy-ion collision data and transport model calculations.

9must-readOptical potentials

Fast computation of microscopic optical potentials: beyond existing capabilities

Ashley Pitt, Natalia Timofeyuk, Matteo Vorabbi, Michael Gennari, Petr Navrátil

nucl-tharXiv:2611.21151

A new harmonic oscillator expansion method for microscopic optical potentials accelerates calculations by two orders of magnitude and enables the inclusion of tensor forces.

Novel idea

The authors develop an HO-expansion version of the spectator expansion approach for optical potentials, allowing for a unified representation in momentum and coordinate spaces and enabling the inclusion of the tensor part of the NN amplitude.

Method

The approach expands both the NCSM-based nuclear densities and the NN t-matrix into a harmonic oscillator basis, reducing the computational cost of the single-folding integral and providing an interface to coordinate-space reaction codes.

Key results

  • Calculations are ~100 times faster than previous momentum-space methods.
  • Successfully includes the tensor NN amplitude, revealing its role in channel coupling.
  • Demonstrates convergence for light nuclei (e.g., 12C, 7Li) with chiral Hamiltonian inputs.

Why it matters to you

Highly relevant to the reader's interest in neutrino-nucleus interactions and ab initio nuclear responses, as it provides a faster, more accurate way to compute optical potentials for scattering.

Caveats

Relativistic corrections and the Møller factor are currently omitted but planned for future work.

Abstract

A microscopic approach to optical potential calculations, based on the leading term in the spectator expansion of the optical potential operator and carried out in momentum space, has been successful in describing nucleon elastic scattering above 100 MeV. However, high computational cost precludes the inclusion of all physics relevant to scattering, such as the tensor part of the nucleon-nucleon (NN) amplitude, three-nucleon forces, multiple scattering and medium effects. Also, this approach does not provide any nucleon-nucleus scattering wave functions needed for reaction calculations. In this Letter, we develop a harmonic oscillator expansion version of this approach, which provides the same representation of optical potential both in momentum and coordinate spaces, accelerates calculations by two orders of magnitude, includes the missing tensor component of the NN scattering amplitude and is applicable to nuclei with any spin. Test calculations have been performed for several light nuclei whose densities were obtained in the No-Core Shell Model using a chiral Hamiltonian. We demonstrate convergence of this expansion and clarify the role of the tensor force, which manifests itself mainly in coupling between channels with different spin. Comparison to experimental data is presented for selected cases. The methodology could be extended for scattering of other projectiles (hadronic and pionic), small complex nuclei and hypernuclei. It also suggests a pathway for including more complicated physics into the scheme and extending it to lower energies. It could be also helpful in assessing the accuracy of upcoming scattering emulators.
