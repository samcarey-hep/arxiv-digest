10must-readMuon Tridents at FASER

Muon Tridents at FASER and NA64$\mu$: Motivation, Predictions, and Prospects for Precision Measurements

Reinaldo Francener, Victor P. Goncalves, Felix Kling, Gabriel Rabelo-Soares

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.33153

FASER's spectrometer and NA64$\mu$ can perform the first precision, percent-level measurements of coherent muon trident scattering at high energies, revealing significant inaccuracies in current Monte Carlo generator implementations.

Novel idea

The paper demonstrates that FASER can measure coherent muon trident production ($\\mu^\\pm A \\to \\mu^\\pm \\mu^+ \\mu^- A$) at TeV energies using solely its electronic tracking spectrometer and scintillators, bypassing the slower emulsion analysis. It provides the first complete theoretical calculation of muon tridents incorporating realistic Woods-Saxon nuclear form factors, identical-fermion interference, and Coulomb corrections.

Method

The authors perform an unapproximated tree-level $2\\to 4$ QED matrix element calculation with FeynCalc, integrated via a dedicated 7-variable Monte Carlo generator for tungsten and lead targets. Full detector acceptance cuts, muon identification criteria, and detailed electromagnetic/hadronic shower background simulations (bremsstrahlung, Bethe-Heitler pair production, and DIS muon misidentification) are modeled for FASER and NA64$\mu$.

Key results

  • FASER is projected to record $>8.5 \\times 10^4$ coherent muon trident events in Run 3 after acceptance cuts (overall acceptance $\\sim 20\\%$), delivering percent-level statistical precision.
  • NA64$\\mu$ Phase II (160 GeV beam, $10^{13}$ MOT) is projected to observe $8.8 \\times 10^7$ events, cleanly testing the energy scaling alongside FASER.
  • Standard event generators deviate substantially at TeV energies: Geant4 and PROPOSAL predict only $\\sim 65\\%$ and $\\sim 25\\%$ of the full cross section, respectively.
  • Higher-order Coulomb corrections depend non-trivially on beam energy and reduce expected event rates by $1.4\\%$ at FASER and $6.1\\%$ at NA64$\\mu$.
  • Trident measurements place competitive bounds on $L_\\mu - L_\\tau$ gauge bosons, reaching $g' \\approx 10^{-2}$ at FASER and $4 \\times 10^{-3}$ at NA64$\\mu$ for $m_{Z'} < 0.1\\text{ GeV}$.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts the reader's interest in forward physics facilities (FASER/FPF), lepton-nucleus interactions, and electroweak/QED event generator validation. It establishes muon tridents as a benchmark standard candle for high-energy forward detectors and highlights deficiencies in standard tools like Geant4.

Caveats

Relies on purely electronic identification without emulsion vertex reconstruction, requiring strict kinematic cuts to suppress shower backgrounds. The BSM sensitivity projections account only for statistical uncertainties.

Abstract

The intense flux of high-energy muons produced in the far-forward region of $pp$ collisions at the LHC provides a unique opportunity to study rare electromagnetic interactions at TeV energies. In this work, we investigate the muon trident scattering in muon-nucleus interactions at the FASER experiment, which is expected to have recorded more than $10^5$ such events. The theoretical description of the process is reviewed and improved. Predictions for the total event rates and differential distributions expected during Run~3 of the LHC are presented, taking into account realistic detector acceptance and the main background contributions. The dependence of the predictions on the treatment of the nuclear form factors and the impact of higher-order QED effects such as Coulomb corrections are investigated. Our results indicate that the muon trident process can be measured for the first time at TeV energies using FASER's spectrometer, with a projected precision on the cross-section at the percent level. We also extended our analysis to the NA64$\mu$ experiment and showed that, together with FASER, these experiments can probe the energy dependence of the muon trident cross-section over a broad unexplored energy range.

10worth readingNeutrino opacities and isobars

$\Delta(1232)$ isobars and neutrino opacities in hot dense matter

Subha Mitra, Bharat Kumar

nucl-th astro-ph.HE hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2610.35156

The $\\Delta(1232)$ isobar dramatically increases electron-antineutrino opacities in hot, dense proto-neutron star matter through the three-body absorption channel $\\bar{\\nu}_e + e^- + n \\to \\Delta^-$, reducing antineutrino mean free paths from meters to centimeters.

Novel idea

The paper identifies and calculates the three-body antineutrino absorption reaction $\\bar{\\nu}_e + e^- + n \\to \\Delta^-$ in dense matter. It demonstrates that this process opens whenever $\\mu_n + \\mu_e$ exceeds the in-medium $\\Delta^-$ rest energy, dominating antineutrino opacity even in regions where the ambient $\\Delta$ population is virtually zero.

Method

Calculations use two relativistic mean-field equations of state (DDME2 and QMC-RMF3) extended with the $\\Delta(1232)$ quartet in $\\beta$-equilibrium with trapped neutrinos. Full Rarita-Schwinger spin-3/2 matrix elements and empirical $N\\Delta$ weak transition form factors are integrated over relativistic phase space using quasi-Monte Carlo techniques, with consistency checks against in-medium $\\Delta^-$ widths and Landau-Migdal parameters for pion condensation stability.

Key results

  • The reaction $\\bar{\\nu}_e + e^- + n \\to \\Delta^-$ reduces the $\\bar{\\nu}_e$ diffusion mean free path from $3\\text{--}300\\text{ m}$ down to $0.5\\text{--}16\\text{ cm}$ at $(2\\text{--}4)n_0$, representing an opacity jump of 2 to 4 orders of magnitude.
  • At low lepton fraction ($Y_L = 0.1$), typical of late proto-neutron star deleptonization, the total lepton-number diffusion coefficient is lowered by up to $32\\%$ at $\\ge 2n_0$ and by a factor of $3.0$ at $1.5n_0$.
  • Analytic derivation of the static limit for spin-3/2 baryons proves that the Gamow-Teller strength per particle is $5/3$ (relative to $g_A^2$), compared to $3$ for nucleons, demonstrating that $\\Delta^-$ scattering is driven primarily by its weak vector charge.
  • The in-medium width of the produced $\\Delta^-$ is found to be $4.5\\text{--}40\\text{ MeV}$, with the matter remaining stable against $\\pi^-$ condensation provided $g'_{\\Delta\\Delta} \\gtrsim 0.6\\text{--}1.3$.

Why it matters to you

Directly links $N \\to \\Delta$ weak transition form factors ($C_5^A$) and resonance kinematics to core-collapse supernova and binary merger neutrino transport. It shows that neutrino transport simulations omitting $\\Delta$ degrees of freedom severely overestimate antineutrino diffusion during late deleptonization.

Caveats

Omits dynamical spin-isospin tensor correlations and non-resonant $N\\pi$ continuum contributions; the in-medium $\\Delta$ potential and dispersion relation far above the Fermi surface carry significant theoretical uncertainties.

Abstract

We calculated the diffusion mean free paths of electron neutrinos and antineutrinos in hot, neutrino-trapped matter containing $\Delta(1232)$ isobars, as found in proto-neutron stars and neutron star merger remnants. We included charged-current absorption with the $N\Delta$ weak vertex of weak pion production, neutral-current scattering on all baryons and three-body antineutrino absorption on electron--baryon pairs, with relativistic mean-field kinematics. The reaction $\bar\nu_e+e^-+n\to\Delta^-$ dominates the antineutrino opacity whenever the neutron and electron chemical potentials exceed the in-medium $\Delta^-$ rest energy, even where the $\Delta$ population is negligible. At two to four times saturation density, where the reaction is open, it shortens the antineutrino mean free path from \nubarLamNucMin--\nubarLamNucMax~m to \nubarLamDMin--\nubarLamDMax~cm. Because antineutrinos are scarce at high lepton fraction, this changes lepton-number diffusion only at low lepton fraction, by up to \DtwoRedHi\% at these densities and by a factor of \DtwoRedLow\ at 1.5 times saturation density. For electron neutrinos, absorption on the $\Delta^-$ and, at low lepton fraction, the opening of nucleonic absorption shorten the mean free path by factors of up to \nuRatioYLthreeMax\ at lepton fraction 0.3 and up to about \nuRatioYLoneMax\ at lepton fraction 0.1. We also showed that, for a given axial coupling, the spin of the $\Delta$ reduces its Gamow--Teller strength per particle from 3 to $5/3$.

10worth readingNeutrino fog for inelastic DM

The neutrino fog for inelastic dark matter

C. S. Amarasinghe

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.35789

Inelastic dark matter kinematically shifts nuclear recoils above coherent neutrino-nucleus scattering backgrounds, dispelling the neutrino fog in xenon experiments above mass splittings of $\\sim 180\\text{ keV}$.

Novel idea

The paper maps the neutrino fog for endothermic inelastic dark matter ($m_\\chi \\delta$) in liquid xenon, showing that the fog clears completely well below the kinematic boundary. It also uncovers a statistical anomaly in multi-background environments where tightening neutrino flux uncertainties counterintuitively raises the fog-derived discovery floor.

Method

Computes nuclear recoil spectra for inelastic scattering on xenon using WimPyDD for both the Standard Halo Model (SHM) and an LMC high-speed component. A binned profile likelihood ratio test evaluates the discovery limit across 8B, hep, atmospheric, and DSNB CEvNS backgrounds to compute the fog opacity index $n(N) = - (d\\ln\\sigma / d\\ln N)^{-1}$.

Key results

  • For $m_\\chi = 1\\text{ TeV}$, the neutrino fog ($n > 2$) vanishes completely for splittings $\\delta \\gtrsim 140\\text{ keV}$ up to $10^{10}\\text{ tonne-years}$, and is absent for any mass above $\\delta \\approx 146\\text{ keV}$ (SHM) or $178\\text{ keV}$ (SHM+LMC).
  • The opacity function $n(N)$ develops a multi-peak structure from distinct neutrino sources, causing the fog-onset threshold to jump discontinuously by orders of magnitude over small variations in $\\delta$ or $m_\\chi$.
  • Reducing the atmospheric neutrino rate uncertainty from $25\\%$ to $10\\%$ shifts the fog onset to lower exposures, counterintuitively raising the fog-derived cross-section floor by up to a factor of 2 at $\\delta = 120\\text{ keV}$.

Why it matters to you

Relevant to the reader's interest in CEvNS as an irreducible background and BSM physics searches. It provides critical context for interpreting high-energy nuclear recoil signals (such as the LZ excess) and highlights structural pitfalls in the standard neutrino fog parameterization.

Caveats

Assumes an idealized detector with perfect energy resolution and $100\\%$ efficiency down to sub-keV recoils, neglecting non-neutrino detector backgrounds and Earth orbital modulation.

Abstract

We characterize the behavior of the neutrino fog for inelastic dark matter scattering on xenon. The inelastic scattering kinematics reshape the recoil spectrum of the signal and produce a qualitatively different neutrino fog landscape. In some regions of the inelastic parameter space the fog is completely absent for future xenon experiments at any scale, while in other regions the fog is dispelled only to reappear at larger exposures. We find that multiple background sources with uncertain normalizations can make the onset of the fog jump discontinuously with the dark matter mass and mass splitting, while the discovery limit varies smoothly. We also describe a mechanism by which reduced uncertainties on neutrino rates may have the unintuitive effect of raising neutrino floors derived from the fog opacity.

Indication of Electron Antineutrinos from the Diffuse Supernova Neutrino Background

Kamiokande Collaboration, K. Abe, Y. Asaoka, M. Harada, Y. Hayato, K. Hiraide + 245 more

astro-ph.HE hep-exarXiv:2610.35619 · cross-list

Super-Kamiokande reports a $2.6\\sigma$ indication of electron antineutrinos from the diffuse supernova neutrino background using a 5003-day dataset enhanced by gadolinium neutron tagging.

Novel idea

Presents the first experimental indication of the diffuse supernova neutrino background (DSNB) flux, achieved by combining pure-water and gadolinium-loaded phases (SK-IV through SK-VIII) with refined atmospheric background rejection and advanced spectral fitting.

Method

Analyzes 5003.0 live-days of inverse beta decay (IBD, $\\bar{\\nu}_e + p \\to e^+ + n$) candidate events with delayed neutron tagging, leveraging Gd concentrations up to $0.033\\%$ (75% capture fraction). A maximum-likelihood spectral fit is conducted over $E_\\nu \\in [13.3, 81.3]\\text{ MeV}$ incorporating six background models (spallation isotopes, neutral-current atmospheric, charged-current atmospheric, reactor, and NC elastic).

Key results

  • Best-fit DSNB $\\bar{\\nu}_e$ flux of $3.6^{+1.6}_{-1.5}\\text{ cm}^{-2}\\text{ s}^{-1}$ for $E_\\nu > 13.3\\text{ MeV}$ under a representative core-collapse model.
  • The observed excess excludes the null hypothesis at $2.6\\sigma$ ($99.5\\%$ C.L., one-sided), with similar significance verified across diverse theoretical spectral shapes.
  • Demonstrates successful operation and neutron background suppression using dissolved $\\mathrm{Gd_2(SO_4)_3 \\cdot 8H_2O}$ across multiple run configurations.

Why it matters to you

A landmark experimental result in low-energy neutrino astrophysics and supernova physics (co-authored by Kate Scholberg). The measured flux directly constrains star formation history, cosmic core-collapse rates, and black hole formation fractions, setting the baseline for future DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande low-energy programs.

Caveats

Statistical significance remains at the indication level ($2.6\\sigma$), requiring confirmation with further Gd-doped exposure, JUNO, or Hyper-Kamiokande; neutral-current atmospheric background modelling remains a critical systematic uncertainty.

Abstract

We report an indication of $\bar{\nu}_e$ from the diffuse supernova neutrino background (DSNB) based on a spectral fit analysis of a 5003.0-day dataset in Super-Kamiokande. Key improvements over previous searches include enhanced rejection of atmospheric $\nu$ backgrounds, updated evaluations of systematic uncertainties, and a refined spectral fitting method. Using a representative DSNB model, the best-fit flux was determined to be $3.6^{+1.6}_{-1.5}~\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ for $E_\nu>13.3$~MeV, corresponding to a $2.6\sigma$ tension with a null hypothesis ($99.5\%$ C.L., one-sided). Similar significances were obtained with signal models of varying spectral shapes.

Subthreshold Resonances in the Scattering of {\mu}^-Mesons by Atomic Nuclei

F. M. Penkov, A. Mukhametkaliuly

nucl-th nucl-exarXiv:2610.33622

Subthreshold Feshbach-Baz resonances in low-energy negative muon-nucleus scattering can resonantly excite nuclear giant dipole resonances, offering a clean, background-free probe of radiative widths and nuclear de-excitation.

Novel idea

The paper adapts Feshbach-Baz pre-threshold resonance theory to leptonic scattering, showing that incident $\\mu^-$ with kinetic energies below the nuclear excitation threshold can form quasi-bound mesoatom states that resonantly excite nuclear giant dipole resonances (GDR) without photon backgrounds.

Method

The authors solve radial Schrödinger equations for muonic bound and continuum states in a finite-size, uniformly charged spherical nuclear potential. Partial-wave expansion links the elastic resonance width $\\Gamma_{el}$ in the P-wave directly to experimental $E1$ photon absorption widths $\\Gamma_\\gamma$ via dipole matrix elements.

Key results

  • Single-particle $E1$ transitions in lighter nuclei yield tiny relative widths ($\\Gamma / E_{res} \\sim 10^{-6}\\text{--}10^{-8}$), making them impractical for current beam resolutions.
  • For heavy actinide nuclei ($^{232}\\text{Th}$, $^{235}\\text{U}$, $^{238}\\text{U}$), GDR excitations match incident muon energies of $1.7\\text{--}2.1\\text{ MeV}$ with relative widths $\\Gamma_{EM}/E_{res} \\approx 0.015\\text{--}0.020$, accessible with percent-level beam resolutions.
  • Resonant electromagnetic cross sections reach $3.5\\text{--}4.5\\text{ barns}$, providing an experimental path to measure direct GDR gamma decay and photofission free of bremsstrahlung background.

Why it matters to you

Provides an interesting electromagnetic perspective on nuclear excitation and de-excitation channels (relevant to nuclear remnants and gamma cascades), though tangential to primary neutrino interaction workflows.

Caveats

Relies on an idealized uniformly charged spherical nucleus; GDR collective modes are treated via an average Lorentzian approximation rather than microscopic RPA or spectral functions; negative muon beams of requisite intensity and energy spread are currently challenging to realize.

Abstract

A theoretical study of pre-threshold resonant excitation of atomic nuclei is presented. A formalism for utilizing leptonic probes in the energy region of a Feshbach-Baz pre-threshold resonance is proposed. The resonance positions and widths are shown to be intrinsically linked to the nuclear structure parameters. The correspondence of the giant dipole resonance (GDR) region for heavy nuclei to low probe energies, as well as its large resonance widths, is demonstrated. Through this framework, free from the intense background, the observable signatures of GDR excitation and de-excitation channels, including nuclear fission, are predicted

9worth readingNucleon Polarizabilities and BEFT Sum Rule

Nucleon polarizabilities revisited: insights from the Bernab\'eu-Ericson-Ferro Fontan-Tarrach sum rule

Xiong-Hui Cao, Ling-Yun Dai, Feng-Kun Guo

hep-ph hep-lat nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.34276

A complete dispersive evaluation of the BEFT sum rule for nucleon dipole polarizabilities resolves long-standing scalar-isoscalar puzzles and provides independent benchmarks.

Novel idea

The paper provides the first complete, modern dispersive evaluation of the Bernabéu-Ericson-Ferro Fontan-Tarrach (BEFT) sum rule using hyperbolic dispersion relations, systematically extending the calculation to include the isovector $\pi ceil/K\bar{K}$ channel associated with the $a_0(980)$ resonance.

Method

The framework utilizes hyperbolic dispersion relations for backward real Compton scattering to avoid double counting between $s$ - and $t$ -channels. The $s$ -channel is updated with modern SAID single-pion photoproduction multipoles, while the $t$ -channel incorporates Roy-Steiner equation solutions for $\pi\pi/K\bar{K}$ and chiral perturbation theory for isovector states.

Key results

  • Proton static polarizabilities: $\alpha_{E1}^p = 11.17(52)$ and $\beta_{M1}^p = 2.84(52)$ in units of $10^{-4}\;\mathrm{fm}^3$.
  • Neutron static polarizabilities: $\alpha_{E1}^n = 12.18(59)$ and $\beta_{M1}^n = 3.03(59)$ in units of $10^{-4}\;\mathrm{fm}^3$.
  • Resolves the historical discrepancy in the $t$ -channel $S$-wave $\pi\pi$ contribution between Holstein-Nathan and Drechsel et al. extractions.

Why it matters to you

Provides crucial precision inputs and data-driven constraints on nucleon polarizabilities and electromagnetic mass differences, highly relevant to low-energy nuclear matrix elements and precision electroweak calculations.

Caveats

Uncertainties are dominated by the $t$ -channel cutoffs and dispersive model inputs, and full crossed-channel treatments for kaon systems are left for future work.

Abstract

We present a complete dispersive evaluation of the Bernab\'eu-Ericson-Ferro Fontan-Tarrach (BEFT) sum rule for the nucleon dipole polarizability difference $\alpha_{E1}-\beta_{M1}$. The analysis is based on hyperbolic dispersion relations, which separate the $s$- and $t$-channel contributions without double counting and are particularly suited to backward real Compton scattering. This framework makes it possible to precisely determine the polarizability difference from independent dispersive input rather than from fits to low-energy Compton scattering data. The $s$-channel part is updated with modern single-pion photoproduction multipoles and model estimates of non-$\pi N$ inelastic channels. For the $t$-channel, we use contemporary dispersive input for $\gamma\gamma\to\pi\pi/K\bar K_{I_t=0}$ together with Roy-Steiner equation solutions for $\pi\pi/K\bar K_{I_t=0}\to N\bar N$, and we extend the calculation to the isovector $\pi\eta/K\bar K_{I_t=1}$ channel associated with the $a_0(980)$. The resulting treatment resolves the long-standing puzzle of earlier BEFT evaluations driven by the scalar-isoscalar two-pion channel and gives a controlled estimate of the subleading isovector contribution. Combining the resulting BEFT values with the Baldin sum rule, we obtain, in units of $10^{-4}\,\mathrm{fm}^3$, \[ \alpha_{E1}^p=11.17(52),\quad \beta_{M1}^p=2.84(52),\quad \alpha_{E1}^n=12.18(59),\quad \beta_{M1}^n=3.03(59). \] For the proton, these values provide a benchmark with a precision comparable to state-of-the-art dispersive and chiral effective field theory extractions. For the neutron, whose polarizabilities are much less directly constrained experimentally, the result provides an independent dispersive benchmark with an uncertainty comparable to that of the proton.

Tetsuo Hatsuda

HAL QCD lattice simulations near the physical pion mass demonstrate that two-pion exchange dominates long-range hadron-hadron potentials.

Novel idea

Provides direct nonperturbative lattice QCD evidence at $m_pi \simeq 146$ MeV that two-pion exchange (TPE) governs the long-range behavior of interactions between flavor-singlet hadrons and nucleons where one-pion exchange is forbidden.

Method

Applies the HAL QCD method to (2+1)-flavor gauge configurations with $a = 0.0846$ fm and $V = (8.12\;\mathrm{fm})^3$, evaluating spatial effective energies to extract $\phi-N$, $J/\psi-N$, and $\eta_c-N$ potentials.

Why it matters to you

Directly links lattice QCD dynamics to chiral EFT frameworks of two-pion exchange and nuclear forces, relevant for understanding nonperturbative hadron interactions and exotic hadronic matter.

Caveats

High statistical uncertainties in long-range tails require larger lattice statistics, and contributions from higher-order chiral diagrams like the $\pi\pi NN$ seagull term need further quantification.

Abstract

We study hadron-hadron interactions in (2+1)-flavor lattice QCD near the physical pion mass ($m_\pi \simeq 146\mathrm{MeV}$) using the HAL QCD method, with particular emphasis on two-pion exchange (TPE). The nucleon--flavor-singlet hadron potentials such as $\phi$-$N$, $J/\psi$-$N$, and $\eta_c$-$N$ are found to be attractive at all distances. Moreover, in the long-range region where one-pion exchange is forbidden, we observe lattice signatures consistent with TPE dominance. These results demonstrate that the HAL QCD method can extract universal long-distance phenomena, such as TPE, directly from nonperturbative QCD dynamics.

Dilepton azimuthal anisotropy from coherent $J/\psi$ photoproduction in hadronic Pb-Pb collisions

Xueli Hua, Xin Wu, Shuai Yang, Wangmei Zha

hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2610.35149

Dilepton azimuthal anisotropies from coherent $J/\psi$ photoproduction in Pb-Pb collisions provide a sensitive geometry reference for heavy-ion collisions.

Novel idea

Demonstrates that the second-order dilepton coefficient $A_2$ relative to the reaction plane, driven by quasireal photon polarization, can map nuclear overlap geometry and constrain interference effects without hadronic nonflow biases.

Method

Calculates the yield and $A_2$ for coherent $J/\psi$ photoproduction in $\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}} = 5.36$ TeV Pb-Pb collisions using an equivalent-photon and generalized VMD-Glauber framework, comparing $N+N$ and $N+S$ overlap prescriptions.

Key results

  • In the long-wavelength limit $p_{\mathrm{T}} \to 0$, $A_2$ approaches $-1/4$, indicating complete polarization alignment with the impact parameter.
  • Suppressing the overlap contribution in the $N+S$ prescription reduces the yield and strengthens the magnitude of integrated $A_2$ compared to $N+N$.
  • Two-source interference alters the vector structure and suppresses $|A_2|$ over broad momentum intervals while leaving the integrated yield nearly constant.

Why it matters to you

Offers a novel electromagnetic probe of collision geometry and coherence in heavy-ion collisions, complementing standard hadronic event-plane estimators and shedding light on low-$p_{\mathrm{T}}$ $J/\psi$ production mechanisms.

Caveats

Calculations neglect subsequent in-medium dissociation and regeneration, and direct comparisons to experimental data require matching precise experimental lepton acceptances and event-plane resolutions.

Abstract

Hadron-based event-plane estimators can be affected by nonflow and autocorrelation biases, especially at low multiplicity. Coherent $J/\psi$ photoproduction offers a complementary geometry reference through the correlation between quasireal-photon polarization and the reaction plane. We calculate the second-order dilepton coefficient $A_2$ relative to the reaction plane in hadronic Pb--Pb collisions at $\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.36$ TeV within an equivalent-photon and generalized vector-meson-dominance Glauber framework. Nuclear diffraction and two-source interference produce a pronounced transverse-momentum dependence, while the rapidity dependence is weak. We also compare two treatments of coherent scattering in the overlap region of the two colliding nuclei. Suppressing the contribution from this region reduces the yield and strengthens the integrated polarization--reaction-plane correlation, with the largest differences in central and semicentral collisions and smaller differences toward peripheral collisions. Joint measurements of the coherent yield and $A_2$ can constrain how hadronic interactions disrupt nuclear coherence and clarify the photoproduction mechanism. With sufficient tagged yields, azimuthal correlations involving decay leptons from photoproduced $J/\psi$ mesons could complement studies of collectivity, with potential future applications to small systems.
