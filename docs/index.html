    Parity-violating optical rotation in the Th-229 nuclear clock transitionSebastian Lahs, Thorsten Schumm, Victor V. Flambaum + 1 more · physics.atom-ph(keyword-ranked)
    Abstract

    The exceptionally low-energy nuclear transition in $^{229}$Th provides a new opportunity to study hadronic parity violation (PV) by optical methods. The PV nucleon--nucleus interaction admixes opposite-parity nuclear states into the $5/2^+$ ground state and the $3/2^+$ isomer, thereby inducing an electric-dipole amplitude $E1_{\rm PV}$ that interferes with the magnetic-dipole clock transition amplitude $M1$. In a single-valence-neutron Nilsson model, we find $|E1_{\rm PV}/M1| \sim 2\times10^{-5}$. This interference can be detected as a parity-violating nuclear optical rotation (PVNOR) of resonant laser light transmitted through a $^{229}$Th-doped crystal. For a highly-doped CaF$_2$ crystal fiber, the estimated rotation is of order $10^{-6}\,{\rm rad}$. A crossed-polarizer measurement can reach this sensitivity with existing ultraviolet laser technology. We also analyze systematic effects in the form of optical birefringence, stray magnetic-field-induced Faraday rotation, and local crystal defects. Such a measurement would provide the first direct probe of the PV neutron-nucleus interaction constant, $g_n$.

    The cosmological lithium problem and how decaying relics still solve itMaksym Ovchynnikov · hep-ph
    Abstract

    Lithium-7 in old stars is about three times below the prediction of standard big-bang nucleosynthesis, and deuterium lies slightly above it. Precise measurements of deuterium, helium-4, and the effective number of neutrino species now disfavor most previously proposed particle solutions. We find that relics that decay or evaporate with lifetimes of a few hours, mostly into electron antineutrinos of about a hundred MeV and into electrons or photons, still lower lithium-7 while respecting these measurements. Such relics include gauge bosons of $L_e-L_\tau$ or $B-3L_e$ and heavy neutral leptons with a dipole coupling or mixing with $\nu_\tau$, with masses of a few hundred MeV, and populations of evaporating Q-balls that carry lepton number. The decaying particles substantially alleviate the lithium problem, while Q-ball populations reach the $2\sigma$ range of lithium-7 adopted by the Particle Data Group. These relics can shift the effective number of neutrino species by up to about $2.5$ times the precision expected from the Simons Observatory, and a future Galactic supernova can test the particles.

    Balancing heavy flavour: Quantum number compensation in the hadronisation of charm and beauty particles in proton-proton collisionsIñaki Pardo Zambrana, Paul Veen, K. Keri Vos + 1 more · hep-ph
    Abstract

    The hadronisation of heavy quarks remains one of the least understood processes of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). While the production of heavy quark pairs can be calculated perturbatively in QCD, the subsequent formation of hadrons involves non-perturbative processes. The description of these processes is predominantly model dependent and sensitive to the underlying mechanisms of colour reconnection and string fragmentation. In particular, the conservation of flavour, baryon number and charge imposes constraints on how heavy-flavour hadrons are formed and balanced within the final state. In this work, we study how the quantum numbers associated with charm and beauty production are balanced in proton-proton collisions, using events simulated with the PYTHIA-8 event generator. Starting from perturbatively produced ($c\overline{c}$) and ($b\overline{b}$) pairs, we examine how the formation of heavy-flavour mesons and baryons, including $D^0$, $B^+$, $\Lambda_c^+$, and $\Lambda_b^0$, is accompanied by compensating particles elsewhere. The analysis focuses on correlations between the identified heavy hadron and the hadronic system responsible for balancing its conserved quantum numbers. We compare three hadronisation configurations that are implemented in PYTHIA: the Monash tune, colour-reconnection models incorporating string junctions (SJ), and close-packing (CP) approaches. We find that the SJ and CP tunes show a substantially enhanced contribution of baryons to the charm and beauty balancing yields compared with the Monash tune; this enhancement becomes more pronounced in high-multiplicity events. This study introduces a framework for characterising quantum-number compensation in heavy-flavour production and provides a new way to test hadronisation dynamics using identified charm- and beauty-hadron correlations, which can be explored at current and future collider experiments.

    Is Neutrino Disappearance Sensitive to T-Violation for Asymmetric Matter Profiles ?Gabriela Barenboim, Stephen J. Parke, Alberto Sánchez Vargas · hep-ph
    Abstract

    It is commonly stated in the literature that T violation is zero in neutrino disappearance channels. We show this claim is not general: it holds only for symmetric matter profiles or when the T/CP-violating phase ($\delta$) is 0 or $\pi$, that is, when the Jarlskog invariant is zero. The $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_\tau$, but not the $\nu_e$, survival probabilities carry a $\sin\delta$-dependent term that is non-zero in asymmetric matter and constitutes a genuine T asymmetry. The confusion arises from an improper T test that subtracts a probability from itself: a mathematical identity, not a physics result. In this paper, we clarify the distinctions between a proper and an improper T test as well as the proper and improper CP and CPT tests, for a general, arbitrary matter profile. For a toy model consisting of two different layers of constant density, we find that the maximum T-violation is obtained when one layer is close to the solar resonance, and the other is close to the atmospheric resonance. We address whether the matter asymmetry for DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande experiments is sufficiently large to allow the $\sin\delta$ dependence to be probed in $\nu_\mu$ survival probability. We also discuss future experimental scenarios that could probe T/CP-violation in $\nu_\mu$ survival probability.

    Abstract

    A proper quantum description of colliding beams reveals the spacetime structure of collision processes, going beyond the conventional picture of classical point-like particles undergoing short-distance scattering. This familiar picture emerges as an approximation in the classical beam limit and under specific conditions. Resolving the t-channel singularity in processes initiated by an unstable particle requires going beyond the short-distance treatment because this singularity is associated with a long-distance effect: the decay of the unstable particle, followed by the propagation of its decay products until they scatter with the other beam.
