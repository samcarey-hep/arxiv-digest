Harder, Better, Faster, Stronger: The $\Delta(1232)$ Resonance in Supernova QCD Axion Emission

Paolo Arcangeli, Konstantin Springmann, Michael Stadlbauer, Stefan Stelzl, Andreas Weiler

hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2611.33292

Including intermediate $\Delta(1232)$ resonances in nucleon-nucleon bremsstrahlung substantially hardens the supernova axion spectrum, doubling emission luminosity and increasing water-Cherenkov detection yields by an order of magnitude.

Novel idea

Identifies an omitted production channel in supernova axion emission where $NN \to NNa$ bremsstrahlung proceeds via an intermediate dynamical $\Delta(1232)$ resonance. Because the $\Delta$ is close to its mass shell when $E_a \sim M_\Delta - M_N \approx 293\text{ MeV}$, the narrow resonant propagator overcomes the Boltzmann phase-space suppression, producing a hard high-energy spectral bump that does not rely on a disputed abundance of thermal pions.

Method

Formulates the bremsstrahlung amplitude in heavy-baryon chiral perturbation theory with dynamical $\Delta$ fields through NLO in the small-scale expansion. Incorporates density-dependent baryon self-energies and in-medium $\Delta$ collisional broadening (using GiBUU inputs) across spherical Garching supernova models (18.6, 18.8, and 20.0 $M_\odot$ with SFHo EoS). Convolutes the resulting rates with magnetic conversion for Fermi-LAT $\gamma$-ray detection and $ap \to p\pi^0$ absorption cross sections for Hyper-Kamiokande.

Key results

  • For a hot $20.0\,M_\odot$ core, the $\Delta$ channel overtakes nucleon-only bremsstrahlung above $E_a \approx 175\text{ MeV}$ and increases the spectral intensity at $E_a = 293\text{ MeV}$ by a factor of 2.3.
  • Total axion luminosity roughly doubles at 1 s post-bounce, while escaping axion counts above $200\text{ MeV}$ over the first 9 s increase by a factor of $\sim 3$.
  • Water-Cherenkov event yields in Hyper-Kamiokande via $ap \to p\pi^0$ increase by a factor of $9.1^{+2.7}_{-5.0}$ due to the compound enhancement of harder emission and $\Delta$-mediated detector absorption.
  • Omitting the $\Delta$ resonance in $\gamma$-ray spectral templates biases reconstructed supernova core temperatures upward by $9\text{--}22\text{ MeV}$ and can falsely mimic thermal pion conversion at the $5\text{--}22\%$ level.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to your work on chiral EFT electroweak and light-mediator interactions, in-medium resonance dynamics (interfacing with transport tools like GiBUU), and low-energy supernova detection signals in large underground detectors.

Caveats

Calculations evaluate the strong interaction in one-pion exchange Born approximation without iterating the nucleon-nucleon tensor force or including $NNN\Delta$ contact terms; external $\Delta$ states in the equation of state are also neglected.

Abstract

The high-energy tail of supernova axion emission controls searches whose response grows with axion energy. We identify a novel production channel, in which the axion is emitted through an intermediate $\Delta(1232)$ resonance, which adds a hard component to the spectrum. We calculate the axion production rate from nucleon-nucleon Bremsstrahlung in chiral effective field theory through next-to-leading order. Including the $\Delta(1232)$ roughly doubles the luminosity at one second after bounce and increases the number of escaping axions above $200$ MeV by a factor of about $3$ over the first nine seconds, relative to the nucleon-only calculation. Adding this new production channel roughly doubles $\gamma$-ray counts from conversion in the progenitor's magnetic field and increases water-Cherenkov counts by about an order of magnitude through its combined effects on emission and absorption. The same hard component can mimic a higher core temperature or thermal-pion emission in $\gamma$-ray spectral fits. The $\Delta(1232)$ resonance is therefore crucial for predicting supernova axion signals and interpreting their spectra.

QED corrections to neutron and nuclear $\beta$-decay at order $\alpha^2$

Zehua Cao, Richard J. Hill, Ryan Plestid, Peter Vander Griend

hep-ph nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2611.34804

Complete two-loop $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ QED corrections to neutron and nuclear $\beta$-decay are calculated with exact electron mass dependence, reducing low-energy radiative correction uncertainties to the $10^{-6}$ level.

Novel idea

Provides the first complete calculation of two-loop $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ virtual, real-virtual, and double-real QED corrections to neutron and superallowed nuclear $\beta$-decay retaining full electron mass ($m_e/E$) dependence. This eliminates the dominant theoretical systematic in the low-energy radiative correction $\delta_{R,\text{static}}$, reducing its uncertainty by a factor of $\sim 70$.

Method

Computes two-loop form factors within heavy-particle electron effective theory using both direct timelike diagram evaluation and a subtracted dispersion relation over background-field cuts. Integrates the 18 master integrals by transforming differential equations to canonical $\epsilon$-form with Goncharov polylogarithms, fixing boundary conditions at zero recoil $w=1$. Combines virtual amplitudes with real-virtual, double-real, and pair-production phase space using a soft-photon slicing prescription.

Key results

  • Determines the static neutron radiative correction to be $\delta_{R,\text{static}} = (29.199 \pm 0.001) \times 10^{-3}$, reducing previous theory error from $\pm 0.07 \times 10^{-3}$ to $\pm 0.001 \times 10^{-3}$.
  • Performs the first direct QED computation of the two-loop zero-recoil heavy-light matching coefficient $E(m)$, analytically confirming the Czarnecki-Melnikov-Grozin result.
  • Computes phase-space-averaged $Z$-independent $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ corrections $\langle \delta^{(2,0)} \rangle$ for 15 superallowed $0^+ \to 0^+$ emitters, showing they contribute $(0.1\text{--}0.5) \times 10^{-4}$ to $\delta_R'$, matching experimental precision in $|V_{ud}|$.
  • Confirms full agreement with recent independent calculations (Crosas and Mereghetti) regarding electron-mass dependence in the $Z\alpha^2$ nuclear corrections.

Why it matters to you

A must-read by favorite authors Richard Hill and Ryan Plestid. It definitively nails the long-distance QED corrections to neutron and nuclear $\beta$-decay, ensuring that low-energy QED systematics are now negligible in $|V_{ud}|$ and CKM unitarity extractions.

Caveats

The calculation operates in the static nucleon limit; nuclear-structure dependent corrections ($\delta_{\text{NS}}$) and hadronic axial-vector inner radiative corrections (the box diagrams) remain external and dominate the overall $|V_{ud}|$ uncertainty.

Abstract

We compute two-loop QED corrections to neutron and nuclear beta decay, including full electron mass dependence and real radiation contributions. The result for the neutron, $\delta_{R,\,\rm static}= ( 29.199 \pm 0.001 )\times 10^{-3}$, implies a factor $\sim 70 $ reduction in the uncertainty of the low-energy radiative corrections compared to previous results obtained in the large energy/small electron-mass limit, $m_e/(m_n-m_p) \to 0$. For superallowed nuclear decays we find that the $Z$-independent order $\alpha^2$ corrections range from $(0.1-0.5) \times 10^{-4}$ i.e., of roughly the expected size and comparable to current uncertainty estimates.

NuSrcAgent: An AI Agent for High-Energy Neutrino Source Searches

Bei Zhou, Po-Wen Chang, Kohta Murase

astro-ph.HE hep-ex hep-pharXiv:2611.33324 · cross-list

NuSrcAgent is an LLM-powered AI agent that automates IceCube high-energy neutrino source searches and catalog stacking through natural-language conversation while preserving full analysis reproducibility.

Novel idea

Builds the first dedicated conversational AI agent for high-energy neutrino astronomy. It wraps unbinned likelihood analysis tools on public IceCube data (IceTracks-DR2), generates verifiable structured commands, enforces strict input checks, and writes custom code on-the-fly for user-specified non-power-law models and signal injections.

Method

Connects LLMs (Codex or Claude Code) via a specialized instructions module to an underlying analysis engine (`run_agent_wrap.py`). Implements an unbinned time-integrated and time-dependent likelihood framework supporting single sources, catalog scans, and stacking. Integrates an improved 'v3' background PDF algorithm utilizing adaptive sliding windows to mitigate energy proxy binning residuals.

Key results

  • Accurately reproduces IceCube's 14-year IceTracks-DR2 analysis of NGC 1068, matching public SkyLLH best-fit parameters ($\hat{n} \approx 98$, $\text{TS} \approx 22.8$).
  • Demonstrates automated code generation and execution for non-power-law neutrino spectra from turbulent accretion disk corona models directly from user-supplied GitHub repositories.
  • The newly introduced v3 background PDFs eliminate unphysical energy residuals at $0.6\text{--}1\text{ TeV}$, preventing spurious signal counts that previously biased stacking analyses.
  • Validates autonomous catalog scans and stacking on 387 dust-echo-like infrared flares and magnetar catalogs, matching published trial distributions.

Why it matters to you

Provides a useful case study in designing reliable, verifiable LLM agent workflows for phenomenological neutrino analysis, demonstrating how to prevent LLM hallucinations via wrapper validations and reproducible run records.

Caveats

Limited to public IceCube muon-track data releases; the accuracy of novel ad-hoc code written by the agent depends on the user's scrutiny of the generated scripts and background PDFs.

Abstract

Identifying the sources of high-energy cosmic neutrinos is crucial for understanding cosmic particle accelerators and probing new physics. IceCube provides public data that allow researchers to search independently for neutrino sources, but these analyses require substantial technical expertise and careful scientific choices. We present \system, the first publicly available AI agent for high-energy neutrino source searches. Compared to public likelihood codes, \system is easier to install and use, and supports more analysis types. To start an analysis, users need only specify a source or catalog. The agent takes natural-language requests, runs likelihood analyses on public IceCube event data, and saves the settings and calculation records so users can repeat the analyses. We demonstrate \system through single-source and catalog stacking analyses, obtaining results consistent with IceCube results. These examples also show its ability to search the literature, analyze non-power-law spectra using user-provided model code, and develop new analysis functions, such as signal-injection simulations. \system is also a powerful tool for improving and testing existing analysis code, as we demonstrate by using it to refine the background probability density functions and reduce residuals and spurious signal contributions in stacking analyses. \system makes high-energy neutrino data analysis more accessible to the broader research community. Code and usage instructions: \href{https://github.com/beizhouphys/NuSrcAgent}{\faGithub\ {\tt github.com/beizhouphys/NuSrcAgent}}.

Measuring the flavor composition of high-energy neutrinos from the Milky Way

Mauricio Bustamante

astro-ph.HE hep-ex hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2611.34797 · cross-list

Future multi-observatory neutrino telescope networks can measure the Milky Way's neutrino flavor composition to within 27%, distinguishing production mechanisms and probing neutrino decay up to $\tau/m \sim 1.5 \times 10^4\text{ s/eV}$.

Novel idea

Proposes using the measured flavor composition of the Galactic plane as an unoscillated, decay-free astrophysical baseline. Comparing this Galactic reference against the diffuse extragalactic neutrino flux breaks degeneracies between source magnetic field cooling (muon damping) and BSM neutrino decay over cosmological distances.

Method

Performs an Asimov binned-Poisson $\chi^2$ sensitivity forecast incorporating 8 running, under-construction, and proposed neutrino observatories (IceCube, KM3NeT, Baikal-GVD, P-ONE, TRIDENT, NEON, HUNT, IceCube-Gen2). Tracks, showers, and double cascades are modeled across three Galactic longitude segments and the diffuse extragalactic sky, profiling over 55 systematic nuisance parameters.

Key results

  • IceCube (15 yr) combined with KM3NeT (10 yr) can reject pure neutron-decay production ($f_{e,\text{S}}=1$, pure $\bar{\nu}_e$) in the inner Galaxy at $\sim 2\sigma$.
  • The full eight-telescope network will determine the source electron fraction $f_{e,\text{S}}$ in the inner Galaxy to within 27% at 68% C.L., separating standard pion decay ($f_{e,\text{S}}=1/3$) from muon damping ($f_{e,\text{S}}=0$) at $3\sigma$.
  • Comparing Galactic and extragalactic flavor compositions extends sensitivity to neutrino mass eigenstate decay ($\nu_2, \nu_3 \to \nu_1$) up to $\tau/m \approx 1.5 \times 10^4\text{ s/eV}$, improving upon current extragalactic bounds by a factor of 70.
  • Extragalactic muon synchrotron cooling can be detected at $3\sigma$ ($5\sigma$) for source classes with mean magnetic fields $B \gtrsim 11\text{ kG}$ ($22\text{ kG}$).

Why it matters to you

Offers an interesting perspective on using high-energy neutrino flavor ratios as a probe of BSM neutrino properties (specifically neutrino invisible decay), complementing laboratory neutrino oscillation programs.

Caveats

Relies on idealized response projections for six future facilities that are not yet constructed; assumes that flavor ratios are energy-independent across the TeV--PeV range.

Abstract

The origin of the Milky Way's high-energy neutrinos is debated. Their flavor composition reflects how they are made and could help settle it, but remains to be measured. We show that IceCube and KM3NeT, which see the inner Galaxy in complementary ways from opposite hemispheres, could start resolving its flavor composition in about a decade. With upcoming telescopes, they would infer the $\nu_e$ fraction at the sources to within 27%. A first comparison of Galactic and extragalactic flavor compositions could reveal strong magnetic fields in extragalactic sources or neutrino decay.

Screening of New Abelian Long-Range Forces

Marco Costa, Andrey Shkerin, Ananda Thomlinson

hep-ph astro-ph.HEarXiv:2611.34838

When ultralight gauge bosons acquire mass via the Higgs mechanism, scalar screening displaces the field in large celestial bodies, substantially weakening fifth-force and neutrino-oscillation bounds on $B-L$ and $L_a-L_b$ forces.

Novel idea

Demonstrates that UV-completing an ultralight gauge mediator into the Abelian Higgs Model (AHM) introduces vector charge screening around astronomical bodies. Large macroscopic charges drive the scalar field away from its vacuum expectation value, generating effective in-medium masses that screen the gauge field and invalidate naive Proca-theory exclusion limits.

Method

Solves the coupled classical nonlinear equations of motion for the gauge potential $A^0$ and scalar field $|\phi|$ for spherically symmetric, static charge distributions (Earth, Sun, neutron stars). Classifies screening into thin-shell ($m_{\phi,\text{eff}}R \gg 1$) and gradient ($m_{\phi,\text{eff}}R \ll 1$) regimes governed by $\tilde{g} = g/\sqrt{\lambda} = m_V/(\sqrt{2}m_\phi)$, mapping the modified field profiles onto experimental bounds.

Key results

  • For gauged $B-L$, screening reduces the interaction range; for $\tilde{g} \gtrsim 10$, MICROSCOPE satellite limits vanish for $g \gtrsim 10^{-24}$, opening an unconstrained parameter window $10^{-24} \lesssim g \lesssim 10^{-23}$ before Eöt-Wash constraints apply.
  • For $L_e - L_{\mu/\tau}$ symmetries, solar screening suppresses the flavor-electric potential at Earth's orbit for $\tilde{g} \gtrsim 10^{-4}$, evading solar-induced matter effects in terrestrial neutrino oscillation experiments.
  • Solar neutrino oscillation limits on $L_e - L_{\mu/\tau}$ relax as $g \propto \tilde{g}$, rendering terrestrial baseline oscillation searches more constraining than solar neutrino bounds for $\tilde{g} \gtrsim 10^{-3}$.
  • For $L_\mu - L_\tau$, strong screening inside neutron stars eliminates dipole radiation in neutron star binaries, evading orbital period decay constraints.

Why it matters to you

Crucial for interpreting matter-induced neutrino non-standard interactions (NSI) and ultralight mediator bounds in neutrino oscillation experiments (DUNE, solar/atmospheric neutrinos), proving that bounds from long-range forces are strongly model-dependent when the vector mass is dynamical.

Caveats

Screening from localized surrounding topography for terrestrial torsion balances at $m_V > 10^{-13}\text{ eV}$ was not comprehensively simulated; binary neutron star screening was evaluated in a quasi-static regime.

Abstract

We study how bounds on new $U(1)$ long-range forces, resulting from gauging the Standard Model symmetries $B-L$ and $L_a-L_b$, $a,b=e,\mu,\tau$, are modified if the mediator mass is generated by the Higgs mechanism. Large baryonic and leptonic charges of astronomical bodies displace the Higgs field from its vacuum value, resulting in charge screening. Depending on the $U(1)$ Higgs mass, the screening relaxes the bounds on the new force strength and mediator mass from equivalence principle violation and neutrino oscillations experiments, and eliminates the bounds on the muonic $L_\mu-L_\tau$ force from neutron star binaries.

Collisional dynamics of neutrino flavor conversion: angular structures, inhomogeneity, and decoherence

Masamichi Zaizen

hep-ph astro-ph.HEarXiv:2611.33815

A linear stability analysis and nonlinear quantum kinetic simulations reveal that anisotropic neutrino backgrounds alter the wavenumber dependence of collisional flavor instabilities, producing distinct near/gapless and far/gapped branches.

Novel idea

The work extends previous studies on collisional flavor conversion (CFC) in isotropic and homogeneous neutrino gases to multi-energy, multi-angle, and spatially inhomogeneous systems with anisotropic distributions.

Method

The author performs a linear stability analysis using the dispersion relation of quantum kinetic equations and conducts direct nonlinear quantum kinetic simulations to track the resulting flavor evolution, spatial mode coupling, and decoherence.

Key results

  • Near/gapless modes exhibit maximum growth rates localized near the zero mode, whereas far/gapped modes peak at finite collective-scale wavenumbers.
  • Eigenvectors of near/gapless modes show strong energy dependence ($ imes 1/(1+E_ u^2)$), while far/gapped modes are energy-insensitive and forward-peaked.
  • Collisional decoherence limits the growth of spatial inhomogeneities, leaving the neutrino populations nearly homogeneous despite mode-coupling cascades.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to neutrino event generators and quantum kinetic treatments of dense neutrino transport in core-collapse supernovae and neutron-star mergers, where interaction modelling and collective phenomena intersect.

Caveats

The analysis assumes specific relaxation-form collision terms and ignores heavy-leptonic opacities ($ au_x = 0$) and classical relaxation processes.

Abstract

Self-interacting neutrino gases exhibit collective behavior, in which additional incoherent collisions can induce collisional neutrino-flavor instability. We investigate the effects of energy dependence, anisotropy, and spatial inhomogeneity on the resulting flavor conversion through linear stability analysis and nonlinear quantum kinetic simulations. We find contrasting wavenumber dependences for the near/gapless and far/gapped branches, with maximum growth rates at collision-scale shifted wavenumbers and at collective-scale wavenumbers, respectively. Their eigenvectors reveal distinct energy and angular structures, which are reflected in the subsequent establishment of flavor swap and equipartition. The nonlinear evolution also features secondary flavor growth associated with changes in the stability of collective modes as the neutrino background evolves. Despite the spreading of flavor coherence across spatial scales due to nonlinear mode coupling, collisional decoherence tends to limit the development of spatial inhomogeneities, leaving the neutrino populations nearly homogeneous. These findings represent a step towards understanding and predicting the nonlinear flavor evolution of dense neutrino gases interacting with their surroundings, particularly in explosive astrophysical environments.

Extends the free-baryon analogue of neutrinoless double-beta decay ($0 uetaeta$) to strange initial states (hyperon channels) while treating light- and heavy-Majorana exchange under consistent form-factor and four-body phase-space prescriptions.
