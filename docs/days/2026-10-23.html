10worth readingHNLs at Forward Facilities

Probing Heavy Neutral Leptons through New Gauge Interactions at the Forward Physics Facilities

Francis M. Burk, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Bhaskar Dutta, Tao Han, Aparajitha Karthikeyan, Doojin Kim

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.30240

Heavy neutral leptons produced via $U(1)$ gauge boson bremsstrahlung and Drell-Yan at LHC forward facilities can probe active-sterile mixing down to canonical seesaw predictions across all lepton flavors.

Novel idea

The paper decouples heavy neutral lepton (HNL) production from active-sterile mixing by utilizing an intermediate $Z'$ gauge boson from $U(1)_{B-L}$, $U(1)_{B-3L_\tau}$, or $U(1)_B$ groups. It provides the first demonstration that the emulsion-based FASER$\nu$ detector has sensitivity to HNLs, and systematically compares production at the ATLAS interaction point against secondary hadron production in forward absorbers (TAN/TAXN).

Method

The authors implement $Z'$ interactions into FeynRules and WHIZARD, computing Drell-Yan production for $m_{Z'} \gtrsim 2\text{ GeV}$ alongside quasi-real initial-state radiation proton bremsstrahlung for $m_{Z'} \lesssim 2\text{ GeV}$ with Breit-Wigner form factors. Geometrical acceptances, beam kinematics at $\sqrt{s} = 14\text{ TeV}$, and detector fiducial volumes are evaluated for FASER, FASER$\nu$, FASER2, and FASER$\nu$2 for displaced decays into $\nu\pi^0$, $\nu\ell^+\ell^-$, and $\ell^\pm+\text{hadrons}$ under flavor-democratic and $\tau$-dominated mixing benchmarks with fixed $m_{Z'}/m_N = 2.1$. Secondary hadron showers in the TAN/TAXN absorbers are modeled using GEANT4.

Key results

  • Bremsstrahlung mediated by $\rho$ and $\omega$ resonances dominates for $m_{Z'} \lesssim 1\text{ GeV}$, while Drell-Yan production drives sensitivity for $m_N \gtrsim 1\text{ GeV}$ up to tens of GeV.
  • For the baryonic $U(1)_B$ benchmark with $g_X = 10^{-2}$, FASER2 reaches active-sterile mixing $|U|^2$ down to canonical Type-I seesaw expectations ($|U|^2 \sim 10^{-11}-10^{-12}$) for $m_N \approx 2-5\text{ GeV}$ across all three neutrino flavors.
  • FASER$\nu$ achieves sensitivity to HNLs for the first time, reaching $|U|^2 \sim 10^{-5}$ via $N\to \nu\mu^+\mu^-$ and $\ell^\pm+\text{hadrons}$ for $m_N \approx 3-5\text{ GeV}$.
  • Signal production directly at the ATLAS interaction point heavily dominates over secondary fixed-target production within the neutral particle absorbers.

Why it matters to you

Directly expands the physics scope of the Forward Physics Facility (FPF) to dark-sector neutrino portals, complementing beam-dump and near-detector searches at SBN and DUNE.

Caveats

The analysis assumes a fixed mediator-to-sterile mass ratio $m_{Z'}/m_N = 2.1$ and treats anomaly-canceling chiral fermions required by the gauge groups as decoupled spectators.

Abstract

We investigate the prospects for improving the heavy neutral lepton (HNL) searches at the far-forward LHC experiments FASER and FASER$\nu$, as well as their proposed HL-LHC successors, FASER2 and FASER$\nu$2, using new HNL production mechanisms. We consider scenarios in which HNLs couple directly to a new $U(1)$ gauge boson and decay into visible Standard Model final states through mixing with active neutrinos. Focusing on the $U(1)_{B-L}$, $U(1)_{B-3L_\tau}$, and $U(1)_B$ gauge extensions as benchmark models, we study HNL production through proton bremsstrahlung and Drell--Yan processes, which probe complementary mediator-mass regimes. We analyze several visible decay channels, including charged leptons, neutral mesons, and other hadrons, and evaluate the projected sensitivities for different neutrino-flavor mixing patterns. We find that these new production mechanisms that differ qualitatively from the conventional picture in which both production and decay are controlled by active-sterile mixing enable the far-forward LHC program to probe previously unexplored regions of HNL parameter space, with the first-ever demonstration of FASER$\nu$ having sensitivity to HNLs and FASER2 and FASER$\nu$2 offering substantially enhanced sensitivity during the HL-LHC era, even reaching the canonical seesaw prediction for all three lepton flavors. Our results demonstrate that new gauge interactions can substantially enhance HNL production independently of the active-sterile mixing that governs their visible decays, opening complementary opportunities to explore HNLs at the forward physics facilities.

Neutrino Transition Polarizability

Matteo Borrello, Michele Tammaro

hep-ph hep-ex hep-tharXiv:2610.30252

Neutrino transition polarizability via an intermediate pseudoscalar enables rapid heavy neutral lepton decays into displaced photon pairs, reshaping search strategies at DUNE ND, LHCb, Belle II, and FCC-ee.

Novel idea

The work introduces Neutrino Transition Polarizability (NTP), coupling active neutrinos to heavy neutral leptons (HNLs) through a light pseudoscalar $\phi$ with a diphoton vertex $\phi F\widetilde{F}$, embeddable into an inverse seesaw model. When $m_N > m_\phi$, the fast two-body decay $N \to \nu \phi$ drastically shortens the HNL lifetime relative to weak decays, suppressing traditional downstream beam-dump signatures in favor of displaced photon pairs produced directly inside detectors.

Method

The authors formulate an effective Lagrangian and a UV-complete inverse seesaw realization with spontaneous $U(1)_L$ breaking. They calculate coherent electromagnetic upscattering $\nu + A \to N + \gamma + A$ at the DUNE Near Detector, as well as production through $B$-meson, $W$, and $Z$ decays at LHCb, Belle II, and FCC-ee. Displaced diphoton vertex detection efficiencies are calculated enforcing angular separation criteria ($\theta_{\gamma\gamma} \gtrsim 3^\circ$) and fiducial decay geometries.

Key results

  • For $m_\phi = 50\text{ MeV}$ and $g_\gamma = 10^{-4}\text{ GeV}^{-1}$, HNL proper decay lengths drop below millimeters for $|U_N|^2 \gtrsim 10^{-10}$, while $\phi \to \gamma\gamma$ yields displaced vertices on centimeter-to-meter scales.
  • The DUNE Near Detector probes the transition coupling down to $\lambda_T \sim 10^{-3}$ and active-sterile mixing $|U_N|^2 \sim 10^{-6}$ for $m_N \lesssim 1\text{ GeV}$ via coherent electromagnetic scattering.
  • LHCb achieves sensitivity to $|U_N|^2 \sim 10^{-7}-10^{-8}$ via displaced $B$ decays and extends mass coverage up to $15\text{ GeV}$ via displaced $W$ decays.
  • FCC-ee at the $Z$ pole can probe mixing down to $|U_N|^2 \sim 10^{-11}$ across $m_N \sim 1-80\text{ GeV}$.

Why it matters to you

Provides a well-motivated dark-portal upscattering scenario with displaced diphotons specifically testable in the DUNE Near Detector liquid argon environment.

Caveats

The DUNE ND projection optimistically assumes near-zero background for isolated displaced diphoton vertices, which requires detailed verification against neutral pion misidentification.

Abstract

We study Neutrino Transition Polarizability, where a light pseudoscalar $\phi$ couples active neutrinos to heavy neutral leptons $N$ and interacts with two photons. We consider an effective description of the active - sterile interaction, and a realization within the inverse seesaw mechanism; the coupling to photons is generated by heavy charged states. For $m_N>m_\phi$, the decay $N\to\nu\phi$ can greatly shorten the heavy neutrino lifetime, while $\phi\to\gamma\gamma$ produces a displaced photon pair. This changes the relevant search strategies, favoring production and decay inside detectors. We discuss existing constraints and estimate the sensitivity of displaced photon searches at DUNE, Belle II, LHCb and FCC-ee, highlighting the complementarity of neutrino scattering and meson and electroweak boson decays.

Machine Learning Neutrino-Nucleus Cross Sections II: More Leptons

Daniel C. Hackett, Joshua Isaacson, Shirley Weishi Li, Karla Tame-Narvaez, Michael L. Wagman

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.30338

Imposing electroweak structure functions in neural-network cross-section models enables multi-channel neutrino-nucleus modeling across flavors and horn polarities, recovering DUNE oscillation parameters without bias under realistic flux uncertainties.

Novel idea

The paper extends data-driven neural-network cross-section modeling to multi-channel long-baseline oscillation analyses involving 10 independent structure functions ($W_{1-5}$ and $\overline{W}_{1-5}$) to account for non-isoscalar argon and finite charged-lepton masses. It demonstrates that near-detector measurements can constrain these structure functions to successfully extract $\sin^2\theta_{23}$, $\Delta m^2_{31}$, and the CP phase $\delta_\text{CP}$ simultaneously without event-by-event neutrino energy reconstruction.

Method

A toy cross-section model based on leading-order deep-inelastic scattering with CT18NNLO parton distribution functions generates simulated DUNE near- and far-detector samples in forward- and reverse-horn-current modes for electron and muon channels. Two neural networks represent neutrino and antineutrino structure functions, trained on 2D outgoing-lepton kinematics using variable projection (VarPro) to optimize linear normalization prefactors. The trained networks evaluate an unbinned Poisson likelihood on oscillated far-detector events, incorporating the full DUNE flux covariance matrix and detector resolution smearing.

Key results

  • The 10 learned structure functions accurately capture differential neutrino and antineutrino event distributions across $E_\ell$ and $\cos\theta$ across all channels.
  • Joint fits to simulated far-detector mock data recover the injected oscillation parameters ($\sin^2\theta_{23} = 0.470$, $\Delta m^2_{31} = 2.521\times 10^{-3}\text{ eV}^2$, $\delta_\text{CP} = 207^\circ$) without significant bias.
  • Confidence regions obtained with the machine-learned model match the statistical precision achieved when using the exact underlying cross section.
  • Incorporating correlated flux nuisance parameters between near and far detectors is shown to be essential; ignoring them introduces noticeable parameter biases in multi-channel fits.

Why it matters to you

Directly addresses core research interests in neutrino-nucleus interaction modeling, generator uncertainty reduction, and oscillation systematics for DUNE.

Caveats

The framework is tested on a simplified deep-inelastic-scattering toy model rather than realistic neutrino-nucleus generator outputs with nuclear exciton and final-state interaction dynamics.

Abstract

Neutrino-nucleus cross sections are essential inputs for long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments, yet remain challenging to model with high precision. Recent work demonstrated that fully black-box data-driven cross-section models cannot accurately generalize between near- and far-detector fluxes, but imposing Standard-Model symmetries via structure-function parameterizations provides a well-posed learning problem in a simplified DUNE-inspired muon-neutrino disappearance analysis. Here, we extend this framework to multiple lepton flavors and both neutrino and antineutrino beam modes, incorporating the electron appearance channel and enabling a simultaneous determination of $\sin^2\theta_{23}$, $\Delta m^2_{31}$, and the CP-violating phase $\delta_{\rm CP}$. We find that the learned cross section model recovers the injected oscillation parameters without significant bias, with constraints comparable to those obtained using the exact underlying cross section. Flux uncertainties have more significant effects in the multi-channel case, and incorporating flux nuisance parameters into the learning problem is required for accurate results. Our results demonstrate that this data-driven approach can be extended to the multi-channel measurements needed for long-baseline neutrino oscillation analyses.

A consistent neutrino determination of the low-energy weak mixing angle

M. Atzori Corona, M. Cadeddu, N. Cargioli, F. Dordei, L. Ferro, C. Giunti + 2 more

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.31254

A global fit combining low-energy CE$\nu$NS and neutrino-electron scattering yields a consistent, atomic-theory-independent determination of the weak mixing angle, $\sin^2\theta_W^\text{low}(\nu\,\text{data}) = 0.233^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$.

Novel idea

The authors perform the first comprehensive, unified global extraction of the low-energy weak mixing angle exclusively from low-energy neutrino scattering, combining CE$\nu$NS and neutrino-electron scattering within a consistent electroweak radiative-correction scheme. They also update the atomic parity violation (APV) determination on $^{133}\text{Cs}$ using Dirac-Hartree-Fock-Slater atomic wavefunctions and proton-cesium elastic scattering constraints on the neutron skin.

Method

The study constructs profiled $\chi^2$ functions for CE$\nu$NS data (COHERENT CsI, Ar, Ge; CONUS+; TEXONO Ge; $\nu$GeN; XENONnT, PandaX-4T, LZ) and $\nu$-electron scattering data (TEXONO, LSND, LAMPF, BNL-E734, CHARM-II). Full experimental and theoretical systematics are included, covering flux normalizations, quenching factors, detector thresholds, and nuclear form factors. Electroweak radiative corrections are consistently evaluated in the $\overline{\text{MS}}$ scheme at zero momentum transfer.

Key results

  • The global neutrino fit yields $\sin^2\theta_W^{\rm low}(\nu\,\text{data}) = 0.233^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$, in excellent agreement with the Standard Model low-energy plateau prediction of $0.23873(5)$.
  • The neutrino-electron scattering subset yields $\sin^2\theta_W^{\rm low} = 0.232^{+0.008}_{-0.007}$, dominating the global fit over the CE$\nu$NS-only extraction of $0.244^{+0.024}_{-0.021}$.
  • The updated $^{133}\text{Cs}$ APV determination yields $\sin^2\theta_W^{\rm low} = 0.2393(24)$ (using Dzuba et al.) and $0.2415(24)$ (using Porsev et al.) after propagating the experimental neutron skin uncertainty $R_n(^{133}\text{Cs}) = 4.94 \pm 0.21\text{ fm}$.

Why it matters to you

Provides a crucial benchmark for precision low-energy electroweak tests and CEvNS interaction physics that is completely independent of atomic many-body theory uncertainties.

Caveats

The combined neutrino determination remains roughly three times less precise than atomic parity violation, primarily due to flux and quenching factor systematics in CE$\nu$NS.

Abstract

The weak mixing angle, $\sin^2\theta_W$, is a fundamental parameter of the Standard Model and a sensitive probe of electroweak physics across various energy scales. At low momentum transfer, the Standard Model prediction approaches a well-defined low-energy plateau, providing a target for precision tests of the electroweak theory. At the lowest momentum transfer, atomic parity violation (APV) in cesium provides one of the most precise determinations of the weak mixing angle. Here, we review and update the status of this determination and compare it with other available probes. We then consistently reanalyze low-energy neutrino scattering data and combine the resulting determinations from coherent elastic scattering (CE$\nu$NS) and neutrino-electron scattering. The complementary sensitivity of these processes to electroweak couplings enables a neutrino-based determination of the low-energy weak mixing angle, accounting for electroweak radiative corrections and relevant theoretical uncertainties. Our global analysis yields $\sin^2\theta_W^{\rm low}(\nu\,\text{data}) = 0.233^{+0.007}_{-0.006}$. We assess the consistency and relative impact of the individual neutrino measurements and discuss the prospects for future CE$\nu$NS and neutrino-electron scattering experiments to improve the precision of this determination. Our analysis provides an independent test of the Standard Model at low momentum transfer, complementary to that provided by atomic parity violation.

Probing imperfect axions with nuclear clocks

Salvatore Bottaro, Andrea Caputo, Gilad Perez, Yotam Soreq, Alfredo Stanzione

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-exarXiv:2610.30238

Departures from the Peccei-Quinn solution generate linear couplings of the axion to nucleons, enabling $^{229}\text{Th}$ nuclear clocks to probe axion dark matter and static fifth forces beyond EDM bounds.

Novel idea

The authors demonstrate that imperfect axions—arising from explicit Peccei-Quinn breaking or quark chromo-electric dipole moments—shift the axion vacuum and generate a nuclear clock response linear in the axion field ($a/f_a$). This linear signal oscillates at the Compton frequency $m_a$ (rather than $2m_a$), scales as $1/f_a$, integrates faster with measurement time, and permits searches for Earth-sourced static fifth forces without requiring an axion dark matter background.

Method

Using two-flavor chiral perturbation theory with an axion and CP-violating spurions, the authors calculate vacuum alignment and evaluate variations in the nucleon mass $\delta m_N/m_N$. This is translated into a $^{229}\text{Th}$ nuclear clock frequency shift via the isomeric transition enhancement factor $K \sim 10^4-10^5$. Constraints on the residual angle $\theta_\text{eff}$ are obtained by profiling over experimental limits on neutron and $^{199}\text{Hg}$ electric dipole moments.

Key results

  • In pure explicit PQ breaking, electric dipole moment constraints restrict the residual angle to $|\theta_\text{eff}| \lesssim 1.5 \times 10^{-10}$, while cancellations with quark EDMs allow $|\theta_\text{eff}| \sim 10^{-9}$.
  • For axion dark matter, the linear clock response overtakes the CP-even quadratic response after an exposure time $t_\text{exp} \gtrsim 2\text{ days} \times (10^{-10}/\theta_\text{eff})^2$.
  • Projected nuclear clock sensitivity probes axion decay constants up to $f_a \sim 10^{16}\text{ GeV}$ for $m_a \lesssim 10^{-17}\text{ eV}$, outperforming equivalence-principle and astrophysical bounds.
  • Static axion fields sourced by the Earth induce frequency shifts in $^{229}\text{Th}$ clocks that constrain $f_a$ across broad mass windows independently of dark matter assumptions.

Why it matters to you

Directly connects precision $^{229}\text{Th}$ nuclear clock physics to fundamental CP-violation, axion EFTs, and low-energy precision probes of physics beyond the Standard Model.

Caveats

The magnitude of the chromo-EDM contribution depends on the poorly known low-energy baryonic matrix element $\tilde{c}_1$, and limits assume the nominal isomeric enhancement factor $K \sim 10^5$.

Abstract

We show that $^{229}$Th nuclear clocks can probe departures from the Peccei-Quinn (PQ) solution to the strong CP problem. Explicit PQ breaking or additional CP-violating interactions can shift the minimum of the axion potential and generate CP-violating couplings to matter, inducing a clock response linear in the axion field. We investigate explicit PQ breaking and quark chromo-electric dipole operators as representative realizations of these \textit{imperfect axion} scenarios, consistent with electric dipole moment constraints. For axion dark matter, the linear signal oscillates at the Compton frequency and can dominate the quadratic response. Even without axion dark matter, the Earth can source a static axion field, enabling fifth-force searches with nuclear clocks.

Localized Neutrinos in Two Dark Dimensions

Waqas Ahmed, George K. Leontaris

hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2610.30526

Localizing sterile neutrinos near the Standard Model brane in two dark dimensions restores perturbative Yukawa couplings and suppresses Kaluza-Klein mixing, evading severe reactor bounds while yielding distinct beta-decay kinks.

Novel idea

In 6D models with two dark dimensions (fundamental scale around 10 TeV), flat-bulk right-handed neutrinos suffer severe volume suppression, demanding non-perturbatively large Yukawas that over-excite a dense 2D Kaluza-Klein tower. The authors introduce a Gaussian localization profile for sterile neutrinos near the 4D brane. This breaks the link between the gravitational compactification radius and the sterile excitation scale, yielding a 1D shell spectrum of brane-visible modes with masses $M_s^2 = 8 u^2 s$ and $1/s$-suppressed mixings.

Method

The authors solve the 2D transverse harmonic oscillator problem on a compact torus, applying parity selection rules to eliminate odd modes that vanish at a pointlike brane. They perform a unitary rotation within degenerate shells to isolate the single brane-coupled state per level. The resulting active-sterile mixing matrix is embedded into a 3-flavor framework to compute reactor antineutrino disappearance and tritium beta-decay spectral modifications, benchmarked against Daya Bay and KATRIN data.

Key results

  • For $M_6 = 10\text{ TeV}$, $R \approx 0.765\ \mu\text{m}$, and localization scale $\mu = 1.2\text{ eV}$, observed active masses are generated with perturbative $\mathcal{O}(1)$ 6D Yukawa couplings.
  • The first brane-visible sterile shell is shifted to $M_1 \approx 3.4\text{ eV}$, producing rapid oscillations that average out at reactor baselines and suppress electron-row non-unitarity ($\\eta_e < 10^{-3}$ for normal ordering).
  • Predicts distinct multi-threshold kinks in the tritium beta-decay spectrum where kink masses scale as $\sqrt{s}$ and transition probabilities fall as $1/s$.

Why it matters to you

It offers an alternative mechanism to generate eV-to-tens-of-eV sterile towers without violating short-baseline reactor constraints, of direct relevance to global neutrino oscillation analyses and KATRIN sterile searches.

Caveats

The Gaussian localizer is an effective IR approximation rather than a complete UV-complete 6D theory. Cosmological bounds from thermal production of the heavy localized shells were not computed in full and require dedicated early-universe modeling.

Abstract

We study neutrino masses in a six-dimensional framework with two compact dark dimensions. In the conventional flat-bulk realization, the sterile-neutrino zero mode is strongly volume suppressed, so a fundamental scale near $10~\mathrm{TeV}$ requires a large higher-dimensional Yukawa coupling to reproduce the observed neutrino masses, while the accompanying two-dimensional Kaluza--Klein tower is strongly constrained by oscillation data. We show that localizing the sterile-neutrino wave function near the Standard-Model brane substantially alleviates both effects. Using a Gaussian localizer, realistic Dirac neutrino masses are obtained with perturbative, order-one Yukawa couplings, while the brane-visible sterile excitations form a shell spectrum with $M_s^2=8\mu^2 s$, and squared active--sterile mixings that decrease as $1/s$. The localization scale therefore separates the experimentally relevant sterile-neutrino spectrum from the gravitational compactification scale. We derive the resulting three-flavour structure and study its phenomenological consequences for reactor oscillations and tritium beta decay, including comparisons with current flat-LED and KATRIN constraints.

Natural Chlorine Enhances Antineutrino Detection in the Deep Ocean

Ruike Cao, Iwan Morton-Blake, Donglian Xu, Qichao Chang, Shuhua Hao, Jingtao Huang + 13 more

hep-ex astro-ph.IMarXiv:2610.30754

Naturally abundant chlorine in seawater provides an efficient delayed-neutron-capture target ($8.6\text{ MeV}$ $\gamma$ cascade, $\tau \sim 130\ \mu\text{s}$) that doubles the core-collapse supernova discovery reach of deep-sea neutrino telescopes.

Novel idea

Instead of relying on collective rate increases or artificial detector doping, deep-sea water Cherenkov telescopes can achieve event-by-event inverse beta decay (IBD) identification via delayed neutron capture on naturally occurring $^{35}\text{Cl}$. Capture on chlorine emits an $\sim 8.6\text{ MeV}$ gamma cascade with a shorter capture time ($130\ \mu\text{s}$) than on hydrogen ($2.2\text{ MeV}$, $200\ \mu\text{s}$), enabling delayed-coincidence tagging in sparsely instrumented multi-PMT modules.

Method

The detection concept was modeled for the TRIDENT telescope using Geant4 simulations of seawater composition, optical propagation, and backgrounds (such as $^{40}\text{K}$, PMT dark noise, and atmospheric muons). Event selection incorporates local PMT coincidence multiplicity, prompt-delayed timing, mean angular spread, and inter-module muon vetoes. The mechanism was validated experimentally at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory (CJPL) using an AmBe source and a TRIDENT hybrid digital optical module immersed in salted water.

Key results

  • Delayed neutron capture on $^{35}\text{Cl}$ extends the TRIDENT $5\sigma$ core-collapse supernova discovery distance from $\sim 50\text{ kpc}$ (prompt only) to $> 100\text{ kpc}$ for a $27\ M_\odot$ progenitor.
  • Underground validation at CJPL in NaCl solution confirmed a correlated delayed-capture excess at $> 3.4\sigma$ with a fitted capture time $\tau_n = 0.14 \pm 0.04\text{ ms}$.
  • The signal remains identifiable at $\sim 2\sigma$ even after adding KCl to replicate the deep-sea $^{40}\text{K}$ radioactive environment.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts low-energy neutrino astrophysics and transient detection programs, providing a concrete delayed-coincidence tagging framework relevant to next-generation water telescopes like TRIDENT, KM3NeT, and P-ONE.

Caveats

Bioluminescence backgrounds were not included in the time-dependent background simulation. Identifying low-energy events requires high optical module cleanliness and low accidental coincidences from $^{40}\text{K}$ decays.

Abstract

Large-volume neutrino telescopes such as IceCube and KM3NeT exploit natural ice or water as detection media, but their sparse instrumentation makes MeV neutrino interactions difficult to identify individually. We show that chlorine naturally abundant in seawater provides an intrinsic neutron-capture target that enables efficient tagging of electron antineutrinos through inverse beta decay. In contrast to the 2.2 MeV $\gamma$-ray from neutron capture on hydrogen, capture on chlorine produces an $\sim$8.6 MeV $\gamma$-ray cascade on a shorter time scale, yielding a distinctive delayed-coincidence signature. Using measured seawater properties at the TRIDENT site and a detector-level simulation, we find that chlorine-assisted tagging can extend the core-collapse supernova 5$\sigma$ discovery reach from $\sim$50 to over 100 kpc. We validate the underlying detection principle with a salted-water experiment at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory, observing correlated neutron-capture signatures under suppressed cosmic muon background conditions. Natural seawater thus transforms the deep ocean into an intrinsically enhanced antineutrino detection medium, opening new opportunities for MeV neutrino astronomy and distributed measurements of reactor and geophysical antineutrinos.

$N \to \Delta$ Transition Form Factors in the Thermal AdS/QCD Soft-Wall Model with Modified Propagators

Narmin Nasibova, Xerxes D. Arsiwalla, Shakir Nagiyev

hep-ph hep-lat hep-th nucl-tharXiv:2610.31395

Regularizing the divergent axial-vector propagator in the thermal soft-wall AdS/QCD model enables the calculation of electromagnetic and axial-vector $N \to \Delta$ transition form factors at finite temperature.

Novel idea

The transverse axial-vector equation of motion in soft-wall AdS/QCD diverges asymptotically due to the scalar vacuum expectation value that breaks chiral symmetry. The authors introduce an interior constraint at small holographic coordinate $r$ matching the axial equation to the analytical vector propagator, implementing a momentum- and temperature-dependent IR cutoff that tames the divergence without discarding the soft-wall background.

Method

The regularized boundary-value problem is solved numerically using a Runge-Kutta scheme for both the standard axial equation and a parametrically deformed version. Transition matrix elements of vector and axial currents are evaluated to extract Jones-Scadron electromagnetic form factors ($G_{M1}, G_{E2}, G_{C2}$) and Adler axial form factors ($C_3, C_4, C_5, C_6$) as functions of $Q^2$ and temperature $T$ up to the deconfinement point.

Key results

  • At $T=0$, the deformed axial propagator ($\\tau = -1$) yields $C_i(Q^2)$ profiles in good agreement with lattice QCD and empirical parametrizations over the full $Q^2$ range, whereas the standard propagator only matches for $Q^2 > 2\text{ GeV}^2$.
  • All seven transition form factors exhibit monotonic thermal suppression as $T \to T_c$, reflecting thermal screening of currents and loss of baryonic spin-isospin coherence.
  • The quadrupole ratios $R_{EM}$ and $R_{SM}$ remain small and negative at low $Q^2$, consistent with pion-cloud dominance that weakens at elevated temperatures.

Why it matters to you

While $N \to \Delta$ axial form factors are fundamental to resonance neutrino-nucleus scattering modeling in generators, this work uses bottom-up holographic AdS/QCD rather than lattice QCD or chiral EFT, making it primarily of formal interest.

Caveats

The proposed regularization and the sign flip in the deformed axial equation ($\\tau = -1$) are purely phenomenological fixes lacking a rigorous 5D action derivation. The calculations do not incorporate nuclear medium effects or finite baryon chemical potential.

Abstract

We investigate electromagnetic (EM) and axial vector (AV) form factors of the nucleon-Delta baryon transition within the thermal AdS/QCD soft-wall model. While the vector bulk-to-boundary propagator admits a well-behaved analytical solution, the transverse axial vector equation of motion involves the vacuum expectation value of the bifundamental scalar (generating chiral symmetry breaking). Due to this, the axial propagator diverges asymptotically, rendering it unsuitable for computing observables in the soft-wall model. To address this problem, we implement a regularization for the axial propagator in the form of a momentum-dependent cut-off, that identifies its correct physical regime. This is achieved via an interior constraint approach to the boundary-value problem, that determines the cut-off point. The consistency of this procedure is verified near the UV limit. As a proof of concept, we test this modified propagator (and also a parametrically deformed version of it) to estimate axial vector form factors of the $N \to \Delta$ transition, and find results moderately comparable with form factor profiles from experimental observations, lattice QCD, and other theoretical models. Equipped with this regularization, the thermal soft-wall model becomes a useful tool to probe thermal characteristics of QCD form factors. We demonstrate this for both, the EM and AV sectors of the $N \to \Delta$ transition.

Boosted Dark Matter Directionality in Large Liquid Scintillator Detectors

Samuel S. H. Tse, Qishan Liu, Kenny C. Y. Ng, Koun Choi, Yu-Feng Li

hep-ph astro-ph.HEarXiv:2610.31666

In large liquid scintillator detectors, boosted dark matter knocking out neutrons from carbon provides superior sensitivity over proton scattering, but reconstructed neutron directionality yields negligible sensitivity improvements due to large scattering angles.

Novel idea

The paper investigates directional detection of cosmic-ray boosted dark matter (CRBDM) by reconstructing the displacement vector between the prompt knockout vertex and the delayed neutron-capture vertex in liquid scintillator. It evaluates whether the directional bias toward the Galactic Center can be retained despite kinematic smearing and optical diffusion.

Method

The flux of relativistic dark matter boosted by cosmic rays is computed with a leaky-box model and propagated through Earth using an energy-loss attenuation model. Dark matter quasi-elastic scattering on $^{12}\text{C}$ is evaluated in the impulse approximation with a relativistic Fermi gas spectral function and Pauli blocking ($|\vec{q}| > 350\text{ MeV}$). Geant4 simulations track secondary particle light, neutron thermalization, and capture, while Poisson likelihood analyses calculate projected 95% C.L. sensitivities for a 100 kt$\cdot$yr exposure.

Key results

  • A 100 kt$\cdot$yr liquid scintillator exposure using the delayed-neutron-capture coincidence channel achieves stronger constraints on spin-independent dark matter-nucleon cross sections than the free-proton channel.
  • For $m_\phi = 1\text{ GeV}$, sensitivity reaches the level of Hyper-K (5 years), while falling just below DUNE projections.
  • Incorporating directional reconstruction between prompt and capture vertices improves the cross-section sensitivity by only $\sim 3\%$ (for $m_\chi = 1\text{ MeV}, m_\phi = 3\text{ GeV}$), because the intrinsic kinematics of the knockout process broadly disperse the initial nucleon angle.

Why it matters to you
