10must-readNeutrino-Induced Eta Production

Nuclear medium and final state interaction effects in $\eta$ production induced by $\nu_\mu(\bar\nu_\mu)$ at SBN and DUNE energies

A. Fatima, S. K. Noor Alam, M. Sajjad Athar, S. K. Singh

nucl-th hep-ex hep-pharXiv:2611.13602

The authors present the first combined calculation of charged- and neutral-current $\nu_\mu(\bar\nu_\mu)$-induced $\eta$-meson production on $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$, incorporating in-medium $N^*(1535)$ modifications and Monte Carlo FSI.

Novel idea

This work performs the first unified theoretical calculation for both CC and NC (anti)neutrino-induced single $\eta$-meson production on nuclear targets ($^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$) targeting SBN and DUNE. It systematically incorporates in-medium renormalization of the dominating $N^*(1535)$ resonance (self-energy and modified decay width) along with semiclassical Monte Carlo intranuclear cascade final-state interactions (FSI) for the outgoing $\eta$.

Method

The elementary cross section uses chiral Lagrangians for non-resonant Born terms and phenomenological Lagrangians fitted to photon/electron $\eta$-production data for resonant channels. Fermi motion and Pauli blocking are treated in a local density approximation (LDA). In-medium $N^*(1535)$ width modifications follow a multi-body collision and decay framework, while $\eta$ FSI (elastic scattering and absorption) is simulated via a semiclassical Monte Carlo approach.

Key results

  • In-medium effects (NME) alone enhance the CC $\nu_\mu$ cross section at $E_\nu = 1\text{ GeV}$ (by ~25% on $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and ~50% on $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$), but suppress it at higher energies with a cross-over near 1.1-1.25 GeV.
  • The full model (NME + FSI) suppresses the cross section at all energies, giving a net reduction relative to free nucleons of ~33-35% at 2-3 GeV on both $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$.
  • The flux-averaged total (CC+NC) cross section on $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$ is $1.45 \times 10^{-41}\text{ cm}^2/\text{nucleon}$ for the MicroBooNE flux, underestimating MicroBooNE's measurement of $(3.22 \pm 0.88 \pm 0.86) \times 10^{-41}\text{ cm}^2/\text{nucleon}$.
  • NME and FSI yield an overall ~30% suppression of flux-averaged cross sections for MicroBooNE/SBND fluxes and ~33% for DUNE.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling, $N^*$ resonance physics, and FSI in LArTPC event generators (GENIE, NuWro, GiBUU) for MicroBooNE, SBND, and DUNE. Exclusive $\eta$ production provides key theoretical background inputs for atmospheric neutrino searches and nucleon decay.

Caveats

The theoretical calculation underestimates current MicroBooNE data by more than a factor of two, signaling potential missing mechanisms or needed generator re-tuning. Additionally, FSI uses an isotropic semiclassical Monte Carlo treatment rather than full coupled-channel transport.

Abstract

We investigate nuclear-medium modification and final-state interaction in charged- and neutral-current $\nu_\mu(\bar{\nu}_\mu)$-induced $\eta$-meson production on $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ and $^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$. The nuclear medium is described within a local density approximation, incorporating in-medium modifications of the $N^*(1535)$ resonance, which is known to play the dominant role in $\eta$ production through its $N \eta$ decay channel. The subsequent propagation and absorption of the $\eta$ meson in the nucleus are simulated using a Monte Carlo approach. We find that nuclear-medium modifications and final state interaction lead to substantial suppression of the production cross section, highlighting the importance of nuclear effects in interpreting $\eta$ production data. Our results provide valuable theoretical input for the analysis of $\eta$-production at the ongoing SBND and MicroBooNE experiments and the forthcoming DUNE experiment.

10worth readingDIS Structure Functions F4 & F5

The DIS structure functions $F_4$ and $F_5$ at NLO and their impact on $\tau$-neutrino scattering

T. Ježo, M. Klasen, P. Risse, I. Schienbein, E. Spezzano

hep-pharXiv:2611.13618

Calculates heavy-quark contributions to DIS structure functions $F_4$ and $F_5$ at NLO in the ACOT scheme, demonstrating up to $-40\%$ cross-section suppression in charged-current $\tau$-neutrino scattering.

Novel idea

The paper extends NLO heavy-quark DIS calculations in the variable-flavor-number ACOT scheme to the lepton-mass-dependent structure functions $F_4$ and $F_5$. It presents the first quantitative evaluation of how $F_4$ and $F_5$ modify charged-current $\nu_\tau$ DIS differential cross sections across kinematic regions accessible at SHiP, IceCube, and DUNE.

Method

Structure functions are calculated at NLO QCD in the ACOT scheme, implemented in APFEL++ with CT18NLO PDFs. Mass dependence of heavy quarks is fully preserved in the hard-scattering coefficient functions, and DGLAP double-counting is removed via ACOT subtraction terms. Differential CC $\nu_\tau$ DIS cross sections are evaluated with complete $m_\tau$ kinematical dependence.

Key results

  • $F_4$ vanishes at LO for massless quarks (satisfying $2xF_4 = F_L$), causing its NLO gluon-initiated corrections to dominate.
  • $F_5$ receives sizeable LO valence-quark contributions, with NLO corrections altering the result by up to $\sim 45\%$.
  • Including $F_4$ and $F_5$ suppresses the CC $\nu_\tau$ differential cross section throughout physical phase space, reaching up to $-40\%$ suppression near the kinematic boundary at low $Q^2$ and large $x$.
  • The $-20\%$ suppression contour spans $Q^2 \lesssim 10-30\text{ GeV}^2$ for $x \gtrsim 0.1-0.3$, while corrections fall below $1\%$ for $Q^2 \gtrsim 10^3\text{ GeV}^2$.

Why it matters to you

Crucial for precision deep-inelastic scattering physics and tau-neutrino event modeling at DUNE, SHiP, and IceCube. $F_4$ and $F_5$ are essential inputs when extracting neutrino DIS parameters or evaluating oscillation backgrounds with heavy leptons.

Caveats

The numerical results evaluate nucleon DIS structure functions using CT18NLO without including nuclear PDF corrections, target mass corrections, or low-$Q^2$ higher-twist effects.

Abstract

We present next-to-leading-order calculations of heavy-quark contributions to the deep-inelastic scattering structure functions $F_4$ and $F_5$ within the Aivazis-Collins-Olness-Tung scheme, implemented in the open-source library APFEL++ and evaluated using CT18NLO parton distribution functions. These structure functions are suppressed by lepton mass effects in light-lepton processes, but become important in $\tau$-lepton and neutrino scattering at facilities such as SHiP, IceCube, and DUNE. We furthermore quantify the direct impact of $F_4$ and $F_5$ on the charged-current $\tau$-neutrino cross section, for the first time presented here, showing they can suppress it by up to $-40\%$ near the kinematic boundary at low $Q^2$ and large $x$.

10must-readNeutrino Mass Ordering & Systematics

Where does the neutrino Mass Ordering preference come from?

Pedro A. N. Machado, João Paulo Pinheiro

hep-pharXiv:2611.13629

Analyzes the neutrino mass ordering preference from combining JUNO, Daya Bay, and accelerator $\nu_\mu$ disappearance data, showing inverted ordering is disfavored at $1.9\sigma$ but highly vulnerable to percent-level energy scale systematics.

Novel idea

The paper provides a detailed dissection of how the mass ordering is inferred from comparing $\Delta m_{ee}^2$ (reactors: JUNO + Daya Bay) with $\Delta m_{\mu\mu}^2$ ($\nu_\mu$ disappearance: T2K, NOvA, MINOS, IceCube). It demonstrates that the current $1.9\sigma$ preference against inverted ordering stems from conflicting pulls between experiments (T2K and NOvA differ by $3.5\%$ in preferred $|\Delta m_{31}^2|$), and shows that future JUNO data alone cannot push this rejection past $\sim 3\sigma$ due to accelerator energy-reconstruction limits.

Method

Combines a full spectral simulation of 207 days of JUNO data with Daya Bay 2D likelihoods and published 1D $|\Delta m_{31}^2|$ likelihood profiles from T2K, NOvA, MINOS, and IceCube/DeepCore (NuFIT 6.1). Due to non-Gaussian multi-minima JUNO likelihoods, hypothesis testing and significances are computed using extensive pseudo-experiments and the $CL_s$ method.

Key results

  • The global combination of JUNO + Daya Bay + Acc+IC disfavors inverted ordering at $1.9\sigma$ ($CL_s$) or $2.2\sigma$ (one-sided $p$-value).
  • Combinations with individual accelerator experiments point in opposite directions: reactor + NOvA disfavors IO at $1.4\sigma$, while reactor + T2K disfavors NO at $0.8\sigma$.
  • The $|\Delta m_{31}^2|$ values preferred by NOvA and T2K differ by $\sim 3.5\%$, which matches the $\sim 3.5\%$ shift expected between $\Delta m_{ee}^2$ and $\Delta m_{\mu\mu}^2$ for different orderings.
  • Accumulating 1500 additional days of JUNO data increases the rejection of IO only to $\sim 2.8\sigma-2.9\sigma$, proving the comparison is bottlenecked by the accuracy of $\Delta m_{\mu\mu}^2$ extraction.

Why it matters to you

Directly links oscillation parameter systematics to neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling. Because determining $\Delta m_{\mu\mu}^2$ relies on reconstructing neutrino energy at the percent level, interaction uncertainties in T2K, NOvA, and DUNE directly control the reach on the mass ordering.

Caveats

The projections freeze current central values and error bands for accelerator experiments without simulating prospective near-detector constraint improvements or joint T2K-NOvA data releases.

Abstract

After the recent JUNO result, the comparison of the effective mass splittings measured in $\bar\nu_e$ disappearance at reactors and in $\nu_\mu$ disappearance has become one of the leading handles on the neutrino mass ordering. In this paper, we study which data sets drive the present preference for the ordering, combining JUNO and Daya Bay with $\nu_\mu$ disappearance data from NOvA, T2K, MINOS, and IceCube/DeepCore. While the combination disfavors the inverted ordering at $1.9\sigma$, this preference comes from a balance between several small preferences. In particular, the combinations of reactor data with T2K or with NOvA point to opposite orderings. The values of the mass splitting preferred by these two experiments differ by about $3.5\%$, comparable to the difference expected between the two orderings, and thus a shift of this size in a single experiment is enough to reverse the preference. Moreover, in the context of this combination of reactor and $\nu_\mu$ disappearance data, further JUNO data would not bring the rejection of the inverted ordering beyond about $3\sigma$, as the comparison is then limited by the precision of the $\nu_\mu$ disappearance experiment. Improving the $\nu_\mu$ disappearance measurements requires control of the neutrino energy reconstruction, and thus the modeling of neutrino-nucleus interaction physics, at the percent level.

10worth readingNonlinear Scalar Sourcing

Running out of Field Range: Nonlinear Sourcing of Light Scalars

Abhishek Banerjee, Shourya Mukherjee, Stefan Stelzl

hep-pharXiv:2611.14473

Shows that nonlinear sourcing in light scalar pNGB models severely weakens equivalence-principle and fifth-force bounds, opening wide unconstrained parameter windows that can be uniquely probed by satellite-ground clock comparisons.

Novel idea

Re-evaluates experimental bounds on light scalars coupled to photons, electrons, and nucleons by accounting for compact field ranges $f \lesssim 100\text{ GeV}$ in pNGB models. Shows that macroscopic sources (Earth, atmosphere, lab shielding) drive the field to its periodic maximum $\pi f / 2$, suppressing local probe linear couplings ($\cos\theta$) and field gradients, thereby turning laboratory exclusions into closed bands and opening wide unconstrained parameter windows.

Method

Solves non-linear static field equations around spherically symmetric bodies (Earth + atmosphere, laboratory setups, stars) using a periodic potential $\sim m_0^2 f^2 (1-\cos\theta)$ and periodic matter couplings. Recasts constraints from MICROSCOPE, ground torsion balances (Eöt-Wash), inverse-square-law tests, stellar cooling, and atomic clock comparisons under field saturation.

Key results

  • For photon coupling $d_e$ at $f=10\text{ MeV}$, ground EP bounds fail below $m_0 \approx 3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ eV}$ while MICROSCOPE loses reach above $m_0 \approx \text{few} \times 10^{-12}\text{ eV}$, leaving an unconstrained window spanning over an order of magnitude in mass.
  • For nucleon coupling $d_N$, because $Q_N \approx 1$, the Earth saturates at $d_{sat} \sim 4 \times 10^{-12} (f/10\text{ MeV})$, preserving open parameter windows at large couplings even for decay constants up to $f \sim 100\text{ GeV}$.
  • Space-ground clock comparisons (e.g. ISS vs ground) retain linear sensitivity to $d_e$ in the saturated regime, serving as a clean, direct probe of the open parameter space.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts searches for fifth forces, ultralight scalars, atomic clock comparisons, and low-energy precision EFT probes. Demonstrates how UV compact field structures alter low-energy experimental bounds.

Caveats

Detailed boundaries of the excluded bands and open windows depend on simplified geometric modeling of laboratory walls, satellite hulls, and atmospheric density profiles.

Abstract

Light scalar fields coupled linearly to the Standard Model are constrained by equivalence-principle tests, fifth-force searches, clock comparison tests, and stellar cooling. These bounds assume that the coupling to ordinary matter can be expanded to linear order for all field values reached. For a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson with decay constant $f$ this fails as soon as the field value reaches $O(f)$. The field around a source then stops growing with the coupling, and in addition the linear coupling of a probe is suppressed depending on its location. We reanalyze all of these bounds for the couplings to photons, electrons and nucleons. Space-based tests are weakened by several orders of magnitude, whereas the terrestrial tests are suppressed far more strongly and lose their sensitivity at low masses. As a result, for each coupling, $f$-dependent windows in the parameter space open up. For a coupling to nucleons a window persists at large couplings even for $f\sim 100 \,\rm{GeV}$. In particular, no current experiment constrains couplings between the naive limit and the stellar-cooling bound in a window of masses above $\text{few}\times 10^{-12}\,$eV, a range of more than ten orders of magnitude in coupling. This presents a unique opportunity for a ground-space clock comparison test to probe uncharted territory.

9worth readingAlpha Clustering & EMC Effect

The Alpha Clustering Model of the EMC Effect

Whitney Armstrong

nucl-th nucl-exarXiv:2611.13234

Proposes the Alpha Clustering Model of the EMC Effect (ACME), demonstrating that $\alpha$-cluster formation in nuclei can account for the magnitude and target dependence of nuclear structure function modifications in DIS.

Novel idea

Introduces a minimal, parameter-free framework (ACME) positing that $\alpha$-particle clustering is the primary microscopic driver of valence quark modification (EMC effect) and short-range correlations (SRCs) in nuclei. Rather than uniform mean-field modifications or isolated $n$-$p$ SRC pair dominance, nuclear targets are modeled as combinations of quasi-free neutrons, 3-nucleon partial clusters ($^3\mathrm{H}/^3\mathrm{He}$), and fully saturated 4-nucleon $\alpha$ clusters.

Method

Uses experimental DIS structure function data directly: $^4\mathrm{He}$ provides the fully saturated cluster modification, MARATHON $^3\mathrm{H}$ and $^3\mathrm{He}$ data set the partially modified cluster input, and quasi-free neutrons remain unmodified. Assembles nuclear targets ($^{12}\mathrm{C}$, $^{11}\mathrm{B}$, $^{27}\mathrm{Al}$, $^{10}\mathrm{B}$, $^{16}\mathrm{O}$, $^{20}\mathrm{Ne}$, $^{48}\mathrm{Ca}$) without free parameters and computes ratio predictions against experimental DIS cross sections.

Key results

  • Data-driven ACME predictions accurately reproduce measured EMC ratios $R_{\mathrm{EMC}}^A$ for $^{12}\mathrm{C}$, $^{11}\mathrm{B}$, $^{27}\mathrm{Al}$, and $^{10}\mathrm{B}$ within experimental uncertainties.
  • Explains the observed equality between $^4\mathrm{He}$ and $^{12}\mathrm{C}$ EMC ratios as a natural saturation effect in $\alpha$-clustered configurations.
  • Predicts that for $^{48}\mathrm{Ca}$, the 8 excess $f_{7/2}$ neutrons lie outside the $\alpha$-clustered $sd$-shell core, acting as quasi-free neutrons and pulling $R_{\mathrm{EMC}}$ down relative to $^{40}\mathrm{Ca}$.
  • Unifies nuclear shell structure, short-range correlations, and $n$-$p$ pair dominance into downstream consequences of $\alpha$ clustering.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to nuclear structure, short-range correlations, electron-nucleus DIS, and nuclear PDF modeling. Offers a fresh perspective on nuclear targets (carbon, oxygen, argon) central to electron-scattering constraints for neutrino experiments.

Caveats

The model is purely phenomenological and lacks a microscopic QCD/ab-initio wave-function derivation. Extension to the spin-dependent EMC effect is currently blocked by the lack of experimental $g_1^{^3\mathrm{H}}$ data.

Abstract

For more than forty years, the EMC effect, the observation that nuclear structure functions in deep-inelastic scattering appear to be modified relative to the free-nucleon expectation, has resisted a consensus microscopic explanation. In this letter, we argue that the strength and $A$ dependence of the EMC effect may be governed by the phenomena of $\alpha$ clustering in nuclei and responsible for a majority of the partonic modification associated with the EMC Effect. We motivate the alpha clustering model of the EMC effect (ACME), and with a data driven approach, we demonstrate how such a model can reproduce the existing deep inelastic scattering data on nuclei. We make predictions for other nuclei such as $^{16}$O, $^{20}$Ne, and $^{48}$Ca and explore its consequences for a spin-dependent EMC effect and global analysis of nuclear parton distributions.

Riccardo Bartocci, Philipp Böer, Kevin Brune, Tobias Hurth

hep-pharXiv:2611.14318

Computes NLO SCET hard-matching coefficients and two-loop radiative charm-loop jet functions for subleading-power $Q_1^q$-$Q_{7\gamma}$ interference in $\bar{B}\to X_s\gamma$ in the $m_c\to 0$ limit.

Novel idea

Presents the first complete NLO factorisation and RG-improved framework for subleading-power resolved-photon contributions in $\bar{B}\to X_s\gamma$ in the massless-charm limit. It introduces an analytical $1/N_c$ expansion solution for non-local hard-matching RG evolution and evaluates the $u$-dependent two-loop radiative charm-loop jet function.

Method

SCET factorisation at subleading power in the photon-energy endpoint region; method-of-regions evaluation of two-loop anti-hardcollinear diagrams; matrix-valued SCET operator renormalisation; large-$N_c$ expansion of the non-local ERBL-type evolution kernel.

Key results

  • Extracted 1-loop hard-matching coefficients $H_{77}$ and matrix $\mathbf{H}_{12}(u;\mu,\nu)$ in SCET.
  • Derived non-local RG evolution equations for $H_1^q(u;\mu)$, obtaining analytic solutions via $1/N_c$ expansion accurate to per-mille level.
  • Calculated 2-loop radiative charm-loop jet function $\bar{J}(u,\omega_1;\mu)$ for $m_c \to 0$ with full momentum-fraction $u$ dependence.
  • Derived 1-loop $Z_{\bar{J}}$ renormalisation kernel from RG consistency, verifying two-loop pole structure.

Why it matters to you

Tangential to primary neutrino focus, but offers advanced SCET subleading-power factorisation techniques and non-local operator RGE methods relevant to precision SCET applications in DIS or lepton-nucleus processes.

Caveats

Calculations are restricted to the massless charm limit $m_c \to 0$; phenomenological implementation requires the two-loop jet function at finite $m_c$.

Abstract

We study next-to-leading-order (NLO) QCD corrections to the subleading-power resolved-photon contribution to $\bar{B}\to X_s\gamma$ arising from the interference of the current-current operator $Q_1^q$ and the electromagnetic dipole operator $Q_{7\gamma}$. This contribution is among the main sources of theoretical uncertainty in Standard Model predictions for the decay rate. Using a factorisation theorem in soft-collinear effective theory for the kinematic endpoint region, $m_b-2E_\gamma=\mathcal{O}(\Lambda_{\rm QCD})$, we determine all previously unknown perturbative ingredients at NLO in the limit of a vanishing charm-quark mass, $m_c\to0$. Specifically, we compute the $m_c$-independent one-loop hard-matching coefficients, derive their renormalisation-group equations, and obtain accurate analytic approximations to their solutions by retaining the first two terms in a $1/N_c$ expansion. Our second main result is the two-loop correction to the radiative charm-loop jet function, including its full dependence on the collinear momentum fraction. Combining these results with the known $\mathcal{O}(\alpha_s)$ correction to the quark jet function and the one-loop renormalisation of the subleading shape function $g_{17}$, previously computed by some of us, we provide the first complete NLO description of this resolved contribution in the massless-charm limit. This leaves the $m_c$ dependence of the radiative charm-loop jet function as the remaining ingredient for a phenomenological analysis within renormalisation-group-improved perturbation theory.

Combines four $N_f=2+1+1$ ensembles at physical pion mass across lattice spacings $a \in [0.049, 0.080]$ fm to perform a direct continuum extrapolation, treating multihadron ($N\pi\pi$) excited-state contamination in 3-point correlators without imposing priors from 2-point spectrum fits.

Method

The four lattice spacings ranging from $0.080$ fm to $0.049$ fm, with an action that has O(a) improvement, enable extrapolating the results to the continuum limit. Both valence and sea quark contributions are analysed accounting for excited-state contamination from low-lying multihadron states. We perform global fits to all ensembles, that yield $\sigma_{\pi N}=53.0(7.4)(1.6)$ MeV, $\sigma_s=58.2(17.0)(2.1)$ MeV and $\sigma_c=112(28)(25)$ MeV, with statistical and systematic uncertainties, respectively.

8must-readMeV Lepton-Number Breaking

Self-interacting dark matter and neutrino masses from MeV-scale lepton-number breaking

Manoranjan Dutta

hep-ph astro-ph.COarXiv:2611.13617

Shows an MeV-scale scalar breaking lepton number in an inverse seesaw model generates pseudo-Dirac dark matter and mediates self-interactions matching galaxy-to-cluster observations.

Novel idea

Proves that central spin-independent forces in pseudo-Dirac DM systems are strictly fixed by mass splitting $|g^{\text{central}}| \simeq \Delta m_\chi / (2v_\phi)$, prohibiting strong inelastic scattering; fits SIDM velocity profiles across scales using exact partial-wave numerical cross sections with relic-density-fixed couplings.

Method

Global lepton number breaking in inverse seesaw framework; exact partial-wave solution of radial Schr&ouml;dinger equation for identical Majorana fermions in Yukawa potential; dark sector freeze-out with distinct $T_D$; limits from $N_{\text{eff}}$, SN 1987A, BBN, and LZ/XENONnT.

Key results

  • Relic-constrained coupling reproduces SIDM cross sections from dwarfs to clusters along $m_s \simeq 1.9 \text{ MeV} \times (m_\chi/\text{GeV})$ with best fit at $m_\chi = 7.6$ GeV, $m_s = 15$ MeV.
  • Derives upper bound on dark matter mass $m_\chi \lesssim 70$ GeV ($95\%$ CL) from cluster cross section constraints.
  • Confines lepton-number breaking scale $v_\phi$ to $100\text{--}400$ MeV using mediator lifetime ($\tau_s < 0.1$ s), SN 1987A neutrino emission, and dark state decays.
  • Establishes a strong theoretical preference for Normal Neutrino Mass Ordering to satisfy SN 1987A bounds.

Why it matters to you

Unifies low-scale neutrino mass generation (inverse seesaw at $v_\phi \sim \text{hundreds of MeV}$) with self-interacting dark matter, offering concrete testable predictions for JUNO, DUNE, $N_{\text{eff}}$, and direct detection.

Caveats

Requires a small soft-breaking tadpole ($\kappa \gtrsim 1$ eV) to avoid domain wall domination; supernova bounds rely on simplified isotropic neutrino emission estimates.

Abstract

Dark matter is a pseudo-Dirac fermion whose mass splitting arises from the same vev and is predicted to be proportional to the self-interaction coupling times the vev. A strong central interaction between the dark states requires this splitting to be sizable. The CP-even mediator decays into neutrinos at a rate fixed by the neutrino masses, independent of its mixing with the Higgs boson. Fixing the coupling by the relic abundance and evaluating exact partial-wave cross sections, the model reproduces the velocity dependence of the self-interaction cross section inferred from dwarf galaxies to clusters along a narrow band, $m_s\simeq1.9~{\rm MeV}\times(m_\chi/{\rm GeV})$, extending up to $m_\chi\simeq12$~GeV with a best fit at $m_\chi\simeq7.6$~GeV and $m_s\simeq15$~MeV, and on a sequence of resonant islands of comparable fit quality at higher masses. The cluster data bound the dark matter mass from above, so that the allowed masses extend from about $3$ to $70$~GeV ($95\%$ confidence level), with correlated mediator masses of $6$-$28$~MeV. Combining constraints from the effective number of neutrino species, SN~1987A and direct detection, we find the lepton-number-breaking scale confined to a window of a few hundred MeV and a strong preference for normal neutrino mass ordering. The allowed parameter space is testable by future measurements of $N_{\rm eff}$, low-threshold direct detection and the neutrino mass ordering.

Calculates in-medium muon-induced baryon-number violation in neutron stars, showing surface cooling of cold pulsars excludes conversion rates at Mu2e/COMET by 16 orders of magnitude.

Novel idea

Computes the exact in-medium phase space for $\mu^- p \to \chi_0 \chi_2 / \chi_1$ conversion in dense $npe\mu$ matter, deriving a universal scaling law for the dark vector repulsion required to prevent stellar collapse based on the baryonic mass headroom of the equation of state.

Method

Monte Carlo integration of degenerate in-medium proton/muon Fermi seas across 60 relativistic mean-field and sound-speed-extended equations of state; impulse approximation calculation for exotic $\mu^-$ capture in $^{27}\text{Al}$; stellar thermal luminosity matching to PSR J2144-3933.

Key results

  • Surface luminosity limits on cold pulsar PSR J2144-3933 exclude conversion rates down to $R \sim 5 \times 10^{-34}$, over 16 orders of magnitude below Mu2e sensitivity ($R \sim 10^{-17}$).
  • Nuclear impulse calculation for $^{27}\text{Al}$ yields capture rate $R \approx 5 \times 10^{-16}$ (Benchmark I) and $R = 2.5 \times 10^{-18}$ (Benchmark II).
  • Derives universal scaling relation for minimum dark vector repulsion $G_V^{\text{min}} \approx \frac{1}{\mu_{c,\max} - m_X} \frac{A}{\Delta M_B}$ necessary to support $2 M_\odot$ stars after conversion.
  • Demonstrates off-shell conversion extends stellar bounds up to $m_{\chi_2} \approx 1145$ MeV.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts stopped-muon BSM searches (Mu2e/COMET) and dark baryon models (e.g. by Hostert, Zupan, Menzo, Pospelov), establishing extreme astrophysical bounds and evaluating realistic nuclear capture rates.

Caveats

Nuclear impulse approximation omits detailed shell-model configuration mixing in $^{26}\text{Mg}$ final states; stellar bounds assume continuous presence of muons in the inner core.

Abstract

Baryon-number violation that needs a muon in the initial state can evade proton-decay searches and be observed in muon capture at Mu2e and COMET. Neutron stars contain muons, so the same interaction converts their protons into dark baryons, but the rate of this conversion in dense matter and its consequences have not been computed. In this paper we compute the conversion rate, the heat it deposits, and the evolution of a star under fast conversion. The coldest known neutron stars exclude conversion rates far below laboratory reach for every dark-fermion mass that capture in aluminium can produce, and faster conversion burns stars down or, unless conserved dark baryons repel each other strongly enough, collapses them. The repulsion they need follows a scaling law in the baryonic mass headroom of the nucleonic equation of state, which applies to any process that puts dark baryons in equilibrium with neutrons.
