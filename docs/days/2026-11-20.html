Measurements of the total and differential inclusive electron neutrino charged-current cross sections on argon with SBND

L. Aliaga-Soplin, D. Andrade Aldana, C. Andreopoulos, M. Andriamirado, A. Antonakis, S. Balasubramanian + 185 more

hep-exarXiv:2611.25098

SBND reports the highest-statistics inclusive CC $\nu_e + \bar{\nu}_e$ cross section on argon to date using the Booster Neutrino Beam, showing preference for lower-cross-section generators.

Novel idea

This is the first inclusive CC $\nu_e + \bar{\nu}_e$ cross section measured on argon in the Booster Neutrino Beam, delivering 729 selected candidate events with an outgoing electron energy $E_e > 500$ MeV. Over 85% of selected signal events have $E_\nu > 1$ GeV, directly probing the energy region corresponding to the first oscillation maximum of DUNE.

Method

Using an exposure of $8.81 \times 10^{19}$ POT, electron showers were reconstructed with the Pandora framework in SBND's 112-tonne active mass LArTPC. A conditional covariance background constraint utilizing a high-momentum $\pi^0$ photon sideband reduced background uncertainty from 20.4% to 10.5%. Total and single-differential cross sections in $E_e$ and $\cos\theta_e$ were extracted via Wiener-SVD unfolding and evaluated against GENIE v3.6.2 (Ar23 tune), GiBUU 2025, NuWro 25.11.1, and NEUT 6.1.4.

Key results

  • Measured flux-averaged total CC $\nu_e + \bar{\nu}_e$ cross section: $\sigma = [3.90 \pm 0.18\,\text{(stat)} \pm 0.41\,\text{(syst)}] \times 10^{-39}\text{ cm}^2/\text{nucleon}$ at $\langle E_\nu \rangle = 1.8$ GeV.
  • Unfolded differential data show strong agreement with GENIE Ar23 ($\chi^2/\text{dof} = 3.6/4$) and NuWro ($\chi^2/\text{dof} = 4.2/4$).
  • Significant tension observed with GiBUU ($\chi^2/\text{dof} = 22.3/4$) and NEUT ($\chi^2/\text{dof} = 13.9/4$), driven largely by overall cross-section overprediction rather than angular or energy shape mismodeling.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts your core interest in argon neutrino-interaction modeling and $\nu_e/\nu_\mu$ cross-section ratios governing DUNE and SBN oscillation systematics. It provides critical benchmarking data for generator tunes (GENIE, NuWro, GiBUU) in the $1\text{--}2$ GeV regime.

Caveats

Imposes an $E_e > 500$ MeV cutoff to suppress MiniBooNE low-energy excess phase space, excluding low-energy nuclear response phenomena; the flux model (especially $K^+$ and $K^0$ production) remains the dominant systematic uncertainty at 8–14%.

Abstract

This work presents measurements of the total and single-differential electron neutrino charged-current cross sections on argon using the Short-Baseline Near Detector (SBND) at Fermilab. We present the highest statistics inclusive $\nu_e + \bar \nu_e$ (95.2% $\nu_e$ and 4.8% $\bar\nu_e$) charged-current cross section measurement on argon to date and the first inclusive $\nu_e + \bar \nu_e$ cross section measurement using the Booster Neutrino Beam. Direct measurements of this channel are crucial for future studies of both long- and short-baseline neutrino oscillations through electron neutrino appearance, which in turn are vital for answering open questions in neutrino phenomenology and searching for beyond the Standard Model physics at the intensity frontier. The data used for this work were collected with an exposure of $8.81 \times10^{19}$ protons on target, and 729 candidate events are selected with a minimum electron energy of 500 MeV. The single-differential cross sections are extracted in electron energy and angle with respect to the beam axis. The flux-averaged total cross section is measured to be $\sigma = [3.90 \pm 0.18\,\text{(stat)} \pm 0.41\,\text{(syst)}] \times 10^{-39}$ cm$^2$/nucleon for a predicted average neutrino energy of $\langle E_\nu \rangle=1.8$ GeV for selected events. The total and differential cross sections are compared to predictions from common neutrino event generators. The data show a preference for generators that predict a lower overall cross section.

A Surrogate Detector Model for the MINERvA Open Dataset

Sparshita Dey, Alexander Himmel, David Last, Liang Liu, Pedro Machado, Vishvas Pandey + 1 more

hep-exarXiv:2611.26271

A transformer-based probabilistic surrogate of the MINERvA detector maps generator final states to reconstructed observables, circumventing experimental software and phase-space-limited event reweighting.

Novel idea

The paper introduces a generative surrogate model trained purely on open simulation data that bypasses the proprietary, computationally heavy Geant4 and reconstruction software of experimental collaborations. Crucially, it takes only physical final-state particles and geometry as input—deliberately excluding generator-dependent process labels—allowing true forward-folding of arbitrary theoretical models into reconstructed detector space.

Method

The architecture combines a transformer set encoder with conditional flow-matching heads to sequentially generate muon momentum, vertex residuals, calorimetric recoil, and hadron prongs. Validation uses receiver-operating-characteristic classifiers (AUC) against full simulation, an ensemble of four models to quantify response uncertainties, and a holdout test where $2p2h$ events were completely removed from training to evaluate generalization to unrepresented kinematics. Predictions from GENIE, NuWro, and GiBUU are forward-folded through the MINERvA CC-inclusive analysis pipeline.

Key results

  • Distinguishability classifier between surrogate and full Geant4 simulation yields an AUC of $0.504$ on reconstructed observables and $0.511$ conditioned on true final states, verifying near-exact distributional closure.
  • In the $2p2h$ holdout study, the surrogate correctly reproduced reconstructed recoil energy, proton kinematics, and transverse kinematic imbalance ($\delta p_T$) within uncertainties, demonstrating true generalization where reweighting fails due to lack of phase-space support.
  • Accurately reproduced MINERvA 101 selection cutflows, efficiencies, and purities, providing direct reconstructed-space comparisons between GENIE, NuWro, and GiBUU.

Why it matters to you

Authored by Pedro Machado and Vishvas Pandey, this is an essential tool for your work on neutrino-nucleus interactions. It provides a practical, open-source bridge to confront novel theoretical calculations (ab initio responses, 2p2h/MEC models, new generators) directly with MINERvA data without unfolding distortions or reliance on collaboration-internal Geant4 chains.

Caveats

Validated only on MINERvA Medium-Energy FHC Playlist 1A; particles with kinetic energies below 10 MeV were omitted from surrogate inputs during training.

Abstract

The growing availability of open neutrino data promises broader scrutiny of interaction models, but a critical barrier remains: mapping theoretical predictions into reconstructed observables, which previously required computationally intensive methods inaccessible outside the original experimental collaborations. In this work, we present a probabilistic surrogate of the MINERvA detector response, trained on an open simulation dataset to map generator-level final-state particles and interaction geometry to reconstructed observables, without relying on generator-specific interaction labels. We assess its fidelity through conditional response distributions, reconstructed observables, and classifier-based closure tests. We further demonstrate generalization beyond the limitations of conventional phase-space-dependent reweighting by withholding two-particle-two-hole interactions entirely from training and showing that the surrogate reproduces their reconstructed response within the estimated uncertainties. In an end-to-end application to MINERvA charged-current inclusive muon-neutrino data, we reproduce selection efficiencies and purities and forward-fold predictions from GENIE, NuWro, and GiBUU for direct comparison with measurements. By moving theoretical predictions into reconstructed space rather than unfolding the data, this work establishes a reusable interface between neutrino interaction models and experimental observations, expanding the scope of physics accessible through open datasets.

Automate the geometric SMEFT at dimension eight

Benoît Assi, Adam Martin

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2611.25016

Authors present the first complete dimension-eight geometric SMEFT UFO implementation for MadGraph5_aMC@NLO, including electroweak input shifts, a diagram selector, and amplitude energy-scaling power counting.

Novel idea

Provides the first complete, automated UFO implementation of the SMEFT at dimension eight compatible with the geometric SMEFT (geoSMEFT). It modifies the Murphy basis $\psi^2 H^2 D^3$ class to preserve the geometric formulation of two- and three-point functions, includes input-parameter shifts consistently through $\mathcal{O}(1/\Lambda^4)$, and introduces an energy-scaling-aware diagram selector.

Method

Built using FeynRules containing all 674 baryon-number-conserving operators under Minimal Flavour Violation, exported in both the geoSMEFT and Murphy bases for the $\{M_W, M_Z, G_F\}$ and $\{\alpha, M_Z, G_F\}$ schemes. A standalone Python tool identifies tree-level operator contributions (including indirect input-scheme shifts) and generates MadGraph restriction cards. Validated against SmeftFR, Ward identities, and evaluated across twelve LHC diboson, triboson, VBF $HH$, and Drell-Yan processes.

Key results

  • Corrected 84 published operator definitions from the Murphy basis, resolving 68 anti-Hermitian operator structures and 2 vanishing operators.
  • Automated tower generation for two- and three-point geometric connections, enabling systematic higher-dimension evaluations up to dimension 12 for Higgs and electroweak pole observables.
  • Evaluated dimension-eight interference and $(\text{dim-8})^2$ truncation diagnostics across LHC benchmarks, identifying substantial energy growth ($E^4/\Lambda^4$) driven by contact classes like $\psi^2 X^2 D$ and $\psi^4 D^2$.

Why it matters to you

Useful reference if your EFT interests require consistent $\mathcal{O}(1/\Lambda^4)$ SMEFT matching, electroweak input-scheme corrections, or truncation error estimation. However, the phenomenological applications and UFO models are strictly tailored toward high-energy LHC collider physics rather than low-energy neutrino or precision nuclear observables.

Caveats

Restricted to Minimal Flavour Violation in the fermion sector; baryon-number-violating operators are excluded from the exported UFO; dimension-eight squared terms serve as an incomplete truncation diagnostic rather than a full $\mathcal{O}(1/\Lambda^8)$ calculation.

Abstract

We present the first complete dimension-eight implementation of the geometric Standard Model Effective Field Theory (geoSMEFT) as UFO models for event generators such as MadGraph. The models contain all 674 baryon-number-conserving operators, with minimal flavour violation for the fermionic operators, and are also provided in the Murphy basis. We include dimension-six contributions and electroweak input-parameter corrections consistently through order $1/\Lambda^4$, and provide dimension-eight squared contributions as a diagnostic of EFT truncation. We validate the implementation against independent calculations and Ward identities. To make these models practical for collider studies, we develop a standalone tool that identifies all operators contributing to a given process at tree level, including those entering through input-parameter shifts. It reports their expected energy scaling and generates a restriction card to reduce the computational cost of event generation. The same geometric construction allows us to calculate two- and three-point functions beyond dimension eight, to arbitrary order in $1/\Lambda^2$. The models and the tool are publicly available.
