Quantum Simulation of Hadronization: From String Breaking to Event Generation

Christian W. Bauer, Anthony N. Ciavarella, Jad C. Halimeh, Yannick Meurice, Stephen Mrenna, Martin J. Savage + 16 more

hep-ph hep-lat nucl-th quant-pharXiv:2611.00214

This white paper develops a roadmap for using quantum simulations of gauge theories to model the non-perturbative real-time dynamics of hadronization and constrain event-generator parameters.

Novel idea

Unlike Euclidean lattice methods restricted to static correlators or tensor networks limited by entanglement, this work outlines how near-term and fault-tolerant quantum hardware can compute real-time string-breaking and soft-function matrix elements to directly test the assumptions of Lund and cluster fragmentation models.

Method

The authors synthesize findings from an IQuS workshop, proposing a hierarchy of toy models ($Z_2$, U(1), and non-Abelian SU($N$) lattice gauge theories) with staggered or dressed-site fermion encodings. They investigate state preparation, Trotterized time evolution, gauge-invariant encodings, and observable extraction (momentum, flavor, entanglement, and magic).

Key results

  • Identified hadronization parameter uncertainties as a dominant LHC systematic (e.g., 50% spread in identified-particle spectra, underpredicted charm baryon ratios).
  • Established that SU(2) and Abelian lattice simulations have reached $ extgreater 100$ qubits, making qualitative modifications to event generators a near-term goal.
  • Outlined resource requirements and algorithmic strategies for transitioning from toy models to $(2+1)$D and $(3+1)$D QCD implementations.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to your interest in neutrino event generators (GENIE, GiBUU, etc.) and their underlying non-perturbative physics models, as well as the intersection of quantum simulation with hadronic final-state interactions and intranuclear cascades.

Caveats

Direct simulation of an end-to-end collider event remains infeasible ($n_L extgreater 10^{15}$ for the LHC), so the framework relies on matching isolated non-perturbative sub-processes (like string breaking) to phenomenological generator tunes.

Abstract

High-energy collisions produce large numbers of quarks and gluons that are bound into hadrons by the confining interactions of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). This hadronization process is intrinsically real-time and non-perturbative. Event generators describe hadronization using phenomenological models with parameters fitted to data rather than calculated from QCD. Uncertainties in these models limit the precision of certain measurements at the Large Hadron Collider. Quantum computers could enable first-principles simulations of these dynamics. Existing quantum simulations of gauge theories have demonstrated string-breaking dynamics. This white paper summarizes the conclusions of the InQubator for Quantum Simulation workshop \textit{From String Dynamics to Event Generation with Quantum Simulation}. We develop a roadmap for extending these simulations to inform phenomenological models of hadronization. A sequence of toy models is proposed for connecting current quantum simulations to QCD. The observables that should be extracted are listed, and the challenges that separate present simulations from quantitative predictions are identified. We propose a staged program in which quantum simulations first provide qualitative modifications to event generators and later provide quantitative input.

This paper presents updated world-average inclusive charged-current muon neutrino cross-section slopes ($ ext{ extsigma}/E_ u$) across $6 ext{--}200 ext{ GeV}$ by synthesizing historical high-energy data and low-$ u$ measurements.

While high-energy averages ($30 ext{--}200 ext{ GeV}$) confirm established values ($ ext{0.677} ppp ext{ and } ext{0.335} imes 10^{-38} ext{ cm}^2/ ext{GeV}$ for $ u$ and $ar{ u}$), this work provides the first systematic world average for the low-energy extension ($6 ext{--}30 ext{ GeV}$) anchored by NOMAD, MINOS, and MINERvA low-$ u$ analyses.

The methods of the Particle Data Group are applied to produce a world average inclusive, charged-current, muon neutrino cross section energy-dependence slope $\sigma/E_\nu$ . For the energy range $30 < E_\nu < 200$, values obtained are (0.677 $\pm$ 0.013) and (0.335 $\pm$ 0.007) $\times 10^{-38}$ cm$^2$/GeV for neutrino and anti-neutrino, respectively. These values are practically identical to those in common use. This paper expands the experiments whose data are used and describes the evolution of measurements leading to the result. These cross sections underpin foundational neutrino quantities such as structure functions, electroweak parameter measurements, and oscillation parameter determinations.

Evaluation of $2\nu\beta\beta$ decay nuclear matrix elements using Lanczos techniques preserving Gamow-Teller strengths from both parent and daughter nuclei

Deepak Patel, Anil Kumar, Noritaka Shimizu, Yutaka Utsuno

nucl-tharXiv:2611.02382

The authors introduce two efficient shell-model Lanczos methods for calculating $2 uetaeta$ and $2 u ext{ECEC}$ nuclear matrix elements that avoid intermediate-state summations while preserving total Gamow-Teller strengths for both parent and daughter nuclei.

Novel idea

Unlike standard single-sided Lanczos strength-function (SLSF) approaches that only conserve Gamow-Teller sum rules for one side of the decay, the dual-sided Lanczos strength-function (DLSF) and block-Lanczos strength-function (BLSF) methods symmetrically preserve total GT strengths from both initial and final nuclei simultaneously.

Method

Using the KSHELL code, the authors apply Löwdin orthogonalization and subspace rediagonalization (DLSF) or block Krylov subspace iterations (BLSF) starting from GT doorway states. They benchmark the framework in the $fp$ shell for $^{48} ext{Ca}$ and extend it to larger model spaces ($sdpf$, $f_5pg_9$, and $g_7ds ext{-}h_{11}$) for $^{58} ext{Ni}$, $^{76} ext{Ge}$, and $^{130} ext{Te}$.

Key results

  • Fully converged $2 uetaeta$ NMEs are obtained with only $ extsim 30 ext{--}110$ iterations, bypassing the need to compute thousands of exact intermediate $1^+$ eigenstates.
  • For $^{48} ext{Ca}$, cross-shell excitations up to $2 ext{--}ar{h} ext{ extomega}$ in the $sdpf$ model space yield an NME closer to the NEMO-3 experimental estimate than the $fp$-shell calculation.
  • Presented the first shell-model calculation of the $2 ueta^+ ext{EC}/2 u ext{ECEC}$ NME for $^{58} ext{Ni}$ ($M_{2 u}  0.1250$), showing it lies two orders of magnitude below current experimental upper limits.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to your interest in ab initio and shell-model electroweak nuclear responses, nuclear matrix elements for double-beta decay, and methods to accurately capture high-lying GT strength distributions without prohibitive computational scaling.

Caveats

Spurious center-of-mass excitations in enlarged model spaces (like $sdpf$) require careful filtering (e.g., Lawson prescription with $eta_{ ext{cm}} = 10$), and extension to $0 uetaeta$ requires handling momentum-dependent neutrino potentials.

Abstract

We present two efficient shell-model approaches for calculating nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) for two-neutrino double-beta ($2\nu\beta\beta$) decay and two-neutrino double-electron capture ($2\nu$ECEC), based on the Lanczos strength-function and block-Lanczos techniques. The dual-sided Lanczos strength-function (DLSF) and block-Lanczos strength-function (BLSF) methods avoid the explicit calculation of a complete set of exact intermediate $1^+$ eigenstates and yield fully converged NMEs with a relatively small number of iterations starting from Gamow--Teller (GT) doorway states. Both methods simultaneously preserve the total GT strengths associated with the initial and final nuclei. We benchmark the methods for $^{48}$Ca in the $fp$ shell and apply them to candidates in different mass regions, including $^{58}$Ni, $^{76}$Ge, and $^{130}$Te. For $^{48}$Ca, a calculation allowing cross-shell excitations of up to $2\hbar\omega$ in the $sdpf$ model space yields a $2\nu\beta\beta$ NME closer to the experimental estimate than the result obtained in the $fp$ shell. For $^{58}$Ni, we present, to our knowledge, the first shell-model calculation of the $2\nu\beta^+$EC/$2\nu$ECEC NME. Comparison with the current experimental upper limit on the NME indicates the need for a more sensitive measurement of the $2\nu\beta^+$EC half-life. As an independent validation, the energy-denominator-free sums obtained with the proposed methods are compared with the corresponding double-GT matrix elements calculated directly between the initial and final ground states. Their agreement demonstrates that the methods correctly recover the complete energy-denominator-free intermediate-state sums.

Tests of an EP-Preserving Wake Force

Abhishek Banerjee, Dawid Brzeminski

hep-ph gr-qcarXiv:2611.00200

The authors demonstrate that universal quadratic couplings of ultralight scalar dark matter generate a differential wake force between concentric test masses due to mutual field distortion, enabling tests with equivalence-principle experiments like MICROSCOPE.

Novel idea

Even though a universal microscopic coupling preserves the weak equivalence principle for point particles, finite concentric bodies distort the ambient scalar field differently, allowing experiments designed for composition-dependent tests (such as MICROSCOPE's reference sensor SUREF) to constrain universally coupled scalar dark matter.

Method

The authors derive the field profile and weak-screening limit using a concentric spherical-shell approximation for the apparatus, extending the calculation to arbitrary screening strengths. They recast published MICROSCOPE measurements by projecting the Earth-induced dark matter wake onto the satellite's sensitive orbital and spin frequency bands.

Key results

  • Recasting MICROSCOPE's SUEP and SUREF sensor data probes scalar dark masses up to $m_{ ext{DM}}  8 imes 10^{-9} ext{ eV}$ and couplings below the supernova SN1987A bound.
  • Including additional frequency sidebands is projected to extend the mass reach to $m_{ ext{DM}}  7 imes 10^{-8} ext{ eV}$ and probe regions below BBN constraints.
  • Confirmed that amplitudes inferred independently from SUEP (different compositions) and SUREF (identical compositions) agree within twice their combined uncertainty.

Why it matters to you

Relevant to your interest in precision atomic and nuclear probes of new physics, ultralight scalar dark matter, and novel signatures in precision space-based instrumentation.

Caveats

The analysis relies on an idealized concentric spherical mass distribution model for the satellite bus and sensor hardware because complete engineering blueprints are publicly unavailable.

Abstract

A universal quadratic coupling of ultralight scalar dark matter to ordinary matter preserves the weak equivalence principle for unscreened point particles. A finite test body is nevertheless not a passive probe: it distorts the scalar field that accelerates it. We show that this field distortion produces a differential acceleration between concentric test masses, even when their microscopic coupling is universal. We calculate this signal including how each test mass distorts the field acting on the other, deriving the weak-screening limit and extending the calculation to stronger screening using concentric spherical layers. Applying the model to MICROSCOPE, we find differential signals in both its equivalence-principle sensor SUEP, whose test masses have different compositions, and its reference sensor SUREF, whose test masses have the same composition. We combine both sensors with the dark matter wake near Earth to recast the published MICROSCOPE measurements. Within this model, existing measurements probe scalar masses up to approximately $8 \times 10^{-9}\,$eV and couplings below the supernova bound. An analysis using the additional frequency components of the scalar signal could extend the mass reach and probe couplings below the BBN bound.

Exothermic Higgs portal: from LUX-ZEPLIN recoil to Gamma Rays

Subhojit Roy, Jure Zupan

hep-ph astro-ph.CO astro-ph.HEarXiv:2611.01158

A minimal Higgs-portal extension of the Standard Model with a complex scalar provides a thermal exothermic dark matter explanation for the LUX-ZEPLIN nuclear-recoil candidate while satisfying indirect and solar constraints.

Novel idea

Unlike endothermic scenarios requiring high-velocity halo tails, exothermic down-scattering ($ ext{ extchi}_2 A o ext{ extchi}_1 A$) utilizes an existing population of the heavier degenerate scalar state ($ ext{ extchi}_2$) to naturally yield high recoil energies ($ extsim 250 ext{ keV}$) without extreme kinematic suppression.

Method

The authors solve coupled Boltzmann equations for thermal freeze-out of coannihilations to fix the off-diagonal Higgs coupling $g  0.05$ for a $ extsim 100 ext{ GeV}$ mass and $ extsim 600 ext{ keV}$ splitting. They evaluate xenon recoil spectra using chiral-EFT nuclear response functions and test parameter spaces against INTEGRAL/SPI, Fermi-LAT dwarf galaxies, CMB, and solar capture/annihilation limits.

Key results

  • Yields an order-one event expectation ($0.5 ext{--}2 ext{ events}$) for the $2.84 ext{ tonne-year}$ LUX-ZEPLIN exposure at the candidate recoil energy ($E_R = 248 ext{ keV}$).
  • Radiative decays $ ext{ extchi}_2 o ext{ extchi}_1 ext{ extgamma} ext{ extgamma}$ produce a sub-MeV photon continuum testable by future COSI observations.
  • Solar capture is dominated by exothermic $ ext{ extchi}_2 A o ext{ extchi}_1 A$ scattering, leaving captured dark matter mostly in the lighter state and suppressing solar neutrino signals from mixed annihilation well below IceCube bounds.

Why it matters to you

Intersects low-energy dark matter searches (LZ), nuclear-recoil physics on xenon targets, and electroweak/Higgs-portal EFT frameworks relevant to your core interest in low-energy neutrino and rare-process precision physics.

Caveats

The predicted xenon recoil spectrum is highly sensitive to the momentum dependence of the nuclear response near diffraction minima (e.g., Helm form factor versus chiral EFT calculations yield different efficiency-weighted event totals).

Abstract

We show that a minimal extension of the Standard Model (SM) by a single complex scalar $\phi$ provides a viable inelastic exothermic, thermal relic, interpretation of the recently reported nuclear-recoil candidate from LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ). The mass terms and Higgs-portal interactions split $\phi$ into two nearly degenerate real scalars, $\phi_{1,2}$. For mass-splitting of a few hundred keV, $\phi_2$ is cosmologically long-lived and the two states have comparable relic abundances. Taking $\phi_{1,2}$ masses to be $\sim100\,$GeV, with off-diagonal Higgs coupling fixed by relic abundance, the exothermic down-scattering yields an order-one event expectation for the LZ exposure, without relying on the high-velocity tail of the dark matter velocity distribution required in endothermic scenarios. This scenario can be tested using different nuclear targets and with indirect dark matter searches: $\phi_2\to\phi_1\gamma\gamma$ decays give a broad sub-MeV photon continuum that can be probed by INTEGRAL/SPI and COSI; $\phi_1\phi_2$ annihilation produces a GeV-scale continuum, extending up to $\sim$100 GeV, that is probed by Fermi-LAT observations of dwarf galaxies; current CMB constraints are satisfied and annihilation among dark matter particles captured in the Sun is strongly suppressed. Next-generation experiments could probe correlated scattering, decay, and annihilation signatures of this thermal Higgs-portal scenario.

New Scaling Relations for Nucleons in Three-Nucleon Short-Range Correlations

Misak M. Sargsian (Florida International University), Ilay Wischnevsky Shlush (Tel Aviv University)

nucl-th hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2611.01093

New dynamical SRC parameters $eta_{2N}$ and $eta_{3N}$ enable the factorization of nucleon momentum distributions, leading to a new scaling relation for three-nucleon short-range correlations (3N-SRCs).

Novel idea

Unlike 2N-SRCs where $pn$-dominance allows straightforward factorization, 3N-SRCs involve mixed $ppn$ and $pnn$ configurations that spoil simple combinatorial scaling. The authors introduce dynamical strength parameters $eta_{2N}$ and $eta_{3N}$ that go beyond combinatorics, successfully deriving a universal scaling relation for proton and neutron momentum distributions in 3N-SRCs.

Method

The authors construct light-front momentum distributions for $j$-nucleon SRCs and define the dynamical parameters $eta_{jN}$ to isolate local interaction dynamics. They test the resulting scaling relations against non-relativistic ab-initio variational Monte Carlo (VMC) wave functions (using AV18, Norfolk NV2+3-Ia chiral potentials, Urbana-X, and TM' 3N forces) and Faddeev calculations for nuclei in the range $3 eq A eq 12$.

Key results

  • Derived a new scaling relation between proton and neutron momentum distributions for 3N-SRCs, valid for light-front momentum fractions $\alpha \gtrsim 1.6$ ($p \gtrsim 700$ MeV/c).
  • Ab-initio checks across $3 \le A \le 12$ confirm that the scaled $n_p/n_n$ ratios converge to unity in the 3N-SRC domain with $\le 8\%$ disagreement for nuclei like $^6$He and $^9$Li.
  • Demonstrated that the predicted scaling is largely insensitive to specific models of 3N nuclear forces, confirming that $\beta_{3N}$ captures the dominant local dynamics.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to neutrino-nucleus interaction modeling and generators (like GENIE, NuWro, and GiBUU) by providing a rigorous way to constrain high-momentum tails and nuclear wave function treatments at kinematics relevant to short-range correlations and cross-section analyses.

Caveats

The derivation uses an input parameter $r \approx 0.8$ characterizing the ratio of majority to minority components in 3N-SRCs, which assumes isospin symmetry and neglects $nnn/ppp$ configurations.

Abstract

The search for three nucleon short range correlations~(3N- SRCs) in nuclei is one of the intensive areas of research in high density nuclear physics. It is in part motivated by the prediction of the above two-solar mass neutron stars based on 3N short-range forces and the subsequent observation of such stars. The 3N-SRCs serve as a "laboratory" for probing the dynamics of largely unknown irreducible 3N-forces. However, the identification of 3N-SRCs in high energy nuclear processes is still elusive making the prospects of their investigation problematic. In this work we introduced new dynamical parameters for 2N- and 3N- SRCs, $\beta_{2N}$ and $\beta_{3N}$ respectively, which characterize the strength of SRCs beyond combinatorial counting of nucleons in SRCs. For 2N-SRCs the introduction of $\beta_{2N}$ parameter resulted in a previously known and experimentally verified relation for proton and neutron momentum distributions in 2N-SRCS. For 3N-SRCs the $\beta_{3N}$ parameter allowed to factorize momentum distributions of nucleons in the correlation resulting in a prediction of a new scaling relation between proton and neutron momentum distributions in 3N SRCs. This relation, if valid gives a new venue for identification of 3N-SRCs in electro-nuclear processes. Even though the obtained scaling relation is relevant in the relativistic domain, the possibility of separating center of mass motion of SRCs on the light-front allows to check the validity of predicted scaling using non-relativistic ab-initio wave functions. We found surprisingly reasonable confirmation of our prediction analyzing ab-initio wave functions of nuclei in the range $3\le A\le 12$. This indicates the robustness of the scaling relation and opens up a new direction in searching for 3N-SRCs experimentally.

Active-subspace learning reveals dominant parameter directions in nuclear observables

Tom Plies, Margarida Companys Franzke, Achim Schwenk

nucl-tharXiv:2611.03628

Active-subspace learning reveals that more than 99% of gradient-based variations in nuclear ab initio observables are concentrated along a single direction in the 17-dimensional chiral EFT LEC space.

Novel idea

Applying active-subspace learning (ASL) directly to many-body calculations of oxygen isotopes bypasses the need for global emulators when extracting dominant parameter directions and derivative-based global sensitivities.

Method

Calculated gradients of IMSRG ground-state energies and charge radii of oxygen isotopes with respect to 17 chiral LECs using nearest-neighbor approximations. Constructed the gradient covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ and performed eigenvalue decompositions to identify active versus inactive subspaces.

Key results

  • The normalized first eigenvalue for $E(^{16}\text{O})$ is $\overline{\lambda}_1 = 0.998$, indicating that the 17-dimensional LEC dependence is effectively one-dimensional.

Why it matters to you

Provides a powerful tool for nuclear structure and ab initio electroweak response calculations, drastically improving uncertainty quantification and surrogate model efficiency for expensive many-body solvers.

Caveats

Abstract

Jointly models the spatial morphology and UHE $\gamma$-ray spectrum of the Cygnus bubble (incorporating an updated gas density profile from atomic, molecular, and ionized tracers) within a Bayesian framework to test different particle diffusion regimes at cluster termination shocks.

Method

Used the Nautilus Bayesian fitting module to constrain cluster wind parameters ($u_{\text{sw}}$, $\dot{M}_*$, $\rho_0$, $\mathcal{T}_{\text{age}}$, $s$, $\eta_B$, $\epsilon_{\text{cr}}$) against Fermi-LAT, HAWC, ARGO, and LHAASO data under Kolmogorov, Kraichnan, and Bohm diffusion assumptions, subsequently computing expected all-flavor neutrino fluxes for IceCube and KM3NeT.

    We investigate the unitary implementability of the canonical transformations corresponding to Darboux transformations in the Hamiltonian formalism recently developed for gauge invariant perturbations of nonrotating black holes. Starting from the formalism in the black hole interior, we demonstrate that these canonical transformations are unitarily implementable under general conditions on the effective potentials appearing in the master equations of the perturbations. We also show that the privileged massless Fock representation of the perturbative variables selected by admitting (Heisenberg) unitary dynamics, employed in our quantization, is not unitarily equivalent to the simple massless representation of the master variables satisfying the effective Regge-Wheeler equation. This highlights the importance of the choice of master variables in which the quantum representation is initially defined. Furthermore, we show that not all such choices of variables possessing unitarily implementable dynamics can be reached through Darboux transformations, but rather through more general canonical transformations. These results advance our understanding of the relationship between well-established classical relations in black hole perturbation theory and their quantum representations, explaining how isospectral Darboux transformations can be extended to include all the infinitely many possible values of the angular momentum such that they are unitarily implementable in a quantum field theory context.

    Excess inner-horizon entropy and the thermodynamics of Kerr formationFrancisco S. N. Lobo, Jorge Ovalle, Manuel E. Rodrigues · gr-qc
    Abstract

    \noindent We develop a charge-matched thermodynamic framework for the regular rotating black-hole family underlying an exact model of Kerr formation. Since the Kerr endpoint already possesses a Cauchy horizon, hidden entropy is identified with the excess inner-horizon area relative to Kerr at fixed mass $M$ and angular momentum $J=Ma$: $\Sexc=\pi[h_c^2-(r_-^{\Kerr})^2]$, where $h_c$ is the regular inner-horizon radius and $r_-^{\Kerr}=M-\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$. Under regularity and interior-profile assumptions, we prove $r_-^{\Kerr}<h_c<h$ and establish the sharp bound $0<\Sexc<4\pi M\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$. The Schwarzschild limit yields $\Sexc=\pi h_c^2$, while $\Sexc=0$ at the Kerr endpoint. The Kerr reference entropy satisfies $S_+^{\Kerr}+S_-^{\Kerr}=4\pi M^2$, so rotation redistributes its two-horizon contributions at fixed mass. For the time-dependent model, we derive exact evolution equations for the inner root and $\Sexc$, interpreted as root-bookkeeping identities unless the roots are established as dynamical horizons. In the minimal $N=1$ trajectory, the apparent pole at $n=2$ is removable in the mass profile, giving a logarithmic continuation of the root equation, although the stationary curvature is singular there. The stationary Ricci-scalar density is locally absolutely integrable for $-1<n\leq2$, with the Kerr endpoint $n=-1$ treated separately. Minkowski breaking occurs at $n=0$, independently of inner-horizon disappearance. Quasi-extremal regular configurations approach, but cannot attain, the entropy bound without losing a regular Killing horizon.

In this short note, we examine the resources required by this idealized conversion.     Projective relativistic dynamics from a quantum Lorentz algebra: time-dependent deformed Hamiltonians on (anti-)de Sitter spacesRutwig Campoamor-Stursberg, Oscar Carballal, Francisco J. Herranz · math-ph
    Abstract

    A (non-standard) quantum deformation of the Lorentz algebra $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$ is used to construct novel time-dependent Hamiltonian systems on the (anti-)de Sitter spacetimes in $(1+1)$-dimensions. The specific deformation considered here contains, among others, the non-standard quantum deformation of $\mathfrak{sl}(2, \mathbb{R})$ and that of the `book' algebra $\mathfrak{b}_{2}$ as quantum subalgebras. The guiding idea is to apply the theory of Poisson--Hopf deformations of Lie--Hamilton systems to the Lorentz deformation, giving rise to a broad family of time-dependent deformed systems that share the same deformed Poisson--Hopf Lorentz algebra symmetry. In particular, this approach provides Hamiltonian vector fields expressed in terms of certain projective canonical variables, which, in turn, are related to the usual Beltrami variables via a canonical transformation. Under this construction, it is shown that the underlying Lorentizan spaces possess constant curvature $\kappa$ and the quantum deformation parameter $z$ can be identified with $\kappa$, such that the cosmological constant is $\Lambda=-z\equiv -\kappa$. Therefore, we deal with the anti-de Sitter, de Sitter and Minkowskian spaces for $z>0$, $z<0$ and $z=0$ (the undeformed case), respectively. Finally, as applications, we study time-dependent deformed oscillators on the (anti-)de Sitter spaces in detail, as well as time-dependent deformed Bateman and generalised coupled Caldirola--Kanai Hamiltonians.

    Abstract

    We construct $p$-adic polynomial approximations to the zero-framing cohomological quasimap vertex of cotangent bundles to complete flag varieties from the trigonometric Calogero--Moser system. Negative integer couplings give regular staircase Jack polynomials with nonnegative integer coefficients. We compare them with complete-flag master polynomials extending the construction of Smirnov--Varchenko. Both families converge coefficientwise to the vertex and satisfy the full commuting equations modulo $p^s$. For $n\geq3$, we prove sharp degree-dependent congruences and compute leading residues at every $p$-power scale.     Enforced Gaplessness by Combining Non-Anomalous SymmetriesTakamasa Ando, Kantaro Ohmori · cond-mat.str-el
    Abstract

    Symmetry-enforced gaplessness is a phenomenon in which a quantum system is forced to be gapless due to its global symmetry. Previous works have mainly focused on anomalous symmetries. Another approach, using symmetry spans, which specify two embeddings of a common symmetry into two larger symmetries, was proposed by the authors. In this work, we show that a symmetry span can enforce gaplessness even when both larger symmetries are invertible and non-anomalous: each alone admits a trivially gapped phase, but because the embeddings differ by a twist associated with a symmetry-protected topological phase, the symmetry they generate admits no gapped phase with finitely many ground states. We present such a span of a finite group and $\mathrm{U}(1)^2$ in 1+1 dimensions, with continuum realizations and a lattice Hamiltonian, with a finite-dimensional local Hilbert space and finitely many parameters, that is gapless in any region of its parameter space. We also discuss a 2+1-dimensional example and spans of only finite symmetries.
