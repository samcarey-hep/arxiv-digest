8skimNeutrino Mixing Model Correlations

Correlations in oscillation data and their impact on neutrino mixing models

Janusz Gluza, Biswajit Karmakar, Szymon Zi\keba

hep-pharXiv:2610.27270

A multidimensional $\tilde{\chi}^2$ method reconstructs 6D oscillation parameter correlations from NuFIT data, tightly constraining neutrino mixing models and excluding trimaximal $\mathrm{TM}_2$ mixing at $>3\sigma$ once JUNO is included.

Novel idea

Rather than testing discrete flavor-symmetry mixing sum rules against decoupled 1D or 2D parameter intervals, the authors construct an interpolated continuous multi-dimensional likelihood surface from public 1D--3D NuFIT datasets to estimate the full 6D parameter correlations. This permits projection of model-specific algebraic constraints directly onto the correlated experimental manifold to identify genuine optimal fit points ($\tilde{\chi}^2_{\text{min}}$).

Method

The framework interpolates available 1D, 2D, and 3D $\Delta\chi^2$ data from NuFIT v6.0 and v6.1 (including initial 59-day JUNO measurements of $\theta_{12}$ and $\Delta m^2_{21}$). Analytical sum rules for $\mathrm{TM}_1$, $\mathrm{TM}_2$, and $\mu-\tau$ reflection symmetries ($\mu 1-\tau 1$, $\mu 2-\tau 2$) are implemented as hypersurface constraints during numerical minimization over the correlated parameter space. The resulting constraints are propagated to predictions for $m_{\beta}$ and $m_{\beta\beta}$.

Key results

  • Inverted ordering (IO) generally yields better fits than normal ordering (NO) for the surveyed schemes, with $\mu 1-\tau 1$ under IO giving the best fit overall ($\tilde{\chi}^2_{\text{min}} = 0.12$).
  • The updated solar precision from JUNO in NuFIT v6.1 decisively rules out $\mathrm{TM}_2$ mixing at $>3\sigma$ C.L. ($\tilde{\chi}^2 > 9$) for both mass orderings due to the $\theta_{12}-\theta_{13}$ constraint.
  • $\mathrm{TM}_1$ remains fully viable under NuFIT v6.1 with $\tilde{\chi}^2_{\text{min}} \sim 0.63$ (NO) and $0.78$ (IO).
  • Correlations significantly contract the allowed $m_{\beta\beta}$ bands at low lightest-neutrino mass $m_0$ in NO, though the impact in IO is comparatively mild.

Why it matters to you

While primarily focused on discrete flavor symmetry model-building (a tangential topic for cross-section modelers), the public Mathematica tool provides a valuable, ready-to-use pipeline for testing any BSM or oscillation hypothesis that introduces algebraic relations among PMNS parameters against correlated global data.

Caveats

The 6D correlation surface is an approximation reconstructed from lower-dimensional projections because full 6D likelihood chains from global-fit collaborations are not publicly available; cosmological bounds on $\sum m_\nu$ are not incorporated.

Abstract

With increasing precision of neutrino experiments, complete correlation among the oscillation parameters will scrutinize theoretical neutrino mass and mixing models. Using available 1D--3D global-fit datasets, we construct a multidimensional $\tilde{\chi}^2$ function that provides a conservative estimate of the full six-dimensional parameter correlations. In our approach, we delineate the model-constrained parameter space by combining the interpolated experimental data with the analytical relations implied by a given mixing scheme. We identify $\tilde{\chi}^2_{\text{min}}$, corresponding to the optimal agreement attainable for parameter points satisfying the model. Applying this framework to several schemes ($\text{TM}_{1,2}$, $\mu1,2$--$\tau1,2$) yields generally better fits for IO than for NO, with the $\mu1$--$\tau1$ scheme under IO providing the best overall fit. While $\mu1,2$--$\tau1,2$ and $\text{TM}_1$ schemes perform significantly better than $\text{TM}_2$, the latter is excluded at $>3\sigma$ C.L. using NuFIT~v6.1 data. We further evaluate the implications of these correlation-respecting constraints on the effective neutrino mass observables relevant to tritium $\beta$-decay ($m_{\beta}$) and neutrinoless double $\beta$-decay ($m_{\beta\beta}$). The proposed approach can be readily extended to test any theoretical framework that predicts algebraic relations among neutrino oscillation parameters, and we provide a GitHub starter kit.

8worth readingHadronization and fragmentation

Hadronization in QCD and Fragmentation Functions

Ignacio Borsa, Marco Stratmann

hep-pharXiv:2610.28207

A comprehensive review of collinear parton-to-hadron fragmentation functions, their NNLO evolution and coefficient functions, sum rules, and current global extractions for pions, kaons, and charged hadrons.

Novel idea

This state-of-the-art review consolidates recent theoretical advancements up to NNLO in time-like DGLAP evolution and hard-scattering cross sections, highlighting the resolution of previous ambiguities in the time-like splitting functions via SCET and all-order reciprocity relations, while synthesizing tension patterns among modern global fits (MAPFF, BDSSV, NPC, JAM).

Method

The authors review pQCD collinear factorization applied to single-inclusive $e^+e^-$ annihilation (SIA), semi-inclusive DIS (SIDIS), and hadron production in $pp$ collisions. They detail both $z$-space (Runge-Kutta) and Mellin-moment solution schemes of matrix RGEs, matching conditions for heavy flavors in variable flavor number schemes (VFNS), and systematic data selections across collider experiments.

Key results

  • Singlet fragmentation distributions ($D_\Sigma$) are tightly pinned down across all modern fits by LEP/SLD data at $\mu = M_Z$, but severe discrepancies persist in gluon fragmentation ($D_g$).
  • Unfavored fragmentation functions, particularly $D_{\bar{u}}^{\pi^+}$ and $D_{s+\bar{s}}^{\pi^+}$, show poor agreement across fits at low $z$ ($z \lesssim 0.2$), heavily influenced by differing flavor/isospin-symmetry assumptions in parameterizations.
  • Discrepancies in the gluon FF are traced to persistent tensions between RHIC and LHC $pp \to h X$ measurements, which await resolution via global fits utilizing newly available NNLO $pp$ calculations.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to high-energy neutrino-nucleus scattering: understanding hadronization and fragmentation functions is essential for transitioning from shallow to deep-inelastic scattering (SIS/DIS) and benchmarking hadron formation and intranuclear cascade models inside neutrino event generators such as GENIE, GiBUU, and NuWro.

Caveats

Focuses strictly on collinear, integrated fragmentation of light hadrons; TMD fragmentation, di-hadron FFs, and heavy-flavor hadronization (e.g., $D$-meson formation) are outside the scope.

Abstract

The hadronization of quarks and gluons is reviewed within the framework of collinear parton-to-hadron fragmentation functions closely associated with the notion of factorization in hard scattering processes in QCD. The state-of-the-art in calculating both the scale evolution of fragmentation functions and the partonic cross sections pertinent to their determination from data up to next-to-next-to-leading order accuracy is presented. Potential improvements up to next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic order are outlined as well. All relevant experimental data sets for single-inclusive pion, kaon, and unidentified charged hadron production are listed. The most recent sets of fragmentation functions and their uncertainties extracted from these data in global QCD analyses utilizing various different strategies are presented and compared to each other.

8worth readingHeavy neutral leptons

Probing a heavy neutral lepton in a radiative neutrino model at a muon collider

Isaac Bamwidhi, Mohamed Belfkir, Mohamed Amin Loualidi, Salah Nasri

hep-pharXiv:2610.28252

A detector-level study at a $3~\mathrm{TeV}$ muon collider shows that combining direct and Higgs-mediated production of a Dirac HNL ($m_Z < m_N < m_h$) constrains $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ down to $6.7\times 10^{-6}$.

Novel idea

The paper provides a model-consistent collider analysis where both direct production ($\mu^+\mu^- \to N\nu$) and Higgs-mediated $WW$-fusion production ($\mu^+\mu^- \to \nu\bar{\nu}h, h \to N\nu$) are simultaneously generated and analyzed within the concrete radiative neutrino-mass model T4-3-i-C1, tying the $hN\nu$ vertex directly to active-heavy mixing $U_{\alpha N}$ rather than treating them as decoupled parameters.

Method

Signal and SM backgrounds were simulated for a $3~\mathrm{TeV}$ muon collider ($1~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$) with MadGraph5_aMC@NLO, Pythia 8, and a Delphes fast detector simulation for six benchmarks ($m_N \sim 103$--$119~\mathrm{GeV}$). A boosted decision tree (XGBoost) separated the common prompt signature $\ell^\pm jj + p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{miss}}$ from backgrounds. Limits at 95% C.L. on visible cross sections and $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ were derived using asymptotic $\mathrm{CL}_s$ in pyhf, assuming $10\%$ systematic uncertainty on background bins.

Key results

  • Projected 95% C.L. upper limits on the visible signal cross section range from $0.027$ to $0.113~\mathrm{fb}$.
  • Corresponding expected limits on $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ range from $6.7\times 10^{-6}$ to $8.3\times 10^{-5}$ across the six mass benchmarks.
  • While Higgs-mediated production accounts for $15\%--72\%$ of the selected signal rate, it improves $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ sensitivity by only $0.18\%--1.19\%$ because its softer kinematics place events into lower-significance classifier bins.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts the BSM neutrino program at future high-energy lepton colliders, providing concrete benchmark sensitivity for intermediate-mass Dirac HNLs and highlighting the pitfall of assuming event yields translate directly into exclusion power when combining distinct production topologies.

Caveats

Analysis neglects beam-induced backgrounds (BIB) characteristic of muon colliders, assumes unpolarized beams, uses a generic detector card rather than a full Muon Collider Detector simulation, and restricts HNL decays to the prompt regime.

Abstract

We investigate direct and Higgs-mediated production of a Dirac heavy neutral lepton in a radiative neutrino-mass model at a future $3~\mathrm{TeV}$ muon collider. We consider heavy neutral lepton masses in the window $m_Z<m_N<m_h$, where the decays $N\to\ell W$ and $N\to\nu Z$ can proceed with on-shell weak bosons. We consider both direct production, $\mu^+\mu^-\to N\nu$, and Higgs-mediated production through $\mu^+\mu^-\to\nu_\mu\bar\nu_\mu h$, $h\to N\nu$. Both channels lead to the common $\ell^\pm jj+p_{\mathrm T}^{\mathrm{miss}}$ final state. We perform a detector-level analysis with an integrated luminosity of $1~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$, assuming unpolarized muon beams, and derive median expected $95\%$ confidence-level upper limits on the visible signal cross section ranging from approximately $0.027$ to $0.113~\mathrm{fb}$, corresponding to limits on the mixing strength parameter $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ ranging from $6.7\times10^{-6}$ to $8.3\times10^{-5}$ for six benchmark points. The Higgs-mediated production channel contributes $\sim15$--$72\%$ of the signal after the common event selection, but improves the expected limits by $\sim0.18$--$1.19\%$ within the common classifier and binning scheme. This small improvement reflects the classifier-score distribution of the Higgs-mediated events. The limits on $|U_{\mu N}|^2$ account for the benchmark-specific flavor mixings and assume an independent $10\%$ background uncertainty in each classifier-score bin.

Reconciling anomalous quadrupole deformation in $^{200,202}$Hg with Coulomb excitation

G. Willmott, J. Heery, J. Henderson, P. Stevenson, C. Cousins, D. T. Doherty + 15 more

nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.28202 · cross-list

Coulomb excitation remeasurements of $^{200,202}\mathrm{Hg}$ resolve previous unphysical spectroscopic quadrupole moments, establishing moderate oblate collectivity within physical rigid-rotor limits.

Novel idea

Prior literature reported $Q_s(2^+_1)$ values for $^{200,202}\mathrm{Hg}$ that severely exceeded the maximum physical limits established by $B(E2)$ transition strengths for an axial rotor. By employing inverse kinematics with heavy Hg beams on $^{48}\mathrm{Ti}$ and $^{120}\mathrm{Sn}$ targets at ATLAS, this experiment removes target-excitation ambiguities and brings the quadrupole moments back into consistency with collective systematics.

Method

Beams of $^{200,202}\mathrm{Hg}$ at $\sim 3.9~\mathrm{MeV/u}$ were Coulomb-excited on $^{48}\mathrm{Ti}$ and $^{120}\mathrm{Sn}$ targets. Reaction products were tagged with an annular double-sided silicon strip detector (DSSD) in coincidence with de-excitation $\gamma$-rays measured in Gammasphere (74 HPGe detectors). Matrix elements and $Q_s(2^+_1)$ were deduced via coupled-channels minimization in GOSIA/GOSIAFitter.

Key results

  • Obtained spectroscopic quadrupole moments $Q_s(2^+_1) = 0.52(14)~e\mathrm{b}$ for $^{200}\mathrm{Hg}$ and $0.63(10)~e\mathrm{b}$ for $^{202}\mathrm{Hg}$ (literature-constrained fit).
  • Confirmed oblate deformation ($Q_s > 0$) while strictly satisfying the physical limit $|Q_s(2^+_1)| \le Q_{\mathrm{rot}}(2^+_1)$ derived from $B(E2)$ values.
  • Potential energy surface calculations confirm shallow oblate minima with near-zero octupole deformation ($\beta_3 \approx 0$), implying a suppressed atomic Schiff moment.

Why it matters to you

Crucial for atomic EDM searches in $^{199}\mathrm{Hg}$ (which constrain $T$- and $CP$-violating BSM physics via the nuclear Schiff moment), as the extracted Schiff moment depends directly on core quadrupole and octupole deformations.

Caveats

Does not measure the odd-mass isotope $^{199}\mathrm{Hg}$ directly; deformation properties must be inferred across isotopes. Also, $B(E3)$ strengths could not be extracted directly from this dataset.

Abstract

Stable isotopes of mercury, with two fewer protons than magic lead, are found experimentally to exhibit oblate deformation, similar to neighbouring platinum isotopes. These isotopes are of special interest due to the use of $^{199}$Hg as an experimental laboratory for electric dipole moment searches, which are a powerful test of charge-parity ($CP$) violation and the interpretation of which depends critically on the quadrupole and octopule shapes of the underlying nucleus. It is observed that the ratio between experimental quadrupole moments and electric quadrupole transition strengths exceed physical limits, placing uncertainty on this underlying shape. In an effort to resolve this situation, a Coulomb excitation experiment was performed to remeasure the spectroscopic quadrupole moment ($Q_s(2^+_1)$) of $^{200}$Hg and $^{202}$Hg utilising $^{48}$Ti and $^{120}$Sn targets. The values obtained were $Q_s(2^+_1)$ $^{200,202}$Hg = $0.52(14)$, $0.63(10)$ eb, lying within physical limits defined by $B(E2)$ values but still consistent with near axially-symmetric oblate deformation. A systematic study of nuclei indicates that, as with neighbouring Pt isotopes, $^{200,202}$Hg are strongly oblate deformed, albeit without fully developing into an axial rotor.

Ijaz Ahmed, Waqas Ahmad, M. Tayyab Javaid, Jamil Muhammad

hep-pharXiv:2610.27006

Projections for mono-photon searches $\mu^+\mu^- \to \gamma a (\to \nu\bar{\nu})$ at 3 and 10 TeV muon colliders constrain $g_{a\gamma\gamma} \gtrsim 0.012~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$, supplemented by an HNL diphoton feasibility check.

Novel idea

The paper investigates an invisible ALP decaying exclusively into neutrinos ($a \to \nu\bar{\nu}$) via a direct pseudoscalar coupling, rather than the standard invisible dark-photon/stable-ALP scenarios, evaluating the mono-photon recoil kinematic line against the irreducible $\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma$ background at multi-TeV lepton colliders.

Method

Signal benchmarks ($m_a = 100$--$1000~\mathrm{GeV}$, $g_{a\gamma\gamma} = 0.01~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$, $y_\nu = 0.1, 0.3$) were simulated using MadGraph5_aMC@NLO, Pythia 8, and Delphes for $\sqrt{s}=3~\mathrm{TeV}$ ($1~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$) and $10~\mathrm{TeV}$ ($10~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$). Background discrimination was tested with cut-based photon-line windows and TMVA BDTG classifiers using photon $p_T$, $\eta_\gamma$, and recoil mass. A distinct parton-level exercise explores an HNL-mediated diphoton signature.

Key results

  • For $\mathrm{BR}(a\to\nu\bar{\nu}) > 99.995\%$, the cut-based analysis yields 95% expected sensitivity of $g_{a\gamma\gamma} \simeq 0.039$--$0.045~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$ at $3~\mathrm{TeV}$ and $0.0137$--$0.0140~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$ at $10~\mathrm{TeV}$.
  • The BDTG classifier reaches held-out sensitivities of $g_{95} = 0.0192$--$0.0400~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$ ($3~\mathrm{TeV}$) and $0.0122$--$0.0144~\mathrm{TeV}^{-1}$ ($10~\mathrm{TeV}$), with AUC values exceeding 0.996.
  • Sensitivities are reported strictly as expected reach rather than formal exclusions because the extreme BDTG signal tails contain only 2--41 unweighted background events.

Why it matters to you

Relevant for BSM phenomenological searches involving neutrinos and ALPs/HNLs at future multi-TeV muon colliders, highlighting the power of mono-photon recoil kinematics.

Caveats

Uses a phenomenological low-energy Lagrangian where the unsuppressed $y_\nu$ coupling to chiral neutrinos violates $SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ gauge invariance without an explicit UV sterile completion; simulation omits beam-induced backgrounds and forward detector tags.

Abstract

We study the mono-photon signature $\mu^+\mu^-\to\gamma a\to\gamma\nu\bar\nu$ at $3$ and $10\TeV$ muon colliders, with integrated luminosities of $1$ and $10\,\mathrm{ab}^{-1}$, in a two-coupling axion-like-particle (ALP) simplified model. Five benchmarks spanning $m_a=100$--$1000\GeV$ are generated at $g_{a\gamma\gamma}=0.01\TeV^{-1}$ and flavor-universal pseudoscalar neutrino couplings $y_\nu=0.1$ or $0.3$, giving invisible branching fractions above 99.995\% and mono-photon cross sections of $0.00854$--$0.01217\,\mathrm{fb}$. Assuming a 1\% background normalization uncertainty, a cut-based photon-line selection gives projected 95\% sensitivity to $g_{a\gamma\gamma}\simeq0.039$--$0.045\TeV^{-1}$ at $3\TeV$ and $0.0137$--$0.0140\TeV^{-1}$ at $10\TeV$. A ROOT/TMVA gradient-boosted decision tree with validation-fixed thresholds reaches held-out AUC values of $0.9967$--$0.9993$ and $g_{95}=0.0192$--$0.0400\TeV^{-1}$ at $3\TeV$ and $0.0122$--$0.0144\TeV^{-1}$ at $10\TeV$. Because only 2-41 irreducible-background test events populate the optimized classifier tails, the mass--coupling contours are reported as projected expected sensitivities rather than profile-likelihood or CL$_s$ exclusions.
