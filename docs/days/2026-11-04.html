10must-readNuclear neutron distributions CEvNS

Electroweak Constraints on Nuclear Neutron Distributions and the Low-Energy Weak Mixing Angle

Xu-Run Huang, Tong-Gang Yue, Zhen Zhang, Lie-Wen Chen

nucl-th hep-ex hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2611.03751

Combining PREX-II and CREX parity-violating electron scattering via an extended Skyrme EDF framework delivers tight neutron form-factor constraints, yielding $\sin^2\theta_W=0.231^{+0.016}_{-0.015}$ from a joint fit to COHERENT CsI, Ge, and Ar data.

Novel idea

Previous CEvNS extractions of the weak mixing angle $\sin^2\theta_W$ suffered from strong degeneracies with target neutron distributions or relied on hadronic scattering inputs. This paper uses an extended Skyrme energy density functional (EDF) family with enhanced isovector spin-orbit interaction to reconcile the CREX and PREX-II PVES measurements, systematically propagating electroweak constraints to CEvNS target nuclei (${}^{133}\mathrm{Cs}$, ${}^{127}\mathrm{I}$, Ge isotopes, ${}^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$) to achieve relative point-neutron radius uncertainties below $1\%$.

Method

The authors constructed an extended Skyrme EDF family incorporating an enhanced isovector spin-orbit interaction with symmetry energy slope $L_c$ varied from 30 to 90 MeV (baseline IVSO250-Lc50). Standard Model weak charges with $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ electroweak radiative corrections were used to relate PVES charge-weak form factor differences $\Delta F_{\mathrm{CW}}$ across nuclei. Target point-neutron form factors $F_n(q)$ were fitted using Helm distributions, which were then deployed in a joint fit to reconstructed recoil spectra and timing distributions from COHERENT CsI, Ge, and Ar datasets.

Key results

  • Extracted point-neutron rms radii with $<1\%$ relative uncertainty for key CEvNS targets (e.g., $R_n = 4.887 \pm 0.038\mathrm{~fm}$ for ${}^{133}\mathrm{Cs}$, $3.468 \pm 0.031\mathrm{~fm}$ for ${}^{40}\mathrm{Ar}$, and $4.076 \pm 0.034\mathrm{~fm}$ for ${}^{74}\mathrm{Ge}$).
  • Determined the low-energy weak mixing angle to be $\sin^2\theta_W = 0.231^{+0.016}_{-0.015}$ at momentum transfer scale $\mu \sim 50\mathrm{~MeV}$ from a combined fit to COHERENT CsI, Ge, and Ar data.
  • Demonstrated that the precision gain is driven primarily by recent COHERENT Ge data, with the CsI+Ge joint fit yielding $\sin^2\theta_W = 0.227^{+0.016}_{-0.015}$.

Why it matters to you

Directly targets core interests in CEvNS, nuclear weak form factors, electroweak radiative corrections, and extracting $\sin^2\theta_W$ from COHERENT data. Provides rigorous, PVES-calibrated Helm form factor inputs for neutrino event generators, low-energy interaction modeling, and dark matter detector predictions.

Caveats

Neglects nuclear spin-orbit current response terms in the CEvNS analysis (though shown to shift yields by only $\sim 0.2\text{--}0.4\%$). Relies on the assumption that an enhanced isovector spin-orbit interaction fully resolves the CREX-PREX puzzle across the nuclear chart.

Abstract

Coherent elastic neutrino--nucleus scattering (CE$\nu$NS) offers a low-energy probe of the weak mixing angle, $\sin^2\theta_W$, and possible new physics in the neutrino sector. Its reliable interpretation, however, requires independent constraints on the neutron distributions of the target nuclei. We exploit correlations across nuclei within an extended Skyrme energy-density-functional framework to propagate constraints from the CREX and PREX-II parity-violating electron-scattering (PVES) measurements to CE$\nu$NS targets. This yields neutron form factors and radii, with relative radius uncertainties below $1\%$. Using these inferred neutron form factors, we jointly analyze the COHERENT CsI, Ge, and Ar data and obtain $\sin^2\theta_W=0.231^{+0.016}_{-0.015}$, consistent with the Standard Model prediction. These results provide the first electroweak-experimentally constrained benchmarks for nuclear-structure studies, neutrino-sector electroweak tests, and CE$\nu$NS signal predictions in dark-matter detectors.

Light Mediators at the Neutrino-Dark Matter Frontier: Coherent Scattering, Precision Constraints, and Complementary Searches

Raoul Serao, Mohamed Ouchemhou

hep-pharXiv:2611.04600

Analyzes light scalar and vector mediators across CEvNS, electron scattering, and DM detection, showing that target degeneracies in Ge and Xe leave blind directions and demonstrating how neutrino background uncertainties shape the neutrino fog.

Novel idea

Quantifies nuclear-target coupling degeneracies explicitly, demonstrating that combining ${}^{73}\mathrm{Ge}$ and ${}^{131}\mathrm{Xe}$ targets leaves nearly parallel blind directions due to a high condition number ($\sim 115$) of the nuclear-charge response matrix. It also introduces an analytic profile-likelihood framework to disentangle spectral overlap from external neutrino background constraints when evaluating dark-matter discovery limits in the neutrino fog.

Method

Formulates effective interaction models for light scalar (S1) and vector (V1, V2) mediators coupled to neutrinos, dark matter, and charged fermions. Computes differential cross sections for CEvNS (CsI, Ge, Xe, Ar), neutrino-electron scattering, and DM direct detection across momentum scales ($q \sim 3\text{--}99\mathrm{~MeV}$). Evaluates target complementarity using a two-target linear response analysis and models dark matter discovery sensitivity via an analytic profile-likelihood with Gaussian-information Fisher matrices.

Key results

  • Proved that the nuclear-charge response matrix for ${}^{73}\mathrm{Ge}$ and ${}^{131}\mathrm{Xe}$ has a condition number of $\approx 115$, demonstrating that multi-target CEvNS alone cannot resolve up- vs down-quark coupling degeneracies ($g_u$ vs $g_d$).
  • Derived an analytic expression $\Delta \sigma_{\alpha}^2 / \sigma_{\alpha}^2 = \rho^2 / [(1 - \rho^2)(1 + \lambda)]$ showing how dark-matter sensitivity degradation depends on spectral overlap $ ho$ and external background constraints $\lambda$.
  • Mapped the distinct kinematic momentum transfer ranges probed by stopped-pion CsI ($35\text{--}99\mathrm{~MeV}$), solar Xe ($11\text{--}27\mathrm{~MeV}$), and reactor Ge ($3\text{--}12\mathrm{~MeV}$) detectors.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to research on BSM light mediator signatures at CEvNS experiments (COHERENT, CONUS+), liquid-xenon DM direct detection (LZ, XENONnT, PandaX-4T), and solar neutrino backgrounds.

Caveats

The profile-likelihood dark-matter sensitivity calculation is illustrative rather than a full experimental global fit, omitting multi-nuisance parameter profiling and detector-specific correlated systematic uncertainties.

Abstract

Low-threshold neutrino and dark-matter experiments constrain light mediators through different momentum transfers, targets, and coupling combinations. We examine scalar and vector interactions in coherent neutrino--nucleus scattering, neutrino--electron scattering, and dark-matter direct detection. Three representative interaction models are used to compare recoil spectra and coupling degeneracies. For $^{73}\mathrm{Ge}$ and $^{131}\mathrm{Xe}$, the nuclear-charge response matrix has a condition number of approximately 115, so two different targets may still leave nearly parallel blind directions. An analytic profile-likelihood calculation shows how spectral overlap and uncertainties in neutrino interactions affect dark-matter discovery sensitivity in the neutrino fog. We discuss the implications of measurements from COHERENT, CONUS+, LZ, XENONnT, and PandaX-4T and specify which theoretical inputs, detector responses, and statistical correlations are needed to combine their constraints. keyword : coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering; light mediators; neutrino--electron scattering; dark matter direct detection; non-standard interactions; solar neutrinos; complementarity

10worth readingGamow-Teller resonance and neutrinos

Information content of the Gamow Teller Resonance: Bayesian inference with self-consistent Skyrme models

Zidu Lin, Gianluca Colò, Andrew W. Steiner, Amber Stinson

nucl-tharXiv:2611.05153

Uses Bayesian inference with Gamow-Teller resonance (GTR) data to constrain Skyrme EDFs (nuSky*), significantly reducing the Landau-Migdal parameter $G'_0$ uncertainty and axial-current neutrino-nucleon opacities in compact-star matter.

Novel idea

Connects terrestrial Gamow-Teller resonance (GTR) measurements ($m_0\%$ and $E_{\mathrm{GT}}$ in ${}^{90}\mathrm{Zr}$, ${}^{132}\mathrm{Sn}$, ${}^{208}\mathrm{Pb}$) via a Bayesian posterior to astrophysical charged-current, axial-current neutrino-nucleon absorption rates in hot, dense compact-star matter. Identifies two new optimal Skyrme EDF parameterizations (`nuSkyI` and `nuSkyII`) that simultaneously describe ground-state nuclear properties, GTR spectra, and neutron-star matter bulk properties.

Method

Employed Charge-Exchange Random Phase Approximation (RPA) to compute GTR strength functions in finite nuclei and linear response theory in frequency space for uniform nucleonic matter. Performed Bayesian inference with intrinsic systematic error parameters over 25 experimental constraints (binding energies, charge radii, neutron skins, spin-orbit splittings, and GTR features). Propagated posterior Skyrme parameter sets (`GTAll`) to compute the RPA spin-isospin residual interaction parameter $G'_0$ and neutrino opacities in dense matter.

Key results

  • Constructed two new Skyrme parameterizations: `nuSkyI` ($L=53.4\mathrm{~MeV}$) and `nuSkyII` ($L=64.2\mathrm{~MeV}$), which satisfy ground-state, GTR, and high-density neutron star matter stability criteria.
  • Demonstrated that GTR constraints substantially narrow the posterior distribution of the Landau-Migdal parameter $G'_0$, heavily reducing the theoretical spread in charged-current axial neutrino opacity ($\mathrm{MB}_{ax}$).
  • Identified sub-saturation symmetry energy and the in-medium potential difference $\delta U = U_n - U_p$ as the remaining dominant source of neutrino opacity uncertainty in compact-star matter.

Why it matters to you

Highly relevant for nuclear density functional theory, spin-isospin electroweak response, Gamow-Teller transitions, and neutrino-nucleon/nucleus cross sections in supernova and neutron star merger environments.

Caveats

Does not account for cluster formation around the neutrinosphere, which can alter sub-saturation nucleon potentials and $\delta U$. Finite-nucleus GTR data cannot constrain the sub-saturation symmetry energy slope, leaving remaining vertical spread in opacity predictions.

Abstract

Many important charge-exchange weak processes, such as beta decays, double beta decays, and neutrino-nucleus and neutrino-nucleon reactions, are closely related to the spin- and isospin-dependent nuclear force, yet few of them can be precisely measured, or even observed, in terrestrial experiments. The Gamow-Teller resonance (GTR) in finite nuclei is one of the few experimental probes of this spin-isospin force. In a companion letter, we obtained the probability distribution of the Landau-Migdal parameter $G'_0$, which characterizes the main part of the spin-isospin force, from GTR measurements using Bayesian inference. In this follow-up work, we quantitatively study the correlations emerging from this Bayesian analysis. From the posterior, we identify two standard Skyrme parameterizations (nuSky*) that reproduce, in a unified manner, ground-state observables of finite nuclei, the salient features of the GTR, and the bulk properties of neutron-star matter. Since collective excitations in finite nuclei and in neutron-star matter are related, we use the Skyrme interactions sampled from the posterior to constrain the axial-current, charged-current neutrino-nucleon reaction rates in hot and dense compact-star matter, as the first astrophysical application of this study. The GTR-informed calculations substantially reduce the spread of the neutrino opacity; nevertheless, significant uncertainties remain due to the poorly constrained symmetry energy at sub-saturation densities. Constraining this quantity on top of a well-constrained spin-isospin interaction would pave the way towards a systematic determination of neutrino interactions in dense matter.

10worth readingTau-sterile neutrino mixing

Constraining tau-sterile neutrino mixing with high-energy astrophysical neutrinos

Mauricio Bustamante, Qinrui Liu

hep-pharXiv:2611.05170

Demonstrates that mixing between tau and sterile neutrinos ($|U_{\tau 4}|^2$) reduces the high-energy astrophysical $\nu_\tau$ fraction at Earth, placing an IceCube limit of $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 < 0.88$ and projected multi-telescope bounds down to $0.12\text{--}0.14$.

Novel idea

Exploits the unique property of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos—where cosmic sources produce almost no $\nu_\tau$, but oscillations generate $\sim 1/3$ $\nu_\tau$ at Earth—to probe $|U_{\tau 4}|^2$ independently of sterile neutrino mass $\Delta m^2_{41}$. Proves that while $|U_{e4}|^2$ and $|U_{\mu 4}|^2$ can be absorbed or obscured by unknown source flavor ratios $f_{e,\mathrm{S}}$, tau-sterile mixing produces an unmistakable $\nu_\tau$ deficit that cannot be mimicked by source uncertainties.

Method

Used standard and 3+1 active-sterile neutrino oscillation probability frameworks over cosmological distances. Formulated a profile likelihood comparing predicted flavor compositions $(f_{e,\oplus}, f_{\mu,\oplus}, f_{\tau,\oplus})$ against 11.4-year IceCube MESE 2025 data and projected mock data for 2040 and 2050 multi-telescope arrays (IceCube, KM3NeT, Baikal-GVD, P-ONE, IceCube-Gen2, TRIDENT, HUNT). Profiled over active oscillation parameters (NuFIT 6.1) and source electron flavor fraction $f_{e,\mathrm{S}} \in [0,1]$.

Key results

  • Derived a 90% C.L. upper limit of $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 < 0.88$ from 11.4-year IceCube MESE data.
  • Projected multi-detector limits of $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 < 0.24\text{--}0.29$ by 2040 and $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 < 0.12\text{--}0.14$ by 2050 (for pion-decay sources).
  • Showed that 2050 multi-telescope combinations reach $3\sigma$ discovery potential if $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 \ge 0.23$ (pion decay) or $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 \ge 0.14$ (muon-damped sources).
  • Demonstrated robustness of limits against up to 10% $\nu_\tau$ emission at sources, non-zero $|U_{e4}|^2, |U_{\mu 4}|^2$, and inverted mass ordering.

Why it matters to you

Relevant for BSM neutrino physics, sterile neutrino mixing limits ($|U_{\tau 4}|^2$), and astrophysical flavor-ratio phenomenology at current and future neutrino observatories (IceCube-Gen2).

Caveats

Sensitivity is fundamentally limited by the experimental $\nu_e\text{--}\nu_\tau$ degeneracy (showers produced by both flavors look similar in Cherenkov detectors). Relies on planned multi-kilometer-scale neutrino telescopes being completed as scheduled.

Abstract

Whether a sterile neutrino mixes with the three active ones remains an open question. Its mixing with the tau neutrino, $|U_{\tau 4}|^2$, is the least constrained, since terrestrial experiments probe it only indirectly and over limited ranges of sterile-neutrino mass. We show that TeV-PeV astrophysical neutrinos probe it differently. Their sources make almost no $\nu_\tau$, but oscillations over cosmological distances turn about one third of them into $\nu_\tau$ before they reach Earth. Mixing with a sterile neutrino would make these $\nu_\tau$ scarcer, lowering the $\nu_\tau$ fraction that neutrino telescopes measure, whatever the sterile-neutrino mass. In contrast, sterile mixing with $\nu_e$ or $\nu_\mu$ is nearly undetectable. Fitting the flavor composition measured by IceCube, with the composition at the sources left free, we find $|U_{\tau 4}|^2 < 0.88$ at 90% C.L. Combining IceCube with present and planned neutrino telescopes, including IceCube-Gen2 and HUNT, could lower this limit to 0.24-0.29 by 2040 and to 0.12-0.14 by 2050. By 2050, these limits would match some present terrestrial limits, though not the strongest.

Constraints on a Long-Lived X17 Vector Boson with FASER

FASER Collaboration, Roshan Mammen Abraham, Xiaocong Ai, Saul Alonso Monsalve, John Anders, Emma Kate Anderson + 130 more

hep-ex hep-pharXiv:2611.03904

Combining FASER's dark photon ($e^+e^-$ tracks) and ALP (preshower/calorimeter) searches excludes the low-coupling window ($6\times 10^{-5} < \epsilon_e < 1.2\times 10^{-4}$) for a 17 MeV protophobic vector boson (X17).

Novel idea

Reinterprets and combines two orthogonal FASER Run 3 dataset searches—dark photons ($27\mathrm{~fb}^{-1}$) and ALPs ($57.7\mathrm{~fb}^{-1}$)—to cover the entire magnetized volume and preshower region of FASER for a protophobic vector boson candidate (X17). This provides the first LHC far-forward direct constraint that fully closes the low-coupling window of the X17 protophobic vector model.

Method

Modeled forward X17 production via proton bremsstrahlung (accounting for Dirac $F_1$ and Pauli $F_2$ form factors and damping factors) and $\eta \to X\gamma$ decays using FORESEE and CRMC/EPOS-LHC. Simulated $X \to e^+e^-$ decays inside FASER's active decay volume and downstream preshower/calorimeter regions. Constructed parameterized signal efficiencies for two-track and single-shower signatures, performing a profile likelihood $\mathrm{CL}_s$ combination at 90% C.L.

Key results

  • Observed zero events in the dark photon signal region and 1 event in the ALP signal region, consistent with expected SM backgrounds ($(2.3\pm 2.3)\times 10^{-3}$ and $0.44\pm 0.39$ events).
  • Excluded electron couplings $2\times 10^{-8} < \epsilon_e < 3\times 10^{-4}$ for $m_X = 17\mathrm{~MeV}$ and fixed neutron coupling $\epsilon_n = 10^{-2}$.
  • Excluded the previously unconstrained low-coupling ATOMKI window ($6\times 10^{-5} < \epsilon_e < 1.2\times 10^{-4}$), leaving only the high-coupling window ($\epsilon_e > 1.2\times 10^{-4}$) viable.
  • For the alternative protophobic model ($\epsilon_n = \epsilon_e = \epsilon$), excluded $2\times 10^{-5} < \epsilon < 1.5\times 10^{-4}$ at $m_X = 17\mathrm{~MeV}$.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to BSM physics with light mediators, X17 anomaly explanations, LHC far-forward searches (FASER), and bounds on electron couplings of feebly interacting vector bosons.

Caveats

The high-coupling window ($\epsilon_e > 3\times 10^{-4}$) remains unconstrained by FASER because short lifetimes cause X17 to decay before reaching the 480 m forward detector. Assumes strict protophobic vector couplings ($\epsilon_p \approx 0, \epsilon_\nu \approx 0$).

Abstract

The ATOMKI anomalies motivate the possibility of a new particle, the X17, with a mass of 17 MeV and weak couplings to nuclei and electrons. Such a particle may be produced at the LHC, travel 480 m in the far-forward direction, and decay to $e^+e^-$ in FASER. In this paper, previous FASER searches for dark photons and axion-like particles (ALPs) are combined and interpreted as searches for a candidate X17 particle, the protophobic vector boson. The searches use data collected from $pp$ collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 13.6$ TeV in LHC Run 3 from 2022 for the dark photon search and from 2022-2023 for the ALP search. No excess events are found above the expected background, and the combined null results exclude a previously-unconstrained "low-coupling window" with electron coupling $6 \times 10^{-5} < \epsilon_e < 1.2 \times 10^{-4}$. A "high-coupling window" remains.

Topological Diagnostics of CP Degeneracies in Long-Baseline Neutrino Oscillations with Non-Standard Interactions via Pancharatnam Open-Path Phases

Neda Razzaghi

hep-pharXiv:2611.03748

Open-path Pancharatnam geometric phases in long-baseline neutrino oscillations resolve parameter degeneracies between Dirac CP violation and non-standard interactions.

Novel idea

The paper extends the kinematic, open-path Pancharatnam-Mukunda-Simon geometric phase framework to non-standard neutrino interactions (NSI) with off-diagonal vector couplings, demonstrating that energy-dependent spectral dispersions in the holonomies break continuous parameter degeneracies.

Method

A discrete-layer transfer matrix formalism and exact analytic eigensystem evaluation (Cardano-Zaglauer-Schwarzer) are applied across the $L = 1300 ext{ km}$ DUNE baseline configuration to compute neutrino and antineutrino transition amplitudes and branch-dependent holonomies.

Key results

  • Demonstrates that neutrino and antineutrino degeneracy bands intersect at non-parallel angles in the $(\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}, \phi_{e\tau})$ plane.
  • Confirms the robustness of the holonomy gradient signature under realistic LArTPC energy smearing (15-20%).

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to long-baseline oscillation systematics and DUNE sensitivity analyses concerning new physics degeneracies in the appearance channel.

Caveats

Restricted primarily to off-diagonal $\varepsilon_{e\tau}$ coupling and single-layer crust approximations, though the formalism supports multi-layer structures.

Abstract

We investigate the topological signatures of neutral-current Non-Standard Interactions (NSI) in long-baseline neutrino oscillations by extending the open-path Pancharatnam geometric phase framework. Incorporating off-diagonal vector couplings ($\varepsilon_{e\tau} = |\varepsilon_{e\tau}|e^{i\phi_{e\tau}}$) into the discrete-layer matter Hamiltonian across the DUNE baseline ($L = 1300\text{ km}$), we analyze the state evolution for both neutrino ($\nu_\mu \to \nu_e$) and antineutrino ($\bar{\nu}_\mu \to \bar{\nu}_e$) appearance channels over the broadband energy spectrum $E_\nu \in [0.5, 8.0]\text{ GeV}$. Our analysis demonstrates that complex NSI phases imprint distinct, energy-dependent spectral dispersions onto the open-path geometric holonomies $\Phi_g^{(n)}$ and their $\mathcal{CP}$-conjugate counterparts. By clarifying that these geometric phases manifest through gauge-invariant interference terms $\Delta\Gamma_{nm}$ rather than asymptotic single-channel measurements, we construct the $\mathcal{CP}$-violating geometric phase differences $\Delta\Phi_g^{\mathrm{CP}}$. We show that the interplay between these geometric dispersions and the matter-potential sign reversal under $\mathcal{CP}$-conjugation forces the neutrino and antineutrino degeneracy bands to intersect at non-parallel angles in the two-dimensional $(\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}, \phi_{e\tau})$ parameter space. This non-trivial topological intersection isolates a unique physical solution, providing a robust, independent diagnostic capable of breaking persistent parameter degeneracies even under realistic calorimetric energy smearing.

Method

Pythia 8 Monte Carlo event generation coupled with a calibrated coalescence model and a semi-analytical two-zone Galactic propagation framework constrained by AMS-02 antiproton data.

Why it matters to you

Abstract

Cosmic-ray antideuterons and antihelium-3 nuclei are promising probes of dark matter (DM). Yu Cheng, Damiano F. G. Fiorillo, Chui-Fan Kong, Edoardo Vitagliano

hep-ph astro-ph.CO astro-ph.HEarXiv:2611.03813

Pre-supernova stellar evolution provides stronger constraints on electrophilic MeV bosons than the core-collapse explosion itself.

Novel idea

Utilizes the prolonged high-temperature carbon, oxygen, and silicon burning phases of massive stellar progenitors as continuous sources of feebly interacting particles that decay into positrons contributing to the Galactic 511-keV line.

Method

Evolutionary tracks of 9-$60\,M_{\odot}$ stars simulated using MESA to extract temperature-density profiles, combined with analytic thermal emission rates for dark photons and axion-like particles and positron propagation smearing.

Key results

  • Derived exclusion limits on dark photon mixing parameters down to $\varepsilon \simeq 10^{-14}$ for masses between 1 and 3 MeV.
  • Improved upon existing supernova bounds (such as from SN 1987A and SN 2023ixf) by up to two orders of magnitude.

Why it matters to you

Demonstrates a novel astrophysical constraint mechanism using pre-supernova stages that transcends traditional supernova cooling bounds.

Caveats

Assumes negligible backreaction of dark photon cooling on stellar structure and depends on uncertainties in Galactic positron propagation lengths.

Abstract

The years preceding a supernova (SN) can constrain new electrophilic bosons with MeV masses more than the explosion itself. Although cooler than SNe, the massive progenitors maintain consistently high temperatures much longer, injecting more particles whose decays to positrons contribute to the diffuse Galactic 511-keV emission. For dark photons, the increase is exacerbated by the lower plasma frequency compared to SNe, where MeV excitations are screened. We find preSNe bounds on dark photon couplings up to two orders of magnitude stronger than SNe for masses between 1 and 3~MeV, with similar improvements for other electrophilic species such as axion-like particles.

Semiparametric Estimation of Physics Parameters and Parton Distribution Functions

Huanbiao Zhu, Jaco ter Hoeve, Mikael Kuusela, Maria Ubiali, Larry Wasserman

hep-ph stat.AParXiv:2611.04267

A semiparametric estimation framework avoids full simultaneous PDF fits when extracting physics parameters like EFT Wilson coefficients.

Novel idea

Introduces an efficient influence function to compute an a posteriori bias correction, yielding a debiased one-step estimator for finite-dimensional parameters in the presence of infinite-dimensional PDF nuisance parameters.

Method

Constructs B-spline basis expansions for PDFs combined with linear deep inelastic scattering (DIS) forward maps and efficient score projections evaluated on BCDMS and HERA pseudo-data.

Key results

  • Demonstrated successful removal of estimation bias in extracting a dimension-6 SMEFT Wilson coefficient and the proton valence distribution $V$.
  • Achieved frequentist confidence intervals with correct nominal coverage without requiring computationally heavy joint PDF refits.

Why it matters to you

Provides a statistically rigorous and computationally efficient tool for precision electroweak and BSM parameter extraction where PDF uncertainties dominate.

Caveats

Demonstrated primarily on linear DIS toy models, leaving bilinear hadronic (proton-proton) processes for future work.

Abstract

The simultaneous determination of Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs) and fundamental physical parameters, e.g. the strong coupling constant $\alpha_s$, the mass of the top quark $m_t$, or Wilson coefficients of an Effective Field Theory operators, is essential for the high-precision physics programme of the Large Hadron Collider. Yet, simultaneous global fits face significant methodological and computational challenges, rooted in the fundamental tension between estimating a finite-dimensional parameter and an infinite-dimensional function.     Abstract

    Bit threads express holographic entropy as the maximal flux of a divergenceless vector field subject to a local capacity bound, but do not identify a physical carrier of that flux. In our previous work, we identified the projected string charge density with a bit-thread flow, reproduced the entropies of BTZ and ten-dimensional three-charge black holes, and showed that the maximal entropy flux is preserved under S- and T-dualities. However, the norm bound was imported from the bit-thread prescription, while the string-current configurations were prescribed along known bit-thread lines. In this paper, we give a stringy foundation for the local capacity and construct dynamical probe-string representatives of maximal flow. At genus zero and two-derivative order, the conical variation of the target-space action derived from the off-shell sphere fixes the string-frame entropy density. The gravitational replica construction and weighted flow-cut duality then yield the string-frame norm bound. We next find regular stationary F1 solutions in an NS-NS supported BTZ black hole whose spatial parts are logarithmic spirals. A uniform ensemble produces a conserved, constant-norm projected string charge density and realizes a maximal flow whose flux equals the BTZ entropy. We finally extend the construction to BTZ backgrounds with mixed NS-NS and RR flux. Varying the flux fraction changes the stationary F1 configurations at fixed metric and dilaton, while the corresponding conserved flows reproduce the same leading BTZ entropy. Thus, both the norm bound and its physical maximal-flow representative arise from string theory, with stationary strings providing physical channels for the entropic flux represented geometrically by bit threads.

    Hamiltonian of open string field theory for time-dependent solutionsVinícius Bernardes, Riccardo Poletti · hep-th
    Abstract

    Using the recently introduced expression for conserved charges of $L_\infty$ theories, we give an explicit form for the Hamiltonian of Witten's bosonic open string field theory. We evaluate it on the Erler-Maccaferri analytic solution, describing any D$p$-brane configuration associated to a BCFT. We use the expression to compute the energy of the rolling tachyon solution and find exact agreement with the boundary state result by Sen.

    Obstruction to local entropy laws on arbitrary black-hole cuts in five-dimensional Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet gravityCendikiawan Suryaatmadja, Robert B. Mann · hep-th
    Abstract

    We study local entropy laws on arbitrary cuts of event horizons in five-dimensional Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet gravity, treated as an exact theory. No covariant local entropy can both reproduce the stationary black-hole entropy and be nondecreasing between every ordered pair of cuts, provided its density is smooth on an invariant open set of off-shell metric and cut jets containing a flat planar cut and every cut used in the argument. No global bound on derivative order is assumed. The counterexamples to the second law are vacuum black holes perturbed by a gravitational wave, with a strongly hyperbolic formulation near the relevant horizon interval and its stationary future. Shear makes the intrinsic curvature of a cut depend on its bending. The stationary entropy fixes the coefficient of this curvature term, and local corrections cannot cancel its response to cut bending. The obstruction appears at second order in wave amplitude on a smooth horizon, without a merger or topology change.
