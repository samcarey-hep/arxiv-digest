10worth readingJUNO-TAO reactor antineutrinos

Reactor Antineutrino Detection with JUNO-TAO: Commissioning Performance and First Results

JUNO Collaboration

hep-exarXiv:2610.23331

JUNO-TAO reports its commissioning and first physics data, achieving an energy resolution of $2.8\%$ at $1\text{ MeV}$ and measuring an IBD candidate rate of $2900\pm 10\text{ events/day}$.

Novel idea

Presents the first operational and physics data from the Taishan Antineutrino Observatory (TAO), a satellite experiment of JUNO utilizing a 2.8-ton cryogenic ($-50^{\circ}\text{C}$) Gd-doped liquid scintillator detector with SiPM readout. It provides an unprecedented light yield ($\,\approx 4000\text{ PE/MeV}$) and energy resolution to measure the unoscillated reactor antineutrino spectrum with minimal model dependence, establishing a benchmark to eliminate flux-shape systematics for JUNO's mass ordering measurement.

Method

Data was collected with 4,024 SiPM tiles (8,048 readout channels) over 31.3 days of reactor-on and 29.4 days of reactor-off periods at a 43.2 m baseline. Reconstructed prompt IBD positron energy and vertices were calibrated using central $^{68}\text{Ge}$ and off-center $^{137}\text{Cs}$ sources alongside mono-energetic environmental backgrounds. A data-driven reactor-off subtraction was applied to isolate the IBD positron spectrum while accounting for energy non-linearity, Cherenkov contributions, and boundary leakage.

Key results

  • Measured a light yield of $\sim 4000\text{ PE/MeV}$ from hydrogen neutron captures in IBD and an energy resolution of $\sim 2.8\%$ for $^{68}\text{Ge}$ at the center and $3.7\%$ for $^{137}\text{Cs}$ at $R=193\text{ mm}$.
  • Demonstrated stable cryogenic operation at $-50^{\circ}\text{C}$ with temperature variations $<0.2^{\circ}\text{C}$ and retained $\sim 90\%$ of readout channels after quality cuts.
  • Measured an IBD candidate rate of $2900\pm 10\text{ events/day}$ (reactor-on) versus a cosmogenic/background rate of $419\pm 4\text{ events/day}$ (reactor-off).
  • Achieved vertex reconstruction bias within $\pm 30\text{ mm}$ and spatial resolution of $\sim 70\text{ mm}$ across the $R < 800\text{ mm}$ fiducial volume.

Why it matters to you

TAO's high-resolution reference spectrum is essential for eliminating nuclear summation and flux-shape model uncertainties in JUNO's mass ordering determination, and provides a clean experimental benchmark for testing reactor flux anomalies and sterile neutrino oscillations.

Caveats

About 10% of readout channels were inactive during this run, and energy-scale non-linearity parameters currently rely solely on gamma-ray calibration without beta-spectrum constraints from cosmogenic $^{12}\text{B}$.

Abstract

The Taishan Antineutrino Observatory (TAO) is a satellite experiment of JUNO, designed to measure the reactor antineutrino energy spectrum with high precision at a baseline of 43.2~m from a reactor core using a 2.8-ton spherical liquid-scintillator detector, 1.8~m in diameter, operated at -50 degree and instrumented with 4,024 silicon photomultiplier tiles. The TAO detector installation was completed in 2025, and physics data taking began on 10 February 2026. This paper reports the detector design, calibration strategy, and performance based on commissioning data and the first physics runs. Stable cryogenic operation has been achieved, and about 90\% of the readout channels are retained for physics analysis after channel-quality selections. The detector light yield is measured to be about 4000~photoelectrons/MeV after correction for dark noise and optical crosstalk, using neutron capture on hydrogen in inverse beta decay (IBD) events. The energy resolution is about 2.8\% for \(^{68}\)Ge at the detector center and about 3.7\% for \(^{137}\)Cs at \(R=193\)~mm. This energy-resolution performance is among the best reported for reactor-antineutrino detectors and enables TAO to measure the reactor antineutrino spectrum with high precision. Within the fiducial volume of \(R < 800\)~mm, the vertex reconstruction bias remains within \(\pm 30~\mathrm{mm}\), and the resolution is approximately \(70~\mathrm{mm}\) over most of the fiducial volume. The combined muon-tagging efficiency of the veto system is about 97\%. Using 31.3 days of reactor-on data and 29.4 days of reactor-off data, a significant excess of IBD candidates is observed in the reactor-on sample relative to the reactor-off sample, with candidate rates of 2900$\pm$10 events/day and 419$\pm$4 events/day, respectively. The results demonstrate that TAO has reached a high performance level in reactor antineutrino measurement.

10must-readNucleon-nucleon scattering

Two-nucleon scattering amplitudes at light quark masses from lattice QCD

Jorge Baeza-Ballesteros (BaSc), André Baião-Raposo (BaSc), Davide Laudicina (BaSc), Malcolm Lazarow (BaSc), Miguel Salg (BaSc), John Bulava (BaSc) + 12 more

hep-lat hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2610.23332 · cross-list

The BaSc Collaboration extracts nucleon-nucleon scattering amplitudes at near-physical pion masses down to $M_\pi \approx 200\text{ MeV}$ from lattice QCD, explicitly accounting for the one-pion exchange left-hand cut.

Novel idea

Pushes lattice QCD nucleon-nucleon ($NN$) scattering determinations to near-physical quark masses ($M_\pi \approx 200\text{ MeV}$ and $280\text{ MeV}$) using distillation. Crucially, it resolves the proximity of the one-pion exchange left-hand cut by employing two modern formalisms—the plane-wave quantization condition (PWQC) and the relativistic left-hand-cut (LHC) formalism—overcoming the breakdown of standard Lüscher quantization.

Method

The calculation uses two $N_f=2+1$ CLS ensembles with $O(a)$-improved Wilson fermions at $a \approx 0.064\text{ fm}$ (D251 with $M_\pi\approx 280\text{ MeV}$ and J250 with $M_\pi\approx 200\text{ MeV}$). Large Hermitian correlation matrices were constructed using LapH distillation perambulators and an extensive operator basis. The spectra were fitted with Bayesian multi-exponential models, and infinite-volume phase shifts were extracted using PWQC and LHC formalisms.

Key results

  • Extracted low-energy $NN$ scattering phase shifts, scattering lengths, effective ranges, and shape parameters for both the deuteron ($I=0$) and dineutron ($I=1$) channels.
  • Near-threshold virtual-state poles (sheet II) are favored by $>1\sigma$ in both channels at $M_\pi \approx 200\text{ and } 280\text{ MeV}$, with the deuteron pole smoothly approaching the threshold as $M_\pi$ decreases.
  • Demonstrated monotonic approach of inverse scattering lengths and binding momenta toward empirical physical-point values when combined with earlier $M_\pi \approx 710\text{ MeV}$ data.
  • Extracted the $NN$ mixing angle and $\beta$-wave in the $I=0$ sector directly through one-pion exchange dynamical coupling.

Why it matters to you

Provides ab initio QCD constraints on two-nucleon low-energy constants, crucial for refining chiral effective field theory potentials, two-body meson-exchange currents (2p2h), and nuclear responses implemented in neutrino event generators (Achilles, NuWro, GENIE).

Caveats

Simulations were performed at a single lattice spacing ($a \approx 0.064\text{ fm}$), leaving discretization systematics unquantified, and the extracted $g_{\pi NN}$ exhibits non-negligible model dependence.

Abstract

We present a lattice-QCD determination of nucleon-nucleon scattering amplitudes at pion masses of $M_\pi \approx 200$ MeV and $M_\pi \approx 280$ MeV at a single lattice spacing $a \approx 0.064$ fm on two CLS ensembles. The finite-volume energy spectra are extracted from large Hermitian correlation matrices constructed within the distillation framework using an extensive basis of two-nucleon interpolating operators. To reliably relate the finite-volume spectra to the infinite-volume scattering amplitudes, we employ two recently developed formalisms that explicitly account for the long-range interaction generated by one-pion exchange: the plane-wave quantization condition and the relativistic left-hand-cut formalism. We discuss the quark-mass dependence of the nucleon-nucleon scattering lengths, effective ranges, shape parameters and pole positions. At all pion masses considered, our analysis prefers virtual states in both isospin channels by more than $1\sigma$. Comparing also results from our previous calculation at $M_\pi \approx 710$ MeV, we observe that the inverse scattering lengths and binding momenta approach their physical-point phenomenological values monotonically as the pion mass is varied.

Neural Network Representation of Generalized Parton Distributions (NNGPD)

Simonetta Liuti, Jitao Xu, Ho Jang, Zaki Panjsheeri, Gia-Wei Chern, Yaohang Li + 7 more

hep-pharXiv:2610.23390

The authors introduce NNGPD, a physics-informed neural network framework that reconstructs Generalized Parton Distributions from integral observables like Compton form factors and Mellin moments without pointwise $x$-space training.

Novel idea

Formulates GPD reconstruction as an ill-posed inverse problem tackled via a physics-informed neural network that trains purely on integral constraints—Compton form factors (CFFs) and Mellin moments—along with endpoint conditions and charge-conjugation symmetries. It also introduces a Bayesian neural network (BNN) formulation to quantify reconstruction ambiguities originating from mathematically degenerate 'shadow GPDs'.

Method

A neural network parameterizes the GPDs $H_q^+(x,\xi,t)$ and $H_q^-(x,\xi,t)$, whose loss function directly evaluates numerical integrations corresponding to LO/leading-twist CFFs and lower Mellin moments constrained by elastic form factors and lattice QCD. Closure tests were executed on pseudodata generated from a phenomenological spectator model. Variational inference was implemented via a BNN with Kullback-Leibler divergence regularization to sample the posterior distribution of GPDs.

Key results

  • Demonstrated successful closure on spectator model pseudodata, reproducing both valence $H_{u_v}$ and sea $H_{\bar{u}}$ distributions across varying $x$, $\xi$, and $t$.
  • Showed that incorporating $n=1$ and higher Mellin moments effectively restricts the space of admissible solutions against CFF shadow ambiguities.
  • Formulated a direct-inference strategy exploring whether Mellin moments (e.g., for partonic angular momentum) can be reliably extracted from CFFs without resolving full $x$-dependence.

Why it matters to you

Co-authored by favorite author S. Pandey; provides advanced physics-informed ML methodology for nucleon structure and hadron matrix elements, bridging experimental scattering with lattice QCD moment constraints.

Caveats

The framework has currently only been validated on synthetic closure tests from a spectator model rather than real experimental DVCS cross-section data, and LO QCD approximations were assumed.

Abstract

Extracting generalized parton distributions (GPDs) from experimental observables, the convolution structures denoted as Compton form factors (CFFs), requires solving an intrinsic inverse problem. We presented results using NNGPD, a flexible regression method based on a physics-informed neural-network framework, for reconstructing GPDs using integral constraints -- including CFFs and Mellin moments -- and symmetry and endpoint constraints. The approach does not require pointwise training information on the underlying GPDs. Closure tests using pseudodata generated from a phenomenological model demonstrate the ability of the framework to reproduce the principal features of the underlying distributions across a range of kinematics. A Bayesian neural-network formulation is also introduced to address uncertainties associated with the reconstruction. Finally, we discussed a broader question in GPD phenomenology: whether the complete momentum-fraction dependence of GPDs is necessary to extract physical quantities such as partonic angular momentum. This motivates investigating approaches that infer Mellin moments directly from experimentally constrained CFFs, while quantifying the remaining ambiguities.

A REST-based Monte Carlo Simulation and Data Analysis Framework for the N$\nu$DEx Experiment

Siyuan Huang, Tao Li, Hao Qiu, Shaobo Wang

hep-exarXiv:2610.23112

A simulation and reconstruction framework based on the REST toolkit is developed for the N$\nu$DEx-100 high-pressure gaseous $^{82}\text{SeF}_6$ TPC searching for $0\nu\beta\beta$.

Novel idea

Presents the first complete end-to-end simulation framework for the N$\nu$DEx experiment, incorporating the negative-ion drift dynamics of electronegative $\text{SeF}_6$ gas and direct charge collection via Topmetal-S CMOS sensors without gas avalanche amplification. It exploits the dual-species drift velocity difference ($\text{SeF}_5^-$ and $\text{SeF}_6^-$) to extract $z$-drift coordinate information from waveform fitting.

Method

Primary $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay events ($Q_{\beta\beta} = 2.996\text{ MeV}$) were generated using Decay0 and transported in Geant4 within the N$\nu$DEx-100 geometry. REST event processes simulated charge generation ($W = 32\text{ eV}, F = 0.19$), negative-ion mobility and longitudinal/transverse diffusion, and Topmetal-S electronics response. A double-Gaussian convolved with an RC circuit response was fitted to reconstructed waveforms to determine drift distance and energy.

Key results

  • Optimized waveform fitting parameters with a $5\sigma_{\text{noise}}$ ($225\,e^-$) amplitude threshold and a $50\text{ Hz}$ low-pass filter.
  • Reconstructed 3D track topologies from 8,192 simulated hexagonal readout channels across 32 modules.
  • Achieved an energy resolution of $0.58\%$ FWHM ($116/100\ \chi^2/\text{ndf}$) at $Q_{\beta\beta}$, satisfying the experiment's target design requirement ($<1\%$ FWHM).

Why it matters to you

Demonstrates technical modeling of negative-ion transport and pixelated direct charge readout in gaseous TPCs, relevant to rare-event and low-energy neutrino instrumentation.

Caveats

The readout plane uses an idealized hexagonal cell approximation rather than full sensor-level electric field focusing, and non-uniform electric field distortions or full radiogenic backgrounds are not yet implemented.

Abstract

The N$\nu$DEx experiment is designed to search for neutrinoless double-beta decay ($0\nu\beta\beta$) of $\mathrm{^{82}Se}$ and is planned to be deployed at the China Jinping Underground Laboratory (CJPL). It employs a high-pressure time projection chamber (TPC) filled with $\mathrm{^{82}SeF_6}$ gas and a Topmetal pixelated charge readout system. The high $Q_{\beta\beta}$ value of $\mathrm{^{82}Se}$, together with the track reconstruction capability of a gaseous TPC, provides strong potential for background suppression. In this work, we develop a full simulation and reconstruction framework for the N$\nu$DEx experiment based on the REST (Rare Event Searches Toolkit) software framework. To meet the detector-specific requirements of N$\nu$DEx, customized simulation processes are implemented to describe the detector geometry, electron drift and diffusion, the Topmetal readout plane, signal formation, and electronics response. These developments enable a complete software description of the detector response from energy deposition to digitized waveforms. A waveform-fitting-based reconstruction method is further applied to the simulated signals to extract the deposited charge and reconstruct the event energy. For simulated signal events, the reconstructed energy resolution reaches a full width at half maximum (FWHM) of less than 1\%, satisfying the design requirement of the experiment. The resulting simulation and reconstruction chain provides a basis for future studies of detector response, background rejection, event classification, and optimization of the N$\nu$DEx detector design.

Neutrino Anomalies and Tensions: Experimental Status and Theoretical Interpretations

Doris Barčot, Raoul Serao, Antonio Capolupo

hep-pharXiv:2610.23474

This review synthesizes experimental neutrino anomalies and parameter tensions, systematically contrasting interpretations including $3+1$ steriles, non-standard interactions, decoherence, and decay across complementary channels.

Novel idea

Provides a structured multi-observable assessment highlighting why single BSM mechanisms struggle to simultaneously explain short-baseline excesses (LSND, MiniBooNE), rate deficits (Gallium/BEST, reactor anomaly), and long-baseline differences (T2K vs NOvA). It emphasizes the critical role of correlated appearance-versus-disappearance limits, nuclear cross-section and flux modeling, and event topology discrimination.

Method

Surveys global experimental data across SBL accelerators, reactors, radioactive sources, atmospheric neutrinos (IceCube), and LBL accelerators. Theoretical frameworks—including $3+1$ unitary mixing, vector CC/NC non-standard interactions, open-system Lindblad master equations, neutrino decay, and dark-sector upscattering—are formulated and mapped against experimental signatures ($L/E_\nu$ scaling, energy dependence, NC/CC ratios).

Key results

  • Reiterates the severe tension in $3+1$ sterile fits between required appearance ($\\sin^2 2\theta_{\mu e} = 4|U_{e4}|^2|U_{\mu 4}|^2$) and null $\nu_\mu$ disappearance results from MINOS+, IceCube, and recent ICARUS data.
  • Highlights MicroBooNE's exclusion of single-sterile explanations for LSND at $99\%$ CL and MiniBooNE at $95\%$ CL, pointing toward dark-sector upscattering ($e^+e^-$/single photon) or interaction mismodelling.
  • Notes that Daya Bay flux measurements and updated beta-decay summation calculations resolve much of the reactor rate deficit, isolating the 5 MeV bump as a nuclear flux artifact.
  • Emphasizes that the recent joint T2K–NOvA Bayesian analysis yields an inverted-to-normal ordering Bayes factor of 1.3, resolving earlier tensions within standard 3-flavor oscillations.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts SBN (SBND, ICARUS, MicroBooNE) physics interpretation, BSM portal searches at accelerator experiments, and interaction-modelling systematics in long-baseline oscillation analyses (T2K, NOvA, DUNE).

Caveats

As a review article, it contains no new global fits or original simulations, and primarily catalogs published tensions and theoretical formulations.

Abstract

Neutrino oscillations successfully describe flavor conversion across a wide range of experiments, yet the origin of several excesses and deficits remains unresolved. This review connects these observations and long-baseline parameter tensions to their proposed physical explanations, clarifying what remains open and how it can be tested. We examine reactor and gallium measurements, accelerator appearance and disappearance searches, and atmospheric constraints, focusing on sterile neutrinos, non-standard interactions, and decoherence in open-system dynamics. For each mechanism, we assess what it modifies, which observations it can address, and which theoretical assumptions and independent constraints limit its viability. Recent results have restricted several interpretations and weakened some earlier tensions; no single extension provides an uncontested account of all observations. We identify the flavor, baseline, energy, and event-topology signatures needed to distinguish competing explanations and assess whether the remaining anomalies require new physics or reflect unresolved flux, interaction, and detector uncertainties.

9skimLevinson's theorem for form factors

A Levinson's theorem for particle form factors

Francesco Rosini, Simone Pacetti

hep-ph hep-th math-ph math.MParXiv:2604.09207 · cross-list

The authors prove a form-factor version of Levinson's theorem, demonstrating that the asymptotic timelike phase is uniquely fixed by the number of zeros and the pQCD power-law falloff.

Novel idea

Levinson's theorem traditionally relates asymptotic scattering phase shifts to the number of bound states. The authors adapt this concept to complex analytic particle form factors with a branch cut along the timelike axis. They prove that the net phase change from threshold to infinity is determined solely by the number of isolated zeros and the integer exponent of the asymptotic space-like power-law falloff predicted by pQCD counting rules.

Method

The derivation employs Cauchy's argument principle on a contour in the cut complex $s$-plane avoiding the branch cut $[s_{\mathrm{th}}, \infty)$ and isolated singularities. Schwarz reflection and the Phragmén-Lindelöf theorem are utilized to relate space-like asymptotic behavior to time-like phase asymptotes. The relation is verified using logarithmic dispersion relations with constituent-quark counting rule power-laws $s^{-n}$.

Key results

  • Derived the form factor Levinson relation: $\delta(\infty) - \delta(s_{\mathrm{th}}) = (n - M)\pi$, where $n$ is the asymptotic power-law index ($G(s) \sim s^{-n}$) and $M$ is the number of zeros.
  • Showed that dressed gluon propagators generating $s^{-n}$ behavior in pQCD can be effectively interpreted as $n$ timelike poles situated above threshold.
  • Established that only a zero-free form factor approaching a non-zero constant asymptotically satisfies identical phases at threshold and infinity.

Why it matters to you

While purely formal, this identity provides a rigorous analytic constraint on dispersion relations and phase parametrizations used in timelike-to-spacelike analytic continuations. It is conceptually relevant when building dispersive representations of nucleon electromagnetic or axial form factors.

Caveats

The proof assumes standard analyticity and asymptotic power-law decay, relying on an effective interpretation where gluon exchanges behave as poles outside the physical contour. Experimental validation of timelike phases at high $s$ remains challenging.

Abstract

We present and demonstrate a version of Levinson's theorem especially dedicated to the asymptotic behavior of form factor phases. Indeed, as required by analyticity, form factors are multi-valued complex functions of a square four-momentum defined in the complex plane with a cut along the positive real axis. Their phases evaluated on the upper edge of this cut, i.e., on the time-like region, tend asymptotically to integer multiples of $\pi$ radians. The Levinson's theorem establishes a univocal relation between such multiples and properties of form factors related to the dynamics of the electromagnetic interaction of the corresponding hadrons.

8worth readingLattice QCD wavelets

Gauge-Covariant Wavelets for Quark-Propagator Sparsening and Distillation in Lattice QCD

Debsubhra Chakraborty, Nilmani Mathur, Mrinmoy Basak

hep-lat hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2610.23207 · cross-list

A gauge-covariant wavelet framework combines fast wavelet transform sparsening and resolvent-filtered Laplacian distillation to dramatically accelerate lattice-QCD correlation function computations and contractions without full Laplacian eigensolves.

Novel idea

The paper introduces multiresolution analysis directly into lattice-QCD hadronic operator evaluation via two complementary methods. First, a gauge-covariant fast wavelet transform (FWT) with biorthogonal filters sparsens propagators while strongly mitigating excited-state aliasing compared to naive decimation. Second, 'wavelet distillation' applies a resolvent filter of the gauge-covariant spatial Laplacian to coarse-grid sources, bypassing costly Laplacian eigensolves and enabling Hansen-Hurwitz importance-sampled Wick contractions.

Method

Calculations are benchmarked on $N_f=2+1+1$ MILC (HISQ sea, Wilson-clover valence, $m_\pi \approx 689$ MeV) and $N_f=3$ CLS ($a \approx 0.098$ fm, $m_\pi \approx 429$ MeV) ensembles. Operators for single hadrons, $I=2$ $\pi\pi$, the deuteron channel, and boosted nucleons are evaluated using GEVP. Stochastic wavelet distillation uses interlace dilution and Hansen-Hurwitz sink sampling, with random grid shifts to remove coarse-grid momentum aliasing.

Key results

  • Wavelet distillation basis construction is approximately $5\times$ computationally cheaper than standard Laplacian eigensolves on the tested $24^3$ lattice.
  • Hansen-Hurwitz importance sampling accelerates nucleon and deuteron Wick contractions by factors of 3.8 and 3.0, respectively, with only a $\sim 5\%$ increase in fitted energy statistical uncertainty.
  • FWT low-pass filtering heavily suppresses high-momentum aliased states compared to direct decimation across coarsening levels $s=1, 2, 3$.
  • Extracted energy levels for single hadrons, $I=2$ $\pi\pi$, the deuteron, and boosted nucleons match standard distillation within statistical errors.

Why it matters to you

Lattice QCD calculations of nucleon axial form factors and multi-nucleon electroweak matrix elements are critical inputs for neutrino-nucleus interaction modelling in generators like Achilles. This framework drastically lowers the computational barrier for boosted nucleons and two-nucleon systems, paving the way for higher-precision lattice inputs to neutrino physics.

Caveats

Coarse-grid spatial anchors induce momentum aliasing that requires stochastic random offsets across configurations to cancel in expectation. The method must still be validated on larger volumes ($L \gtrsim 48$) and at physical pion masses.

Abstract

We develop a novel gauge-covariant wavelet framework for computing hadronic correlation functions in lattice QCD. Two complementary approaches connect spatial localization and resolution-scale separation to propagator sparsening and distillation. A gauge-covariant fast wavelet transform, based on compactly supported biorthogonal filters, coarse-grains quark propagators and reduces early-time excited-state contamination relative to direct decimation at equal spatial reduction. A second construction applies a resolvent filter of the spatial covariant Laplacian to localized sources, generating a distillation subspace without computing Laplacian eigenpairs. We detail its gauge covariance, smearing profiles, and implementation in exact and stochastic distillation. Spatial localization enables unbiased Hansen-Hurwitz sampling of hadronic contractions, reducing their computational cost substantially. We also examine momentum aliasing induced by fixed coarse grids and the use of random grid shifts to remove unwanted contributions in expectation. Numerical benchmarks of the proposed methods are performed with single hadrons, the I = 2 two-pion system, the deuteron channel, and boosted nucleons. The extracted energy levels agree very well with full or conventional distillation calculations within statistical uncertainties. We quantify the costs of on-demand basis construction and contractions, demonstrate contraction speedups at comparable statistical precision. These studies establish a practical foundation of wavelet-based propagator compression and operator construction for multi-operator calculations of hadronic and nuclear systems, particularly at larger lattice volumes.

Leading-twist proton parton distribution amplitude from scalar and axial-vector diquark correlations

B. Almeida-Zamora, L. Albino, C. Mezrag, J. Segovia

hep-ph hep-ex hep-lat nucl-exarXiv:2610.22994

The leading-twist proton parton distribution amplitude is calculated from Poincaré-covariant quark-diquark Faddeev amplitudes, revealing how axial-vector diquarks soften the strong longitudinal momentum asymmetry produced by scalar diquarks.

Novel idea

The work provides the first explicit decomposition of the leading-twist proton PDA into its scalar and axial-vector diquark components using an algebraic perturbation-theory integral representation (PTIR) of the Faddeev wave function. It derives closed-form expressions for both chiral-even and chiral-odd axial-vector diquark PDAs and assesses how each correlation channel shapes the proton light-front momentum distribution.

Method

The three-quark bound state is formulated via Poincaré-covariant Faddeev equations projected onto light-front coordinates. Spectral representations inspired by Nakanishi representations model the dressed-quark propagators and diquark Bethe-Salpeter amplitudes. The resulting proton PDA at $\mu_0 = 0.51$ GeV is evolved to $\mu = 2.0$ GeV at leading order by projecting onto an orthogonal Jacobi-Gegenbauer polynomial eigenbasis of the Brodsky-Lepage kernel.

Key results

  • Scalar diquarks alone produce a highly asymmetric PDA with peak momentum at $(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (0.55, 0.20, 0.25)$ and Mellin moments $\langle x_1 \rangle = 0.448$, $\langle x_2 \rangle = 0.276$, $\langle x_3 \rangle = 0.276$.
  • Including axial-vector diquarks (65% scalar, 35% axial-vector) redistributes momentum, shifting low-energy Mellin moments to $\langle x_1 \rangle = 0.392$, $\langle x_2 \rangle = 0.274$, $\langle x_3 \rangle = 0.334$.
  • LO QCD evolution to $\mu = 2$ GeV further symmetrizes the distribution toward the conformal limit, yielding evolved moments $\langle x_1 \rangle = 0.366$, $\langle x_2 \rangle = 0.302$, $\langle x_3 \rangle = 0.332$ while preserving a distinct non-perturbative imprint.

Why it matters to you

Accurate proton PDAs underpin exclusive hard scattering predictions and high-$Q^2$ nucleon electromagnetic and transition form factors. Understanding diquark-driven momentum distributions provides physical insight into the nucleon wave function, informing form factor models used across intermediate- and high-energy neutrino-nucleon interactions.

Caveats

The Faddeev amplitudes rely on an algebraic PTIR model rather than direct numerical solutions of the coupled DSE-Faddeev equations. Omitted higher partial waves and orbital angular momentum may further alter the momentum sharing.

Abstract

We compute the leading-twist proton parton distribution amplitude (PDA) using algebraic representations of the nucleon Faddeev wave function inspired by perturbation-theory integral representations. The PDA is obtained from a light-front projection of the covariant quark--diquark Faddeev amplitude and analysed in terms of its scalar- and axial-vector-diquark components. We derive analytic expressions for the corresponding diquark distribution amplitudes and their contributions to the proton PDA. The resulting distribution is broad and concave, with its maximum $(x_1,x_2,x_3)\approx(0.55,0.20,0.25)$ displaced from the symmetric configuration $(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, which corresponds to the maximum of the perturbative-QCD asymptotic form. Scalar-diquark correlations generate a strong asymmetry in the longitudinal momentum distribution, whereas axial-vector correlations redistribute momentum among the valence quarks and significantly reduce this asymmetry. We calculate low-order Mellin moments and obtain a compact parametrisation of the proton PDA at the hadronic scale, $\mu_0=0.51\,\mathrm{GeV}$. We then perform the leading-order QCD evolution of its normalized shape to $\mu=2\,\mathrm{GeV}$, finding that evolution reduces the momentum asymmetry while preserving a clear imprint of the non-perturbative quark--diquark correlations. The results provide insight into the role of such correlations in shaping the proton light-front structure and furnish non-perturbative input for studies of hard exclusive baryonic processes.

Andreev Beta Conversion and Bulk Viscosity in Superfluid Neutron-Star Matter

Tingyu Zhang, Honoka Hoshino, Hiroyuki Tajima, Motoi Tachibana, Mariusz Sadzikowski

nucl-th astro-ph.HE astro-ph.SRarXiv:2610.23581

In superfluid neutron-star matter, coherent Andreev beta conversion mediated by the neutron condensate yields an unsuppressed $T^8$ weak conductance that dominates bulk viscosity over exponentially suppressed quasiparticle Urca at low temperatures.

Novel idea

The authors identify an Andreev-reflection analogue in nuclear beta equilibration: a normal proton and electron convert into a neutron quasiparticle while transferring a Cooper pair to or from the neutron condensate. Unlike standard direct Urca, which suffers exponential gap suppression $\sim e^{-\Delta_n/T}$, this coherent fourth-order channel is mediated by anomalous neutron Green's functions and remains algebraically active at low temperatures.

Method

The process is formulated within the Schwinger-Keldysh nonequilibrium field theory formalism using an effective tunneling Hamiltonian where leptons are integrated into the weak vertex. The net beta conversion rate and corresponding beta conductance $\lambda_A$ are calculated from fourth-order Keldysh perturbation theory. Thermodynamic susceptibilities from an equation of state with realistic symmetry energy are then combined with $\lambda = \lambda_{\mathrm{qp}} + \lambda_A$ to compute dynamic bulk viscosity $\zeta(\omega)$.

Key results

  • The Andreev beta conductance scales asymptotically as $\lambda_A \propto \Delta_n^2 T^8 / |\xi_*|^3$, in contrast to the exponentially suppressed quasiparticle conductance $\lambda_{\mathrm{qp}} \propto T^3 e^{-E_*/T}$.
  • At temperatures $T \sim 0.1$ MeV, the Andreev channel surpasses ordinary quasiparticle conversion, dominating beta equilibration.
  • The resulting low-temperature bulk viscosity scales as $\operatorname{Re}\zeta(\omega) \propto T^8$ rather than freezing out exponentially, directly modifying the viscous damping of neutron-star pulsations and mergers.

Why it matters to you

This mechanism alters the dissipation and weak-interaction equilibration rates in cold, dense neutron star matter, directly impacting post-merger gravitational wave damping and neutrino emissivity/cooling.
