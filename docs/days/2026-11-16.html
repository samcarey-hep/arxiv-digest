Resonant Trapping of Neutrinophilic Dark Matter in Supernovae

Christopher V. Cappiello, P. S. Bhupal Dev, Amol V. Patwardhan

hep-ph astro-ph.HEarXiv:2611.17604

Resonant $s$-channel scattering between neutrinophilic dark matter and supernova neutrinos efficiently traps sub-GeV dark matter within the protoneutron star, opening an unconstrained parameter window in conventional supernova cooling limits.

Novel idea

Prior supernova cooling constraints on dark matter (DM) coupled to neutrinos focused on $s$-channel annihilation production and $t$-channel scattering. This work investigates a chiral/lepton-number carrying interaction $\bar{\chi}\nu\phi$ where DM production from neutrino annihilation proceeds via $t$-channel exchange, but DM-neutrino scattering proceeds via an $s$-channel resonance with scalar mediator $\phi$. The resulting resonant scattering cross section creates a strong, energy-dependent trapping mechanism that prevents DM escape.

Method

The authors compute neutrino-annihilation DM production rates and subsequent DM mean free paths inside a protoneutron star using 1D core-collapse supernova simulation profiles (a $20\,M_\odot$ progenitor at 1 s post-bounce). DM-neutrino scattering incorporates Pauli blocking due to neutrino degeneracy in the core. Energy loss is integrated over the neutrinosphere with an escape condition, and bounds are derived using the Raffelt SN1987A limit ($L_\chi \le 5\times 10^{52}\,\text{erg/s}$).

Key results

  • For $m_\phi / m_\chi = 3$, an unconstrained window opens for mediator masses between $\sim 5\,\text{MeV}$ and $100\,\text{MeV}$, where resonant scattering traps DM inside the core and completely eliminates the cooling exclusion.
  • Supernova cooling excludes small mediator masses ($m_\phi \lesssim 5\,\text{MeV}$) and an intermediate high-mass window ($100\,\text{MeV} \lesssim m_\phi \lesssim 300\,\text{MeV}$), above which thermal phase-space suppression shuts off production.
  • In the non-resonant heavy mediator limit ($m_\phi \sim 10\,\text{GeV}$), the standard effective-operator cooling constraint is recovered.

Why it matters to you

Provides a clear mechanism where light BSM particles coupled to neutrinos evade standard SN1987A bounds. This directly informs the viable parameter space for neutrino-portal dark sectors and light mediators probed at low-energy neutrino and stopped-pion facilities.

Caveats

Assumes any DM that scatters once is trapped and carries away zero net energy (a conservative bound, but neglected energy transport could still deposit heat differentially), and considers flavor-universal couplings without sterile neutrino-antineutrino conversion effects.

Abstract

Supernova cooling provides some of the strongest constraints on light particles beyond the Standard Model, including sub-GeV dark matter (DM). While sufficiently large couplings to the Standard Model sector can trap these particles inside the supernova or cause them to decay before escaping, there typically exists an intermediate range of couplings in which their efficient production and subsequent escape lead to excessive energy loss, resulting in stringent constraints on new light particles. In this work, we demonstrate for the first time that, for certain types of DM-neutrino interactions, resonantly enhanced $s$-channel scattering with neutrinos can efficiently trap DM inside the supernova and can fundamentally alter the conventional supernova cooling constraints. In particular, we show that for mediator masses comparable to the supernova core temperature, this resonant scattering can prevent a substantial fraction of the produced DM from freely escaping, thereby significantly weakening, or even evading, the usual supernova cooling constraints on such models.

$^8$B Solar Neutrino Measurements of Solar Core Size and Temperature from 5805 Days of Super-Kamiokande

Kamiokande Collaboration, Y. Jiang, K. Abe, Y. Asaoka, M. Harada, Y. Hayato + 246 more

hep-ex astro-ph.HE astro-ph.SRarXiv:2611.17651

Super-Kamiokande uses 5805 days of $^8\text{B}$ solar neutrino data and MSW adiabatic conversion to constrain the solar core radius to $< 0.499\,R_\odot$ and determine the central temperature.

Novel idea

Rather than testing neutrino oscillation parameters assuming a fixed Standard Solar Model (SSM), this analysis inverts the problem: it exploits the radial dependence of MSW matter effects and the steep temperature dependence of $^8\text{B}$ production to directly measure solar core parameters (core size, electron density profile, and central temperature) using neutrino observations alone.

Method

The analysis uses recoil electron kinetic energy spectra and day/night variations from 5805 days across SK-I through SK-IV. Solar electron density profiles are parameterized under high-metallicity (B16+GS98) and protosolar accretion (K2-A09-MZvar-12) models. Fits are performed on SK data alone and in combination with SNO polynomial survival probability parameters.

Key results

  • Sets a 90% C.L. upper bound on the solar core size of $0.499\,R_\odot$ for B16+GS98 and $0.381\,R_\odot$ for K2-A09-MZvar-12 using SK data alone.
  • Combined SK + SNO fits tighten the core size limit to $0.268\,R_\odot$ (B16+GS98) and $0.215\,R_\odot$ (K2-A09-MZvar-12).
  • Measures the solar central temperature as $T_{\text{C}} = (15.599 \pm 0.0016) \times 10^6\,\text{K}$ and temperature at the $^8\text{B}$ production peak as $T_{\text{npz}} = (15.492 \pm 0.0017) \times 10^6\,\text{K}$.

Why it matters to you

Provides an experimental milestone for solar neutrino MSW physics and serves as a benchmark for matter-effect modeling and low-energy solar neutrino detection in water Cherenkov experiments.

Caveats

The extracted core size and temperature remain dependent on the adopted baseline composition and opacity models (high vs. low metallicity SSMs).

Abstract

The detection of solar neutrino provides direct evidence for on-going fusion in the core of the Sun and serve as a probe for that solar core. Production rate of $^8$B is sensitive to the solar core temperature. Adiabatic flavor conversion of $^8$B solar neutrinos depends on the local solar electron density. Combined with the roughly exponential decrease in solar electron density with increasing distance from the center of the solar sphere, the solar core size, density, and temperature are inferred. This article presents an outer bound on solar core size and measurements of the density and temperature in the solar core based on Super-Kamiokande phase I-IV solar neutrino data. An outer bound is set for the solar core at $90\%$ confidence level as $0.499R_\odot$ for the high metallicity B16+GS98 Standard Solar Model and $0.381R_\odot$ for K2-A09-MZvar-12 which includes protosolar accretion. A combined fit with the SNO polynomial coefficients improves the limit to $0.268R_\odot$ for B16+GS98 and $0.215R_\odot$ for K2-A09-MZvar-12. Results for other metallicity cases are also reported. In the high metallicity scenario assuming nominal solar core density and solar core size, temperature at the solar center is reported at $T_\text{C}=(15.599\pm0.0016)\times10^6$ K and $T_{\text{npz}}=(15.492\pm0.0017)\times10^6$ K at the peak radius of the $^8$B production profile.

Self-Consistent Subtracted Second Quasiparticle Random-Phase Approximation for Dipole Responses in Neutron-Rich Oxygen Isotopes

M. J. Yang, X. Lian, C. L. Bai, C. L. Zhang, H. Sagawa, H. Q. Zhang

nucl-tharXiv:2611.17721

A self-consistent subtracted second QRPA (SSQRPA) incorporating 2qp and 4qp configurations simultaneously resolves the long-standing discrepancy in isovector and isoscalar dipole strength across wide energy scales in neutron-rich oxygen isotopes.

Novel idea

Establishes the first self-consistent second quasiparticle RPA (SSQRPA) for open-shell nuclei that unifies pairing (HF+BCS), full forward/backward QRPA amplitudes, explicit four-quasiparticle (4qp) excitations in a large model space, and an operator subtraction scheme to remove functional double-counting and eliminate dynamical instabilities.

Method

Calculations use the SkM* Skyrme energy density functional in the particle-hole channel with a volume pairing force in a harmonic-oscillator basis ($N_{\max} = 12$). The 4qp configuration space includes $2p\text{-}2h$ type quasiparticle states controlled via an occupation probability cutoff $V_{\text{cut}}$. Subtracted QRPA equations are solved for the isovector (IV) and isoscalar (IS) compressional dipole response in $^{18,20,22}\text{O}$.

Key results

  • In $^{18}\text{O}$ and $^{20}\text{O}$, coupling to 4qp configurations shifts the dominant IV GDR centroid downward by $\sim 4\,\text{MeV}$, directly matching the experimentally observed medium-energy strength at $10\text{--}18\,\text{MeV}$.
  • Reduces the RMS energy deviation of low-lying $1^-$ states below the neutron separation energy from $5.31\,\text{MeV}$ (QRPA) to $0.35\,\text{MeV}$ (SSQRPA) in $^{18}\text{O}$, and from $4.07\,\text{MeV}$ to $0.74\,\text{MeV}$ in $^{20}\text{O}$.
  • Demonstrates that high-energy GDR states ($22\text{--}25\,\text{MeV}$) contain substantial IS compressional character, consistent with electromagnetic dissociation data.

Why it matters to you

Oxygen isotopes ($^{16}\text{O}$, $^{18}\text{O}$) are key nuclear targets in water Cherenkov detectors (Super-K, Hyper-K). The microscopic incorporation of 4qp correlations provides the exact theoretical framework needed to model multi-nucleon de-excitation, spreading widths, and dipole response functions relevant to neutral-current and low-energy neutrino-nucleus cross sections.

Caveats

The current 4qp matrix implementation omits the particle-particle residual interaction channel, and low-lying transition strengths remain smaller than experiment due to the omission of continuum coupling.

Abstract

Describing the nuclear dipole response in open-shell nuclei across wide energy scales, from low-lying states below the neutron separation energy to giant resonances near 30MeV, within a single microscopic framework is a long-standing open problem. Here we establish, for the first time, a self-consistent subtracted second quasiparticle random-phase approximation (SSQRPA) that simultaneously incorporates pairing correlations (HF+BCS), explicit two-quasiparticle (2qp) and four-quasiparticle (4qp) configurations in a large model space, the full forward- and backward-going QRPA structure, and a subtraction procedure to eliminate double counting and ensure stability. No existing framework unifies all of these ingredients in the quasiparticle basis. We apply SSQRPA to the dipole excitations of neutron-rich oxygen isotopes $^{18,20,22}$O, where the standard QRPA fails to simultaneously account for high-energy giant dipole resonance (GDR) states near 22--25MeV, medium-energy GDR states in the 10--18MeV region, and isolated low-lying $1^-$ states below the neutron separation energy. In SSQRPA, the dominant isovector (IV) GDR strength shifts downward by approximately 4MeV into the experimentally observed medium-energy region, while the compressional isoscalar (IS) GDR strength remains concentrated at higher energies, in qualitative agreement with the observed electromagnetic dissociation cross sections. For the low-lying states, the root-mean-square deviation from experiment is reduced from 5.31MeV (QRPA) to 0.35MeV (SSQRPA) in $^{18}$O, and from 4.07MeV to 0.74MeV in $^{20}$O, demonstrating that 4qp correlations are essential for a simultaneous description of dipole excitations over a wide range of excitation energies. The SSQRPA provides a powerful and general framework for future microscopic studies of collective excitations in open-shell nuclei across the nuclear mass table.

Probing Dirac Heavy Neutral Leptons through EFT-Induced Meson Decays

Sai Gade, Sanjoy Mandal, Manimala Mitra, Sushant K. Raut

hep-pharXiv:2611.18060

Dimension-six $\nu$SMEFT four-fermion operators mediate heavy meson decays into pairs of Dirac heavy neutral leptons, extending displaced-vertex sensitivities at SHiP and FPF to active-sterile mixings well below conventional limits.

Novel idea

Standard heavy neutral lepton (HNL) searches link production and decay through the same active-sterile mixing $|V_{\alpha N}|^2$, causing event yields to plummet at small mixing. By utilizing lepton-number-conserving dimension-six four-fermion operators $(\bar{q}q)(\bar{N}N)$, HNL pair production in $c \to u$ and $b \to d, s$ meson decays is governed by $c_{\mathcal{O}}/\Lambda^2$, while decay remains governed by $|V_{eN}|^2$, effectively decoupling production from lifetime.

Method

The authors compute two-body ($P \to N\bar{N}$) and three-body ($P \to P'/V + N\bar{N}$) semileptonic decays for $D$ and $B$ mesons across $M_N$. Production kinematics at HL-LHC and FCC-hh are modeled with Pythia 8 rescaled to FONLL cross sections, while SPS fixed-target charm yields are used for SHiP. Geometrical acceptances, boosts, and decay probabilities are simulated for FASER2, FACET, SHiP, and two FPF@FCC configurations (FCC-LLP1 and FCC-LLP2).

Key results

  • For $\Lambda = 10\,\text{TeV}$, pair-production displaced-vertex searches probe $|V_{eN}|^2$ down to $10^{-10}\text{--}10^{-12}$ at SHiP ($c \to u$) and $10^{-12}\text{--}10^{-14}$ at FCC-LLP2 ($b \to d, s$), orders of magnitude below existing laboratory limits.
  • Three-body decays dominate the reach at lower $M_N$, while two-body leptonic modes dictate sensitivity near the kinematic thresholds ($M_N \to m_P/2$).
  • At fixed mixing $|V_{eN}|^2 = 10^{-10}$, SHiP tests cutoff scales $\Lambda \sim 100\,\text{TeV}$ for charm transitions, while FCC-LLP2 probes $\Lambda \sim 100\text{--}200\,\text{TeV}$ for bottom transitions, significantly surpassing bounds from $B \to K^{(*)}\nu\bar{\nu}$ and invisible meson decays.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts phenomenological studies of forward physics facilities (FPF, FASER2, SHiP) and $\nu$SMEFT phenomenology. It provides an efficient mechanism for generating macroscopic HNL lifetimes while retaining sizable production yields at beam-dump and collider neutrino facilities.

Caveats

Assumes zero background and 100% reconstruction efficiency for visible decay modes, and limits active mixing exclusively to the electron-neutrino sector.

Abstract

We investigate dimension-six operators involving pairs of Dirac-type heavy neutral leptons (HNLs) at beam-dump and far-forward experiments. In the $\nu$SMEFT framework, lepton-number-conserving four-fermion operators couple HNL pairs to quarks and induce two and three-body charm and bottom meson decays. We study the $c\to u$, $b\to d$, and $b\to s$ transitions at FASER2, FACET, SHiP, and two FPF@FCC configurations. HNL production depends on the cut-off scale $\Lambda$, while the lifetime depends on the active-sterile mixing $|V_{eN}|^2$. Sizable production can therefore coexist with the small mixing needed for HNLs to reach distant detectors. We project sensitivities in the $(M_N,|V_{eN}|^2)$ and $(M_N,\Lambda)$ planes, accounting for two and three-body decays and constraints from invisible and semivisible meson decays. SHiP is particularly sensitive to $c\to u$, while the larger FCC-LLP2 configuration probes $b\to d, s$. We find that EFT-induced pair production extends long-lived HNL searches to very small mixing and complements other probes of the $\nu$SMEFT heavy-neutrino sector.

Emerging understanding of the rare kaon decay $K^+\to\pi^+\ell^+\ell^-$ from lattice QCD

Zhao-Long Zhang, Xu Feng, Lu-Chang Jin, Bo-Lin Li, Chuan Liu, Christopher T. Sachrajda

hep-lat hep-pharXiv:2611.17800 · cross-list

A domain-wall lattice QCD calculation near physical pion mass provides the first first-principles evidence that the vector form factor $V(0)$ in $K^+ \to \pi^+ \ell^+ \ell^-$ is negative.

Novel idea

Experimental decay spectra for $K^+ \to \pi^+ \ell^+ \ell^-$ measure only $|V(z)|^2$, leaving a fundamental sign ambiguity in the low-energy constants $(a_+, b_+)$. This work establishes that local vector currents with a subtracted Fourier kernel and GIM cancellation share the identical UV-finite continuum limit as conserved lattice currents, enabling a tractable, noise-reduced lattice calculation of the nonlocal four-point function at near-physical pion mass.

Method

Simulations use two RBC/UKQCD domain-wall fermion ensembles with near-physical pion mass ($M_\pi \approx 143\,\text{MeV}$, $a^{-1} = 1.023\,\text{GeV}$ and $1.378\,\text{GeV}$). Exponentially growing single-pion intermediate states are removed via four cross-checked subtraction schemes. Quark-loop noise is suppressed using the one-end trick (even-split method). Form factors are evaluated at six $q^2$ values spanning spacelike and timelike regions, followed by a logarithmic charm-quark mass extrapolation.

Key results

  • Fits consistently yield $V(0) < 0$ with $5.8\sigma\text{--}7.9\sigma$ significance at $M_{\eta_c} = 1.1\,\text{GeV}$.
  • Following extrapolation to the physical charm mass, the negative sign solution persists across all polynomial and chiral fit forms with a significance of $2.0\sigma\text{--}3.4\sigma$.
  • The computed timelike form factor is consistent with the negative branch preferred by chiral perturbation theory ($(a_+, b_+) \approx (-0.58, -0.75)$) and disfavors the positive branch.

Why it matters to you

Resolves a long-standing discrete ambiguity in low-energy effective field theory and rare FCNC kaon decays. The computational advances for nonlocal electroweak matrix elements directly map onto precision calculations of second-order weak amplitudes, radiative corrections, and neutrinoless double beta decay kernels.

Caveats

Discretization errors (estimated at 10%–20%) are not removed via a continuum extrapolation due to having only two lattice spacings, and the charm mass extrapolation from $M_{\eta_c} \le 1.3\,\text{GeV}$ to the physical point introduces the dominant uncertainty.

Abstract

Rare kaon decays provide sensitive probes of the Standard Model and potential new physics. For $K^+\to\pi^+\ell^+\ell^-$, the experimental decay rate determines only the squared vector form factor $|V(z)|^2$, leaving its sign undetermined. Previous lattice QCD calculations have either used unphysical pion masses or suffered from uncertainties too large to resolve this sign. Here we present a first-principles calculation using two domain-wall fermion ensembles with near-physical pion masses. We develop a computational strategy that establishes the ultraviolet finiteness and continuum equivalence of local and conserved vector currents, substantially simplifying the calculation of nonlocal matrix elements. Four independent subtraction schemes provide nontrivial consistency checks, while the one-end trick reduces noise from quark-self-loop contributions. We determine $V(z)$ at six spacelike and timelike momentum transfers. Fits consistently yield $V(0)<0$, with a statistical significance of $5.8\sigma$--$7.9\sigma$ at $M_{\eta_c}=1.1,\mathrm{GeV}$. After extrapolation to the physical charm-quark mass, the negative sign persists across all fit forms, with a significance of $2.0\sigma$--$3.4\sigma$. Our results provide direct first-principles evidence favoring the negative-sign solution and establish a framework for more precise lattice QCD determinations.

Many-body Response to Spin-Independent and Spin-Dependent Dark Matter Scattering in a Superfluid Helium-3 Target

A. Ting, N. Darvishi, S. Autti, L. Bloomfield, A. Casey, N. Eng + 22 more

hep-ph astro-ph.CO hep-exarXiv:2611.17432

A first-principles many-body calculation of light dark matter scattering in superfluid $^{3}\text{He}$ shows that target Fermi-Dirac statistics extend the kinematic recoil tail, improving low-mass detector reach.

Novel idea

The authors extend dynamic structure factor calculations from bosonic $^{4}\text{He}$ and solid-state systems to the B-phase of fermionic superfluid $^{3}\text{He}$. They compute both spin-independent (scalar/density) and spin-dependent (axial-vector/spin-density) responses from first principles. Crucially, they show how Fermi degeneracy kinematics and Bogoliubov quasiparticle pairing break the traditional single-atom scattering approximation near threshold.

Method

The differential rate is evaluated via Fermi's Golden Rule using NREFT operators $\mathcal{O}_1$ and $\mathcal{O}_4$. The target response is formulated in BCS mean-field theory where scattering from the quasiparticle vacuum produces two-quasiparticle excitations across the B-phase gap $\Delta_0$. In the rare-event regime where recoil energy $\omega \gg \Delta_0$, the structure functions are evaluated in the degenerate Fermi-gas limit and folded into a profile-likelihood analysis incorporating QUEST-DMC radiogenic backgrounds and SQUID shot noise.

Key results

  • Finite initial Fermi momentum of the $^{3}\text{He}$ atoms broadens the kinematically allowed recoil spectrum, improving the projected low-mass reach by $\approx 2.5\%$ at the $0.51\text{ eV}$ benchmark threshold relative to the single-atom model.
  • Demonstrates that lowering the experimental threshold to $5\text{ meV}$ would extend the sensitivity of QUEST-DMC into the sub-MeV dark matter mass regime.
  • Proves that at momentum transfers $q \gg k_F$ (corresponding to $m_\chi \gtrsim 20\text{ MeV}/c^2$ and $E_R > 1\text{ eV}$), the many-body structure function rigorously converges to the incoherent single-atom cross section.

Why it matters to you

While targeted at dark matter direct detection, the dynamic structure function formalism for spin-dependent and spin-independent responses in light degenerate nuclear targets directly parallels calculations of low-energy CEvNS and inelastic neutrino-nucleus response functions in condensed or dense environments.

Caveats

The calculation treats liquid $^{3}\text{He}$ in the Fermi-gas/mean-field BCS approximation and does not yet incorporate Landau Fermi-liquid interactions or surface-adsorbed quasiparticle states that could modify the response at low temperatures.

Abstract

The QUEST-DMC experiment employs an ultra-low-temperature superfluid $^3$He target in an effort to detect sub-GeV dark matter (DM). The low-momentum scattering of such light DM is sensitive to the many-body structure of superfluid $^3$He, making it necessary to account for the collective behaviour of the medium. We present a first-principles calculation of the DM scattering rate in this inherently quantum many-body medium for both spin-independent and spin-dependent interactions. The leading consequence of this many-body behaviour is a kinematic enhancement of the scattering rate arising from the Fermi-Dirac statistics of the ultra-low-temperature target. Accounting for this extends the projected reach to lower DM masses for both spin-independent and spin-dependent interactions under the benchmark detector assumptions considered here. The resulting sensitivity improvement becomes increasingly pronounced as the detector threshold drops into the sub-eV regime. It is negligible at the conventional readout threshold of approximately $31$ eV, but becomes significant at the $0.51$ eV benchmark threshold for SQUID-based readout. Specifically, at this threshold, incorporating the many-body response extends the higher-energy recoil tail and improves the projected low-mass reach by approximately $2.5\%$ relative to the single-atom approximation. This improvement becomes more pronounced at lower thresholds. Reaching a threshold of $5$ meV could further extend the sensitivity of QUEST-DMC into the realm of sub-MeV DM.

Improved Bounds on the Dark Matter-Neutrino Cross Section using LSBGs

Akari Suezaki, Shunsaku Horiuchi

hep-ph astro-ph.GAarXiv:2611.17922

Analyzing dark matter core formation from core-collapse supernova neutrino energy injection in Low Surface Brightness Galaxies yields an upper limit of $\sigma_{\nu\chi} < 8.5 \times 10^{-25}\text{ cm}^2$ at $m_\chi = 1\text{ GeV}$.

Novel idea

The paper applies the mechanism where core-collapse supernova (CCSN) neutrinos heat dark matter halos to transform central cusps into cores to Low Surface Brightness Galaxies (LSBGs), which possess much higher stellar masses and larger halos than dwarf spheroidals. Additionally, it corrects earlier counting methods to ensure dark matter particles are not counted multiple times within the single-scattering regime.

Method

The authors analyze kinematic and rotation-curve data for LSBGs from SPARC, DensePak, and de Blok samples, fitting cored NFW profiles to determine core radii and mass deficits. Total historical neutrino fluence is calculated from host galaxy stellar mass using a Kroupa IMF and a time-integrated pinched Fermi-Dirac spectrum ($E_{\nu,\text{SN}} = 3\times 10^{53}\text{ erg}$, $\langle E_\nu \rangle = 15\text{ MeV}$). Constraints on $\sigma_{\nu\chi}$ are derived separately for the mass-loss regime ($m_\chi < m_{\chi,\text{lim}}$) and the bound energy-injection regime ($m_\chi > m_{\chi,\text{lim}}$).

Key results

  • Correcting the overcounting in previous literature weakens the Fornax dSph bound by a factor of $\sim 46$, shifting it to $\sigma_{\nu\chi} < 1.56 \times 10^{-21}\text{ cm}^2$ for $m_\chi < 130\text{ GeV}$.
  • The LSBG F579-V1 establishes an upper bound of $\sigma_{\nu\chi} < 8.5 \times 10^{-25}\text{ cm}^2$ for $m_\chi \le 35\text{ GeV}$, improving on the corrected dwarf spheroidal bound by roughly three orders of magnitude.
  • The derived bound is slightly stronger than existing constraints from the diffuse supernova neutrino background (DMDSNB) at $E_\nu \sim 15\text{ MeV}$.

Why it matters to you

Provides a stringent astrophysical benchmark for MeV-scale neutrino non-standard interactions with dark matter mediators, constraining dark portal models that evade direct laboratory bounds.

Caveats

Assumes neutrino scattering is the sole driver of halo flattening, neglecting baryonic feedback outflows; also assumes a flavor-universal neutrino spectrum and central point-source supernova distribution.

Abstract

We derive new constraints on the interaction cross section between dark matter and neutrinos ($\sigma_{\nu\chi}$) that improve upon existing limits. We adopt a mechanism where neutrinos emitted from core-collapse supernovae transfer their energy to the dark matter halo via elastic scattering, transitioning a centrally cusped profile to a flat-cored profile. We apply this mechanism to Low Surface Brightness Galaxies (LSBGs), which are characterized by their relatively large sizes, low stellar densities, and measurements of dark matter cores. We improve upon previous methods so that each dark matter particle is counted at most once, which weakens existing limits, e.g., from the Fornax dSph by a factor of $\sim 50$. Applying our new method to LSBGs, we derive an upper limit of $\sigma_{\nu\chi}< 8.5 \times 10^{-25}\,\rm cm^2$ for a dark matter mass of $m_\chi =1\,\rm GeV$ at a typical supernova neutrino energy of $E_\nu=15\,\rm MeV$. This constraint is approximately three orders of magnitude stronger than the Fornax limit derived with the same method and slightly stronger than the bound from the dark matter diffused supernova neutrino background (DMDSNB).

Suprathermal bulk viscosity in binary neutron star mergers

Pranjal Tambe, Debarati Chatterjee

nucl-th astro-ph.HE hep-pharXiv:2611.18146

Calculating amplitude-dependent bulk viscosity in neutron star matter with thermal blurring and collisional broadening reveals non-linear viscous enhancements of up to five orders of magnitude for $10\%$ density oscillation amplitudes.

Novel idea

Extends nucleonic bulk viscosity calculations into the suprathermal regime ($|\mu_\Delta| \ge T$) while simultaneously incorporating both thermal blurring of the Fermi surface and in-medium collisional broadening via the Nucleon Width Approximation (NWA). It abandons the traditional odd-power expansion of reaction rates in favor of a complete polynomial expansion in $\mu_\Delta/T$.

Method

The authors model non-trapped degenerate $npe$ matter undergoing $1\text{ kHz}$ density oscillations. Net direct and modified Urca re-equilibration rates are computed within the NWA framework across true chemical equilibrium for two equations of state: IUF (with a direct Urca threshold at $n_B = 4.1n_0$) and QMC-RMF3 (no threshold). The chemical imbalance $\mu_\Delta(t)$ is integrated dynamically over the oscillation cycle to extract the net dissipative bulk viscosity.

Key results

  • Non-linear suprathermal effects become dominant once the density oscillation amplitude $\Delta n / n$ reaches approximately $0.10$.
  • At low temperatures ($T = 0.5\text{ MeV}$), the bulk viscosity increases by three to five orders of magnitude relative to the subthermal limit for both sub-threshold and super-threshold densities.
  • At $T = 3\text{ MeV}$, the bulk viscosity remains largely independent of amplitude, confirming that suprathermal enhancements are strictly confined to cooler regions ($T \lesssim 1\text{ MeV}$).
  • In the subthermal limit, the NWA framework yields a bulk viscosity roughly one order of magnitude larger than standard Fermi-surface modified Urca approximations.

Why it matters to you

Although primarily focused on gravitational-wave damping in binary neutron star mergers, the underlying physics involves weak-interaction Urca reaction rates and nucleon spectral broadening in dense matter, touching nuclear many-body response under extreme conditions.

Caveats

Assumes neutrinos are completely untrapped ($npe$ matter without trapped lepton number), an assumption that breaks down in hot, dense post-merger cores where neutrino diffusion must be coupled to the fluid dynamics.

Abstract

Numerical relativity simulations of binary neutron star mergers and core-collapse supernovae indicate that the neutron star matter can be driven far from equilibrium due to large amplitude density oscillations, with frequency in the kHz range. Urca processes in neutron star matter try to restore the system towards equilibrium providing strong bulk viscous damping of these density oscillations. Standard bulk viscosity calculations typically assume small amplitude density oscillations, such that the deviation from chemical equilibrium $\mu_{\Delta}$ is less than the temperature $T$. In this work we compute the complete amplitude dependent bulk viscosity, taking into account, for the first time, thermal blurring effects and collisional broadening of in-medium nucleons. Our results show that the non-linear effects in bulk viscosity start to become important when the oscillation amplitude is about $10$ percent of the background density. In this regime the bulk viscosity can increase by three to five orders of magnitude. These results have important consequences for simulations of binary neutron star mergers as well as their multi-messenger observations.

Construction and Commissioning of the Warwick TPC Platform

Matthew Snape, Hong-Kai Tan, Gary Barker, Alan Burton, Alexander Deisting, Philip Hamacher-Baumann + 1 more

physics.ins-det hep-ex nucl-exarXiv:2611.17389 · cross-list

The Warwick TPC R&D platform has been constructed and commissioned, demonstrating optical Timepix3 tracking and measuring pressure- and voltage-dependent secondary scintillation up to 7.5 bar in argon gas mixtures.

Novel idea

The paper reports on the design and commissioning of the WarTPC platform: a high-pressure (up to 10 barg) active-target gas TPC specifically dedicated to testing gas mixtures (notably hydrogen-rich mixtures) and optical readout configurations to eliminate nuclear uncertainties in neutrino-nucleus interaction measurements.
