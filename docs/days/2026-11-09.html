Entanglement in Collective Neutrino Oscillations on a Quantum Computer

Katarina Bleau, Nikolina Ilic, Joachim Kopp, Ushak Rahaman, Xin Yue Yu

hep-ph astro-ph.HE quant-pharXiv:2611.08595

Evaluates the emergence of mean-field QKEs from entangled multi-neutrino systems and identifies physical regimes where quantum entanglement remains phenomenologically relevant in dense environments.

Novel idea

Combines analytic proofs of mean-field QKE recovery at large $N$ with quantum circuits run on IBM Kingston (Heron R2) hardware and classical tensor/spin simulations up to $N = 2.6 \times 10^6$. It introduces concrete astrophysical counterexamples to the conventional 'irrelevant entanglement' paradigm, such as spatial neutrino streams with overlapping wavepackets ($\sim 10^{-12}\text{ cm}$) or multi-encounter scattering geometries.

Method

Formulates two-mode $\nu_e\nu_\mu$ and bipolar $\nu_e\bar{\nu}_e$ systems in a collective spin basis $\hat{\mathbf{S}}_{\text{tot}}$. Implements a QFT-based sum-register adder ($W$-transform) to isolate single-register time evolution on quantum hardware. Runs quantum hardware circuits using Trotter steps ($|\Delta|\tau = 0.25, 30$) with error mitigation, alongside classical simulations up to $N > 10^6$.

Key results

  • Demonstrates that mean-field QKE behavior emerges at $N \gtrsim 300$ for two-neutrino systems and $N \gtrsim 4000$ for bipolar $\nu\bar{\nu}$ systems.
  • Quantum circuits executed on the IBM Kingston QPU accurately match noiseless classical simulations across $>10$ Trotter steps.
  • Spatial streams with overlapping wavepackets ($10^{-12}\text{ cm}$) induce persistent nearest-neighbor interactions that break mean-field QKE predictions.
  • Multi-encounter 3-neutrino toy models demonstrate persistent entanglement entropy $S(\rho_0) > 0$ building up even under short-duration Gaussian encounters.

Why it matters to you

Directly addresses collective neutrino oscillation dynamics in supernova cores and neutron star mergers, providing concrete criteria for when Boltzmann-type kinetic equations fail and quantum computing is required.

Caveats

The spatial stream and multi-encounter models rely on simplified toy geometries; the actual extent of wavepacket delocalization under realistic core-collapse supernova conditions (e.g., via Landau levels) remains unquantified.

Abstract

Simulations on a quantum computer have been propounded as a next-generation tool for studying neutrino flavour oscillations in dense environments like supernovae or neutron star mergers. However, quantum advantage hinges on the presence of quantum entanglement in the neutrino ensemble, and it has been argued that such entanglement becomes negligible in the limit of large neutrino number density. Here, we address this question by explicitly simulating the multiparticle dynamics of entangled neutrino ensembles with a varying number of particles, $N$, on both classical and quantum hardware, and comparing our results to those obtained with conventional ``mean-field'' methods based on Boltzmann-type kinetic equations. For simple toy systems, we demonstrate both analytically and numerically that, indeed, the predictions of full multiparticle simulations agree with those of mean-field methods in the large-$N$ limit. Along the way, we develop novel algorithms for the efficient simulation of entangled neutrino gases on both classical and quantum hardware. We then discuss physical situations in which an entangled neutrino system \emph{cannot} be described through mean-field dynamics alone and quantum entanglement therefore becomes phenomenologically relevant. We comment on the conditions under which these scenarios may be realized in supernova cores or neutron star mergers.

Possible Solution to the Gallium Anomaly? Nuclear Transition Densities

D. Castillo, D. Frycz, J. Menéndez

nucl-th hep-ex hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2611.09788

Proves via nuclear shell model calculations that non-factorization of lepton and nuclear wavefunctions cannot resolve the Gallium anomaly, as realistic transition densities yield factorized and non-factorized cross sections agreeing within 1%.

Novel idea

Rigorously evaluates a recent proposal claiming the Gallium anomaly is solved by breaking lepton-nuclear factorization using ad hoc nuclear transition densities. By calculating realistic Gamow-Teller (GT) transition densities within the Nuclear Shell Model (NSM) convolved with Dirac-Hartree-Fock-Slater electron wavefunctions, the authors demonstrate that ad hoc density fits require unphysical multi-order-of-magnitude scale modifications and extreme fine-tuning.

Method

Computes GT transition densities $C(r)$ for $^{71}\text{Ga}(\nu_e,e^-)^{71}\text{Ge}$ and $^{71}\text{Ge}$ electron capture across 9 NSM setups (JUN45, GCN2850, RG.PROLATE, JJ4BB, and LNPS interactions in $pf$ and $0g_{9/2}$ spaces, with bare and effective GT operators). Convolves $C(r)$ with relativistic bound and continuum electron wavefunctions $g_\kappa(r), f_\kappa(r)$ solved via RADIAL. Normalizes total strength using the measured $^{71}\text{Ge}$ EC half-life.

Key results

  • Calculated NSM radial GT transition densities $C(r)$ are remarkably robust across all 9 nuclear Hamiltonians, valence spaces, and GT operators.
  • Non-factorized cross sections for $^{51}\text{Cr}$ and $^{37}\text{Ar}$ sources match detailed-balance factorized cross sections within 1%, leaving a persistent $\sim 20\%$ deficit (ratio $\sigma_{\text{exp}}/\sigma_{\text{th}} \approx 0.83$).
  • Ad hoc transition densities that 'solve' the anomaly require overall radial scales $100-1000\times$ larger than NSM and severe percent-level cancellations between positive and negative lobes.
  • Single-particle orbital ansatz fits require density coefficients $\sim 3000\times$ larger than shell-model predictions to reproduce experimental capture rates.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts research on low-energy neutrino-nucleus cross sections, Gamow-Teller matrix elements, and the Gallium anomaly. It rules out nuclear wavefunction non-factorization as a solution, firmly refocusing the anomaly on experimental systematics or BSM physics (sterile neutrinos).

Caveats

Shell model calculations were restricted to valence spaces ($pf$ and $0g_{9/2}$); ab initio VS-IMSRG calculations with explicit two-body currents remain a desirable future check.

Abstract

The gallium anomaly, a 20% deficit in the measured $\nu_e$ captures on $^{71}$Ga, remains unexplained. A recent work [Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 131805 (2026), arXiv:2512.20560] has proposed a solution based on the breakdown of the factorization of lepton and nuclear wavefunctions that underlies the prediction of the cross section, combined with ad hoc Gamow-Teller nuclear transition densities. We test this proposed solution with transition densities calculated within the nuclear shell model (NSM), normalized to the $^{71}$Ge electron-capture half-life and convolved with Dirac-Hartree-Fock-Slater electron wavefunctions. We explore several NSM interactions, bare and effective Gamow-Teller operators, as well as the impact of the valence space. In all cases, we obtain remarkably consistent NSM transition densities. Our factorized and nonfactorized cross sections agree, remaining 20% higher than the measured value. In addition, we use NSM single-nucleon transition densities as ansatz and find that solving the anomaly requires fine-tuned densities, with percent-level cancellations and an overall scale orders of magnitude larger than the calculated NSM ones. We conclude that the gallium anomaly is not solved when using realistic nuclear transition densities.

Neutrino pair annihilation in curved spacetime: a fast moment method and the geometry of flavour conversion

Rafael C. R. de Lima, Luciano de Oliveira Junior, Pedro Martins Bailer

astro-ph.HE gr-qc hep-pharXiv:2611.08742 · cross-list

Shows that GR light bending around black holes preferentially enhances $\nu\bar{\nu}\to e^+e^-$ annihilation involving inner-ring heavy-lepton neutrinos, altering flat-space flavor conversion factors by up to 2.4x and reversing net conversion effects.

Novel idea

Establishes an exact separability theorem for neutrino annihilation when initial heavy-lepton emission is negligible, and shows how spatial segregation between flavor-dependent neutrinospheres breaks flat-space conversion factorizations in GR. It identifies a mechanism where GR light bending amplifies annihilations involving inner-ring neutrinos over outer-disc pairs by a factor of 3.

Method

Uses backward ray tracing in Schwarzschild and Kerr spacetimes combined with an exact angular-moment expansion (rank 0, 1, 2) of redshifted energy moments without moment closures (implemented in ANNIHILUS). Evaluates local rates and deposited Killing energy $\dot{E}_\infty$ across thin Keplerian discs under parametrized linear flavor conversion maps.

Key results

  • Proves exact separability ($\delta = 0$): for uniform conversion and zero initial $\nu_x$ emission, the conversion ratio $\mathcal{R}$ is strictly independent of spacetime geometry and emission profiles.
  • For overlapping flavor neutrinospheres, GR enhances net deposition by factors of $1.4-5\times$, while changing uniform conversion factors by $\le 6\%$ (up to $22\%$ for non-uniform conversion at $a=0.99$).
  • For spatially segregated neutrinospheres (inner $\nu_x$ ring, outer $ u_e$ disc), GR alters flat-space conversion factors by up to $141\%$ ($2.4\times$).
  • Demonstrates sign reversal of flavor conversion: flavor equipartition reduces energy deposition by $54\%$ in flat space, but increases it by $11\%$ in Kerr spacetime ($a=0.99$).

Why it matters to you

Crucial for neutrino annihilation and transport in black-hole accretion discs and post-merger environments, demonstrating that flavor conversion and relativistic transport cannot be factorized when neutrinospheres are spatially segregated.

Caveats

Flavor conversion is parametrized via linear emission maps rather than dynamically solving collective quantum kinetic equations along geodesics, and free streaming without reabsorption is assumed.

Abstract

Neutrino-antineutrino annihilation above black-hole accretion discs is shaped both by general-relativistic transport and by neutrino flavour conversion. Whether these effects can be treated independently depends on how gravity weights the different flavour populations. We investigate this interplay using backward ray tracing in Schwarzschild and Kerr spacetimes, combined with an exact angular-moment formulation of the annihilation kernel that we validate against the analytic spherical solution and published disc calculations. We first establish that flavour-conversion corrections are independent of the spacetime geometry when conversion is uniform and the initial heavy-lepton neutrino emission is negligible. For uniform conversion, this separability remains approximately valid when the flavour-dependent emitting regions overlap: compared with the same discs in flat space, moving with the same local velocities, gravity enhances the deposited energy by factors of 1.4-5, while changing the relative conversion effect by no more than 6% for most temperature profiles considered; conversion that varies across the disc raises this to 22% around a rapidly spinning hole. A qualitatively different regime emerges when heavy-lepton neutrinos originate from an inner ring separated from the electron-flavour emitting regions. Relativistic bending and energy shifts preferentially enhance annihilations involving inner-ring neutrinos, producing relativistic conversion factors up to 2.4 times the flat-space ones. In one configuration, flavour equipartition reduces the deposition by 54% in flat space but increases it by 11% in Kerr spacetime. These results identify the spatial arrangement of flavour-dependent neutrinospheres as a key determinant, in the models considered, of whether flavour conversion and relativistic transport can be treated separately.

The ATAR, a dense and compact vertex detector for the PIONEER experiment

Tilman Rohe (Paul Scherrer Institut, Villigen, Switzerland), for the PIONEER collaboration

physics.ins-det hep-ex nucl-exarXiv:2611.09489 · cross-list

Presents the design and performance requirements of ATAR, a 48-layer LGAD active target for the PIONEER experiment to measure $R^{\pi}_{e/\mu}$ to 0.01% precision.

Novel idea

Details the Active Target (ATAR) for the PIONEER experiment at PSI, combining a 48-layer stack of thin ($120\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) trench-isolated LGAD strip sensors with off-axis fast waveform digitization to achieve 2 ns double-pulse pair separation and sub-200 ps timing in a 300 kHz pion beam environment.

Method

Constructs ATAR using $2\times 2\text{ cm}^2$ trench-isolated LGAD microstrips ($200\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ pitch) rotated $90^\circ$ layer-to-layer. Signals travel via 15 cm flex cables outside the calorimeter acceptance to FAST3 amplifier ASICs, digitized by HD-SoC or Sampic chips. Performs topological and pulse-shape rejection of the $\pi\to\mu\to e$ decay chain.

Key results

  • Achieves timing resolution of $\sim 200\text{ ps}$ and pulse-pair separation down to $2\text{ ns}$ in a single strip channel.
  • Pulse-height digitizations cleanly distinguish $14\text{ MeV}$ pion stops, $4.1\text{ MeV}$ decay muons, and MIP positrons.
  • Timing selection suppresses $\pi\to\mu\to e$ background by a factor of 160 while retaining $60\%$ of $\pi\to e$ events.
  • Combined topological and per-hit energy deposit analysis yields a factor 500 tail rejection while maintaining $50\%$ signal efficiency.
  • Constructed the ATAR 8 prototype stack (8-10 layers) for a PSI piM1 test beam run in late 2026.

Why it matters to you

Relevant to precision tests of lepton flavor universality ($R^{\pi}_{e/\mu}$) and CKM unitarity ($|V_{ud}|$ via pion beta decay), detailing the vertex detector technology required to suppress systematic tail fractions.

Caveats

LGAD gain suppression under dense energy deposits (stopping muons/pions) requires ongoing calibration; final digitization chip selection between HD-SoC and Sampic remains to be finalized.

Abstract

PIONEER is an approved experiment at the Paul Scherrer Institut (PSI) in Villigen, Switzerland that aims to measure the charged-pion branching ratio to electrons vs. muons with an accuracy of 0.01%. This is an order of magnitude improvement in precision compared to its predecessors and approaches the accuracy of the standard model prediction. The key elements of PIONEER are a high resolution calorimeter built concentrically around the high granularity active target (ATAR). The ATAR is composed of a stack of 48 low gain avalanche strip detectors (LGADs) to stop the beam pions and provide detailed information of the event topology. This will include precise timing (in the order of 200ps), spatial information (to an accuracy of about 60 micro meter) and pulse height information with a high dynamic range to separate pion stops from muons and MIPs. To avoid affecting the energy measurement of the positrons, the readout electronics is placed outside the acceptance angle of the calorimeter. It is composed of a fast amplifier and a fast sampling chip digitizing the LGAD wave form for real time analysis. The paper describes the PIONEER-experiment with focus on the ATAR and its readout. The status of the development and activities in 2026 will be reported.

Features of Non-Local Quantum Correlations in Nuclei

James W. T. Keeble, Caroline E. P. Robin, Federico Rocco, Martin J. Savage

nucl-th quant-pharXiv:2611.08776

Evaluates non-local magic and entanglement spectrum antiflatness across 260+ nuclei, proving the bound $4\mathcal{F}_A \le \mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NL}}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ and uncovering distinct quantum complexity signatures in odd-odd nuclei.

Novel idea

Presents the first systematic calculation of non-local non-stabilizerness (magic, $\mathcal{M}^{\text{NL}}_{\text{lin}}$) and entanglement-spectrum antiflatness ($\mathcal{F}_A$) in nuclear shell-model ground states across $>260$ nuclei using a proton-neutron bipartition. Discovers that odd-odd nuclei exhibit quantum complexity structures distinctly different from even-even and odd-even systems.

Method

Solves nuclear shell-model ground states using BIGSTICK with Cohen-Kurath ($p$), USDB ($sd$), GXPF1A ($pf$), and JUN45 ($pf_{5/2}g$) interactions. Maps nucleonic orbital occupations to 6-44 qubits via Jordan-Wigner transform. Computes proton-neutron entanglement entropy $S$, antiflatness $\mathcal{F}_A = \text{Tr}(\rho_A^2) - \text{Tr}(\rho_A^3)$, total SRE $\mathcal{M}_2$, and non-local SRE $\mathcal{M}^{\text{NL}}_2$ using auxiliary Schmidt states.

Key results

  • Confirms the lower bound $4\mathcal{F}_A \le \mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NL}}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ across all $>260$ calculated nuclei, with saturation approaching near separable states.
  • Even-even and odd-even nuclei trace a characteristic 'boomerang' trajectory in $\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{NL}}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ vs $4\mathcal{F}_A$, whereas odd-odd nuclei exhibit low antiflatness ($4\mathcal{F}_A \le 0.1$) despite high non-local magic.
  • Total non-stabilizerness per qubit is roughly constant across shells ($\mathcal{M}_2/N_q \approx 0.16-0.17$), peaking in strongly deformed nuclei such as $^{24}\text{Mg}$ ($\mathcal{M}_2/N_q \approx 0.44$).
  • Non-stabilizerness acts as an indicator of shell closures (dropping at $N=28, 40$), whereas antiflatness increases when approaching shell closures.

Why it matters to you

Provides quantitative measures of quantum complexity and non-stabilizerness in nuclear wavefunctions, informing the classical computational cost (tensor networks vs. quantum algorithms) for nuclear structure and proton-neutron correlations.

Caveats

Calculations rely on shell-model valence spaces and effective two-body Hamiltonians without explicit three-nucleon forces or continuum couplings; orbital occupation qubit mappings depend on single-particle basis choices.

Abstract

Classical simulation of quantum many-body systems is challenged by the combined effects of entanglement and non-stabilizerness (magic). Non-local magic, $M^{\mathrm{NL}}_\alpha$, quantifies the component of non-stabilizerness that cannot be removed by local unitary transformations and provides a measure of quantum complexity beyond entanglement alone. It is bounded from below by the antiflatness of the entanglement spectrum, ${F}_A$. We calculate stabilizer R\'enyi entropies, non-local magic, entanglement-spectrum antiflatness, and entanglement entropy in shell-model ground states of more than 260 even-even, odd-even, and odd-odd nuclei, using a bipartition into proton and neutron sectors. We find that the previously established bound $4{F}_A\leq{M}^{\mathrm{NL}}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ holds for every nucleus studied and approaches saturation in the limit of separable states. Odd-odd nuclei exhibit markedly different patterns of entanglement and magic compared with even-even and odd-even systems, revealing distinctive signatures of proton-neutron quantum correlations and providing new insight into the complexity generated by nuclear interactions.

Exact and hybrid neutrino oscillation probabilities via the evolution operator

Juan Carlos D'Olivo, José Arnulfo Herrera Lara, Oscar A. Sampayo

hep-pharXiv:2611.08690

Exact closed-form expressions for three-flavor neutrino oscillation probabilities in constant-density matter are derived via the evolution operator and Vieta's formulas, yielding a high-accuracy hybrid approximation for DUNE.

Novel idea

By utilizing Lagrange-Sylvester interpolation on the flavor-basis evolution operator and Vieta's traces, the authors avoid introducing matter-dependent mixing angles and non-standard CP phases. The resulting exact probability formulas are structurally identical to vacuum expressions, neatly separating genuine CP violation (proportional to the Jarlskog invariant) from matter-induced effects.

Method

The Schrödinger equation for flavor evolution in a constant-density medium (symmetric potential) is solved via the evolution operator matrix components. The Cayley-Hamilton theorem and Vieta's formulas for the cubic Hamiltonian eigenvalues are applied. A hybrid approach replaces the cumbersome trigonometric eigenvalues with approximate ones derived from two successive Hamiltonian rotations (1-3 and 1-2 planes).

Key results

  • Derived concise, exact analytical formulas for $P_{ee}$, $P_{ u_ullet u_ullet}$ (specifically $P_{ u_ullet u_ullet}$ and $P_{ u_ullet u_ullet}$) that reduce correctly to standard vacuum formulas when $V o 0$.
  • For a DUNE-like setup, the hybrid probabilities match exact probabilities with absolute errors of $\sim 2 \times 10^{-6}$ and relative errors under $0.01\%$ across all channels.
  • Absolute errors of the hybrid approach are at least two orders of magnitude smaller than standard zeroth-order double-rotation approximations using the same eigenvalues.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to long-baseline oscillation systematics (DUNE) and interaction/propagation modeling workflows, offering clean analytical control over matter-induced CP violation without resorting to uncontrolled series expansions.

Caveats

Restricted strictly to constant-density media (or piecewise constant profiles); does not solve arbitrary position-dependent density profiles analytically.

Abstract

We revisit the three-flavor neutrino oscillations in constant-density matter using the evolution matrix expressed in terms of the Lagrange-Sylvester interpolation. Taking advantage of Vieta's formulas for the Hamiltonian of the system, we derive exact closed expressions for the oscillation probabilities $P_{ee}$, $P_{\mu e}$, and $P_{\mu\tau}$, which are concise and simple, and structurally identical to their vacuum counterparts. The CP-violation contribution, proportional to the Jarlskog invariant $\mathcal{J}$, appears as a single, clearly separated term. Our approach does not require introducing neutrino mixing in the medium and avoids using a matter-dependent CP-violation phase, facilitating the separation of genuine CP violation from that induced by matter effects. We explicitly verify that, in the limit $V \to 0$, our expressions for the flavor transition probabilities reduce to the well-known vacuum formulas. The exact structure of the probabilities is preserved if the exact, not very enlightening, trigonometric eigenvalues are replaced by approximate expressions in radicals. We illustrate this hybrid approach with eigenvalues obtained from two successive rotations of the Hamiltonian. For an experiment like DUNE, the hybrid probability values match those of the exact probabilities within absolute errors of about $2\times 10^{-6}$ in all channels and relative errors less than $0.01 \%$ for $P_{ee}$, $P_{\mu e}$, and $P_{\mu\tau}$.

Novel idea

Combines flavor-dependent coherent forward scattering (MSW potentials) and incoherent scattering attenuation over cosmological distances from a dense DM spike. Discovers an 'eigenstate-isolation' regime at large potentials where only the interacting flavor's eigenstate is attenuated, producing a survival plateau.

Method

Models adiabatic neutrino propagation from the emission radius inside the TXS 0506+056 SMBH dark matter spike ($R_{ ext{em}} = 0.023$ pc) through the Galactic halo to Earth. Computes medium-modified PMNS mixing matrices and evaluates flavor composition changes and flux attenuation against IceCube 68% and 95% C.L. boundaries.

Key results

  • Local Galactic DM density constraints require the effective potential parameter $A_\alpha \lesssim 10^{-30}\text{ cm}^3$ to prevent distortion of vacuum PMNS mixing at 100 TeV.
  • For $A \ll 10^{-38}\text{ cm}^3$, standard attenuation limits give $\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi \lesssim 10^{-28}\text{ cm}^2/\text{GeV}$.
  • For $A \gg 10^{-36}\text{ cm}^3$, eigenstate isolation confines the interacting flavor to a single matter eigenstate, creating a survival plateau that relaxes $90\%$ flux attenuation bounds up to $\sim 5.6 \times 10^{-25}\text{ cm}^2/\text{GeV}$.

Why it matters to you

Connects low-energy/astrophysical BSM physics (neutrino-dark matter interactions, vector portal models) with high-energy IceCube astrophysical neutrino flavor data and MSW medium propagation.

Caveats

Assumes a specific $\gamma_{\text{sp}} = 7/3$ DM spike profile around TXS 0506+056 and neglects energy-redistribution/regeneration from forward scattering via light mediators.

Abstract

High-energy astrophysical neutrinos probe non-standard neutrino interactions over cosmological distances. We study flavor-dependent interactions between neutrinos and dark matter (DM) for neutrinos produced inside the dense DM spike surrounding the supermassive black hole of the blazar TXS~0506+056. A flavor-diagonal potential $V_\alpha=A_\alpha\,\rho_\chi(r)$ induced by coherent forward scattering modifies the mixing in the medium, and the flavor-dependent scattering cross section attenuates the neutrino flux along the line of sight. We show that the propagation is adiabatic throughout the spike and the halo, so that the matter eigenstates produced at the source are conserved and only re-projected onto flavors at Earth. Two regimes emerge. For $A\ll 10^{-38}\,{\rm cm^3}$ the mixing is unaffected and the IceCube three-flavor composition limits the cross section to $\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi\lesssim 10^{-28}\,{\rm cm^2/GeV}$. For larger $A$ the interacting flavor is isolated into a single matter eigenstate at the source; the two remaining eigenstates escape without attenuation, producing a survival plateau that relaxes the limits by up to four orders of magnitude. The sign of the potential can differ between neutrinos and antineutrinos, so that the isolated state connects to different vacuum mass eigenstates for $\nu$ and $\bar\nu$, which leaves a characteristic imprint on the flavor composition even without attenuation. The requirement that the potential induced by the dark matter density around Earth do not affect the observed PMNS matrix, so that the flavor projection at Earth is performed with the local matter mixing, gives A$\lesssim 10^{-30}\text{cm} ^3$. The combined constraints are presented in the $(A,\sigma_{\nu\chi}/m_\chi)$ plane and compared with a vector-mediator benchmark.

The PICO-40L detector

PICO Collaboration, E. Adams, B. Ali, R. Anderson-Dornan, D. J. Auty, E. Behnke + 60 more

PICO-40L is a novel Right-Side-Up (RSU) bubble chamber designed to eliminate buffer-fluid and bellows-particulate backgrounds for dark matter searches, serving as a prototype for PICO-500.

Novel idea

Introduces the Right-Side-Up (RSU) design where pressure is transferred via two nested synthetic quartz vessels connected to a bellows *below* the active volume, completely removing the traditional buffer fluid interface and isolating bellows-particulate backgrounds.

Method

Constructed a two-vessel quartz assembly containing a $\text{C}_3\text{F}_8$ target, housed inside a pressure vessel filled with mineral oil. Integrated a dedicated thermal system (using a bottom coil chiller and HDPE insulation) to maintain a cold sub-cooled region in the lower bellows and a stably superheated active volume above. Deployed 12 piezoelectric acoustic transducers, four optical cameras, and fast pressure accumulators.

Key results

  • Achieved a measured radon emanation rate of $20.1 \pm 2.0$ atoms/day of $^{222}\text{Rn}$ dominated by the nitrile O-ring seal.
  • Successfully demonstrated stable operation and background discrimination capabilities (acoustic, optical stereo reconstruction, and Dytran fast pressure growth) during $^{252}\text{Cf}$ calibration and commissioning runs at SNOLAB.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to low-background experimental hardware and dark matter direct detection techniques, validating background suppression strategies critical for future low-energy noble-gas/fluorine detectors.

Abstract

The PICO collaboration has designed and constructed a novel bubble chamber to search for evidence of Weakly Interacting Massive Particles, a leading dark matter candidate. The PICO-40L detector is the successor to PICO-60, and uses a fully redesigned system for pressurization of the active volume after a bubble nucleation event. The newly designed detector concept of PICO-40L is referred to as the Right-Side-Up (RSU) design and requires a dedicated thermal system to keep the lower region of the detector cold while the upper region is stably superheated. The main goal of PICO-40L is to establish the performance characteristics of the RSU detector concept, which will inform the operating configuration of the final generation PICO dark matter detector, PICO-500, which is currently under construction at SNOLAB. This paper reviews the design, installation, and performance of the PICO-40L detector.

Opening up derivative baryon-number-violating operators

Julian Heeck, Diana Sokhashvili

hep-pharXiv:2611.08621

A systematic construction of tree-level UV completions for derivative baryon-number-violating (BNV) operators up to mass dimension 12 reveals that derivative BNV operators need not be suppressed.

Novel idea

Demonstrates that derivative BNV operators are not generically suppressed relative to non-derivative ones. Specifically identifies non-trivial UV completions (via heavy scalars and fermions) where odd-derivative operators lack non-derivative counterparts due to Lorentz/gauge structure, or where multi-derivative operators become the leading contribution because lower-order non-derivative terms vanish due to Higgs field antisymmetry.

Method

Generated simplified BNV operators up to dimension 12 using Sym2Int and systematically matched them against tree-level topologies using scalar and fermion mediators in SMEFT and $\nu$SMEFT (excluding gauge bosons). Developed public code and data repositories for UV-completing derivative operators.

Key results

  • Constructed over one million non-trivial tree-level UV completions for derivative BNV operators up to mass dimension 12.
  • Identified that non-trivial one-, two-, three-, and four-derivative operators first emerge at mass dimensions $d = 8$, $10$, $12$, and $14$, respectively.
  • Highlighted that Higgs-field antisymmetry forces certain non-derivative operators to vanish, making derivative insertions the leading term in the heavy-mass expansion without fine-tuned cancellations.

Why it matters to you

Directly touches SMEFT/LEFT matching, BNV operator bases, and effective field theory frameworks used for precision nucleon decay and beyond-the-Standard-Model phenomenology.

Caveats

Non-trivial UV completions guarantee operator dominance in the heavy-mass expansion only prior to considering loop-induced lower-dimensional operator regeneration, which must be checked model by model.

Abstract

Baryon number violation is our most sensitive probe of physics beyond the Standard Model, typically encoded in higher-dimensional effective operators. Operators involving derivatives are often ignored in phenomenological analyses since they are generically suppressed compared to non-derivative operators. We systematically construct tree-level UV completions of derivative baryon-number-violating operators up to mass dimension 12 in the Standard Model effective field theory (extended by right-handed neutrinos) and show that derivative operators need not be suppressed. Our public code can be used to UV-complete other derivative operators.
