10worth readingIceCube neutrino cross sections

Measurement of the neutrino cross section across the TeV scale using IceCube data

Rasmi Hajjar, Mauricio Bustamante, Sergio Palomares-Ruiz

hep-pharXiv:2610.24068

Eight years of IceCube upward-going muon data yield energy-dependent neutrino-nucleon charged-current cross sections from 150 GeV to 40 TeV with sub-20% uncertainties, consistent with Standard Model predictions.

Novel idea

Prior TeV-scale neutrino telescope analyses constrained only a single overall cross-section normalization across hundreds of TeV, leaving an unmeasured gap between collider data below 2 TeV and astrophysical data above 10 TeV. This work performs the first energy-binned cross-section extraction across 150 GeV--40 TeV in five bins using Earth absorption and event rates. It also provides the first, albeit mild (1.6$\sigma$), empirical separation of neutrino and antineutrino cross sections above 1 TeV without event-by-event charge identification.

Method

The analysis uses 305,735 through-going muon track events from public 2011--2019 IceCube data. Neutrino propagation through the PREM Earth model is computed using nuSQuIDS, accounting for attenuation, neutral-current energy redistribution, and tau-neutrino regeneration. A Bayesian MCMC fit is performed across 260 reconstructed energy and zenith bins, marginalizing over 16 systematic nuisance parameters while applying a 16% Gaussian prior from fixed-target data only to the lowest-energy bin (150--360 GeV).

Key results

  • Extracted average charged-current cross sections $(\sigma_\nu^{\rm CC} + \sigma_{\bar{\nu}}^{\rm CC})/2$ across five bins between 150 GeV and 40 TeV, showing agreement with CSMS Standard Model calculations within uncertainties below 20%.
  • Obtained separate $\nu_\mu$ and $\bar{\nu}_\mu$ cross sections above 1 TeV, establishing a 1.6$\sigma$ indication of non-zero $\bar{\nu}_\mu$ CC cross section above 360 GeV.
  • Projected that a tenfold increase in telescope exposure would establish a non-zero $\bar{\nu}_\mu$ cross section at $>7\sigma$ and reduce average cross-section errors below 10%.

Why it matters to you

Provides empirical constraints on high-energy deep-inelastic scattering cross sections that bridge fixed-target, forward collider (FASER$\nu$, SND@LHC), and astrophysical domains. It directly tests parton distribution functions and neutrino transport models used in high-energy neutrino simulations.

Caveats

The extracted cross sections suffer from a strong anti-correlation with the conventional atmospheric neutrino flux normalization, and removing the fixed-target low-energy prior shifts high-energy cross sections upward by 20--30%.

Abstract

Neutrinos interact with matter exclusively through the weak force, resulting in extremely small cross sections that are difficult to measure. At TeV energies, neutrino-nucleon interactions probe the structure of the nucleon in regimes where our knowledge of it is less complete, and signatures of physics beyond the Standard Model could manifest. Recent measurements using collider, atmospheric, and astrophysical neutrinos have begun to explore this regime, but no single measurement has provided precise coverage from sub-TeV to tens of TeV, and uncertainties remain at the level of about 20-40%. Here we present a determination of the muon-neutrino-nucleon charged-current cross sections as a function of energy across 150 GeV-40 TeV using eight years of through-going muon neutrino data collected by the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. We measure the average neutrino-antineutrino cross section, enabling direct comparison with previous results, and find, for the first time above 1 TeV, mild indications that the neutrino and antineutrino cross sections can be measured separately. The measured cross sections are consistent with Standard Model predictions and are determined with uncertainties below 20% throughout the explored energy range, achieving the most precise characterization of neutrino-nucleon interactions across the TeV scale to date. These results provide a unified measurement of neutrino-nucleon cross sections in this energy regime, connecting accelerator-based and previous higher-energy neutrino observations - thereby advancing high-energy neutrino physics into the precision era.

10must-readBeAGLE++ event generator

BeAGLE++: A modern modular framework for eA physics at the EIC

Arjun Kumar, Abhay Deshpande, Zhoudunming Tu

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-exarXiv:2610.24095

BeAGLE++ is a modular C++ lepton-nucleus event generator coupling PYTHIA-8 hard scattering to an extended time-ordered INCL++ intranuclear cascade and ABLA++ statistical de-excitation.

Novel idea

Unlike the legacy BeAGLE Fortran code, BeAGLE++ models the target nucleus as a dynamical system where nucleons evolve in a potential well and can reflect or transmit at the nuclear surface. The intranuclear cascade replaces generation-ordered stepping with a global-clock, chronologically time-ordered interaction list. It also incorporates space-time formation zones into the multi-particle cascade, includes in-cascade light-cluster coalescence up to $A=8$, and expands hadron reinteraction channels up to 20 GeV.

Method

Primary DIS lepton-nucleon scattering and partonic hadronization are handled via PYTHIA-8 with nuclear PDFs. Hadronic final-state interactions are simulated by injecting formed hadrons into the Liege Intranuclear Cascade (INCL++) using an average formation-time parameter $\tau_0$. The residual excited nuclear prefragment is de-excited using the ABLA++ statistical decay model, describing evaporation, Fermi breakup, and de-excitation gammas. Predictions are calibrated against E665 $\mu$--Pb forward neutron multiplicities and validated against E665 $\mu$--Xe charged-hadron data.

Key results

  • Constrained the formation-time parameter to $\tau_0 = 14\text{ fm}$ using E665 $\mu$--Pb forward neutron multiplicity data ($\langle N_n \rangle = 4.77 \pm 0.48$), higher than legacy BeAGLE ($\tau_0 = 10\text{ fm}$) due to richer FSI channels.
  • Satisfactorily reproduced inclusive DIS kinematic distributions ($W, Q^2, \nu, x_{\rm Bj}$) in 490 GeV $\mu$--Xe collisions.
  • Demonstrated improved descriptions of charged-hadron multiplicities in the target-fragmentation region ($y^* < 0$), with visible effects from light-cluster emission in backward rapidity.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts core interests in neutrino and electron event generator physics (such as Achilles, GENIE, and GiBUU), specifically regarding intranuclear cascade dynamics, formation-zone treatments, and nuclear remnant de-excitation gammas and nucleons.

Caveats

Nucleon Fermi motion is currently omitted from the primary DIS hard vertex in PYTHIA-8 (absorbing four-momentum mismatch into the remnant), coherent diffractive processes are not yet implemented, and deviations remain in the low-$Q^2$ transition regime.

Abstract

We present the Benchmark eA Generator for LEptoproduction (BeAGLE++), a next-generation, general-purpose Monte Carlo event generator for high-energy lepton-nucleus collisions and the successor to BeAGLE. The framework incorporates a formation-zone intranuclear cascade to describe hadronic final-state interactions (FSIs), followed by statistical de-excitation of the residual nucleus. Compared with the legacy implementation, the BeAGLE++ cascade introduces a discrete time-ordered treatment of hadronic interactions and light-cluster production during intranuclear transport. Comparisons with E665 measurements and legacy BeAGLE calculations show broad agreement for selected observables, while revealing differences in final states sensitive to FSIs. These differences provide opportunities to assess the model dependence of nuclear-breakup predictions and constrain the underlying cascade dynamics. With its modular architecture, BeAGLE++ provides a flexible simulation framework for the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC) and a platform for developing and testing new physics models against existing data and future EIC measurements.

10worth readingQuantum simulation of DIS

Real-time quantum simulation of hadron production in deep inelastic scattering

Xianghui Cao, Meijian Li, Yang Li, Wenyang Qian

hep-ph nucl-th quant-pharXiv:2610.25350

Meson production and nuclear modification in deep inelastic scattering are computed using real-time Trotterized unitary evolution of a $q\bar{q}$ dipole within a light-front QCD Hamiltonian formalism.

Novel idea

Provides a nonperturbative, real-time quantum simulation framework for in-medium vector meson ($J/\psi$) production in DIS, preserving quantum coherence and transverse dynamics beyond semiclassical cascade generators or factorized collinear fragmentation functions. It also explicitly demonstrates the mapping of light-front in-medium dipole scattering onto digital quantum circuits using direct fermionic qubit encoding.

Method

The light-front QCD Hamiltonian is discretized in a transverse momentum box ($N_\perp$ modes) in the $|q\bar{q}\rangle$ Fock sector, coupled to stochastic background gluon fields from the McLerran-Venugopalan model representing proton or gold targets. Unitary time evolution along light-front time $x^+$ is Trotterized, executed classically for $N_\perp=4$ via matrix exponentiation and emulated for $N_\perp=2$ on a digital quantum circuit with Qiskit. Observables such as $p_\perp$-broadening, dipole cross sections, and $J/\psi$ yields are extracted via state projections, alongside the reduced quark von Neumann entropy.

Key results

  • Verified that quark transverse momentum broadening $\langle p_\perp^2 \rangle$ grows linearly before saturating due to finite-lattice momentum boundary effects, matching classical and quantum circuit benchmarks.
  • Total and elastic dipole cross sections reproduce analytical eikonal MV calculations and approach the black-disk limit ($d\sigma_{\rm tot}/d^2b \to 2$) for Au targets.
  • Evaluated $J/\psi$ formation probabilities vs. photon virtuality $Q^2$, revealing a non-monotonic peak near $Q^2 \approx 10\text{ GeV}^2$ governed by light-front wavefunction overlap.
  • Tracked the von Neumann entanglement entropy, demonstrating saturation at $\log_2[N_c(2N_\perp)^2]$ as an indicator of medium-induced decoherence.

Why it matters to you

Directly connects to interests in quantum entanglement, spin/color correlations in scattering final states, and eikonal-versus-Hamiltonian treatments of in-medium hadronization.

Caveats

The calculation is limited to the leading $|q\bar{q}\rangle$ Fock space (neglecting dynamical gluon emission), operates strictly in the eikonal $P^+ \to \infty$ limit, and quantum circuit implementations were limited to small lattices ($N_\perp=2$, $N_c=2$).

Abstract

We develop a quantum simulation framework for meson production in deep inelastic scattering based on light-front QCD Hamiltonian formalism. A quark-antiquark dipole, prepared either as a single-momentum state or as the $q\bar q$ component of a virtual photon, is evolved in real time through the color field of a nuclear target via Trotterized unitary evolution. We consider both proton and gold nuclear targets, each characterized by their respective saturation scale. From the evolved state we extract the transverse momentum broadening, the total and elastic cross sections, and the $J/\psi$ production rate by projecting onto the $J/\psi$ light-front wavefunction. We then extract the nuclear modification factor $R_A$ at different photon virtuality $Q^2$ and analyze nuclear modification effect. Results of classical simulations show good agreement with analytical eikonal predictions across a broad range of parameters. For the single-momentum dipole, quantum simulation is demonstrated on a quantum circuit emulator with a smaller lattice size, and results are consistent with the classical benchmark. This work establishes a first-principles, amplitude-level pathway toward computing exclusive hadron production in deep inelastic scattering, directly relevant to the physics program of the Electron-Ion Collider.

10must-readWeak magnetism in neutron decay

First observation of weak magnetism in neutron decay

Miriam Camarasa-Domene, Martín González-Alonso, Martín Novoa-Brunet

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.26792

Combining neutron beta asymmetry measurements with the free neutron lifetime and superallowed $0^+\to 0^+$ decays lifts the $g_A$--$g_M$ degeneracy, yielding the first direct observation of weak magnetism at 5.1 sigma.

Novel idea

Weak magnetism ($g_M$) in free neutron decay has historically been unobservable because the beta asymmetry $\tilde{A}$ cannot disentangle $g_M$ from the axial-to-vector coupling ratio $\lambda = g_A/g_V$. The authors break this degeneracy without relying on nuclear mirror transitions by importing $\lambda$ determined from the neutron lifetime $\tau_n$ and superallowed $0^+ \to 0^+$ $\mathcal{F}t$ values, isolating $g_M$ directly from experimental asymmetry data.

Method

The ratio $\lambda$ is constrained using the relation $\tau_n(1+3\lambda^2) \propto \mathcal{F}t$, adopting the updated baseline $\mathcal{F}t(^{26m}\text{Al}) = 3072.71(191)\text{ s}$ and PDG bottle lifetime $\tau_n = 878.4(5)\text{ s}$. The effective asymmetry $\tilde{A}$ is re-evaluated by integrating first-order recoil and weak-magnetism corrections over the specific energy acceptance windows of individual spectrometers (such as PERKEO III). A global $\chi^2$ minimization is performed across neutron decay observables, supported by Asimov mock analyses evaluating spectral shape sensitivity.

Key results

  • Extracted the weak magnetism coupling $g_M = 4.47(87)$, in excellent agreement with the CVC prediction $g_M^{\rm CVC} = 4.71$ at $0.3\sigma$.
  • Excluded the absence of weak magnetism ($g_M = 0$) at the 5.1$\sigma$ level, achieving its first direct observation in the free nucleon system.
  • Demonstrated that the discovery significance remains robust between 5.0$\sigma$ and 6.1$\sigma$ across various dataset selections (e.g., modern-only asymmetries, single most precise experiments, or inclusion of beam lifetime data).
  • Showed that analyzing the full energy spectrum of the asymmetry reduces the uncertainty on $g_M$ by only 3--7% over the window-averaged asymmetry $\tilde{A}$.

Why it matters to you

Weak magnetism is an essential vector form factor correction in quasielastic neutrino-nucleon and neutrino-nucleus cross sections, driving significant cross-section differences between neutrinos and antineutrinos; this provides the cleanest empirical confirmation of CVC in the nucleon weak sector.

Caveats

Relies on the theoretical control of nuclear-structure and radiative corrections ($\delta_C, \delta_{\rm NS}$) in superallowed Fermi decays, and assumes the absence of non-standard Fierz interference terms.

Abstract

Weak magnetism, introduced by Gell-Mann as a hallmark consequence of the conserved vector current (CVC) hypothesis, has remained unobserved in the decay of the free neutron, the simplest $\beta$-decay system. The reason is that the relevant observable, the $\beta$ asymmetry, cannot separate the weak-magnetism form factor $g_M$ from the axial-to-vector ratio $\lambda$. We show that this degeneracy is lifted by combining the $\beta$ asymmetry measurements with the neutron lifetime and the $\mathcal{F}t$ value of superallowed $0^+\to0^+$ decays. Current data yield $g_M = 4.47(87)$, in agreement with the CVC prediction of $4.71$ and excluding the absence of weak magnetism at the $5.1\sigma$ level. Importantly, the significance remains between $5.0\sigma$ and $6.1\sigma$ for all considered variations of the neutron dataset, despite its well-known internal tensions. We further show that, under present experimental conditions, a full analysis of the energy dependence of the asymmetry would improve the $g_M$ extraction by only a few percent. These results constitute the first observation of weak magnetism in the decay of the free neutron nearly seven decades after Gell-Mann's prediction.

10worth readingTMDs in Lattice QCD

Recent Advances in Transverse-Momentum--Dependent Distributions in Lattice QCD

Huey-Wen Lin, Wei Wang, Yong Zhao

hep-lat hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2610.24785 · cross-list

This review synthesizes recent progress in extracting transverse-momentum-dependent parton distributions, the Collins-Soper kernel, and soft functions from lattice QCD using Large-Momentum Effective Theory and Coulomb-gauge methods.

Novel idea

Documents the transition from exploratory Euclidean moment-ratio computations to systematic, full $x$- and $b_\perp$-dependent evaluations of 3D hadron structure via Large-Momentum Effective Theory (LaMET). It highlights recent breakthroughs including moving auxiliary-field formulations for the nonperturbative TMD soft function, kinematically enhanced boosted-hadron operators, and Coulomb-gauge quasi-distributions that circumvent linear Wilson-line divergences.

Method

The authors review the theoretical formulation of TMD factorization in the Collins scheme, including beam functions, rapidity divergences, and the Collins-Soper (CS) evolution kernel. They detail the construction of equal-time Euclidean quasi-TMDPDFs in boosted hadron states, nonperturbative renormalization schemes (RI/MOM, ratio schemes), and two-loop perturbative matching to lightcone distributions. Experimental and lattice results are surveyed across both unpolarized and spin-dependent distributions (Sivers, Boer-Mulders, worm-gear, transversity) as well as meson TMD wavefunctions.

Key results

  • Reviews nonperturbative lattice extractions of the Collins-Soper kernel $K(b_\perp, \mu)$ up to $b_\perp \approx 1\text{ fm}$, showing consistency across quasi-TMDPDF ratios, pion form-factor approaches, and auxiliary-field Wilson loops.
  • Summarizes progress on quasi-TMDPDF calculations in nucleons and pions at boosted hadron momenta up to $P^z \sim 2$--$3\text{ GeV}$.
  • Discusses the Coulomb-gauge formulation where the absence of linear divergences allows discrete Fourier transforms to commute with renormalization, eliminating long-distance coordinate extrapolation artifacts.

Why it matters to you

Provides an overview of the nonperturbative QCD and LaMET methods that govern modern lattice determinations of nucleon partonic structure, directly complementary to lattice efforts determining axial and vector form factors and collinear PDFs.

Caveats

As a review paper, it presents no original lattice data, and full control over the continuum limit, physical quark masses, and large-momentum extrapolations remains a significant challenge for 3D TMD observables.

Abstract

Unraveling the three-dimensional structure of hadrons is a central objective in modern nuclear and particle physics. Transverse Momentum Dependent parton distributions (TMDs) provide crucial insights into the interplay between partonic transverse motion and spin, and are indispensable for describing a wide range of high-energy processes at facilities such as JLab, COMPASS, the LHC, and the upcoming Electron-Ion Collider. In recent years, significant progress has been made in accessing TMDs from lattice QCD using novel approaches. Methods based on ratios of Mellin moments, large-momentum effective theory (LaMET), and Coulomb-gauge techniques have enabled first-principles investigations of the Collins-Soper kernel, intrinsic soft functions, TMD wave functions, and TMDPDFs. Recent lattice results for both unpolarized and spin-dependent distributions, including the Boer-Mulders function, demonstrate the feasibility of extracting transverse-momentum--dependent information directly from QCD. This paper reviews recent advances in lattice-QCD studies of TMDs, outlines remaining challenges such as continuum and infinite-momentum extrapolations, and discusses future directions toward precision determinations of hadron structure in three dimensions.

10worth readingHyperonic direct-Urca form factors

Hyperonic Direct-Urca Neutrino Emission with Lattice-QCD-Informed Form Factors

Andreas Konstantinou

astro-ph.HE nucl-tharXiv:2610.25514 · cross-list

Incorporating momentum-dependent, lattice-QCD-informed baryonic transition form factors into relativistic hyperonic direct-Urca calculations modifies dense neutron-star neutrino emissivities by up to 80% and stellar luminosities by up to 60%.

Novel idea

Direct-Urca neutrino emission in dense hyperonic matter has historically relied on static $q^2=0$ flavor-SU(3) relations, omitting momentum transfer dependence and second-class currents. This work incorporates complete six-form-factor weak vertices informed by vacuum lattice QCD (including physical-point determinations for $\Lambda \to p$) evaluated at the kinetic momentum transfer sampled across Fermi surfaces in relativistic mean-field matter.

Method

The author derives the relativistic emissivity for general spin-1/2 baryon transitions using the FSU2H and FSU2R relativistic mean-field equations of state, retaining all six form factors and interference terms. Weak current inputs are constructed from continuum-limit lattice QCD for $n \to p$ and $\Lambda \to p$, and an SU(3)-hybrid scheme for remaining hyperonic channels, evaluating the form factors at the Fermi-surface kinetic momentum transfer $q_F^2$.

Key results

  • Nucleonic $n \to p$ direct Urca samples $q_F^2 \approx 0$ and shows negligible momentum-transfer sensitivity, whereas $\Lambda \to p$ and $\Xi^- \to \Lambda$ sample timelike transfers up to $q_F^2 \sim 0.1-0.15\text{ GeV}^2$.
  • At local densities $n_B \sim (3-4)n_0$, momentum-dependent form factors shift the total direct-Urca emissivity by up to $\sim 80\%$ relative to static SU(3).
  • For $\Lambda \to p$, the dominant shift originates from the momentum dependence and interference of $f_1, g_1, f_2$, and $g_3$, whereas second-class currents ($f_3, g_2$) produce subdominant effects.
  • The redshifted stellar neutrino luminosity increases by $10-15\%$ for low-mass stars and up to $\sim 60\%$ near $(1.9-2.0)M_\odot$.

Why it matters to you

Directly touches the reader's core interest in electroweak transition form factors, lattice-QCD inputs to baryonic weak currents, and the impact of second-class currents. The explicit six-form-factor tensor reduction provides valuable reference matrix elements for baryonic weak transitions.

Caveats

Vacuum lattice-QCD form factors are analytically continued into dense-matter kinematics without genuine in-medium vertex corrections or density-dependent form-factor modifications, and hyperon channels beyond $\Lambda \to p$ rely on hybrid nucleon modeling.

Abstract

We investigate the sensitivity of direct-Urca neutrino emission in hyperonic neutron-star matter to the structure and momentum dependence of baryonic transition form factors. Using the zero-temperature FSU2H relativistic mean-field equation of state, we derive the relativistic emissivity for general spin-$1/2$ baryon transitions with the complete six form-factor contribution. We compare conventional static flavor-SU(3) couplings with vacuum lattice-QCD-informed form factors, using direct lattice vacuum input for $n\rightarrow p$ and $\Lambda\rightarrow p$ and a hybrid construction for the remaining hyperonic channels. Evaluating the latter both at $q^2=0$ and at the kinetic momentum transfer sampled at the Fermi surfaces separates changes in the zero-momentum couplings from momentum-transfer effects. The sensitivity is strongly channel dependent. The nucleonic transition probes $q^2\simeq0$ and is only weakly affected, whereas hyperonic channels can sample substantially larger momentum transfers and receive correspondingly larger corrections. These modifications propagate to the integrated neutrino luminosity of stellar configurations. Our results estimate the possible scale of the sensitivity coming from momentum transfer dependence, absent from static-coupling treatments and motivate future first-principles studies of baryonic weak currents in dense matter.

$\Delta(1232)$ isobars and weak-interaction bulk viscosity in neutron-star merger matter

Bharat Kumar

nucl-th gr-qcarXiv:2610.25224

Evaluating weak-interaction bulk viscosity in $\Delta(1232)$-admixed merger matter reveals that $\Delta^-$ charge-buffering triggers nucleonic direct Urca, shifting bulk viscosity resonant peaks to lower temperatures and accelerating kHz oscillation damping.

Novel idea

Extends merger-matter bulk viscosity calculations beyond nucleonic and hyperonic compositions to include the full $\Delta(1232)$ isobar quartet in strong equilibrium, evaluating full phase-space relativistic $\Delta \leftrightarrow N$ and $\Delta \leftrightarrow \Delta'$ weak processes and in-medium $\Delta \to N\pi$ widths.

Method

Employs relativistic mean-field models (QMC-RMF3 and DDME2) with four $\Delta$-coupling sets. Evaluates full five-dimensional phase-space Urca rates using the Nucleon Width Approximation with Rarita-Schwinger spin-summed matrix elements and Pauli-blocked in-medium $\Delta \to N\pi$ widths, computing bulk viscosity via coupled two-variable ($Y_e, Y_\mu$) linear response about finite-temperature equilibrium.

Key results

  • Onset of $\Delta^-$ at $(2-3)n_0$ replaces leptonic negative charge, doubling the proton fraction and kinematically opening nucleonic direct Urca in models where it was previously forbidden.
  • Below $T \approx 3\text{ MeV}$, flavor relaxation is governed by the newly opened nucleonic direct Urca rather than direct $\Delta$ weak transitions.
  • The resonant peak of the bulk viscosity moves from $4-6\text{ MeV}$ in nucleonic matter down to $2-3.7\text{ MeV}$ in $\Delta$-admixed matter.
  • Damping times for kHz density oscillations near resonance are reduced by several times relative to pure nucleonic matter, and populated $\Delta$ states are fully Pauli-blocked against strong decay.

Why it matters to you

Connects to the reader's interest in $\Delta(1232)$ weak-interaction vertices, Rarita-Schwinger hadronic tensors, and in-medium isobar widths, although the primary application is neutron-star merger hydrodynamics.

Caveats

Assumes neutrino transparency (valid only up to $T \lesssim 10\text{ MeV}$), sets degenerate $\Delta$ collisional widths equal to nucleon widths, and remains within the linear-response approximation.

Abstract

Binary neutron star mergers probe nuclear matter at densities of a few times nuclear saturation density and temperatures of tens of MeV, conditions under which heavier baryons such as the $\Delta(1232)$ isobars may appear. Previous calculations of the bulk viscosity of merger matter have considered nucleonic matter, with and without muons, and hyperonic matter, but not $\Delta$ isobars. In this work, we extend these calculations to neutrino-transparent matter in which $\Delta$ isobars are present in strong equilibrium. We derive the relativistic matrix elements for the charged-current $\Delta\to N$ and $\Delta\to\Delta'$ transitions, evaluate all electronic and muonic Urca processes over the full phase space using the nucleon width approximation, and include the in-medium $\Delta\to N\pi$ width with Pauli blocking. The bulk viscosity is obtained from the coupled linear response of the electron and muon fractions about the finite-temperature flavor equilibrium. We find that the $\Delta^-$ takes over the negative charge from the leptons, which raises the proton fraction and opens the nucleonic direct Urca process. Up to about 3~MeV this change in composition, rather than the $\Delta$ reactions themselves, controls flavor equilibration. As a consequence, the resonant maximum of the bulk viscosity moves to lower temperatures for all oscillation frequencies we consider, and kHz density oscillations near resonance are damped several times faster than in nucleonic matter. We also show that, for nucleonic matter, the choice of the equilibrium state about which the rates are linearized shifts the resonance temperature by up to a few MeV at high density.

This work leverages advances from highly scaled mm-wave, x-ray, and gamma-ray TES systems, adapting them to the unique needs of low-threshold rare event measurements. We quantify the basic readout functionality and noise level in our dilution refrigerator testbed and demonstrate operation of a variety of TES devices with saturation powers significantly lower than mm-wave and x-ray devices. We demonstrate proof-of-concept multiplexed readout of background event pulses, bound the readout's contribution to the parasitic TES background load to <1 fW, and discuss a cryogenic inline RF filtering scheme. These results demonstrate practical solutions to several of the key challenges that must be solved before a next-generation athermal phonon detector at scale is realized.
