New Physics in Ultra-High-Energy Neutrino Flavor

Samiur R. Mir, Carlos A. Argüelles, Vedran Brdar

hep-ph astro-ph.HE hep-exarXiv:2611.14624

Combining projected ultra-high-energy in-ice radio and Earth-skimming tau-neutrino flavor measurements provides world-leading constraints on Lorentz invariance violation operators of dimension $d \ge 4$.

Novel idea

This work presents the first study of physics beyond the Standard Model using projected ultra-high-energy (UHE) neutrino flavor composition measurements between $100\text{ PeV}$ and $10\text{ EeV}$. By combining in-ice radio detectors with Earth-skimming tau-neutrino experiments, the authors evaluate the full three-flavor triangle reach for Lorentz invariance violation (LIV). This leverages the strong $E^{d-3}$ energy scaling of higher-dimensional LIV operators to access previously unexplored parameter space.

Method

The authors parameterize isotropic LIV using an effective Hamiltonian with dimension $d=3\text{--}8$ operators. Cosmogenic neutrino fluxes and production redshift distributions are simulated using SimProp-v2r4 across three benchmark scenarios (optimistic, moderate, pessimistic). They then apply energy-averaged three-flavor transition probabilities to projected 68% and 95% CL sensitivity contours combining in-ice radio detectors (e.g., IceCube-Gen2, RNO-G) and Earth-skimming air-shower experiments (e.g., GRAND, POEMMA, TAMBO).

Key results

  • UHE flavor measurements improve existing bounds on LIV operators of dimension $d \ge 4$ by up to 20 orders of magnitude, reaching sensitivities down to $\sim 10^{-72}\text{ GeV}^{-4}$ for $d=8$.
  • Combining all three flavor channels enhances LIV sensitivity by up to an order of magnitude compared to using Earth-skimming tau-neutrino detectors alone.
  • Sensitivity to $e\tau$ and $e\mu$ LIV coefficients benefits most significantly from combining radio in-ice cascade/track channels with tau air showers.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to neutrino phenomenology and high-energy BSM probes, connecting to flavor composition analysis, cosmological neutrino propagation, and recent work by favorite author Vedran Brdar.

Caveats

Results depend on projected array configurations (such as GRAND antenna counts) and theoretical cosmogenic flux models, while assuming single-operator dominance and isotropic LIV couplings.

Abstract

Neutrino flavor measurements test physics beyond the Standard Model that modifies neutrino production, propagation, or detection. IceCube has measured the flavor composition of the astrophysical neutrino flux up to PeV energies. Next-generation experiments will extend flavor measurements to the ultra-high-energy (UHE) regime, $100\,\mathrm{PeV}$-$10\,\mathrm{EeV}$, by combining in-ice radio detection with Earth-skimming tau neutrinos. We present the first study of the new-physics reach of these projected UHE flavor measurements. We take Lorentz invariance violation (LIV) as a benchmark, since its effects grow as a power of the neutrino energy. We find that UHE flavor ratios will be the most sensitive probe of LIV for operators of dimension four and higher, improving on current bounds from all sectors by up to twenty orders of magnitude. Three-flavor observables improve this sensitivity by up to an order of magnitude over Earth-skimming tau neutrinos alone.

Relativistic Second Random-Phase Approximation: Formalism and Applications to Giant Resonances

Deni Vale, Gaurav Saxena, Nils Paar

nucl-tharXiv:2611.14707

A self-consistent Relativistic Second RPA framework incorporating $2p\text{--}2h$ configurations and a subtraction method successfully describes giant resonance fragmentation and spreading widths in $^{16}\mathrm{O}$.

Novel idea

The paper introduces a fully self-consistent Relativistic Second Random-Phase Approximation (RSRPA) based on covariant energy density functionals with density-dependent point-coupling interactions. It systematically incorporates $2p\text{--}2h$ configurations alongside Dirac antiparticle-hole ($1\alpha\text{--}1h$) pairs via an extended Thouless theorem. Furthermore, it implements the subtraction method in a relativistic framework to remove static correlations, preventing double counting and matrix instabilities.

Method

The linearized equations of motion are derived using a time-dependent Dirac-Frenkel variational principle with the DD-PCX point-coupling functional. Calculations are performed for isoscalar and isovector monopole and quadrupole giant resonances (ISGMR, IVGMR, ISGQR, IVGQR) in $^{16}\mathrm{O}$ using spherical harmonic oscillator bases. Energy cutoffs for $2p\text{--}2h$ configurations up to $240\text{ MeV}$ are varied to evaluate numerical convergence.

Key results

  • Incorporating $2p\text{--}2h$ configurations generates substantial strength fragmentation and spreading widths, systematically lowering resonance centroid energies toward experimental data.
  • The subtracted extension (RSSRPA) eliminates unphysical complex eigenvalues and secures rapid convergence of strength distributions for $2p\text{--}2h$ cutoffs above $150\text{ MeV}$.
  • The diagonal approximation for the $2p\text{--}2h$ submatrix ($A_{22}$) works well for monopole modes but exhibits noticeable discrepancies in quadrupole channels.

Why it matters to you

Directly touches core research on nuclear electroweak responses, nuclear structure, and microscopic $2p\text{--}2h$ correlation modeling essential for neutrino-nucleus interaction calculations.

Caveats

The current implementation is restricted to spherical closed-shell nuclei and omits correlations in the Hartree reference ground state as well as $2\alpha\text{--}2h$ Dirac sea pairs.

Abstract

We develop a self-consistent relativistic second random-phase approximation (RSRPA) based on covariant energy density functional with density dependent point-coupling interaction. The formalism extends the conventional simple configuration space composed of particle-hole ($p$-$h$) transitions by including two-particle-two-hole ($2p$-$2h$) configurations through a generalized Thouless ansatz and a time-dependent variational principle. To ensure consistency with the underlying mean-field ground state and avoid the double counting of correlations, we implement the subtraction method, in which the static contribution is removed from the residual interaction. The first applications of the RSRPA include studies of isoscalar and isovector giant monopole (ISGMR and IVGMR) and quadrupole resonances (ISGQR and IVGQR) in $^{16}$O, which demonstrate the importance of $2p$-$2h$ couplings to describe the fragmentation of the transition strength and the stabilizing role of the subtraction method.

Tau polarization, new physics and CP violation in $\nu_\tau$-nucleus hyperon production

E. Hernández, J. Nieves, J. E. Sobczyk

hep-ph nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2611.15252

Nuclear impulse approximation calculations show that tau polarization observables in $\nu_\tau$-induced hyperon production on nuclei provide a zero-background, CP-odd probe of new physics.

Novel idea

This work establishes the nuclear impulse approximation framework for sequential CC $\nu_\tau$/$\bar{\nu}_\tau$ hyperon/charmed-baryon production ($\nu_\tau n \to \tau^- \Lambda_c^+$ and $\bar{\nu}_\tau p \to \tau^+ \Lambda$) and subsequent $\tau \to \pi \nu_\tau$ decay on nuclear targets ($^{16}\mathrm{O}$). It demonstrates that an event-by-event pion azimuthal asymmetry with respect to the reaction plane survives nuclear Fermi motion smearing as a clean, CP-odd observable that vanishes identically in the Standard Model.

Method

The authors integrate the sequential two-body production and decay phase space using an arbitrary nuclear hole spectral function evaluated in a Local Fermi Gas (LFG) model. The calculation incorporates the full dimension-6 NP effective Hamiltonian and lattice QCD / phenomenological $n\to\Lambda_c$ and $p\to\Lambda$ transition form factors. They extract averaged tau lepton polarization vector components (${\cal P}_L, {\cal P}_T, {\cal P}_{TT}$) and the resulting decay pion angular and energy distributions.

Key results

  • Nuclear binding and Fermi motion lower reaction energy thresholds from $8.3\text{ GeV}$ to $6.3\text{ GeV}$ for $\Lambda_c^+$ and from $4.0\text{ GeV}$ to $3.0\text{ GeV}$ for $\Lambda$ production.
  • Per-participant-nucleon pion differential distributions ($d\sigma/dE_\pi$ and $d\sigma/d\cos\theta_\pi$) retain shapes close to free-nucleon predictions.
  • The CP-odd pion azimuthal asymmetry $\Delta\sigma$ remains strictly zero in the SM, reaching percent or sub-percent levels in representative CP-violating NP scenarios.

Why it matters to you

A must-read paper authored by favorite authors J. Nieves and J. E. Sobczyk, directly addressing $\nu_\tau$-nucleus scattering, tau polarization, nuclear Fermi gas models, and CP-violating EFT probes relevant to DUNE, SHiP, and FPF.

Caveats

Transition form factors require analytic continuation to large spacelike $q^2$ introducing large uncertainties, and final-state rescattering (FSI) of the produced hyperon is omitted.

Abstract

Charged-current interactions of the $\tau$ neutrino are the least constrained sector of the Standard Model (SM) flavour structure, and the polarisation of the produced $\tau$ lepton probes new-physics (NP) couplings, including CP-violating ones. Hyperon and charmed-baryon production gives access to the $u\to s$ and $d\to c$ transitions, but any measurement would be made on nuclei, which calls for a proper treatment of nuclear effects. We therefore study the sequential reactions $\nu_\tau\,{}^A Z\to\tau^-(\to\pi^-\nu_\tau)\,\Lambda_c^+ X$ and $\bar\nu_\tau\,{}^A Z\to\tau^+ (\to\pi^+\bar\nu_\tau)\,\Lambda X$ within the impulse approximation. The energy and angular distributions of the pion, which encode the $\tau$ polarization, are derived for an arbitrary nuclear hole spectral function. As for free nucleons, an azimuthal pion asymmetry with respect to the plane spanned by the neutrino and the outgoing baryon is shown to remain. This is a CP-odd observable that vanishes in the SM, so that a nonzero value would signal CP violation irrespective of the hadronic form factors. Local Fermi gas results for ${}^{16}$O show that nuclear effects lower the reaction thresholds but leave the pion distributions, and hence the polarization information, close to their free-nucleon form. For representative NP scenarios the predicted asymmetries are at the percent level or below, with a magnitude that, in the $\Lambda$ channel, depends strongly on the transition form factors.

Deeply inelastic scattering as an inverse problem

Hsiang-nan Li

hep-pharXiv:2611.15665

Deeply inelastic scattering structure functions are calculated nonperturbatively from heavy-baryon Compton amplitude dispersion relations solved as an inverse matrix problem without requiring light-cone factorization or PDFs.

Novel idea

The paper introduces a novel framework for high-energy inclusive QCD processes that replaces light-cone factorization with dispersion relations for double deeply virtual Compton scattering (DDVCS) evaluated on a complex baryon mass plane. By framing structure function extraction as an inverse problem with OPE inputs in the heavy-quark regime, nonperturbative structure functions are computed directly without introducing parton distribution functions (PDFs).

Method

Short-distance OPE inputs for heavy-baryon DIS are derived up to NLO in $\alpha_s$ at leading power in $1/Q$. Physical prior constraints are built into the formulation, including photon virtuality scaling, positivity, and threshold quark-counting laws $(1-x_B)^3$. The resulting ill-posed integral equation is solved numerically using the inverse matrix method expanded in generalized Laguerre polynomials.

Key results

  • Using only the proton mass $m_p$ as a nonperturbative parameter, the method accurately reproduces measured proton structure functions $F_2$ across $x_B = 0.18\text{--}0.65$ and $Q^2 = 10^2\text{--}10^4\text{ GeV}^2$.
  • Yields $F_L \approx 0.1$ at intermediate $x_B$, confirming the Callan-Gross relation $F_L \approx 0$ in the high-$Q^2$ Bjorken limit in agreement with HERA data.
  • Successfully describes the pion structure function $F_2^\pi$ at $x_\pi = 0.12$ using the pion mass $m_\pi$.

Why it matters to you

Relevant to your interests in DIS cross sections, QCD short-distance factorization, hadron structure, and inverse problem methods for extracting nonperturbative hadronic functions.

Caveats

Applicable primarily at intermediate to large $x_B$ where valence quarks dominate; soft-parton contamination breaks the dispersion relation at small $x_B$, and $1/Q^2$ power corrections are currently neglected.

Abstract

A novel formalism for high-energy inclusive QCD processes based on analyticity of a scattering amplitude is proposed, which is conceptually distinct from the conventional factorization theorem. We illustrate how to analyze proton deeply inelastic scattering by means of the dispersion relations for double deeply virtual Compton scattering defined on a complex baryon mass plane. Inputting the perturbative results for the heavy baryon scattering amplitudes, we solve for the proton structure functions $F_2$ and $F_L$ in the nonperturbative low-mass region directly from the dispersion relations, i.e., as an inverse problem. The prior information, including the scaling of the structure functions in photon virtuality $Q^2$ and the quark counting rules for the threshold region with the Bjorken variable $x_B\to 1$, is incorporated. The only nonperturbative parameter at leading-power precision in $1/Q$ is the proton mass, with which we demonstrate the agreement between our predictions and the data for $F_2$ and $F_L$ at intermediate $x_B=0.18$-0.65 in a wide range of $Q^2=10^2$-$10^4$ GeV$^2$. Parton distribution functions can be extracted from the obtained structure functions as a byproduct of the analysis by treating the associated factorization formulas as another inverse problem. The same formalism with the pion mass as the nonperturbative parameter explains the data for the pion structure function $F_2^\pi$.

Relating Short Range Correlated Pair Abundances to Electron-Scattering Cross Section Ratios

Ilay Wischnevsky Shlush, Igor Korover, Eli Piasetzky

nucl-th nucl-exarXiv:2611.16489

Factorized quasielastic electron scattering off short-range correlated pairs yields a coupled system of cross-section ratios where single-knockout channels are interdependent and constrain microscopic pair abundances.

Novel idea

The authors derive a coupled system of algebraic equations connecting inclusive $(e,e')$, semi-exclusive single-nucleon knockout $(e,e'p), (e,e'n)$, and double-coincidence $(e,e'pp), (e,e'pn)$ cross-section ratios in short-range correlation (SRC) kinematics to microscopic $np$, $pp$, and $nn$ pair populations. They prove that for an isospin-symmetric reference nucleus, the three single-knockout/inclusive ratios are mathematically dependent, establishing a direct experimental closure test of SRC factorization.

Method

The derivation uses a factorized description of quasielastic scattering from 2N-SRC pairs in SRC kinematics ($x_B \ge 1.2$, $Q^2 \ge 1.5\text{ (GeV/c)}^2$, $p_{\text{miss}} \in [375, 700]\text{ MeV/c}$). Final-state interactions are incorporated through nuclear transparency and single charge exchange (SCX) probabilities. Algebraic elimination yields predictions for unmeasured channels and links single/double ratios to relative $np$, $pp$, and $nn$ abundances.

Key results

  • Establishes a consistency relation showing any two single ratios predict the third; measured $^{48}\mathrm{Ca}/^{40}\mathrm{Ca}$ inclusive $(e,e')$ and $(e,e'p)$ ratios predict $R_{48/40}(e,e'n) = 1.35 \pm 0.07\text{ (exp)} \pm 0.15\text{ (model)}$.
  • Demonstrates that measuring same-target proton-to-neutron ratios $R_{p/n}^A$ directly constrains SCX probabilities independently of SRC pair populations.
  • Proves that combining two target-to-target single ratios, two double-coincidence ratios, and one same-target ratio uniquely extracts the relative $np$, $pp$, and $nn$ pair populations between any two nuclei.

Why it matters to you

Touches core research topics in electron scattering constraints for neutrino interactions, nuclear $2p\text{--}2h$ modeling, short-range correlations, and the isospin composition of nuclear ground states.

Caveats

Assumes strict two-nucleon SRC dominance (neglecting 3N-SRCs and mean-field contamination), relies on transparency corrections, and truncates charge exchange at first order.

Abstract

We derive a coupled set of relations between inclusive and semi-exclusive nucleon-knockout electron-scattering cross-section ratios measured for any two nuclei in short-range-correlation (SRC) kinematics and the microscopic neutron-proton, (np-), proton-proton (pp-), and neutron-neutron (nn-) SRC pair populations in those nuclei. The derivation uses a factorized description of quasielastic scattering from SRC pairs and includes final-state interactions through nuclear transparency and single charge exchange. We show that the inclusive $(e,e')$, proton-knockout $(e,e'p)$, and neutron-knockout $(e,e'n)$ ratios are not independent: for any two nuclei where one is isospin symmetric ($N=Z$), two of these three observables determine the third within the model. For $^\mathrm{48}\mathrm{Ca}$ and $^\mathrm{40}\mathrm{Ca}$, for example, the measured inclusive $(e,e')$ and proton-tagged $(e,e'p)$ ratios predict $^\mathrm{48}\mathrm{Ca}/^\mathrm{40}\mathrm{Ca}(e,e'n)=1.35\pm0.07\,(\mathrm{exp})\pm0.15\,(\mathrm{model})$. A measurement of the $(e,e'n)$ ratio therefore provides a direct consistency test of the factorized pair-counting description. It also constrains the single-charge-exchange probabilities. Combining all three ratios with the two double-coincidence ratios determines the relative np-, pp-, and nn- SRC pair populations of any two nuclei. If one nucleus is isospin symmetric, any two of the three ratios suffice for the same result.

Development and Experimental Characterization of a Compact $^{228}$Th--D$_2$O Photoneutron Source for Low-Energy Nuclear Recoil Calibration

Yueqiang Tian, Yuxin Cui, Xunan Guo, Zichao Guo, Pingquan Wang, Hui Zhang + 2 more

physics.ins-det hep-exarXiv:2611.16415 · cross-list

This paper presents the development and characterization of a compact $^{228}\mathrm{Th}$--$\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}$ photoneutron source producing 0.17--0.22 MeV neutrons for low-energy nuclear recoil calibration in liquid xenon detectors.

Novel idea

The authors develop a long-lived photoneutron source using $^{228}\mathrm{Th}$ (half-life 1.91 years) to produce sub-MeV neutrons via $^{2}\mathrm{H}(\gamma,\mathrm{n})\mathrm{p}$ driven by 2.615 MeV $\gamma$ rays from $^{208}\mathrm{Tl}$. This provides a significantly longer operational lifetime than standard $^{88}\mathrm{Y}$--$\mathrm{Be}$ sources (106.6 days) while avoiding broad high-energy neutron continua from $(\alpha,\mathrm{n})$ or fission sources.

Method

Geant4 simulations using the LEND physics list were employed to optimize the $\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}$ vessel geometry and source placement (1 cm from the forward face). The forward neutron yield was measured with a 4-cm polyethylene-moderated $^{3}\mathrm{He}$ proportional counter, and the neutron energy spectrum was unfolded from Bonner sphere spectrometer measurements using MAXED. Calibration performance in the PandaX-20T LXe TPC was evaluated using the BambooMC framework.

Key results

  • Measured forward neutron emission rate of $1734 \pm 76\text{ (stat.)} \pm 65\text{ (sys.)}\text{ h}^{-1}$ for a 72 kBq $^{228}\mathrm{Th}$ source.
  • Unfolded neutron energy spectrum is concentrated between 0.17 and 0.22 MeV, matching two-body photodisintegration kinematics.
  • Monte Carlo simulations for PandaX-20T show ~1000 single-scatter nuclear recoil events ($E_{\mathrm{NR}} > 1\text{ keV}_{\mathrm{nr}}$) in 220 hours under a ~30 Hz electron-recoil background.

Why it matters to you

While focused on LXe dark matter detectors (PandaX), the technique is relevant to low-energy nuclear recoil calibrations necessary for CEvNS and supernova neutrino detection in large noble liquid detectors.

Caveats

The low photodisintegration cross section of deuterium (~1.38 mb) requires intense $\gamma$-ray emission, generating a ~30 Hz electron-recoil background in the detector that demands dedicated tungsten shielding.

Abstract

Calibrating the few-keV nuclear-recoil response of liquid xenon detectors calls for a low-energy neutron source. We have developed and characterized a $^{228}$Th--D$_2$O photoneutron source that converts the 2.615~MeV $\gamma$ rays from $^{208}$Tl into sub-MeV neutrons through the D($\gamma$,n)p reaction. After optimizing the D$_2$O geometry to balance neutron production against escape efficiency, we validated the chosen configuration experimentally. The forward emission rate is $1734 \pm 76~(\mathrm{stat.}) \pm 65~(\mathrm{sys.})~\mathrm{h}^{-1}$, and the spectrum unfolded from Bonner sphere data is concentrated at $0.17$--$0.22~\mathrm{MeV}$. In a 220~h exposure of PandaX-20T, the same configuration should provide about 1000 selected nuclear-recoil events. These results support the use of a long-lived $^{228}$Th--D$_2$O source for low-energy neutron calibration of large liquid xenon detectors.

9must-readLong-range neutrino forces

Earth-sized tests of long-range neutrino interactions

Dyer F. Cardenas, Mauricio Bustamante

hep-ph hep-ex hep-tharXiv:2611.14642

Using 8-year IceCube DeepCore data, this work sets limits on flavor-dependent long-range forces by evaluating the exact position-dependent Earth potential along neutrino paths rather than assuming a constant potential.

Novel idea

This work presents the first atmospheric neutrino oscillation fit that tracks the exact position-dependent long-range potential along each trajectory inside a 12-layer PREM Earth model, integrating over the atmospheric production height distribution. Previous bounds assumed a constant potential fixed at the detector location or a simple local-density scaling. Exact trajectory integration is shown to strengthen limits on the gauge coupling $g'$ by factors of 1.2 to 3.0 for force ranges shorter than the Earth radius.

Method

The authors calculated closed-form radial integrals for the matter potential of $14$ $U(1)'$ gauge symmetries across mediator masses $m' \in [10^{-15}, 10^{-9}]$ eV. Flavor evolution was computed via Magnus expansion over MCEq atmospheric fluxes (SIBYLL-2.3d, GSF cosmic-ray model). A frequentist fit was performed on 200 bins of the 8-year public IceCube DeepCore dataset, profiling over oscillation and detector/flux systematic parameters.

Key results

  • Derived 90% C.L. upper bounds on $g'$ for 8 flavor-dependent symmetries that differentiate $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_\tau$ potentials.
  • Limits on $g'\sqrt{|k|}$ for $L_\mu - L_\tau$ and $B - L_e - 2L_\mu$ are $\sim 1.45$ times stronger than for other flavor combinations due to maximal $\nu_\mu - \nu_\tau$ energy splitting.
  • Extracted limits outperform equivalence-principle tests (MICROSCOPE, Eöt-Wash) for mediator ranges exceeding 12 km ($m' < 10^{-11} - 10^{-10}$ eV).
  • No upper bounds are obtained for 6 symmetries that act on $\nu_e$ alone ($k>0$ for $B-3L_e$, $L-3L_e$, etc.) because strong potentials decouple $\nu_e$ without shifting the $\nu_\mu$ survival minimum.

Why it matters to you

Crucial for neutrino BSM phenomenology, non-standard interactions (NSI), and atmospheric oscillation systematics. The methodology provides a baseline for exact path-dependent potential evaluation applicable to DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande, and KM3NeT/ORCA.

Caveats

Limits rely on asymptotic Wilks-theorem thresholds ($\Delta\chi^2 = 1.64$), which display minor under-coverage (87% actual vs 90% nominal) in pseudo-experiments.

Abstract

Neutrinos may experience a new long-range force that distinguishes their flavors, mediated by an ultralight neutral boson of a new $U(1)'$ gauge symmetry and sourced by the electrons and nucleons of ordinary matter. Atmospheric neutrinos that cross the Earth would then feel a flavor-dependent potential that alters their oscillations. When the range of the force is about one Earth radius or less, this potential varies from point to point along each neutrino's path, since it comes mostly from the matter near the neutrino. Earlier limits approximated it, either as constant along the path or as following the local density of matter. We compute it exactly at every point of each path, for the Earth model of the IceCube analysis and over the distribution of heights at which the neutrinos are produced, and use it to analyze the 8-yr public IceCube DeepCore sample with the systematic uncertainties of that analysis. For mediator masses from $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-9}$ eV, where the Earth alone sources the potential, we set upper limits at 90% confidence level on the coupling of eight of 14 $U(1)'$ symmetries, those whose potential differs between $\nu_\mu$ and $\nu_\tau$. A potential held at its value at the detector would weaken our limits by factors of about 1-3. Below about $10^{-11}$-$10^{-10}$ eV, depending on the symmetry, our limits are stronger than those from tests of the equivalence principle.

A unified mean-field description of dense hadron matter constrained from $\chi$EFT and lattice QCD

Md. Emanuel Hoque (Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics/HBNI), Arunava Mukherjee (Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics/HBNI), Sayantan Sharma (The Institute of Mathematical Sciences/HBNI)

hep-ph astro-ph.HE gr-qc hep-latarXiv:2611.15388

The authors construct a relativistic mean-field theory of dense hadronic matter up to 3 GeV resonances using a two-stage Bayesian framework constrained by chiral EFT and lattice QCD susceptibilities.

Novel idea

The paper establishes a unified relativistic mean-field theory (RMFT) applicable from zero-temperature neutron stars to finite-temperature lattice QCD regimes. It uses a two-stage Bayesian analysis: Stage 1 constrains ground-state nucleon couplings via chiral EFT ($\chi$EFT) pure neutron matter EOS using a Gaussian-process correlation kernel; Stage 2 uses this posterior as a prior to constrain hyperon and resonance couplings using continuum lattice QCD conserved-charge susceptibility ratios at $T=135$ MeV.

Method

An extended Walecka-type Lagrangian was formulated including $\sigma, \omega, \rho, \sigma^*, \phi$ mesons coupled to all baryons and resonances up to 3 GeV. Stage 1 constrained 6 nuclear empirical parameters using $\chi$EFT energy per neutron data ($n_B \in [0.08, 0.32]\text{ fm}^{-3}$). Stage 2 constrained excited state scaling factors against lattice QCD cumulants $\chi_4^B/\chi_2^B$ and $\chi_{31}^{BS}/\chi_{11}^{BS}$ expanded to $\mathcal{O}(\hat{\mu}_B^6)$. The resulting EOS was applied to cold $\beta$-equilibrated neutron star matter matched to a sound-speed parametrization above $3-4\,n_{\mathrm{sat}}$.

Key results

  • Inferred 90% credible intervals for strange-baryon vector couplings: $g_{\omega Y} = 3.72\ [2.22, 5.63]$ and $g_{\rho Y} = 4.41\ [2.53, 7.00]$, showing strong hyperonic vector repulsion.
  • Scalar coupling to strange baryons $g_{\sigma Y}$ is constrained to a 90% upper limit of 2.82.
  • Predicted neutron star properties for $1.4\,M_\odot$ stars are $R_{1.4} = 11.52^{+1.11}_{-1.22}\text{ km}$ and $\Lambda_{1.4} = 245^{+260}_{-140}$, consistent with NICER and GW170817 measurements.

Why it matters to you

Provides rigorous constraints on nuclear interactions and equations of state derived jointly from ab initio $\chi$EFT and lattice QCD, relevant for nuclear structure, CEvNS modeling, and electroweak responses in dense media.

Caveats

Assumes universal coupling scale factors across all hyperon states in the 3 GeV resonance tower, and relies on Taylor-series extrapolations of lattice susceptibilities up to $\hat{\mu}_B = 3.0$ where truncation errors reach ~15%.

Abstract

We construct a relativistic mean field theory (RMFT) of hadrons in an attempt to describe the confined phase of QCD across a wide range of temperatures and baryon densities. This includes the scalar and vector mesons interacting with baryons and resonances up to $3$ GeV, including those which are not yet discovered but predicted within the quark model and lattice QCD. The baryon--meson couplings are constrained through a two-stage Bayesian analysis. In the first stage, we constrain six different couplings in our theory using six nuclear empirical parameters inferred from the chiral effective field theory predictions of the energy per nucleon for pure neutron matter over baryon densities $n_B \in [0.08, 0.32]~\mathrm{fm}^{-3}$, employing a Gaussian-process likelihood to account for correlations in the input data. In the second stage, this posterior is set as the prior for constraining different baryon--meson couplings in our RMFT construction, using lattice QCD input. The resulting posterior provides substantially improved constraints for different baryon--meson couplings and non-linear meson self-interactions, which are consistent with the current astrophysical observations of neutron star masses, radii and tidal deformability parameters.

Probing the internal structure of the $\Omega\Omega$ dibaryon via its weak decays and lifetime

Qi-Fang Lü, Deng-Wang Tang, Yu-Bing Dong, Peng-Nian Shen, Jia-Jun Wu

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2611.16508

This study calculates multi-body weak decay widths and lifetimes of the $\Omega\Omega$ dibaryon, establishing distinct decay patterns and lifetimes that discriminate between compact hexaquark and molecular structures.

Novel idea

This work performs the first systematic calculation of 3-body and 4-body weak decay channels for the $J^P=0^+$ $\Omega\Omega$ dibaryon ($S=-6$) across a broad range of binding energies ($E_b = 1 - 120$ MeV). It incorporates both single- and double-weak vertex mechanisms and accounts for empirical isospin-breaking effects in $\Omega \rightarrow \Xi \pi$ decays.

Method

An effective interaction Lagrangian for $\mathcal{D}_{\Omega\Omega}\Omega\Omega$ was constructed using a Gaussian cutoff form factor ($\Lambda = 0.8 - 1.2$ GeV). The effective coupling constant $g_{\mathcal{D}_{\Omega\Omega}\Omega\Omega}$ was determined via the Weinberg-Salam compositeness condition. Partial decay widths for tree-level and loop-induced rescattering diagrams were calculated by integrating matrix elements over 3-body and 4-body phase space.

Key results

  • For weakly bound states ($E_b = 1$ MeV), total width is dominated by $\Omega\Lambda\bar{K}$ (lifetime $\tau = 0.51-1.62 \times 10^{-10}$ s, decay length $c\tau = 1.53-4.86$ cm); $\Omega\Xi\pi$ dominates at $E_b=20$ MeV.
  • For deeply bound states ($E_b = 100-120$ MeV), $\Omega\Lambda\bar{K}$ is phase-space forbidden; dominant modes are $\Omega\Xi\pi$ and loop-induced $\Xi\Xi\bar{K}$.
  • Double-weak mode $\Lambda\bar{K}\Lambda\bar{K}$ and 4-body mode $\Xi\bar{K}\Xi\pi$ are negligibly small across all binding energies.
  • Lifetime extends by an order of magnitude from $\sim 10^{-10}$ s (shallow molecular state) to $\sim 10^{-9}$ s (deeply bound compact hexaquark).

Why it matters to you

Focuses on exotic hyperon dibaryon spectroscopy and heavy-ion decay signatures, which lies outside the core neutrino and low-energy precision EFT/NSI profile.

Caveats
