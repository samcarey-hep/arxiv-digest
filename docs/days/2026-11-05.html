9skimQuantum computing nuclear

Quantum computing for high energy nuclear physics

João Barata

nucl-th hep-lat hep-ph quant-pharXiv:2611.05491

Reviews how quantum information science and quantum simulation methods can tackle real-time, out-of-equilibrium, and sign-problem bottlenecks in high-energy nuclear physics, focusing on collider and heavy-ion phenomena.

Novel idea

Rather than focusing primarily on formal lattice gauge theories or quantum hardware implementations, this review structures the quantum information science (QIS) toolkit specifically around open problems in high-energy nuclear collider phenomenology. It synthesizes recent progress applying tensor networks, Hamiltonian digitizations, and analog simulators to real-time jet evolution, non-perturbative lightcone PDFs, and out-of-equilibrium Quark-Gluon Plasma (QGP) dynamics.

Method

The author outlines the mapping of gauge theories to spin Hamiltonians (using the 1+1D Schwinger model as a paradigm) and compares classical tensor network techniques (DMRG, MPS) with digital and analog quantum simulation. The review evaluates end-to-end scattering algorithms based on the Jordan-Lee-Preskill framework, the ladder-operator approach for lightcone Wilson lines, and open-quantum-system master equations for parton propagation through dense media.

Key results

  • Details quantum circuit and open-quantum-system formulations of medium-induced jet decoherence and radiative energy loss in heavy-ion collisions.
  • Reviews the digital implementation of light-like Wilson lines in PDF correlators via discrete alternations of spatial and real-time evolution steps.
  • Surveys analog quantum simulation experiments with ultracold atomic Fermi gases that emulate the spatial inversion and elliptic flow characteristic of relativistic hydrodynamics.

Why it matters to you

While the discussion of real-time dynamics and fragmentation touches upon concepts relevant to parton showers and event generator development, the review is centered on heavy-ion collisions, QGP thermalization, and collider physics, making it peripheral to your primary neutrino-nucleus program.

Caveats

Current quantum hardware demonstrations remain confined to 1+1D toy models or analog systems without native gauge symmetries; scaling these algorithms to full 3+1D QCD at collider scales requires lattice sizes ($\\mathcal{O}(10^9-10^{11})$ sites) far beyond near-term capabilities.

Abstract

Quantum information science (QIS) offers a new set of conceptual and computational tools ideally suited to exploring the many-body, out-of-equilibrium, and real-time properties of strongly interacting quantum systems. These themes find a natural counterpart in high energy nuclear physics, whose central goal is to describe the properties of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), the theory of the strong interaction, under extreme conditions. Relevant examples range from the formation and internal structure of hadrons, to the collective and thermalization dynamics of strongly interacting QCD matter, and the properties of complex many-body scattering processes. Although tremendous progress has been made over the past decades in our understanding of QCD in the nuclear physics context, through perturbative methods, lattice QCD, and effective theory descriptions, phenomena that are intrinsically connected to real-time dynamics, highly entangled many-body evolution, or finite density regimes remain only partially accessible with existing approaches. In this article, we argue that QIS can help realize a new paradigm for addressing these open questions. With the aim of making the discussion accessible to both high energy nuclear physics and QIS audiences, we provide an overview of selected open problems in high energy nuclear physics, with emphasis on aspects motivated by collider phenomenology, and discuss how they can be approached using QIS tools. This connection is illustrated through representative examples drawn from the recent literature, making the case that QIS can become a driving force for discovery in nuclear physics in the coming years.

9worth readingProton GPD reconstruction

Proton generalized parton distributions from electromagnetic form factors: A theoretically constrained phenomenological reconstruction

A. Capelo-Astudillo, T. Aguilar, M. Conde-Correa, A. Duenas-Vidal, P. G. Ortega, J. Segovia

hep-ph hep-ex hep-lat nucl-exarXiv:2611.05635

Reconstructs proton unpolarized GPDs $H^q$ and $E^q$ from electromagnetic form factors using a constrained zero-skewness ansatz and double distributions, strictly enforcing positivity, polynomiality, and angular momentum sum rules.

Novel idea

The paper develops a phenomenological GPD reconstruction where the forward helicity-flip distribution $e_v^q(x)$ is decoupled in shape from the unpolarized valence PDF $q_v(x)$. This additional shape flexibility allows the simultaneous satisfaction of the low-scale angular momentum condition $B^{u+d}(0)=0$ and rigorous pointwise Gaussian impact-parameter positivity bounds without violating Dirac and Pauli form-factor normalizations.

Method

Longitudinal valence PDFs at a low hadronic matching scale are coupled with flavor-dependent exponential profile functions $f_q(x)$ and $g_q(x)$ calibrated simultaneously against proton and neutron Sachs form factor parametrizations ($0.01 \le Q^2 \le 4.0\text{ GeV}^2$). The resulting zero-skewness GPDs are extended to non-zero skewness $\xi$ via the Radyushkin double-distribution ansatz, supplemented by an ERBL-supported $D$-term expanded in Gegenbauer polynomials.

Key results

  • Saturates total quark angular momentum $J^{u+d}(\mu_0) \approx 0.5003$ and $A^{u+d}(0) \approx 1.0005$ at the low scale while enforcing $B^{u+d}(0)=0$.
  • Demonstrates that impact-parameter positivity strictly requires the profile ordering $0 < g_q(x) < f_q(x)$, revealing mild tensions with low-$Q^2$ Sachs slopes ($r_E^p = 0.819\text{ fm}$ and $\langle r_E^2 \rangle^n = -0.093\text{ fm}^2$).
  • Validates Mellin moment polynomiality numerically across $\xi \in [0.05, 0.35]$ at $t = -0.5\text{ GeV}^2$ with precision better than $2\times 10^{-10}$.

Why it matters to you

Provides a consistent framework for the flavor separation of nucleon vector form factors and their transverse spatial profiles, directly informing the vector current inputs and low-$Q^2$ modeling used in quasi-elastic and resonance lepton-nucleus scattering cross sections.

Caveats

The model operates in a valence-only approximation at a low hadronic scale without active sea quarks or gluons, and the mechanical properties derived from the $D$-term remain model-dependent assumptions independent of the electromagnetic calibration.

Abstract

We present a theoretically constrained phenomenological reconstruction of the unpolarized, chiral-even, leading-twist proton generalized parton distributions $H^q$ and $E^q$. At zero skewness, valence-quark distributions are combined with flavor-dependent exponential profiles calibrated simultaneously against phenomenological representations of the proton and neutron electromagnetic form factors. The construction imposes exact valence and anomalous-magnetic-moment normalizations, the low-scale condition $B^{u+d}(0)=0$, the required endpoint behavior, and the complete Gaussian impact-parameter positivity bound. The resulting parametrization provides a reasonable description of the electromagnetic form factors and their slopes while revealing the tension between strict GPD consistency and a precision reproduction of all low-$Q^2$ observables within the adopted ansatz. We extend the distributions to nonzero skewness using normalized double distributions and introduce an independent $D$-term to complete the relevant Mellin polynomials. We examine electromagnetic and energy--momentum-tensor form factors, impact-parameter distributions, positivity, polynomiality, and illustrative mechanical properties. The analysis distinguishes information constrained by electromagnetic data from additional assumptions governing skewness and the mechanical sector, thereby providing a transparent elastic baseline for future studies of nucleon-to-resonance transition GPDs.

Earthquake Forecast via Neutrino Tomography at the DUNE Baseline

Bipin Singh Koranga, Samrat Vashisht, Vivek Kumar Nautiyal

hep-pharXiv:2611.06755

Exact three-flavour oscillation calculations along the DUNE baseline show that earthquake-fault density anomalies cause small, $\delta_{\rm CP}$-independent appearance probability shifts, demonstrating that short-term seismic forecasting with DUNE is statistically impossible.

Novel idea

The paper adapts a reactor-antineutrino geophysics proposal to the 1300 km DUNE accelerator baseline using exact, non-perturbative three-flavor slab evolution. Importantly, it derives the necessary event statistics for detection, formally establishing that short-term earthquake prediction is experimentally unfeasible with accelerator neutrino beams.

Method

A seismic fault zone is modeled as an embedded slab of width $w = 20-100\text{ km}$ with an enhanced effective electron density $\eta \rho_0$ ($\\eta = 3$, $\rho_0 = 2.8\text{ g/cm}^3$). Full three-flavor neutrino propagation is solved numerically via exact Hamiltonian matrix exponentiation across three piecewise-constant crustal slabs, computing changes in $P_{\mu e}$ and $P_{\mu\mu}$ at $E_\nu = 2.5\text{ GeV}$.

Key results

  • Yields absolute appearance probability shifts $\Delta P_{\mu e} \approx +0.11\%$ to $+0.55\%$ for neutrinos and $-0.08\%$ to $-0.37\%$ for antineutrinos at $E_\nu = 2.5\text{ GeV}$ for $w = 20-100\text{ km}$.
  • Finds that the induced shift $\Delta P_{\mu e}$ varies by less than $0.08\%$ across $\delta_{\rm CP} \in [0.5\pi, 1.5\pi]$, demonstrating that time-differential measurements effectively eliminate the CP-phase degeneracy.
  • Calculates that a $3\sigma$ discovery requires $10^2-10^4$ appearance events within the anomaly timeframe, exceeding the sample size accessible during short precursor windows by orders of magnitude.

Why it matters to you

Illustrates exact layer-by-layer three-flavor matter effect evolution and sensitivity to crustal density variations along the DUNE chord, but the core geophysical application is marginal and demonstrated by the authors to be practically unviable.

Caveats

The geophysical assumption of localized electron density tripling ($\\eta = 3$) prior to seismic events is controversial and lacks empirical grounding; additionally, the Fermilab-SURF chord crosses a tectonically stable continental craton.

Abstract

We investigate the sensitivity of accelerator-based neutrino oscillations to localized electron-density enhancements associated with seismic fault zones along the Fermilab SURF baseline of the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE). Motivated by the reactor antineutrino proposal of Wang, Chen, and Li, we model the anomaly as a uniform-density slab embedded in the surrounding crust and calculate exact three flavour oscillation probabilities using layer by layer evolution. Our results show that localized density enhancements can produce measurable variations in muon-to-electron appearance probabilities for both neutrinos and antineutrinos, with potentially significant relative effects. The predicted variations exhibit weak dependence on the leptonic CP violating phase, suggesting that time-differential measurements may help reduce uncertainties associated with the unknown CP phase. However, the event statistics required for a statistically significant detection substantially exceed those expected during a short-duration precursor interval. Consequently, detecting sufficiently persistent density anomalies may be possible under favourable conditions, whereas short-term earthquake forecasting is not feasible with the nominal DUNE configuration. Our findings establish a theoretical framework for exploring localized Earth-density perturbations through long-baseline neutrino oscillations.

A search for sterile neutrinos at ultrahigh energies at the Pierre Auger Observatory

The Pierre Auger Collaboration, A. Abdul Halim, P. Abreu, M. Aglietta, M. Ahmed, I. Allekotte + 348 more

astro-ph.HE hep-pharXiv:2611.06284 · cross-list

The Pierre Auger Observatory sets 90% C.L. upper limits on ultrahigh-energy sterile neutrinos (10-100 MeV) mixing with $\nu_\tau$ via Earth-skimming and upward-going showers, constraining decaying superheavy dark matter lifetimes.

Novel idea

Exploits the suppressed interaction cross section of predominantly sterile neutrino mass eigenstates $N_1$ ($\\propto \sin^2\theta_N$) to probe Earth chords that are completely opaque to standard model neutrinos. Because sterile states can traverse dense rock before interacting near the exit surface, they provide enhanced sensitivity in upward-going and Earth-skimming air-shower channels.

Method

The collaboration performs a full Monte Carlo simulation incorporating $N_1$ propagation through the Earth using the PREM density profile, neutral- and charged-current deep-inelastic scattering, continuous $\tau$-lepton energy losses, and TAUOLA $\tau$ decays in the atmosphere. The resulting shower exposures are evaluated against 14-17 years of Pierre Auger Fluorescence Detector and Surface Detector data across Earth-skimming ($90^\circ-95^\circ$) and upward-going ($110^\circ-180^\circ$) geometries.

Key results

  • Sets a 90% C.L. upper limit on an isotropic diffuse flux of $E^2 \Phi_N \le 2\times 10^{-9} \sin^{-2}\theta_N\text{ GeV cm}^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}\text{ sr}^{-1}$ over $0.3-200\text{ EeV}$ for $\sin^2\theta_N \lesssim 10^{-1}$.
  • Constrains decaying superheavy dark matter (mass $M_\chi = 10^{13}\text{ GeV}$ decaying into $k$ sterile neutrino pairs) to lifetimes $\tau_\chi \gtrsim 10^{22}-10^{24}\text{ yr}$ for $m_N = 10\text{ MeV}$ and $\sin^2\theta_N \in [10^{-3}, 10^{-1}]$.
  • Demonstrates analytically that matter-induced MSW potential modifications inside the Earth remain negligible for $m_N \ge 1\text{ MeV}$ even at $E = 10^{21}\text{ eV}$, ensuring that vacuum mixing governs propagation.

Why it matters to you

Directly touches BSM neutrino phenomenology, heavy neutral leptons, $\nu_\tau$ detection physics, and tau transport/interaction modeling in matter, offering a useful benchmark for non-standard neutrino signatures in large-volume detectors.

Caveats

Cosmogenic sterile neutrino fluxes produced via standard active oscillations fall several orders of magnitude below current detector sensitivity; dark matter constraints are dependent on specific phenomenological multi-body effective operators.

Abstract

We investigate the sensitivity of the Pierre Auger Observatory to sterile neutrinos with masses in the MeV-to-100 MeV range. To this end, we consider a minimal $3+1$ framework in which a single sterile state mixes mainly with the $\nu_\tau$ flavor through an active--sterile mixing angle $\theta_N$ to produce a mass eigenstate $N_1$. At ultrahigh energies, $N_1$ neutrinos can traverse substantial chord lengths through the Earth and interact through their active component, producing $\tau$ leptons that may emerge near the surface and decay in the atmosphere, thereby generating detectable air showers. We compute the corresponding exposures with a detailed Monte Carlo simulation of neutrino propagation, interactions, $\tau$ energy losses and decay, and derive upper limits at $90\%$ confidence level on an isotropic diffuse flux of $N_1$ neutrinos. For an $E^{-2}$ spectrum, we obtain $E^2\Phi_N\leq 2\times10^{-9}\sin^{-2}\theta_N\,\mathrm{GeV\,cm^{-2}\,sr^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$ over $0.3$--$200$ EeV for $\sin^2\theta_N\lesssim10^{-1}$. We further consider $N_1$ neutrinos arising from the decay of a superheavy dark-matter particle. For a benchmark dark-matter mass of $10^{13}$ GeV, we derive constraints on the active--sterile mixing angle and dark-matter lifetime for sterile-neutrino masses of $10$ and $100$ MeV. These results illustrate the potential of the Observatory to probe long-lived weakly interacting particles.

Probing Heavier-Baryon Oscillations through Light-Baryon Mixing

Arnau Bas i Beneito, Svjetlana Fajfer, Alexey A. Petrov

hep-pharXiv:2611.05465

Standard Model weak transitions connect heavy-baryon ($\Xi_b^0, \Lambda_b^0, \Xi_c^0, \Omega_c^0$) oscillations to $n-\bar{n}$ and $\Lambda-\bar{\Lambda}$ mixing, yielding indirect bounds orders of magnitude stronger than direct collider limits.

Novel idea

The paper establishes that Standard Model $| Delta S|=1$ and $| Delta C|=1$ weak transitions unavoidably communicate $| Delta B|=2$ baryon-number-violating mass insertions from heavy neutral baryons to light baryons ($n$ and $\Lambda$). This links heavy-baryon oscillations to ultra-sensitive searches for $n-\bar{n}$ transitions and dinucleon decays, producing indirect constraints far beyond direct experimental reach.

Method

Off-diagonal baryon self-energies are evaluated by saturating the hadronic correlators with ground-state $J^P = 1/2^+$ intermediate baryons (such as $\Lambda_c^+, \Xi_c^+, \Xi^-, \Sigma^-$). The weak charged-current vertices are parametrized using modern semileptonic form factors from lattice QCD (converted from helicity to Dirac bases using $z$-expansions), light-cone sum rules, and quark models, with loop integrals regulated by a benchmark UV cutoff $\Lambda_{\rm cutoff} = 10\text{ GeV}$.

Key results

  • Derives an indirect bound on the mass-mixing parameter $|\delta m_{\Xi_b^0}| \lesssim 7.9\times 10^{-21}\text{ GeV}$, roughly three orders of magnitude more stringent than the direct LHCb limit ($2.3\times 10^{-18}\text{ GeV}$).
  • Derives an indirect bound $|\delta m_{\Xi_c^0}| \lesssim 4.6\times 10^{-19}\text{ GeV}$, approximately five orders of magnitude stronger than the direct Belle limit ($2.4\times 10^{-14}\text{ GeV}$).
  • Establishes indirect sensitivity benchmarks for previously unconstrained modes: $|\delta m_{\Lambda_b^0}| \lesssim 1.7\times 10^{-30}\text{ GeV}$ (from $n-\bar{n}$) and $|\delta m_{\Omega_c^0}| \lesssim 6.0\times 10^{-18}\text{ GeV}$ (from $\Lambda-\bar{\Lambda}$ via dinucleon decay).

Why it matters to you

Co-authored by Alexey Petrov (a favorite author), this work directly engages core interests in baryon-number violation, $n-\bar{n}$ oscillations, lattice QCD input, and the analytical use of $z$-expansion semileptonic form factors in electroweak loop processes.

Caveats

The results are order-of-magnitude estimates subject to significant hadronic uncertainties arising from intermediate-state truncation, form factor extrapolation into deep unphysical/spacelike loop regions, and sensitivity to the UV cutoff parameter.

Abstract

Motivated by the existing direct limits on $\Xi_b^0$ and $\Xi_c^0$ oscillations, we investigate how Standard Model weak interactions connect baryon-number-violating mixing in heavy neutral baryons to the much more strongly constrained neutron-antineutron and $\Lambda-\bar\Lambda$ mixing. We focus on $\Xi_b^0$, $\Lambda_b^0$, $\Xi_c^0$, and $\Omega_c^0$ oscillations and derive indirect constraints on their mass-mixing parameters from existing limits on $n-\bar n$ oscillations and on $\Lambda-\bar\Lambda$ mixing, the latter inferred from dinucleon-decay searches. The weak baryon-to-baryon transition amplitudes are estimated by saturating the relevant hadronic correlators with low-lying intermediate baryons. The required weak vertices are modelled using semileptonic form factors from lattice QCD, quark models, and QCD sum rules. Since these form factors must be continued outside their physical semileptonic domains and the resulting loop integrals depend on a UV cutoff, our results are order-of-magnitude estimates subject to sizeable hadronic uncertainties. For a benchmark cutoff of $10~{\rm GeV}$, the indirect bounds on the $\Xi_b^0$ and $\Xi_c^0$ mixing parameters are roughly three and five orders of magnitude stronger than the direct LHCb and Belle limits, indicating that light-baryon $|\Delta B|=2$ searches can probe heavy-baryon oscillations far beyond present direct sensitivities.

Goldstone theory of gluon saturation in high energy QCD: From color glass to color superfluid condensate

V. P. Nair, Raju Venugopalan

hep-th hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2611.05498

The Color Glass Condensate is reformulated as a color superfluid effective field theory by incorporating spontaneous global color symmetry breaking, yielding colored Goldstones that drive dynamical condensate depletion.

Novel idea

The authors identify that the saturated gluon state in Regge asymptotics spontaneously breaks global $SU(N_c)$ color symmetry down to its Cartan subgroup $U(1)^{N_c-1}$. This breaking requires an effective field theory containing colored Goldstone bosons with decay constant $f_G = \sqrt{N} Q_S$. Coupling these Goldstones to collinear gluons introduces a dynamical relaxation timescale $\tau_G \sim 1/(\alpha_S Q_S)$, causing the condensate to deplete and fundamentally modifying the static shockwave picture of the Color Glass Condensate.

Method

The EFT is constructed by coupling non-Abelian gauge fields to a Goldstone coset action on $G/H = SU(3)/[U(1)\times U(1)]$ alongside a coarse-grained Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau action that parameterizes classical color charges. The action is formulated in the condensate comoving frame and Lorentz-boosted to the lab frame, recovering the classical Wong action in the static limit. Classical equations of motion are derived for the coupled system of Goldstones, diagonal Cartan currents, and collinear gluon fields.

Key results

  • Identifies six colored Goldstone bosons for $N_c=3$ with effective decay constant $f_G = \sqrt{N} Q_S \sim Q_S/\sqrt{\alpha_S}$.
  • Demonstrates that collinear gluons acquire an effective mass $m \sim O(1/\sqrt{N})$ by eating Goldstones, realizing an Anderson-Higgs mechanism analogous to a charged superconductor with finite London penetration depth.
  • Derives a combined conservation law showing that collinear gluon emission directly depletes the color charges of the condensate on timescales $\tau \sim 1/(\alpha_S Q_S)$, distinct from BFKL/JIMWLK quantum rapidity evolution.

Why it matters to you

While primarily targeted at high-energy QCD saturation and heavy-ion collisions, the field-theoretic mechanism reveals the physical origin of sub-eikonal and non-static corrections that can inform high-energy forward lepton-nucleus scattering models.

Caveats

The formalism is developed strictly in Regge asymptotics ($x \ll 1$) with $\alpha_S \ll 1$ and currently omits sea-quark degrees of freedom and confinement effects near $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$.

Abstract

In the high energy Regge asymptotics of QCD, the rapid overpopulation of gluons leads to gluon saturation, characterized by nonperturbative semi-classical lumpy configurations of size $1/Q_S$, where $Q_S\gg \Lambda_{\rm QCD}$ is a dynamical semi-hard saturation scale. We discuss here a generalization of the Color Glass Condensate (CGC) framework describing this phenomenon that is obtained by carefully treating the breaking of global symmetries by the condensate. In particular, we show that the breaking of a global subgroup of color results in a Goldstone effective field theory (EFT) whose dynamics is characterized by a Goldstone decay constant $f_G = \sqrt{N} Q_S$, where $N=O(1/\alpha_S)$ is the phase space occupancy of the condensate, which is large for $\alpha_S(Q_S)\ll 1$. Further, the coupling of these colored Goldstones to collinear gluon modes leads to a depletion of the condensate, thereby modifying the static shockwave scattering framework of the CGC on timescales controlled by $f_G$. The CGC EFT on these longer time scales is a novel color superfluid. Low frequency modes with $\omega\leq \alpha_S Q_S$ in an external probe of the CGC are sensitive to superfluid dynamics, with interesting physical consequences for deeply inelastic scattering and ultrarelativistic nuclear collisions.

Indirect probes of heavy neutral leptons through the dipole portal at FCC-ee

Natascia Vignaroli

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2611.05501

Virtual loops of Majorana heavy neutral leptons with electroweak dipole interactions induce dimension-six and dimension-eight SMEFT thresholds, yielding constraints at FCC-ee competitive with HL-LHC direct searches.

Novel idea

The paper calculates the complete electroweak gauge-invariant matching at one loop generated by two dipole operator insertions of Majorana heavy neutral leptons (HNLs). By incorporating the full $SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y$ structure—crucially including multi-gauge contact interactions from $W_{\mu\nu}^I$—it maps dipole coefficients directly onto Warsaw basis operators ($Q_{HWB}$, $Q_{Hl}^{(1)}$, $Q_{Hl}^{(3)}$) and evaluates their indirect impact on precision $Z$-pole observables and radiative Higgs decays.

Method

The author implements the gauge-invariant dipole Lagrangian in FeynRules and calculates two-point gauge self-energies and three-point vertex corrections using FeynArts, FeynCalc, and Package-X. One-loop amplitudes are matched onto dimension-six and higher-derivative dimension-eight Green-basis SMEFT operators at the threshold $\mu \sim M_N$. A $\chi^2$ likelihood analysis using twelve $Z$-pole pseudo-observables at FCC-ee is performed across three SM theoretical uncertainty scenarios, profiling over independent ultraviolet Wilson coefficients.

Key results

  • Under dipole dominance with a matching scale $\Lambda_B = 2 M_N$, FCC-ee probes equal dipole coefficients $C_B = C_W$ down to $0.14\text{ TeV}^{-2}$ ($0.22\text{ TeV}^{-2}$) for $M_N = 1$ TeV under aggressive (conservative) SM theory errors.
  • The indirect sensitivity scales as $\sim 1/M_N$ due to the $M_N^2 C_X C_Y$ threshold enhancement, matching or exceeding $3\text{ ab}^{-1}$ HL-LHC same-sign dilepton direct reach for $M_N \ge 1$ TeV.
  • Demonstrates that the oblique part of the two-dipole self-energy matrix is strictly rank-one, obeying the leading-log relation $X_{\rm LL} = -[g'/g(1+d_W^2/d_B^2)] \widehat{S}_{\rm LL}$.
  • Radiative Higgs decays ($h \to \gamma\gamma, Z\gamma$) provide complementary constraints that tighten the dipole parameter space radius by $1\text{--}7\%$.

Why it matters to you

HNL dipole portals are actively searched for in neutrino experiments (e.g., DUNE ND upscattering and MiniBooNE explanations). This work provides the exact SMEFT matching dictionary and electroweak precision bounds needed to consistently connect low-energy dipole phenomenology to high-energy and precision electroweak constraints.

Caveats

The extracted limits rely on the dipole-dominance assumption; independent tree- or loop-level UV contributions to $Q_{Hl}^{(1,3)}$ and $Q_{HWB}$ can introduce blind directions that cancel the virtual dipole shifts.

Abstract

We study indirect probes of the gauge-invariant dipole portal of Majorana heavy neutral leptons (HNLs) at FCC-ee. Virtual HNLs modify electroweak gauge-boson propagation and lepton couplings through loops with two dipole interactions. We calculate the resulting finite matching corrections and their effects on $Z$-pole observables. For real, flavour-aligned couplings, the local response is specified by four combinations of coupling shifts. Independent local ultraviolet interactions can reproduce all four, making the dipole interpretation dependent on assumptions about those interactions. With improved Standard Model predictions, FCC-ee can probe equal, same-sign hypercharge and weak dipole coefficients as small as $0.14\,\mathrm{TeV}^{-2}$ at an HNL mass of 1 TeV. This projection assumes dipole dominance, with independent ultraviolet coefficients set to zero at twice the HNL mass. We quantify how additional ultraviolet contributions can weaken or strengthen the sensitivity. At 1 TeV, the projected reach is competitive with HL-LHC same-sign dilepton searches for Majorana HNLs, while larger masses allow smaller dipole coefficients to be probed within the effective theory's domain of validity. Radiative Higgs decays offer a modest further improvement. Quantum field theory treatment of the spin-flavor precession of Dirac neutrinos with diagonal magnetic moments

Maxim Dvornikov (IZMIRAN)

hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2611.06643

A quantum field theory treatment of Dirac neutrino spin-flavor precession in a transverse magnetic field using exact dressed propagators recovers the standard quantum mechanical oscillation probability in the ultrarelativistic limit.

Novel idea

The paper applies an $S$-matrix field-theoretic framework to Dirac neutrino spin-flavor precession with diagonal magnetic moments in an external transverse magnetic field, treating neutrinos as virtual particles and utilizing exact non-perturbative propagators that incorporate the neutrino magnetic moment interaction.

Method

The author calculates the macroscopic $S$-matrix amplitude for an electron-to-muon transition mediated by external-field-dressed massive Dirac neutrino propagators. Spatial integrals over intermediate neutrino momenta are computed using cylindrical coordinates and residue calculus around the positive-energy ultrarelativistic pole. The resulting transition probability is evaluated at macroscopic baseline $L$ and compared with the four-component Schrödinger evolution in quantum mechanics.

Key results

  • Derives the QFT transition probability $P(\nu_{e\mathrm{L}} \to \nu_{\mu\mathrm{L}}) = \sin^2(2\theta) \cos^2(\mu_1 B L) \cos^2(\mu_2 B L) \sin^2(\Delta m^2 L / 4E)$ for ultrarelativistic neutrinos.
  • Proves analytically that the leading $1/L^2$ macroscopic QFT transition probability coincides exactly with the quantum mechanical wave-function approach.
  • Identifies subleading corrections $\mathcal{I}_1(EL)$ that are suppressed when $E L \gg 1$ but could lead to short-baseline macroscopic quantum corrections.

Why it matters to you

Provides a formal consistency check between quantum field theory and quantum mechanical descriptions of neutrino spin-flavor precession, relevant to foundational aspects of neutrino propagation in external fields.

Caveats

Considers only diagonal magnetic moments (ruling out Majorana neutrinos and Dirac transition moments), assumes ultrarelativistic plane-wave external leptons, and neglects finite wave-packet effects.

Abstract

We apply the quantum field theory (QFT) approach to describe the spin-flavor precession of Dirac neutrinos. The magnetic moments matrix of these neutrinos is taken to be diagonal in the mass basis. Neutrino mass eigenstates are considered to be virtual particles in the QFT approach. The neutrino contribution to the matrix element of the overall process, which includes the emission, the propagation and the detection of neutrinos, is contained in the propagators of massive neutrinos. Using the recently derived propagator of a massive neutrino, which exactly accounts for the magnetic moment interaction, we derive the transition probability of the process $\nu_{e\mathrm{L}}\to\nu_{\mu\mathrm{L}}$ under the influence of a magnetic field. The result is compared with the prediction of the standard quantum mechanical (QM) approach. We briefly discuss the possibility to derive the QFT corrections to the QM expressions.

A Note On Lattice Definition of Gauge Theories With Chiral Fermions

Edward Witten

hep-lat hep-ph hep-tharXiv:2611.05051 · cross-list

Witten proposes a nonperturbative formulation of anomaly-free chiral gauge theories on the lattice by interpolating admissible lattice gauge fields to the continuum and regularizing fermions via 5D Atiyah-Patodi-Singer eta-invariants.

Novel idea

Extending recent work by Craig and Hsu, the paper resolves the fermion doubling problem for general anomaly-free chiral gauge theories (including the Standard Model) on arbitrary compact Euclidean spin manifolds. It does so by using continuum interpolation of admissible lattice gauge fields to evaluate the continuum chiral fermion path integral directly, expressing its phase via 5D anomaly inflow and the APS eta-invariant.

Method

The construction enforces Lüscher admissibility on plaquette holonomies ($|U_P - 1| < \epsilon$) and extends link variables to a smooth continuum connection by iteratively solving the Landau-gauge Dirichlet problem on lattice hypercubes. The chiral fermion partition function is defined as $|\det \mathcal{D}_W^+| \exp(-i\pi \eta_Y)$ on a bounding 5-manifold $Y$ (or generalized using cobordism invariants and topological theta-angles). The determinant modulus and the phase are evaluated via the spectra of second-order, Laplace-like elliptic operators ($\mathcal{D}_W^{+\dagger} \mathcal{D}_W^+$ and $\mathcal{D}_Y^2$) that are free of spectral doubling.

Key results

  • Provides a constructive lattice definition for any anomaly-free chiral fermion representation in any dimension, without relying on special gauge group properties.
  • Demonstrates that both $|\det \mathcal{D}_W^+|$ and $\eta_Y$ can be computed in principle without encountering species doubling by formulating them through self-adjoint Laplace-type operators.
