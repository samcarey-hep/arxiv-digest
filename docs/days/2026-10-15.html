10worth readingSolar Neutrinos and NSI

Direct Detection of Solar Neutrinos to Probe Tensions and Break Degeneracies in Long-Baseline Neutrino Experiments

Dorian W. P. Amaral, David Cerdeño, Andrew Cheek, Valeria Costa, Peter B. Denton, Patrick Foldenauer

hep-pharXiv:2610.18971

Solar neutrino CEvNS and electron scattering in dark matter direct detection experiments can break parameter degeneracies in long-baseline neutrino experiments and constrain non-standard neutrino interaction solutions to the NOvA-T2K tension.

Novel idea

The authors connect solar neutrino measurements at dark matter direct detection (DM-DD) facilities directly to long-baseline (LBL) accelerator anomalies. They show that combining nuclear recoils (CEvNS from $^8$B) and electron recoils (E$\nu$ES from $pp$) breaks diagonal parameter degeneracies (such as $\mathcal{E}_{ee} - \mathcal{E}_{\mu\mu}$) that even DUNE cannot resolve on its own.

Method

The authors compute differential event rates using generalized CEvNS and E$\nu$ES cross sections that preserve neutrino flavor density-matrix coherences from propagation to detection. They build a profile log-likelihood incorporating experimental and solar flux nuisance parameters using existing data from LZ, XENONnT, and PandaX-4T, alongside sensitivity projections for next-generation observatories (XLZD, PandaX-xT).

Key results

  • Current LZ and XENONnT nuclear recoil data already rule out portions of the complex NSI parameter space ($|\mathcal{E}_{e\tau}| \sim 0.2$, $\phi_{e\tau} \sim 3\pi/2$) preferred as a solution to the NOvA-T2K $\delta_{\text{CP}}$ tension.
  • A future multi-ton xenon detector (such as XLZD) with a $1\text{ keV}_{\text{nr}}$ threshold can probe almost the entire $1\sigma$ preferred NOvA-T2K tension region for $\mathcal{E}_{e\tau}$ and a significant fraction for $\mathcal{E}_{e\mu}$.
  • Profiling over absolute diagonal NSI parameters demonstrates that DM-DD solar scattering breaks the blind diagonal LBL degeneracy in $(|\mathcal{E}_{e\tau}|, \mathcal{E}_{ee} - \mathcal{E}_{\mu\mu})$.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to your work on BSM neutrino interactions, CEvNS phenomenology, and oscillation analysis systematics. It provides an external handle from low-energy scattering to break degeneracies in DUNE and Hyper-K matter-effect fits.

Caveats

Assumes a contact dimension-6 vector operator framework, requiring mediator masses above $\sim 10\text{ MeV}$ to remain consistent with BBN while avoiding momentum-transfer dependence in CEvNS; relies on standard solar model flux uncertainties (6-12%).

Abstract

Long-baseline (LBL) accelerator experiments are sensitive not only to the standard neutrino oscillation parameters but also to new physics. Currently, there exists a tension between NOvA and T2K data that can be explained by vector non-standard neutrino interactions (NSI) with complex couplings, which in turn introduce degeneracies in LBL measurements. We demonstrate that dark matter direct detection experiments can use their measurements of solar neutrinos to test the NSI resolution of this tension and to break these degeneracies. Using recent solar neutrino data from LZ, XENONnT, and PandaX-4T, we show that dark matter direct detection experiments already constrain part of the NSI solution of the NOvA-T2K tension. These experiments can also probe parts of the NSI parameter space that even DUNE will be blind to. Our analysis combines nuclear recoils from $^8$B neutrinos and electron recoils from $pp$ neutrinos. A future xenon experiment, such as XLZD or PandaX-xT, will be able to cover a significant fraction of the NSI solution of the NOvA-T2K tension.

10worth readingExcited-state effects in nucleon sigma terms

Excited-State Effects in Nucleon Sigma Terms from a Variational Analysis

Constantia Alexandrou, Giannis Koutsou, Yan Li, Srijit Paul, Ferenc Pittler

hep-lat hep-ph nucl-tharXiv:2610.20362 · cross-list

A variational GEVP analysis with multihadron $N\sigma$ interpolators and Laplace filtering suppresses excited-state contamination in lattice QCD extractions of nucleon scalar sigma-terms at physical pion masses.

Novel idea

The study demonstrates the implementation of an enlarged interpolator basis combining standard nucleon and multihadron $N\sigma$-meson operators at physical pion masses in $N_f=2+1+1$ twisted-mass lattice QCD. It pairs this variational (GEVP) setup with time-slice Laplace filtering to directly probe and control multihadron contamination in nucleon scalar matrix elements.

Method

Simulations use twisted-mass clover-improved fermions at maximal twist on three gauge ensembles, prominently the physical-point ensemble cB211.072.64 ($m_\pi \approx 139\text{ MeV}$, $a \approx 0.08\text{ fm}$). A $2\times 2$ GEVP is solved to construct an optimal GEVP-improved nucleon operator, and three-point correlation functions are analyzed using multi-state spectral fits and localized Laplace filtering.

Key results

  • For the light-quark sigma term on the physical ensemble, the analysis yields $\sigma_{\pi N} = 45.7(3.0)_{\text{stat}}(3.3)_{\text{syst}}\text{ MeV}$, favoring the lower range of existing lattice extractions over Roy-Steiner pionic-atom determinations ($55.9 \pm 3.5\text{ MeV}$).
  • GEVP improvement significantly flattens the source-sink and insertion time dependence of the strange sigma term three-point ratio, resulting in $\sigma_s = 42.1(6.5)_{\text{stat}}(2.3)_{\text{syst}}\text{ MeV}$.
  • The charm sigma term shows mild excited-state sensitivity, yielding $\sigma_c = 107.4(30.4)_{\text{stat}}(0.6)_{\text{syst}}\text{ MeV}$, consistent with the heavy-quark expansion.

Why it matters to you

Highly relevant to lattice QCD methodology for hadron structure. The techniques developed here to control low-lying multihadron excited states ($N\pi, N\pi\pi, N\sigma$) directly transfer to reducing systematics in nucleon axial and vector form factor calculations needed for neutrino-nucleus event generators.

Caveats

Despite GEVP improvement, Laplace filtering reveals residual excited-state contamination in $\sigma_{\pi N}$, indicating that a larger operator basis (including explicit $N\pi$ or $N\pi\pi$ interpolators) will be required to resolve the tension with phenomenological extractions.

Abstract

We investigate excited-state effects in nucleon sigma-terms in lattice QCD using twisted-mass clover-improved fermion ensembles. We study correlation functions with nucleon and multihadron nucleon-$\sigma$-meson interpolating operators. The analysis is performed using three gauge ensembles, including one ensemble simulated with two degenerate light, a strange, and a charm quark with quark masses tuned to approximately their physical value. We solve the generalized eigenvalue problem that arises from the matrix of correlators and show that the three-point function for the light sigma-term obtained using the optimal linear combination of interpolating operators has substantially reduced time dependence without loss of statistical precision. We apply Laplace filtering and investigate residual excited-state contributions in the extraction of $\sigma_{\pi N}$. Using the same optimal linear combination of correlators as for $\sigma_{\pi N}$ for the strange sigma-term we also observe suppressed excited state contributions over an extended range of source--sink time separations. For the charm sigma-term, we observe negligible time dependence within the large statistical uncertainties, and these improvements show no significant effect.

9skimNeutrinos in Rotating Matter

Coherent states of neutrinos in rotating matter

Alexander Breev, Maxim Dvornikov

hep-tharXiv:2610.19078

Generalized coherent states for massless Dirac neutrinos electroweakly interacting with rotating matter are constructed in light-cone variables, yielding an effective mass and a smooth transition to plane waves.

Novel idea

Stationary solutions of the Dirac equation for neutrinos in rotating matter do not smoothly reduce to plane waves as the angular velocity vanishes. The authors solve this by using light-cone coordinates and building generalized coherent states that diagonalize non-stationary integrals of motion, ensuring continuous transitions to free-particle plane waves.

Method

The Dirac equation with a coordinate-dependent axial-vector potential representing forward scattering in a uniformly rotating medium ($v = \Omega \times r$) is projected onto light-cone null hyperplanes ($u^0 = x^-$). For massless Dirac neutrinos, the transverse Hamiltonian maps onto a quantum harmonic oscillator, allowing the algebraic construction of coherent states via Bogoliubov transformations, followed by application to dense neutron star matter.

Key results

  • Constructed exact algebraic annihilation and creation operators acting directly on bispinor space for active neutrinos in rotating matter.
  • Demonstrated that the expectation values of neutrino position and momentum satisfy Ehrenfest relations matching a classical particle in a uniform magnetic field with cyclotron frequency $\omega_c = -s\Omega G_N / (\sqrt{2}(\lambda - G_N))$.
  • Calculated that electroweak interactions with rotating dense neutron star matter ($n_n \sim 10^{38}\text{ cm}^{-3}, \Omega \sim 10^3\text{ s}^{-1}$) induce an effective neutrino mass $m_{\text{eff}}$ on the order of a few $\text{eV}$.

Why it matters to you

Only tangentially relevant to core neutrino phenomenology. While it treats neutrino propagation in astrophysical environments (neutron stars), the mathematical formalisms are largely detached from neutrino-nucleus cross sections or oscillation experiment pipelines.

Caveats

Strictly relies on massless neutrinos to decouple the chiral projections in light-cone variables, and assumes an idealized infinite planar velocity profile that must be restricted geometrically to avoid superluminal rotation speeds.

Abstract

We study coherent states of neutrinos electroweakly interacting with rotating matter. We start with the Dirac equation for a massive neutrino propagating in background matter with a coordinate dependent effective potential. Then, we consider the limit of massless particles and the specific matter velocity profile. It allows us to reduce the neutrino Hamiltonian to the harmonic oscillator form. We find the exact solutions of the Dirac equation and construct the creation and annihilation operators. Using these results, we build the generalized coherent states of neutrinos. We provide the interpretation of these coherent states in terms of the classical physics. In particular, we demonstrate that the coherent states constructed approach plane waves in the limit of slow matter rotation. We also define the effective neutrino mass owing to the interaction of a massless neutrino with rotating matter. Then, we consider a special case of the generalized coherent states which are called the neutrino coherent states. The situation of the degenerate parameters of the system is also studied. Finally, we apply our results for astrophysical neutrinos propagating in dense matter of a rapidly rotating neutron star (NS). We calculate the effective neutrino masses for different neutrino energies for realistic parameters of NS.

9skimUniversal pairing gap in dilute Fermi gas

Universal enhancement of the pairing gap in a dilute Fermi superfluid

Silas R. Beane, Roland C. Farrell, Cameron P. Shotwell

cond-mat.quant-gas cond-mat.supr-con nucl-th quant-pharXiv:2610.20434 · cross-list

The pairing gap in a dilute Fermi superfluid exhibits a trans-series expansion where low-energy virtual quasiparticles generate a large linear-in-gap correction that enhances the gap beyond the Gor'kov-Melik-Barkhudarov prediction.

Novel idea

The paper reveals that the weak-coupling expansion of the superfluid gap is fundamentally a trans-series in coupling $\lambda = k_F a / \pi$ and the asymptotic Gor'kov-Melik-Barkhudarov (GMB) gap $x_0$. It identifies an entirely new kinematic sector linear in the gap $\Delta$, arising from virtual soft quasiparticles with energies $E \sim \Delta$, possessing an anomalously large coefficient.

Method

Starting from the exact Goldstone-pole condition on the amputated pairing vertex via the matrix Bethe-Salpeter equation in Nambu-Gor'kov space, the authors systematically expand the gap equation in explicit powers of the contact interaction $g$ and the gap $\Delta$. Soft and hard momentum integrations are separated, evaluating loop integrals in closed analytic form via modified Bessel function moments and the PSLQ algorithm.

Key results

  • Analytically evaluated the leading linear-in-gap coefficient as $c_{2,1} = 24.323\ldots$, which is roughly $25\times$ larger than typical perturbative coefficients ($|c_{n,0}| \le 1$).
  • Corrected prior literature errors in the hard-region next-to-next-to-leading order coefficient, finding $c_{1,0} = \frac{1}{3}\ln 2 + \frac{3}{2}\ln^2 2 - \frac{\pi^2}{36} - \frac{7}{2}\zeta(3) + \frac{14}{\pi^2}\text{G} + \frac{28}{\pi^2}\text{Li}_3(1/2) \approx 0.811$.
  • Found that the linear-in-gap correction dominates over GMB for $|k_F a| \gtrsim 0.3$, significantly increasing the pairing gap and creating a $\sim 2\sigma$ tension with available Quantum Monte Carlo and cold-atom data around $|k_F a| \approx 1$.

Why it matters to you

Touches nuclear many-body theory relevant to neutron star crust pairing and neutron matter equations of state. However, it focuses on contact-interaction superfluids rather than nuclear-structure or neutrino-interaction physics.

Caveats

Uses a zero-range contact interaction without finite-range nuclear forces or tensor correlations; the trans-series expansion loses perturbative stability for $|k_F a| \sim 1$ where corrections exceed the leading gap.

Abstract

At zero temperature, a dilute Fermi gas with an attractive contact interaction forms a superfluid with gapped fermionic excitations. We show that the gap at weak coupling can be organized as a trans-series expansion in the interaction strength and the asymptotic Gor'kov-Melik-Barkhudarov gap. Low-energy virtual quasiparticles generate corrections that are linear in the gap with a leading coefficient nearly 25$\times$ larger than the other known coefficients. This substantially increases the gap at intermediate densities, highlighting that systematic corrections are crucial for quantitative predictions.

Measuring $V_{cb}$ at electron-proton colliders

David Marzocca

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.18961

Bottom-tagged charged-current deep-inelastic scattering ($e^- c \to \nu_e b$) at future electron-proton colliders like the LHeC and FCC-eh can measure $|V_{cb}|$ with sub-percent precision, independent of semileptonic $B$ decays.

Novel idea

The author proposes an independent, high-energy determination of $|V_{cb}|$ using bottom-tagged charged-current (CC) deep-inelastic scattering ($e^- c \to \nu_e b$) at $ep$ colliders. Measuring ratios of $b$-tagged to inclusive CC DIS events in kinematic bins allows extraction of $|V_{cb}|$ free from the longstanding experimental and theoretical tensions between inclusive and exclusive semileptonic $B$ decays.

Method

The sensitivity is evaluated via a profiled Fisher information analysis using Born-level massive DIS kinematics ($m_b = 4.75\text{ GeV}$). The fit floats all other CKM elements, projected proton PDFs (NNPDF3.1 for EIC, PDF4LHC15_nnlo_lhec for LHeC/FCC-eh), single-top backgrounds modeled with MadGraph5_aMC@NLO, and experimental tagging/mistag efficiency calibrations and shape distortions.

Key results

  • The LHeC with $1\text{ ab}^{-1}$ can achieve an expected relative precision on $|V_{cb}|$ of $0.79\%$ with polarized electrons ($P_e = -0.8$) and $0.89\%$ unpolarized.
  • FCC-eh ($2\text{ ab}^{-1}, P_e = -0.8$) achieves $0.67\%$ precision, whereas the EIC is statistically limited to a $20-30\%$ determination.
  • Reaching sub-percent precision at the LHeC requires calibrating the $b$-tagging efficiency to $\sim 1\%$ and controlling mistag kinematic shape variations across $y$ to within a few percent.
  • Backgrounds from neutral-current DIS with a lost electron are shown to impact $|V_{cb}|$ precision by $\le 1\%$ at the LHeC provided the electron loss probability is $p_{\text{loss}} \lesssim 10^{-3}$.

Why it matters to you

Directly intersects your interest in lepton-hadron scattering, CC DIS kinematics, and charm/bottom PDF inputs. High-energy CC DIS modeling and tagging calibration techniques map directly onto high-energy neutrino scattering physics (such as at FASER$\nu$, the Forward Physics Facility, or a muon collider neutrino program).

Caveats

The calculation relies on Born-level matrix elements without higher-order QCD/electroweak radiative corrections or detector-level hadronization/smearing simulations.

Abstract

We study the prospects for an independent determination of $V_{cb}$ from bottom-tagged charged-current deep-inelastic scattering at future electron-proton colliders: the EIC, LHeC, and FCC-eh. The transition $e^- c \to \nu_e b$ probes $|V_{cb}|^2$ through a process with different theoretical and experimental uncertainties from semileptonic $B$ decays. We fit ratios of $b$-tagged charged-current event counts over flavour-inclusive ones in kinematic bins, including correlated parton-distribution and flavour-tag calibration uncertainties, and floating the other CKM parameters. With projected parton distributions and tagging performance motivated by existing studies, we find a potential relative sensitivity in $|V_{cb}|$ of about $0.8\%$ at the LHeC with $1\,\rm{ab}^{-1}$ of integrated luminosity and $0.7\%$ at FCC-eh with $2\,\rm{ab}^{-1}$. The corresponding EIC sensitivity is instead at the level of $20 - 30 \%$, depending on the configuration. The LHeC could thus provide a high-energy determination competitive with low-energy ones, but with substantially different dominant systematic uncertainties, on the timescale of its proposed operation between the HL-LHC and FCC-ee. We identify precise flavour-tag calibration and control of the kinematic response as the main requirements for realising this potential sensitivity.

Inspired by FastJet-Contrib, Pythia-Contrib creates an official repository and build harness for runtime-loadable Pythia 8 plugins. Method

The framework utilizes C++ runtime polymorphism where contributors derive plugin classes from standard Pythia 8 base classes. A centralized 'use' repository provides unified management and configuration tools.

Key results

Caveats

Long-term reliability depends heavily on third-party package maintainers updating their shared plugins against major Pythia 8 API revisions.

Abstract

Pythia is a general-purpose Monte Carlo event generator used in particle, astroparticle, and nuclear physics, by theorists and experimentalists, to simulate particle collisions. Because of the size and diversity of the user community, it is challenging to address all of the needs for new and varied physics models. Constraining three-nucleon forces in nuclear lattice effective field theory

Serdar Elhatisari, Ulf-G. Meißner, Zhengxue Ren, Shuang Zhang

nucl-th hep-lat nucl-exarXiv:2610.20072

Constrains short-range chiral three-nucleon forces in nuclear lattice effective field theory at N3LO using nuclear binding energies up to $A=40$ and neutron-alpha scattering data.

Novel idea

The authors construct a minimal short-range three-nucleon force (3NF) for Nuclear Lattice EFT by decomposing leading $c_D$ and $c_E$ terms into locally and nonlocally smeared components. They supplement these with partial-wave-resolved two-nucleon contact operators coupled to the scalar-isoscalar density of a third spectator nucleon, bypassing full three-body Jacobi partial-wave expansions.

Method

Calculations employ an N3LO two-nucleon chiral interaction and a fixed Roy-Steiner two-pion-exchange 3NF on the lattice. Matrix elements are evaluated in first-order perturbation theory via wave-function matching across six leading-order reference Hamiltonian variants. The low-energy constants for up to 40 candidate 3NF operators are fitted using stochastic optimization against 38 observables: $E/A$ for 26 nuclei ($A=3$ to $40$, including $^{12}\text{C}$, $^{16}\text{O}$, $^{36}\text{Ar}$, $^{40}\text{Ca}$) and finite-volume $n\text{--}\alpha$ levels.

Key results

  • Without short-range 3NFs, the relative binding-energy RMSD across the nuclear chart is $21.6\%$; fitting five 3NF terms reduces this RMSD to $2.22\%$ (and $2.15\%$ for six terms).
  • Finite-volume $n\text{--}\alpha$ scattering energies achieve RMSDs of $0.174\text{ MeV}$ ($^2P_{3/2}$) and $0.234\text{ MeV}$ ($^2P_{1/2}$) for the top-50 five-term ensemble.
  • All top-50 five-term fits select two extended Cartesian contact terms ($[c_E^{(0)}]_{044}$, $[c_E^{(0)}]_{222}$) alongside the partial-wave-resolved $^3P_1$ contact $D_{{}^3P_1}$.
  • Accurately reproduces ground-state binding energies for primary neutrino target nuclei, including $^{12}\text{C}$, $^{16}\text{O}$, $^{36}\text{Ar}$, and $^{40}\text{Ca}$, within $\sim 2\text{--}4\%$ relative deviations.

Why it matters to you

Constraining 3NFs up to $^{36}\text{Ar}$ and $^{40}\text{Ca}$ in chiral EFT is an essential step toward precision nuclear wave functions and spectral functions needed to model axial current matrix elements and 2p2h responses in DUNE and Hyper-K event generators.

Caveats

The reported uncertainties omit chiral EFT truncation and lattice-spacing extrapolation errors, and a persistent signed residual remains in the $n\text{--}\alpha$ $^2P_{1/2}$ finite-volume level.

Abstract

We study short-range three-nucleon forces (3NFs) in nuclear lattice effective field theory with a chiral two-nucleon interaction at N${}^3$LO. The one-pion-exchange ($c_D$) and contact ($c_E$) terms of the leading 3NF are decomposed into locally and non-locally smeared components and supplemented by partial-wave-resolved pair contacts coupled to the density of a third nucleon. We determine their coefficients from nuclear binding energies for nuclei in the mass range from $A=3$ to $A=40$ and neutron--alpha finite-volume energies. This allows to work out the minimal three-nucleon force that can be used for future precision studies in NLEFT.

astro-ph.HE hep-pharXiv:2610.19649 · cross-list

Demonstrates that the spatial coincidence between 90 LHAASO very-high-energy gamma-ray sources and 348 IceCube high-energy track alerts is statistically consistent with random background.

Novel idea

The paper performs the first systematic spatial correlation and hadronic flux comparison between the complete First LHAASO Catalog of 90 VHE/UHE gamma-ray sources and IceCube's IceCat-1 alert track dataset ($E_\nu \ge 100\text{ TeV}$).

Method

Spatial coincidences are defined by the geometric intersection of LHAASO containment radii (combining 2D-Gaussian $r_{39}$ and centroid errors) with IceCat-1 $90\%$ angular error regions. Statistical significance is evaluated via 10,000 Monte Carlo pseudo-experiments randomizing neutrino right ascensions while preserving declination-dependent acceptance. Optimistic hadronic neutrino fluxes are calculated assuming purely $pp$-driven gamma-ray emission and compared to single-event Poisson upper limits.

Key results

  • Identified 9 spatial coincidences involving 7 neutrino events and 8 LHAASO sources under the primary cut $\Omega_{90} \le 10\text{ deg}^2$.
  • Obtained post-trial p-values of 0.237 for the full source catalog, 0.312 for KM2A sources, and 0.115 for WCDA sources, failing to reject the null hypothesis of chance coincidence.
  • Demonstrated that the most optimistic hadronic neutrino fluxes for candidate sources fall below the sensitivity threshold required to produce IceCat-1 alert triggers.

Why it matters to you

The analysis constrains Galactic PeVatron neutrino production but lies outside the reader's primary focus on neutrino-nucleus scattering mechanisms, generator physics, and low-energy neutrino phenomenology.

Caveats

The search utilizes a simple geometric overlap metric rather than an unbinned likelihood analysis with per-event reconstruction PSFs, and only considers high-energy alert tracks rather than IceCube's full point-source archival track dataset.

Abstract

We conducted an investigation into the possible correlation between very-high-energy (VHE, $E>$ 0.1 TeV) gamma-ray sources, as identified by the Large High Altitude Air Shower Observatory (LHAASO), and high-energy neutrino events captured by the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. For this analysis, we utilized the First LHAASO catalog, which comprises 90 VHE gamma-ray sources, and the IceCube Event Catalog of Alert Tracks (IceCat-1), which includes 348 track-like neutrino events. Through Monte Carlo simulations, we assessed the correlations between the neutrino events and LHAASO sources, achieving the best p-values of 0.237, 0.312, and 0.115, respectively, for all LHAASO sources, KM2A sources, and WCDA sources. These findings imply that the spatial coincidence between LHAASO sources and neutrino alerts is relatively weak. Furthermore, under the assumption that gamma-rays are exclusively generated through hadronic interactions, we estimated the most optimistic theoretical neutrino fluxes from LHAASO sources and compared them with the IceCube observed fluxes. The results indicate that the majority of these sources are improbable counterparts to the detected neutrinos. In conclusion, LHAASO sources could be potential galactic neutrino emitters but currently do not exhibit compelling evidence, thereby underscoring the necessity for elevated sensitivity levels in forthcoming detection instruments like IceCube-Gen2.

Why it matters to you

Caveats

Born-Oppenheimer chiral effective field theory and its application to $X_b$

Yu Zhang, Feng-Kun Guo, Christoph Hanhart, Alexey Nefediev, Paul Pütz

Formulates Born-Oppenheimer chiral EFT in momentum space to describe the $1^{++}$ bottomonium spectrum, predicting that the observed $\chi_{b1}(3P)$ is a molecular $X_b$ and identifying an undiscovered compact partner.

Novel idea

The work develops Born-Oppenheimer chiral effective field theory (BO$\chi$EFT) in momentum space, merging unquenched lattice-QCD static quark-antiquark potentials (including string-breaking couplings) with a systematic chiral meson-exchange potential (including singular tensor forces and contact terms) in a coupled-channel Lippmann-Schwinger framework.

Method

The authors solve coupled-channel Lippmann-Schwinger equations combining bare $\bar{b}b(nP)$ states with $B\bar{B}^*$ and $B^*\bar{B}^*$ continuum channels. String-breaking transition matrix elements are taken from lattice QCD in the static limit, while light pseudoscalar meson exchanges are computed with chiral EFT power counting. Two free parameters (the static potential energy offset $V_0$ and an S-wave contact term $C$) are fixed using the masses of the three known axial-vector $\chi_{b1}$ states.

Key results

  • Identifies the physical state at $10513.4\text{ MeV}$ (currently assigned to $\chi_{b1}(3P)$) as a predominantly molecular $X_b$ state ($98\%$ hadronic compositeness: $41\%$ S-wave, $36\%$ D-wave, and $21\%$ 3-body).
  • Predicts the existence of a predominantly compact $\chi_{b1}(3P)$ bottomonium state at $10.544\text{ GeV}$ ($10\text{--}17\%$ hadronic compositeness), roughly $60\text{ MeV}$ below the $B\bar{B}^*$ threshold.
  • Identifies and successfully projects out a regulator-induced spurious deep bound state arising from the attractive chiral tensor force without shifting physical low-energy observables.

Why it matters to you

Provides technical insights into non-perturbative chiral EFT methods, coupled-channel Lippmann-Schwinger equations, and the regularization of singular tensor forces, but focuses entirely on heavy quarkonium exotic spectroscopy rather than neutrino physics.

Caveats

Short-range contact counterterms cannot yet be systematically matched between the charm and bottom sectors due to unconstrained heavy-quark mass scaling in four-hadron operators.
