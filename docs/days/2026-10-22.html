10must-readDouble-beta radiative corrections

Radiative corrections to double-$\beta$ decay and their impact on experimental analyses

Jordy de Vries, Emanuele Mereghetti, Saad el Morabit, Stefan Sandner

hep-ph hep-ex nucl-ex nucl-tharXiv:2610.28742

We compute complete $\mathcal{O}(\alpha)$ radiative corrections to $2\nu\beta\beta$ and $0\nu\beta\beta$ decays in heavy-nucleus EFT, deriving a universal double-weak Sirlin function and closed-form loop integrals.

Novel idea

Prior analyses approximated $2\nu\beta\beta$ radiative corrections as the sum of two single-$\beta$ Sirlin functions, ignoring inter-electron correlations and nuclear excitation spectra. This paper provides the first exact evaluation of ultrasoft corrections via a heavy-nucleus EFT, introducing a universal double-weak Sirlin function and new closed-form auxiliary-mass representation loop integrals.

Method

The framework utilizes heavy-nucleus effective field theory with initial/final scalar $0^+$ fields and intermediate $1^+$ vector fields. The authors compute all virtual and real-emission $\mathcal{O}(\alpha)$ diagrams, evaluate scalar one-loop integrals using auxiliary masses, and perform a large-$\omega_n$ expansion.

Key results

  • Neglecting radiative corrections shifts extracted nuclear matrix element ratios like $\xi_{31}$ significantly.
  • For $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay, radiative corrections uniformly increase the inclusive decay rate by $5.2\%$, independent of NMEs or $m_{\beta\beta}$.
  • The double-weak Sirlin function differs drastically from the sum of two single-$\beta$ Sirlin functions, cutting the correction magnitude nearly in half.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts precision spectral analysis and background templates for tonne-scale $0\nu\beta\beta$ experiments (LEGEND, nEXO, CUPID) and $2\nu\beta\beta$ extractions of $\xi_{31}$ relevant to nuclear matrix element benchmarks.

Caveats

Higher-order $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2 Z)$ terms and nuclear-structure-dependent energy-dependent radiative corrections $\delta_{\text{NS}}^E$ remain to be calculated.

Abstract

We present a comprehensive study of the $\mathcal{O}(\alpha)$ radiative corrections to two-neutrino and neutrinoless double-$\beta$ decay within a heavy-nucleus effective field theory. We compute all virtual and real-emission corrections for intermediate nuclear states of arbitrary excitation energy, including new closed-form expressions for the required one-loop integrals with heavy-nucleus propagators. For excitation energies large compared with the lepton energies, the corrections factorize into the tree-level rate times a universal double-weak Sirlin function, which differs substantially from the sum of two single-$\beta$ Sirlin functions used so far in the literature. We discuss the impact of the radiative corrections on the analysis of measured spectra in terms of ratios of nuclear matrix elements, $\xi_{31}$, and show that neglecting radiative corrections can significantly bias the extraction. Real-photon emission, not considered in previous analyses, plays a central role and the impact of radiative corrections depends crucially on the detector setup. We provide the photon spectrum, fully differential in the photon energy and direction, and recommend that experiments simulate the photon explicitly. For $0\nu\beta\beta$ decay, the radiative corrections increase the inclusive rate by about $5\%$, independently of the nuclear matrix elements or the neutrino Majorana mass.

\textit{Ab initio} no-core shell model calculations of the electric dipole polarizabilities and photoabsorption cross sections of the $A=3$ mirror nuclei

H. T. Zhao, C. Y. Zhai, J. P. Vary, H. Li, J. M. Dong, H. J. Ong + 8 more

nucl-th nucl-exarXiv:2610.29711

Ab initio no-core shell model calculations using Daejeon16 determine $E1$ polarizabilities and photoabsorption cross sections for the $A=3$ mirror nuclei $^3\text{H}$ and $^3\text{He}$.

Novel idea

Extends the ab initio discretized-continuum no-core shell model approach from spin-zero $^4\text{He}$ to nonzero-spin systems, isolating $1/2^-$ and $3/2^-$ contributions and establishing model-space convergence with multiple final-state angular momenta.

Method

Calculations utilize the Daejeon16 $NN$ interaction plus Coulomb forces, diagonalized in a harmonic-oscillator basis via MFDn. Polarizabilities are summed directly from discretized continuum states, and photoabsorption cross sections are reconstructed via the finite-spectrum Lorentz integral transform (LIT).

Key results

  • The $3/2^-$ contribution to the $E1$ polarizability is approximately twice ($\sim 2.12$ times) the $1/2^-$ contribution in both $^3\text{H}$ and $^3\text{He}$.
  • Calculated polarizabilities are $\alpha_E(^3\text{H}) = 0.1529^{+0.0013}_{-0.0012}~\text{fm}^3$ and $\alpha_E(^3\text{He}) = 0.1626^{+0.0021}_{-0.0020}~\text{fm}^3$.
  • The mirror difference $\Delta_C \alpha_E = 0.0097(8)~\text{fm}^3$ (a $6.3\%$ enhancement in $^3\text{He}$) is driven entirely by isospin-breaking Coulomb effects.

Why it matters to you

Provides rigorous ab initio nuclear structure and electroweak response calculations for light nuclei relevant to low-energy nuclear matrix element evaluations and benchmark neutrino-nucleus interaction models.

Caveats

Results depend mildly on the choice of Hamiltonian and high-energy truncations, though uncertainties are carefully bounded.

Abstract

We present \textit{ab initio} no-core shell-model calculations of the electric dipole ($E1$) polarizabilities and photoabsorption cross sections of the $A=3$ mirror nuclei $^3$H and $^3$He using the Daejeon16 nucleon-nucleon interaction. The polarizabilities are obtained by direct summation over discretized continuum states in the harmonic-oscillator basis, while the same spectral information is used to reconstruct the cross sections. This work provides the first extension of the approach from the spin-zero $^4$He to nonzero-spin nuclei by calculating the $1/2^-$ and $3/2^-$ final-state contributions separately and demonstrating their model-space convergence. In both nuclei, the $3/2^-$ contribution is approximately twice the $1/2^-$ contribution. After quantifying finite-basis and excitation-energy-truncation uncertainties, we identify a clear difference between the mirror polarizabilities, induced entirely by the Coulomb interaction because the Daejeon16 interaction is isospin symmetric. The reconstructed cross sections remain stable under variations of the inversion parameters of the Lorentz integral transform and exhibit the same angular-momentum hierarchy. These results extend the discretized-continuum approach to integrated and energy-resolved $E1$ observables in nonzero-spin nuclei with multiple final-state angular momenta.

10worth readingReactor CEvNS Spectrum Unfolding

Unfolding the low-energy reactor neutrino flux from CE$\nu$NS data with a finite Dirac sum

Muping Chen, Graciela Gelmini, Danny Marfatia, Koichiro Yasuda

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2608.16107 · cross-list

A halo-independent 'finite Dirac sum' (FDS) method based on convex geometry unfolds the sub-threshold reactor antineutrino spectrum from CEvNS mock data without smoothness priors.

Novel idea

Adapts dark matter halo-independent analysis techniques to CEvNS reactor neutrino experiments, replacing subjective Tikhonov regularization with a rigorous convex-geometry formulation where the differential neutrino rate is cast as a finite Dirac sum.

Method

The differential rate is modelled as a convolution of a detector response function with an integrated flux parameterized as a finite Dirac sum. The Neyman $\chi^2$ is minimized subject to positivity and monotonicity constraints across thousands of numerical intervals using mock low-threshold Ge detector data.

Key results

  • Yields best-fit piecewise-constant integrated fluxes with explicit pointwise confidence bands and degeneracy manifolds.
  • Demonstrates that FDS successfully removes human-tuned regularization biases and handles complex backgrounds (like exponential-plus-constant models) without unphysical negative flux values.
  • Confirms that identifying the $^{238}\text{U}$ neutron-capture component below 1.8 MeV requires unrealistically high exposures ($\sim 300~\text{kg}\cdot\text{year}$) even with 1 eV thresholds.

Why it matters to you

Presents an advanced, assumption-free statistical workflow for analyzing CEvNS data from low-threshold experiments like NUCLEUS, directly impacting low-energy neutrino flux extraction.

Caveats

Translating the integrated flux constraints into precise differential flux confidence bands remains computationally intensive, and the bands show noticeable insensitivity to exact background scaling.

Abstract

We present a novel method to analyze coherent elastic neutrino--nucleus scattering (CE$\nu$NS) data to extract the reactor antineutrino spectrum below the inverse beta decay threshold of 1.8 MeV, where it remains unmeasured. Adapting halo-independent analysis techniques developed for direct dark matter detection, we show how to obtain a best-fit and a pointwise confidence band for the integrated neutrino flux, without assuming a parametric form or smoothness prior for the spectrum. In our approach, which follows from convex geometry arguments, the differential neutrino rate is written as a linear combination of Dirac delta functions -- a finite Dirac sum (FDS) -- with a maximum number of terms determined by the number of data points. We apply our "FDS method" to mock CE$\nu$NS data for a low-threshold Ge detector and compare it with Tikhonov-regularized unfolding.

10must-readTeV Neutrino-Nucleon Cross Sections

Measurement of the neutrino cross section across the TeV scale using IceCube data

Rasmi Hajjar, Mauricio Bustamante, Sergio Palomares-Ruiz

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.24068 · cross-list

Using 8 years of IceCube data, we present an energy-resolved measurement of muon-neutrino charged-current cross sections from 150 GeV to 40 TeV with uncertainties below 20%.

Novel idea

Bridges the gap between accelerator/collider energy scales and multi-TeV astrophysical measurements by providing a unified, energy-resolved cross-section profile using through-going atmospheric and astrophysical muon neutrinos from IceCube.

Method

Analyzes 305,735 upward-going muon neutrino events across 260 energy-zenith bins (2011–2019 data) using a Bayesian MCMC framework that jointly fits cross-section normalizations and 16 systematic nuisance parameters, accounting for Earth attenuation via nuSQuIDS.

Key results

  • Measured average neutrino-antineutrino CC cross sections are consistent with Standard Model predictions (CSMS) across five energy bins spanning $150~\text{GeV}$ to $40~\text{TeV}$ with $<20\%$ uncertainties.
  • Provides the first mild indications above 1 TeV that neutrino and antineutrino cross sections can be statistically disentangled via Earth absorption profiles.
  • With a tenfold exposure increase (next-gen telescopes), separate antineutrino cross-section measurements above 360 GeV could reach $>7\sigma$ significance.

Why it matters to you

Directly constrains high-energy neutrino-nucleus interaction cross sections and nucleon structure at small $x$, validating theoretical inputs critical for IceCube oscillation and BSM searches.

Caveats

Precision is currently limited by strong anti-correlations between the cross-section normalizations and the atmospheric neutrino flux normalization.

Abstract

Neutrinos interact with matter exclusively through the weak force, resulting in extremely small cross sections that are difficult to measure. At TeV energies, neutrino-nucleon interactions probe the structure of the nucleon in regimes where our knowledge of it is less complete, and signatures of physics beyond the Standard Model could manifest. Recent measurements using collider, atmospheric, and astrophysical neutrinos have begun to explore this regime, but no single measurement has provided precise coverage from sub-TeV to tens of TeV, and uncertainties remain at the level of about 20-40%. Here we present a determination of the muon-neutrino-nucleon charged-current cross sections as a function of energy across 150 GeV-40 TeV using eight years of through-going muon neutrino data collected by the IceCube Neutrino Observatory. We measure the average neutrino-antineutrino cross section, enabling direct comparison with previous results, and find, for the first time above 1 TeV, mild indications that the neutrino and antineutrino cross sections can be measured separately. The measured cross sections are consistent with Standard Model predictions and are determined with uncertainties below 20% throughout the explored energy range, achieving the most precise characterization of neutrino-nucleon interactions across the TeV scale to date. These results provide a unified measurement of neutrino-nucleon cross sections in this energy regime, connecting accelerator-based and previous higher-energy neutrino observations - thereby advancing high-energy neutrino physics into the precision era.

9must-readNeutrino mixing running

The renormalization-group running of neutrino mixing is undetectable

Gabriela Barenboim, Mauricio Bustamante, Qinrui Liu

hep-ph astro-ph.HEarXiv:2610.28744

Renormalization-group running of neutrino mixing parameters is physically undetectable because external on-shell neutrinos evaluate self-energies at $p^2 = m_i^2$, cancelling momentum-transfer scale dependencies.

Novel idea

Refutes the premise that the running mixing matrix evaluated at $\mu = Q$ is directly observable as a momentum-transfer-dependent mixing matrix in neutrino production and detection, proving that large logarithms in the external self-energy undo any apparent running.

Method

Performs a rigorous field-theoretic derivation using on-shell renormalization scheme definitions, tracking the exact cancellations between tree-level running matrices $U(Q)$ and neutrino self-energy loop logarithms in various BSM frameworks (MSSM, seesaws, SMEFT).

Key results

  • The physical mixing matrix $\hat{U}$ governing oscillations is fixed by the pole masses and propagator self-energies, independent of the interaction momentum transfer $Q$.
  • Short-baseline data (NuTe, NOMAD) independently constrain any hypothetical zero-baseline mixing angle shift between pion decay and GeV-scale scattering to be $< 0.7^\circ - 2.4^\circ$.
  • Shows that claims of detecting RG running via zero-baseline flavor change, $\delta_{\text{CP}}$ shifts, or astrophysical neutrino composition anomalies are theoretically invalid.

Why it matters to you

Corrects a widespread theoretical misconception in neutrino phenomenology regarding the physical interpretation of renormalization-group running in mixing parameters across energy scales.

Caveats

Assumes standard perturbative quantum field theory and on-shell fermion propagation over macroscopic baselines.

Abstract

Quantum corrections make the parameters of a theory depend on an arbitrary renormalization scale, $\mu$. The renormalization-group (RG) running of neutrino masses and mixing is a standard tool to relate models defined at a high mass scale to measured values. A growing body of work holds that the running is also directly observable: a neutrino produced or detected at momentum transfer $Q$ would carry the mixing matrix evaluated at $\mu = Q$. Proposed signatures include flavor change at zero distance from the source, shifts in the measured CP-violation phase, and an anomalous flavor composition of high-energy astrophysical neutrinos. We show that this premise is incorrect. The mixing is a property of the neutrino itself, fixed by its self-energy on the mass shell, not by the process that produces or detects it. Setting $\mu = Q$ is legitimate, but then large logarithms in the self-energy undo the running; the premise incorrectly drops them. Hence, apart from small vertex and box corrections, every experiment measures the same mixing: the running is undetectable. We illustrate this in the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model, two seesaw models, and the Standard Model effective field theory. Independently, short-baseline data already limit the change of any one mixing angle between pion decay and detection at $Q$ of a few GeV to below $0.7^\circ$--$2.4^\circ$.

9must-readPionless EFT

Reformulation of the pionless effective field theory for practical few- and many-body applications

Silas R. Beane, Roland C. Farrell, Cameron P. Shotwell, Ubirajara van Kolck

nucl-th hep-pharXiv:2610.29077

Effective Pole Theory (EPT) reformulates pionless effective field theory using energy-independent, momentum-dependent separable potentials that reproduce the effective-range expansion to any order and remain perturbative up to pion-production threshold.

Novel idea

Unlike standard EFT of contact forces that expands the amplitude, EPT formulates an expansion in the inverse scattering amplitude via rational S-matrices with simple poles. This guarantees exact S-matrix unitarity, generates softening potentials that avoid pathologies at high momentum, and bypasses the uncontrolled cutoffs often present in naive chiral EFT treatments.

Method

The authors construct rank-1 separable potentials whose form factors match fixed-order effective-range expansions in single and coupled channels. They utilize dimensional regularization with power divergence subtraction (PDS), evaluate Weinberg eigenvalues to diagnose the perturbativeness of the Lippmann-Kernel, and construct "designer" potentials extending up to the pion-production threshold ($|k|acksimeq 405$ MeV).

Key results

  • Constructed order-by-order separable potentials that fit Nijmegen partial-wave phase shifts up to the pion-production threshold through f-waves.
  • Demonstrated via Weinberg eigenvalue diagnostics that the EPT potentials remain strictly perturbative across center-of-mass momenta up to $405$ MeV, unlike standard divergent contact potentials.
  • Derived explicit relations for coupled-channel ${}^3S_1-{}^3D_1$ and p-wave systems, reproducing asymptotic D/S ratios and binding momenta without generating unphysical high-momentum repulsion.

Why it matters to you

Provides powerful, soft, momentum-dependent two-body potentials designed for exact few- and many-body calculations (such as neutron matter superfluid gaps and Quantum Monte Carlo simulations) without the uncontrolled UV artifacts plaguing standard contact EFTs.

Caveats

Certain higher-order shape-parameter fits generate unphysical, acausal asymptotic phase shifts or non-Hermitian potentials, necessitating strict physical filtering; three-body forces in this separable scheme require future study.

Abstract

The effective field theory (EFT) of contact forces which describes few-nucleon systems at distance scales larger than the pion Compton wavelength ${\rm EFT}({\pi\hskip-0.54em /})$ can be defined in terms of momentum-dependent potentials that contain a fixed number of simple poles and generate the effective-range expansion to a given fixed order. These potentials provide a practical generalization of the standard ${\rm EFT}({\pi\hskip-0.54em /})$ power-counting scheme which, for instance, naturally accounts for the large effective ranges in the s-wave channels. Nucleon-nucleon phase shifts computed in this formulation of ${\rm EFT}({\pi\hskip-0.54em /})$ are compared to modern phase-shift analyses, and effective-range parameters are tabulated. The ${\rm EFT}({\pi\hskip-0.54em /})$ potentials, which provide a rational approximation of the S-matrix, are easily extended into the regime of pion physics through the inclusion of additional S-matrix poles and zeros, and provide an accurate description of data up to pion-production threshold. The potentials in this scheme are in an important sense weak over all relevant distance scales, and are therefore of utility for systematic calculations of many-nucleon observables.

Testing the statistical compatibility of data sets with a common prior

Pilar Coloma, Michele Maltoni, Thomas Schwetz

hep-ph astro-ph.CO hep-ex physics.data-anarXiv:2610.28681

The authors propose an extension of the Parameter Goodness-of-fit (PG) test to evaluate the statistical compatibility of data sets tied by a common prior, deriving its generalised chi-squared distribution under the Gaussian approximation.

Novel idea

Standard PG tests assume statistical independence between tested datasets, which breaks down when nuisance parameters are constrained by a common auxiliary prior. This work introduces two modified test statistics ($ ext{PG}^*$ and $ ext{PG}^+$) that explicitly account for prior-induced correlations and provide robust tension metrics.

Method

Using quadratic form theory and the Gaussian approximation, the authors express the prior-constrained test statistics as linear combinations of $ ilde{ u}$ standard normal variables, yielding generalized $ rac{2}{ ilde{ u}}$ distributions whose characteristic functions are evaluated numerically. Monte Carlo simulations of neutrino oscillation disappearance experiments validate the framework.

Key results

  • Demonstrated that $ ext{PG}^*$ can assume negative values and follows a generalized $ ilde{ u}$ distribution with an expectation value equal to unconstrained parameters $p$, whereas $ ext{PG}^+$ is strictly non-negative.
  • Proved analytically and via Monte Carlo that in the strong-prior limit ($s_P o 0$), both $ ext{PG}^*$ and $ ext{PG}^+$ converge to the standard PG distribution ($ ilde{ u} o ilde{ u}_p$), while in the weak-prior limit they retain generalized $ rac{2}{ ilde{ u}}$ structures.
  • Provided a ready-to-use Python script to compute exact $p$-values and cumulative distribution functions for arbitrary covariance setups.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts oscillation-analysis systematics and joint data combinations (e.g., T2K, NOvA, DUNE) where common flux or cross-section priors introduce hidden correlations across independent datasets.

Caveats

The analytic distributions rely on the Gaussian approximation, requiring minima to be sufficiently far from physical parameter space boundaries.

Abstract

The Parameter Goodness-of-fit (PG) test [hep-ph/0304176] offers a simple test statistic to study the compatibility of different data sets within a given theoretical model. In its original formulation it assumes that the data sets are statistically independent. We propose an extension of the PG test for the situation when some parameters of the model are constrained by an auxiliary data set called prior here. As the prior is imposed for all data sets, they are no longer statistically independent. We show that under the Gaussian approximation, the prior-constrained PG test statistic follows a generalised $\chi^2$ distribution, which can be used to obtain $p$-values in a straight-forward way. In the approximation of a strong prior constraint, the test converges towards the standard PG test for the unconstrained model parameters.
