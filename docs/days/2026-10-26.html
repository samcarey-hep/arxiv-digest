10must-readStrangeness-changing 2p2h currents

Strangeness-changing two-body weak currents from SU(3) chiral dynamics

Jaroslaw A. Nowak

nucl-tharXiv:2610.32375

Constructs the first microscopic tree-level SU(3) chiral model for $|\Delta S|=1$ two-body (2p2h) weak currents in antineutrino-nucleus scattering, evaluating family dynamics and channel hyperon compositions.

Novel idea

Provides the first microscopic calculation of semileptonic strangeness-changing 2p2h weak currents ($\bar{\nu} [NN] \to \ell^+ Y N'$). It identifies a charge-exchange mechanism that generates primary kaon-less $\Sigma^+$ production from $pp$ pairs, a process forbidden at the one-body level, and shows that 2p2h hyperon production is intrinsically less $\Lambda$-rich than quasi-elastic scattering.

Method

The hadronic current is constructed from the lowest-order SU(3) chiral Lagrangian, incorporating contact, meson-in-flight, octet baryon-pole, kaon-exchange, and decuplet-pole ($\Sigma^*(1385)$) diagrams. The vector current satisfies the SU(3) Ward identity pointwise, and the axial current utilizes a Goldberger-Treiman longitudinal completion. Operators are evaluated over a three-body phase space on a free correlated nucleon pair using local density and formation-length matching prescriptions.

Key results

  • Identifies primary $\Sigma^+$ hyperon production from $pp$ pairs, contributing $1.13-1.20\%$ of the total two-body hyperon rate in argon.
  • Finds that the 2p2h current yields a lower $\Lambda/\Sigma^0$ ratio ($\sigma_\Lambda/\sigma_{\Sigma^0} = 3.32$ at $1\text{ GeV}$) than the one-body current ($4.12$), remaining $0.81-0.87\times$ the QE ratio across $1-5\text{ GeV}$.
  • Shows octet poles dominate at $0.6\text{ GeV}$ ($77\%$ of $\Lambda$ family sum), while decuplet poles become significant ($26-48\%$) at $1-5\text{ GeV}$.
  • Demonstrates $15-17\%$ destructive interference between the two nucleon line assignments in $pn$ pairs.

Why it matters to you

Directly targets core research interests in 2p2h/meson-exchange currents, SU(3) chiral model implementations, antineutrino-nucleus cross sections on argon targets, and hyperon production models for DUNE/SBN event generators.

Caveats

Results are evaluated on a factorized free correlated pair rather than a full nuclear response function, and interference between 1-body and 2-body currents is not computed.

Abstract

We know of no microscopic calculation of the semileptonic strangeness-changing 2p2h current. We construct it at tree level for nubar [NN] -> l+ Y N' from vertices of the lowest-order SU(3) chiral Lagrangian: contact, meson-in-flight, baryon-pole and kaon-exchange families, both pn line assignments summed coherently, and decuplet poles with N -> Delta transition form factors and SU(3) route signs. The undressed vector current is the Noether current of the flavour rotation and satisfies the SU(3)-limit Ward identity pointwise. The axial current has a vertex-level kaon-pole completion fixed by the Goldberger-Treiman relation, whose divergence equals (m_s+m_u)P_su by construction; we omit the axial contact operator, its kaon-pole partner, and the remaining inverse-propagator and mass-splitting terms, and the form factors break the vector identity. The flavour routes allow a primary kaon-less Sigma+ n final state from a pp pair that no one-body weak vertex reaches; antisymmetrised, it is 1.13-1.20% of the isolated two-body hyperon rate over the seven charge channels. In a factorised free-pair benchmark, octet baryon poles carry 77% of the Lambda-channel incoherent family sum at 0.6 GeV but 43-58% at 1-5 GeV, where the decuplet carries 26-48%; the line assignments interfere destructively. The isolated two-body current is 0.81-0.87 times as Lambda-rich as the quasi-elastic one at 1-5 GeV (0.67-0.95 across prescriptions); the axial contact operator with its kaon-pole partner at the Lagrangian coefficient lowers this by 2-6%, and a Ward-compatible form-factor dressing changes it by about 4%. A correlation on every meson changes the composition by under 3%; a Landau-Migdal contact on the pions alone leaves kaon exchange unscreened and roughly triples Lambda/Sigma0. The KNY couplings carry the axial N -> Y flavour combinations, so the composition is not the quasi-elastic one rescaled.

Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering Simulation Using GENIE Event Generator

L. F. B. M. Rodrigues (Federal University of Alfenas, Poços de Caldas - MG, Brazil), F. G. Gardim (Federal University of Alfenas, Poços de Caldas - MG, Brazil) + 4 more

hep-pharXiv:2610.32656

Uses GENIE simulations to highlight the breakdown of the Relativistic Fermi Gas model in MicroBooNE CCQE data and quantifies cross-section reductions from updated 2021 Bodek-Yang DIS parameters for DUNE.

Novel idea

Evaluates how unphysical tuning of the axial mass $M_A$ in the Relativistic Fermi Gas (RFG) model masks initial-state nuclear dynamics in Liquid Argon TPCs, advocating for standardizing LFG+RPA and modernized DIS parameterizations in GENIE for DUNE.

Method

Performs Monte Carlo event generation using GENIE to compare RFG ($M_A=1.03$ vs $1.20\text{ GeV}$) and LFG+RPA against MicroBooNE $\nu_\mu$ inclusive data. It further evaluates the cross-section impact of switching from default GENIE DIS settings to the updated 2021 Bodek-Yang parameter set across the DUNE energy range.

Key results

  • Reaffirms that RFG requires an inflated axial mass $M_A \approx 1.20\text{ GeV}$ to match MicroBooNE data, misrepresenting true nuclear dynamics.
  • Shows that the modernized 2021 Bodek-Yang DIS parameter update causes a $\sim 7\%$ cross-section reduction at the DUNE flux peak ($2.5\text{ GeV}$).

Why it matters to you

Directly relates to interaction modeling in GENIE, CCQE nuclear corrections, DIS parameters, and oscillation systematics for DUNE LArTPCs.

Caveats

This is a brief master's summary manuscript presenting standard benchmark comparisons within GENIE rather than introducing new theoretical frameworks or code implementations.

Abstract

The Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE) relies on the precise reconstruction of incident neutrino energy to extract oscillation parameters. The use of Liquid Argon introduces significant theoretical complexities due to nuclear effects. This work evaluates neutrino-nucleus scattering models using the GENIE Monte Carlo event generator. We demonstrate the limitations of the Relativistic Fermi Gas (RFG) model in the Charged Current Quasi-Elastic (CCQE) channel and its reliance on an inflated Axial Mass to match experimental data from MicroBooNE. We show that transitioning to advanced many-body frameworks, specifically the Local Fermi Gas (LFG) supplemented with Random Phase Approximation (RPA), is needed. Additionally, we evaluate the impact of the 2021 Bodek-Yang parameter updates on Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS), concluding that modernized constraints are a prerequisite for mitigating systematic biases in DUNE.

Presents a systematic Master's thesis study benchmarking initial-state nuclear models, resonant pion production form factors, and modernized DIS parameterizations within GENIE for DUNE neutrino-argon interactions.

Novel idea

Offers a detailed, multi-channel systematic comparison in GENIE evaluating initial-state nuclear frameworks (RFG, LFG, RPA, SuSAv2), updated resonant axial form factors ($M_A^{\rm RES}=0.96\text{ GeV}$), and 2021 Bodek-Yang DIS parameters specifically tailored to $^{40}\text{Ar}$.

Method

Uses the GENIE Monte Carlo generator to model CCQE, CCRES, and CCDIS cross sections on argon. Key results

  • Demonstrates that LFG with RPA screening naturally accounts for low-$Q^2$ suppression in CCQE without artificial $M_A$ inflation.
  • Finds that updated resonant inputs ($M_A^{\rm RES}=0.96\text{ GeV}/c^2$) and 2021 Bodek-Yang DIS settings yield significant cross-section suppressions in the $1-3\text{ GeV}$ region.
  • Reviews final-state interaction (FSI) effects where pion absorption creates fake QE topologies that distort energy reconstruction.

Why it matters to you

Provides a useful reference for generator physics models, GENIE tunes, and LArTPC cross-section systematics affecting DUNE oscillation analyses.

Caveats

As a Master's thesis, much of the text consists of introductory pedagogical reviews, and the generator simulation findings largely overlap with Paper 2610.32656 and established GENIE benchmarks.

Abstract

The recent generation of high-precision neutrino oscillation experiments, such as the Deep Underground Neutrino Experiment (DUNE), relies on the accurate reconstruction of incident neutrino energy to determine fundamental parameters like Charge-Parity (CP) violation and the neutrino mass hierarchy. The use of Liquid Argon, introduces significant theoretical complexities. This dissertation provides a investigation into neutrino-nucleus scattering phenomenologies, connecting fundamental electroweak theory with computational simulations using the GENIE Monte Carlo event generator. The study evaluates the limitations of traditional frameworks, such as the Relativistic Fermi Gas (RFG) model, specifically addressing the Axial Mass parameter and its impact on the Charged Current Quasi-Elastic (CCQE) channel. By comparing baseline RFG predictions against experimental data from MicroBooNE, this research quantifies the necessity of advanced many-body nuclear frameworks. The implementation of the Local Fermi Gas (LFG) model, Random Phase Approximation (RPA) screening, and the SuperScaling Approximation (SuSAv2) are systematically analyzed to resolve kinematic biases. Additionally, the impact of modernized phenomenological constraints, including the updated $M_A^{RES} = 0.96$ GeV for resonant pion production and the 2021 Bodek-Yang parameter updates for Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS), is evaluated. Furthermore, this research integrates university extension initiatives by developing and presenting macroscopic 3D-printed educational models, consisting of a Linear Accelerator (LINAC) analog and a scattering experiment, and finally presenting it into the festival "Mais Ci\^encia Por Favor".

10worth readingLZ limits on neutrino up-scattering

Heavy Sterile Fermions from the Atmosphere: LZ Limits on Neutrino Up-Scattering

D. Aristizabal Sierra, V. De Romeri, D. K. Papoulias, I. M. Shoemaker

hep-ph hep-exarXiv:2610.32747

Limits heavy sterile neutrino up-scattering using LZ atmospheric neutrino recoil data up to $270\text{ keV}$, showing up-scattering cannot convincingly explain LZ's single $248\text{ keV}$ event.

Novel idea

Uses high-energy nuclear recoils ($5.4-270\text{ keV}$) recorded at LZ to set the first direct-detection constraints on atmospheric neutrino up-scattering to heavy sterile fermions ($m_\chi \gtrsim 50\text{ MeV}$ to multi-GeV), and tests this mechanism against LZ's single $248\text{ keV}$ event.

Method

Constructs differential up-scattering cross sections for vector, scalar, axial, and dipole interactions using nuclear shell-model and Klein-Nystrand form factors for natural xenon. Performs a two-bin Poisson likelihood analysis on $2.84\text{ tonne-years}$ of LZ data: Bin 1 ($5.4-225\text{ keV}$) sets exclusion limits, while Bin 2 ($225-270\text{ keV}$) tests the $248\text{ keV}$ candidate.

Key results

  • Sets competitive exclusion bounds on heavy sterile up-scattering for $m_\chi \gtrsim 50\text{ MeV}$ up to several GeV, surpassing low-energy CEvNS limits and rivaling LEP bounds above $100\text{ MeV}$ for photon dipoles.
  • Places new constraints on vector/scalar mediator couplings down to $m_X \gtrsim 6\text{ MeV}$ (for $m_\chi = 10 m_X$) and $m_X \gtrsim 500\text{ MeV}$ (for $m_\chi = 0.1 m_X$).
  • Finds that explaining the $248\text{ keV}$ event requires couplings near the unphysical perturbativity limit for vector/scalar/axial mediators ($m_\chi \sim 1-20\text{ GeV}$) and fails for dipole mediators.

Why it matters to you

Highly relevant to BSM physics at neutrino and dark matter detectors (CEvNS, atmospheric neutrinos, dipole portals, heavy neutral leptons); authored by favorite authors Dimitrios Papoulias and Ian Shoemaker.

Caveats

Assumes simplified mediator scenarios and coherent nuclear scattering near the form factor minimum ($T_{\text{nr}} \sim 250\text{ keV}$); does not include incoherent nucleon scattering, which could alter high-recoil predictions.

Abstract

The LUX-ZEPLIN (LZ) experiment has recently reported data extending to nuclear-recoil energies of about 270 keV, including a single event at 248 keV in a nearly background-free region, for an exposure of 2.84 tonne-years. We examine this dataset through neutrino up-scattering to a heavy sterile fermion induced by atmospheric neutrinos, mediated by vector, axial-vector, scalar and dipole interactions. Our main result is a set of constraints: using the part of the LZ data where no excess is observed, we obtain new bounds on all four scenarios that probe regions of parameter space not tested before, and show that dark matter detectors can provide competitive limits on neutrino up-scattering for sterile fermion masses above $\sim 50$ MeV. We also test whether the 248~keV event can be explained in this way while remaining consistent with the absence of events at lower recoil energies. For the scalar, vector and axial mediators this requires couplings at the edge of perturbativity, while the dipole does not provide a good fit, so we conclude that up-scattering is not a compelling explanation of the event.

10worth readingChiral EFT neutrinoless double-beta decay

The renormalization of the shell-model neutrinoless double-beta decay operator starting from effective field theory at next-to-next-to-leading order

S. L. Lyu, U. Carusone, G. De Gregorio, N. Itaco, L. Coraggio

nucl-th hep-ex hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2610.32947

Computes shell-model $0\nu\beta\beta$ matrix elements for $^{48}\text{Ca}$, $^{76}\text{Ge}$, and $^{82}\text{Se}$ using ChEFT operators up to N2LO two-body and N3LO three-body currents, quantifying theoretical uncertainties.

Novel idea

Introduces ChEFT N3LO three-body weak currents into nuclear shell-model neutrinoless double-beta decay matrix element calculations for experimentally critical medium-mass nuclei ($^{48}\text{Ca}$, $^{76}\text{Ge}$, $^{82}\text{Se}$), using consistent chiral 2NF+3NF Hamiltonians.

Method

Constructs effective shell-model decay operators via 3rd-order many-body perturbation theory starting from Entem-Machleidt N3LO 2NF + N2LO 3NF potentials ($\Lambda=500\text{ MeV}$). Incorporates ChEFT $0\nu\beta\beta$ two-body currents up to N2LO and three-body currents at N3LO, using Padé approximants [2|1] to assess convergence and comparing local vs non-local regulators for the LO short-range contact term $g_\nu^{\rm NN}$.

Key results

  • Limits many-body perturbation theory truncation uncertainty on $M^{0\nu}$ to $\le 15\%$ for ground-state decays ($\le 10\%$ for yrare $0^+$ decays).
  • Determines that ChEFT operator expansion uncertainty is $\le 7\%$, with N3LO three-body currents contributing only a few percent.
  • Demonstrates that local vs non-local regulators for the $g_\nu^{\rm NN}$ contact term yield consistent matrix elements (e.g., $M^{0\nu} = 1.64 \pm 0.32$ for $^{76}\text{Ge}$).

Why it matters to you

Touches core interest areas in chiral EFT for electroweak processes, beta decay, nuclear matrix elements for precision probes, and effective operator renormalization.

Caveats

Restricted to a truncated shell-model valence space and depends on specific $g_\nu^{\rm NN}$ contact coupling estimates derived from $nn \to pp e^- e^-$ matching.

Abstract

This paper is the continuation of a previous study, where shell-model calculations of the matrix elements for the neutrinoless double-beta decay were performed within a fully-consistent framework, starting from expressions of the nuclear Hamiltonian and decay operators which have been derived through chiral perturbation theory. The decays under our investigation are those involving 48Ca, 76Ge, and 82Se, as in our earlier analyses of electroweak nuclear decays with Hamiltonians and operators derived within effective field theory. In this work we focus on perturbative properties of the chiral expansion of the decay operator, namely in assessing the weight of terms beyond the leading order on the nuclear matrix elements of medium-mass candidates. To this end, we have constructed effective shell-model decay operators starting from two-body currents up to next-to-next-to-leading order and, for the first time, we introduce for nuclei of experimental interest contributions of the three-body current operators, which emerge at next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order of the chiral perturbation theory. Our investigation is aimed to evaluate the theoretical uncertainties affecting the calculated nuclear matrix elements of the neutrinoless double-beta decay, by conjugating the estimate of the inaccuracy related to the finiteness of the degrees of freedom of the nuclear model and the truncation of the chiral expansion of the decay operator.

Prediction of the Geoneutrino Signal at the Jinping Neutrino Experiment

Kaikai Meng, Yang Zhang

hep-exarXiv:2610.32084

The authors present refined geoneutrino flux predictions for the 500-tonne Jinping Neutrino Experiment by combining a regional $0.2^\circ \times 0.2^\circ$ seismic velocity model with global crustal and geochemical models.

Novel idea

Previous Jinping geoneutrino calculations relied on coarse $1^\circ \times 1^\circ$ global crustal grids that do not capture local geological heterogeneity. This work builds the first dedicated near-field geophysical model of the Jinping region with $0.2^\circ \times 0.2^\circ$ resolution, using the regional SWChinaCVM-2.0 seismic tomography and ordinary kriging interpolation of regional Moho depths. It quantitatively evaluates the shift in predicted crustal uranium and thorium fluxes caused by regional refinements versus global model variations.

Method

The authors integrate regional seismic velocity and Moho interface data with the global CRUST1.0 model and a medium-$Q$ bulk silicate Earth composition. Middle and lower crustal heat-producing element abundances are dynamically inferred from regional $P$-wave velocities using empirical amphibolite- and granulite-facies mixing models. The inverse beta decay event spectrum and rates are computed for a $500\text{ t} \times 1500\text{ day}$ exposure with an assumed energy resolution of $4.4\%/\sqrt{E_{\text{vis}}/\text{MeV}}$, incorporating full reactor antineutrino backgrounds from the INFN/IAEA database.

Key results

  • The Combined Model predicts a total geoneutrino signal of $60.99^{+10.91}_{-11.00}$ TNU at Jinping, dominated by a lithospheric contribution of $51.39^{+11.57}_{-11.62}$ TNU.
  • The near-field crustal refinement increases the geoneutrino signal by 1.35 TNU ($\sim 2\%$) over CRUST1.0 alone, while switching the global model to ECM1 reduces the flux by 6.30 TNU ($\sim 10\%$).
  • The reactor antineutrino background is calculated to be 27.59 TNU, yielding approximately 41 reactor IBD events compared to 90 expected geoneutrino events.
  • The upper crust contributes roughly 70% of the lithospheric signal and nearly 60% of the total geoneutrino signal at the site.

Why it matters to you

Provides experimental inputs and background benchmarks for low-energy antineutrino detection at CJPL. However, its focus is predominantly on geophysics and crustal geochemical modeling rather than neutrino-nucleus interaction physics or electroweak BSM searches.

Caveats

Global crustal model ambiguities (CRUST1.0 vs. ECM1) induce a $\sim 10\%$ systematic variation that easily outweighs the local near-field seismic refinement. Middle- and lower-crust elemental distributions rely on simplified binary felsic-mafic petrological relations.

Abstract

We present refined predictions of the geoneutrino flux for the 500-tonne Jinping Neutrino Experiment (JNE). We construct a local crustal model with a resolution of \(0.2^\circ\times0.2^\circ\) based on the SWChinaCVM-2.0 seismic velocity model and integrate it with CRUST1.0 to establish our reference model. For comparison, we also calculate the geoneutrino flux using ECM1 and CRUST1.0 as standalone global crustal models. Assuming an energy scale of 500~p.e./MeV and an exposure of 500~t$\times$1500~days, we predict the inverse beta decay event spectrum, including both the geoneutrino signal and the reactor antineutrino background. The distinct spectral shapes of the $^{238}$U, $^{232}$Th, and reactor antineutrino components allow their respective contributions to be obtained through spectral fitting. These predictions provide inputs for the JNE data analysis, particularly for extracting the mantle geoneutrino signal and testing competing bulk silicate Earth models.

9skimTimelike Compton scattering on deuteron

Timelike Compton scattering on a deuteron target

Jing Han, Bo-Qiang Ma, Bernard Pire, Qin-Tao Song

nucl-th hep-ex hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2610.32741

The authors evaluate coherent timelike Compton scattering on the deuteron, computing leading-order cross sections and formulating next-to-leading-order QCD corrections to access deuteron generalized parton distributions and gluon transversity.

Novel idea

While timelike Compton scattering (TCS) has been measured on the proton, this study extends the formalism to coherent scattering on a spin-1 deuteron target. It demonstrates how Bethe-Heitler-TCS interference and forward-backward lepton asymmetries isolate spin-1 generalized parton distributions (GPDs), and develops the complete NLO formalism identifying unique dilepton angular harmonics generated by gluon transversity.

Method

The coherent reaction $\gamma d \to \ell^+ \ell^- d$ is analyzed within collinear QCD factorization in the generalized Bjorken limit. The deuteron quark GPDs are computed using a light-front impulse convolution model combining Goloskokov-Kroll nucleon GPDs with an Argonne $v_{18}$ deuteron wave function. The Bethe-Heitler background is evaluated with soft-wall AdS/QCD electromagnetic form factors, focusing on JLab kinematics ($s \approx 30\text{--}37\text{ GeV}^2$, $2 < Q < 3\text{ GeV}$, $\eta \approx 0.1$).

Key results

  • The pure TCS cross section is approximately one order of magnitude smaller than the Bethe-Heitler cross section across the forward low-$|t|$ region.
  • The BH-TCS interference term has a magnitude comparable to the pure TCS cross section and changes sign at $|t| \approx 0.35\text{ GeV}^2$ for $\eta = 0.08$ and $|t| \approx 0.64\text{ GeV}^2$ for $\eta = 0.12$.
  • The forward-backward asymmetry $A_{\text{FB}}$ directly extracts the BH-TCS interference and is predominantly sensitive to the unpolarized deuteron GPD $H_1$ at small $|t|$ and $\eta$.
  • Gluon transversity GPDs enter at order $\alpha_s$ and give rise to distinctive $\cos(2\phi)$ and $\cos(3\phi)$ angular modulations in the dilepton center-of-mass frame.

Why it matters to you

While primarily focused on electromagnetic QCD observables at JLab, the impulse convolution framework and nuclear wave function treatments touch nuclear structure modeling and off-forward matrix elements relevant to lepton-nucleus reactions.

Caveats

Numerical estimates are restricted to leading-twist LO in $\alpha_s$, with NLO gluon-transversity predictions awaiting dedicated deuteron gluon GPD parameterizations, and nucleon-nucleon final-state interactions in deuteron breakup must be experimentally vetoed.

Abstract

We investigate timelike Compton scattering (TCS) on a deuteron target as a probe of the three-dimensional partonic structure of the deuteron encoded in generalized parton distributions (GPDs). Such measurements may provide new insight into the internal structure of the deuteron, and help to clarify the unresolved tensor-polarization puzzle. We present numerical estimates of the leading-order (in the strong coupling $\alpha_s$) TCS cross section in the kinematic region relevant to future measurements at Jefferson Lab. The deuteron GPDs are evaluated within a convolution model, while the Bethe-Heitler contribution is calculated using a phenomenological parametrization of the deuteron electromagnetic form factors. In addition, we derive the theoretical expressions for the next-to-leading-order corrections to deuteron TCS, with particular emphasis on contributions from the gluon transversity GPDs. We show that these contributions can be isolated through their characteristic angular dependence in the center-of-mass frame of the produced lepton pair. Our results demonstrate the potential of deuteron TCS as a complementary probe of both the quark and gluon structure of the deuteron at Jefferson Lab.

hep-ph hep-th quant-pharXiv:2610.31717

The authors propose pre-exciting the $\mathrm{TM}_{010}$ mode in a microwave cavity haloscope to generate a first-order response linear in $g_{a\gamma\gamma}$, detectable via quadrature balanced homodyne detection.

Novel idea

Standard axion haloscopes measure an in-cavity power scaling quadratically as $g_{a\gamma\gamma}^2$ because the mode begins in a passive vacuum state. This proposal pre-excites a steady-state coherent $\mathrm{TM}_{010}$ seed field inside the cavity, producing an in situ interference that yields a first-order power response linear in $g_{a\gamma\gamma}$ that can be measured despite the stochastic phase of the axion dark matter field.

Method

The axion-modified Maxwell equations are solved in a 3D cylindrical resonant cavity in the presence of an axial static magnetic field $\bar{B}$ and a driven $\mathrm{TM}_{010}$ mode. The authors propose an I/Q quadrature balanced homodyne readout to reconstruct the first-order power amplitude independently of the axion phase. A complete noise propagation analysis is formulated including pump relative intensity noise (RIN), pump phase noise, shot noise, and cavity thermal noise.

Key results

  • The detectable signal power amplitude scales linearly with $g_{a\gamma\gamma}$, amplifying the detectable amplitude by up to 8 orders of magnitude over passive second-order haloscopes.
  • For benchmark parameters ($B=8\text{ T}$, $\tilde{B}_0=3\ \mu\text{T}$, $Q=10^4$, $V=5.13\text{ L}$), the first-order amplitude evaluates to $P_s^{(1)} \approx 8.7 \times 10^{-19}\text{ W}$, compared to second-order power $P_s^{(2)} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-32}\text{ W}$.
  • The authors argue that seed-field shot-noise suppression of thermal fluctuations lowers the system's equivalent noise temperature to $\sim 0.7\text{ K}$, claiming room-temperature sensitivity surpassing DFSZ and KSVZ benchmarks.

Why it matters to you

Concerns light BSM dark matter detection techniques, but microwave engineering of cavity haloscopes is outside your primary research program in neutrino interactions, event generators, and precision low-energy EFTs.

Caveats

The claim of reaching sub-Kelvin equivalent noise at room temperature relies heavily on ideal balanced homodyne cancellations and strong assumptions about pump relative intensity noise (RIN) and phase noise that may be hard to realize in practical setups.

Abstract

Microwave cavities in strong magnetic fields are among the most sensitive detectors for axion dark matter detection. Its sensitivity, however, is fundamentally limited by the second order nature of axion photon energy conversion, which yields an extremely weak signal. Here we propose a feasible approach to break this limitation, by pre-exciting the target cavity mode to generate a first order power response signal in situ. The resulting signal amplitude scales linearly with the axion-photon coupling parameter, which is in sharp contrast to the quadratic scaling of conventional schemes, and is detectable via standard quadrature balanced homodyne detection system, even though its phase remains stochastic. Numerical simulations demonstrate that this pre-excited seed field can effectively suppress thermal noise, and thus the detection sensitivity of the system can approach the lower quantum noise limit. Consequently, the sensitivities predicted by the KSVZ and DFSZ models could be surpassed. This proposal might offer a pathway to a major leap in axion dark matter searches, as it is within reach of current technology.

Bayesian analysis of the Coulomb breakup of C19

Quentin Bozet, Pierre Capel, Isak Svensson

nucl-tharXiv:2610.32331

A Bayesian framework combining Halo-EFT at next-to-leading order with the Coulomb-corrected eikonal approximation extracts the one-neutron separation energy and asymptotic normalization constant of $^{19}\text{C}$ from Coulomb breakup data.

Novel idea

This paper presents the first systematic Bayesian inference applied to the Coulomb breakup of a one-neutron halo nucleus ($^{19}\text{C}$). By coupling NLO Halo effective field theory to the computationally efficient Coulomb-corrected eikonal (CCE) approximation, it systematically maps the posterior distribution of EFT low-energy constants (LECs) directly to physical halo structure observables while quantifying statistical and model-space uncertainties.

Method

The $^{19}\text{C}$ projectile is treated as an effective $^{18}\text{C}(0^+) + n$ system interacting via an NLO Halo-EFT potential regularized by a Gaussian cutoff $r_0$. The three-body reaction on $^{208}\text{Pb}$ at 67 MeV/nucleon is modeled using the CCE formalism. Importance sampling ($10^6$ samples) is used to infer posterior distributions from RIKEN breakup cross-section data, testing uniform and Gaussian priors across cutoffs $r_0 \in [1.5, 2.5]\text{ fm}$, complemented by sensitivity scans over core-target optical potentials and continuum partial waves.

Key results

  • The extracted one-neutron separation energy is $S_n(^{19}\text{C}) = 0.60 \pm 0.02\text{ (stat)} \pm 0.09\text{ (syst)}\text{ MeV}$, significantly refining the Atomic Mass Evaluation value ($0.58 \pm 0.10\text{ MeV}$).
  • The asymptotic normalization constant of the $1/2^+$ ground state is inferred to be $\mathcal{C}_{1/2^+} = 0.85 \pm 0.02\text{ (stat)} \pm 0.13\text{ (syst)}\text{ fm}^{-1/2}$, confirming an extended halo wave function.
  • Inferred structure observables are strictly invariant under changes of the EFT cutoff $r_0 \in [1.5, 2.5]\text{ fm}$, confirming the peripheral nature of the reaction.
  • Model systematic uncertainties are dominated by the nuclear optical potential radii and continuum $p$-wave dynamics, each contributing $\sim 10\%$, motivating an overall $\sim 15\%$ theoretical uncertainty.
  • Restricting the experimental dataset to $E < 1.2\text{ MeV}$ shifts the maximum a posteriori value to $S_n = 0.52 \pm 0.02\text{ MeV}$, illustrating the sensitivity to potential continuum resonances.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to your work on low-energy EFTs for electroweak and nuclear processes, carbon-isotope nuclear structure, and eikonal reaction frameworks. It demonstrates a fast, robust Bayesian method for constraining nuclear bound-state inputs (ANCs and binding energies) with rigorous theoretical error bars.

Caveats

Optical potential parameters and higher continuum partial waves ($p$ and $d$ waves) were explored via one-at-a-time variations rather than fully marginalized within the Bayesian prior.

Equation of state of hot and dense QCD using the complex Langevin equation

The authors compute the QCD equation of state at physical pion mass up to $\mu_B/T \sim 10$ in the high-temperature regime using continuum-extrapolated complex Langevin simulations.

Novel idea

This work performs the first complex Langevin simulations of lattice QCD with physical quark masses ($2+1+1$ flavors) to evaluate the finite-density equation of state directly without importance-sampling sign problems, Taylor expansions, or imaginary chemical potential continuations, successfully accessing baryon chemical potential to temperature ratios up to $\mu_B/T \approx 10$.

Method

The simulations use $2+1+1$ flavors of staggered quarks with four steps of stout smearing and a tree-level Symanzik improved gauge action on $N_s^3 \times N_t$ lattices ($N_s/N_t = 2$, $N_t \in \{10, 12, 16\}$). Gauge cooling and adaptive step sizes are employed to stabilize the SL$(3,\mathbb{C})$ Langevin evolution. Simulations enforce strangeness neutrality, charm neutrality, and isospin symmetry across $T \in [260, 600]\text{ MeV}$. The pressure and trace anomaly are extracted from numerical integration and temperature derivatives of the measured baryon density.

Key results

  • Continuum-extrapolated results for baryon density, pressure, and trace anomaly are established for physical pion mass up to unprecedentedly high $\mu_B/T \lesssim 10$.
  • The dimensionless baryon density $\hat{n}_B = n_B/T^3$ shows a predominantly cubic dependence on temperature at fixed $\hat{\mu}_B$ in the small-to-moderate $\hat{\mu}_B$ regime.
  • The complex Langevin equation of state agrees with hard-thermal-loop perturbation theory and Taylor-resummed hybrid Monte Carlo simulations in the overlapping region of validity ($\mu_B/T \lesssim 3$).
  • The trace anomaly $\hat{I}$ increases with $\hat{\mu}_B$ at the lower temperatures studied and decreases monotonically with increasing $T$.

A technical breakthrough in finite-density lattice QCD methods, but it focuses on high-temperature quark-gluon plasma thermodynamics and heavy-ion physics, which is peripheral to your core interests in neutrino scattering and precision low-energy nuclear structure.

Caveats

The method is currently restricted to temperatures well above the chiral crossover ($T \ge 260\text{ MeV}$) because complex Langevin drift singularities near Dirac operator zero modes destabilize the stochastic trajectories at lower temperatures.
