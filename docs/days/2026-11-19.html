10must-readNeutrino decay

Geoneutrinos (and reactor neutrinos) do not visibly decay

Mauricio Bustamante

hep-ph hep-ex hep-tharXiv:2611.23978

The first search for visible decay of $\nu_1$ using geoneutrinos and reactor $\bar{\nu}_e$ at Borexino, KamLAND, SNO+, and JUNO sets a 90% C.L. limit $\tau_1/m_1 > 3.7 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s/eV}$.

Novel idea

This paper presents the first search for BSM neutrino decay utilizing geoneutrinos, taking advantage of their low energies (few MeV) and long terrestrial baselines (up to Earth's diameter). It builds a detailed visible decay framework that includes energy-dependent daughter flux regeneration across eight decay channels. The analysis fits published prompt energy spectra from four major liquid scintillator experiments under three bulk silicate Earth models as well as with free radiogenic heat power.

Method

The author computes two-body decays $\nu_i \to \nu_j + \phi$ incorporating matter oscillations and exact spectral regeneration of daughter neutrinos. Binned Poisson likelihood fits are performed on published prompt positron energy spectra from Borexino, KamLAND, SNO+, and JUNO, treating 75 background, energy scale, mixing, and geochemical parameters as nuisances. Lower limits on $\tau_i/m_i$ are extracted via profile likelihood ratios at 90% and 95% C.L.

Key results

  • Sets the first terrestrial limit on the visible decay $\nu_1 \to \nu_3$ in the inverted mass ordering: $\tau_1/m_1 > 3.7 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s eV}^{-1}$ at 90% C.L. with Earth radiogenic power left free.
  • With bulk silicate Earth models assumed, the combined 90% C.L. limit strengthens to $4.4 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s eV}^{-1}$ (geodynamical Earth) up to $1.6 \times 10^{-8}\text{ s eV}^{-1}$ (cosmochemical Earth).
  • Establishes that geoneutrinos dominate the $\nu_1$ decay limit under fixed Earth models, whereas reactor $\bar{\nu}_e$ contribute equally when radiogenic power is unconstrained.
  • Sets bounds on $\nu_2$ and $\nu_3$ decays primarily via reactor antineutrinos (notably JUNO), yielding $\tau_2/m_2 > 1.5 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s eV}^{-1}$ and $\tau_3/m_3 > 1.0 \times 10^{-10}\text{ s eV}^{-1}$ with free Earth power.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts low-energy neutrino phenomenology, geoneutrino physics, and BSM decay searches. Provides concrete terrestrial baseline bounds that complement astrophysical/cosmological constraints and informs low-energy systematic studies at DUNE, JUNO, and SNO+.

Caveats

Limits vary by a factor of 3–4 across different bulk silicate Earth models, and KamLAND's mild deficit of geoneutrinos produces a localized $2.7\sigma$ preference for decay under the geodynamical Earth model.

Abstract

Neutrinos that decay on their way to a detector arrive depleted, more so at low energies, where time dilation lengthens their lifetime the least. Geoneutrinos---electron antineutrinos from the radioactive decay of uranium and thorium inside the Earth---have energies of a few MeV and travel up to the diameter of the Earth. We use them to test neutrino decay for the first time. We compute the geoneutrino signal at Borexino, KamLAND, SNO+, and JUNO for visible decay, in which the daughter neutrinos are themselves detected, and compare it against their published data. We find no evidence of decay that holds across Earth models. Combining the four experiments, we limit the decay $\nu_1 \to \nu_3$ in the inverted mass ordering at 90% C.L.: $\tau_1/m_1 > 3.7 \times 10^{-9}$ s eV$^{-1}$ when the radiogenic power of the Earth is left free and $\tau_1/m_1 > 4.4 \times 10^{-9}$ to $1.6 \times 10^{-8}$ s eV$^{-1}$ when it is taken from Earth models. With an Earth model assumed, the geoneutrinos set the limit; with the power free, the antineutrinos from nuclear reactors that the same experiments detect count as much as the geoneutrinos. These are the first terrestrial limits on the visible decay of $\nu_1$.

10worth readingNeutrino self-interactions

On the cosmic window for neutrino self-interactions

Ivan Pérez-Castro, Jorge Venzor, Josue De-Santiago, Pablo Roig, Abdel Pérez-Lorenzana

hep-ph astro-ph.COarXiv:2611.24674

A self-consistent cosmic neutrino self-interaction ($\nu$SI) framework incorporating non-zero background collision terms shows resonant mediators yield $\Delta N_{\text{eff}}^{\text{CMB}} \approx 0$ and tighter coupling bounds.

Novel idea

The authors derive modified Boltzmann hierarchy equations (BHEs) that explicitly account for non-zero zeroth-order collision terms ($C_{\nu}^{(0)} \neq 0$) in cosmological neutrino self-interactions. They show that low-energy renormalizable extensions leading to $\nu$SI intrinsically feature resonant mediator behavior ($T_\nu \sim M$), invalidating standard relativistic fluid assumptions and BBN bounds derived from unsuppressed $2\leftrightarrow 1$ thermalization.

Method

Linear perturbation BHEs in conformal Newtonian gauge are modified by introducing background energy density evolution parameters ($\Delta_i$). Neutrino scattering and inverse decay rates are calculated in the complex-mass scheme with the narrow-width approximation. Cosmological perturbation signatures on the CMB TT power spectrum are evaluated using an effective relaxation-time approximation (eRTA) implemented within CLASS.

Key results

  • Demonstrates that scalar-mediated resonant $\nu$SI leads to $\Delta N_{\rm eff}^{\rm CMB} \approx 0$ at the CMB epoch, in contrast to the canonical $\Delta N_{\rm eff} \simeq 0.12$ derived under fluid approximations.
  • Delineates two distinct cosmological $\nu$SI regimes: (i) tightly coupled background evolution before equality, and (ii) pure resonant scattering with negligible background evolution for mediator masses $M \sim 10^{-6}\text{--}1\text{ MeV}$.
  • Finds that CMB TT spectrum enhancements from resonant $\nu$SI are most pronounced for mediator masses $m_\phi \lesssim 1\text{ keV}$.
  • Shows that self-interacting dark radiation models matching $\nu$SI phenomenology require larger couplings ($\lambda_{\phi\chi}$) than previously estimated due to reduced scalar energy densities.

Why it matters to you

Extremely relevant for BSM neutrino interactions, Majoron/light mediator models, and cosmological constraints on non-standard interactions (NSI/$\nu$SI). Corrects standard literature assumptions about $\Delta N_{\text{eff}}$ and background evolution in Boltzmann solvers.

Caveats

Perturbative power spectra rely on an effective relaxation-time approximation (eRTA), and full numerical implementation of momentum-dependent background evolution within CLASS remains to be finalized.

Abstract

Neutrino self-interactions ($\nu$SI) beyond the Standard Model have arisen as a promising probe of new physics in cosmology. In this Letter, we show that $\nu$SI emerging in the low-energy limit of renormalizable theories implies resonant mediator behavior. We identify two regions for $\nu$SI: (i) neutrinos become tightly coupled with background evolution; for the first time, we establish how this evolution modifies the Boltzmann hierarchy equations (BHEs), and (ii) resonant $\nu$SI dominates the dynamics with negligible background evolution, relevant for mediator masses $\sim 10^{-6}-1$ MeV. In region (i), before matter-radiation equality, neutrino free-streaming is determined by the effective four-fermion interaction, known as the heavy-mediator limit (HML); we explore the impact of background evolution on this scenario, and from our modified BHEs, we show that self-interacting dark radiation scenarios require larger couplings than previously estimated to mimic $\nu$SI. Region (ii) shows that the simplest realization of $\nu$SI mediated by a (pseudo)scalar particle leads to an extra radiation contribution of $\Delta N_{\rm eff}^{\rm CMB} \approx 0$, instead of the commonly quoted $\simeq 0.12$, at the CMB epoch. Overall, we highlight that our findings should be considered in any plausible scenario involving $\nu$SI.

10must-readSuperallowed beta decay

Three-nucleon potentials and their matrix elements for $\delta_{\mathrm{NS}}$ in superallowed $\beta$ decays

Graham Chambers-Wall, Maria Dawid, Emanuele Mereghetti, Saori Pastore, Maria Piarulli

nucl-th hep-pharXiv:2611.24825

Derives $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_chi^2)$ three-nucleon potentials for $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$ in superallowed $\beta$ decay, showing $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ 3N terms shift $V_{ud}$ by $\sim 10^{-4}$ while $\mathcal{O}(\alpha\epsilon_chi^2)$ corrections are $\sim 2\%$.

Novel idea

The paper derives the full set of three-nucleon (3N) electroweak radiative correction potentials at order $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_chi^2)$ in chiral effective field theory for the nuclear-structure-dependent correction $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$. Using CVC and current algebra, the authors explicitly prove that vector-vector (VV) 3N contributions cancel out, leaving only vector-axial (VA) operators to be evaluated in nuclear matrix elements.

Method

Using Heavy Baryon Chiral Perturbation Theory (HB$\chi$PT), 21 electroweak 3N diagrams are derived and reduced via CVC constraints. Variational and Green's Function Monte Carlo (VMC/GFMC) methods with Norfolk chiral two- and three-nucleon interactions (NV2+3) are used to compute 3N matrix elements for ${}^6\text{He}\to{}^6\text{Li}$ and ${}^{10}\text{C}\to{}^{10}\text{B}$. The extracted matrix elements are used to re-evaluate $V_{ud}$ from ${}^{10}\text{C}$.

Key results

  • Proves that vector-vector (VV) 3N radiative corrections cancel identically via CVC, leaving only vector-axial (VA) 3N operators for $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$.
  • Computes $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ 3N matrix elements, showing they scale as $Z^2$ and shift $V_{ud}$ by $\sim 10^{-4}$, demonstrating their necessity for precision CKM unitarity tests.
  • Evaluates $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_chi^2)$ 3N matrix elements in ${}^{10}\text{C} \to {}^{10}\text{B}$, finding a small $\approx 2\%$ correction to $\bar{\delta}_{\text{NS}}$, confirming good chiral EFT convergence.
  • Extracts an updated value of $V_{ud} = 0.97386(61)$ from the superallowed ${}^{10}\text{C}$ Fermi decay, shifting it closer to neutron decay determinations.

Why it matters to you

Directly impacts precision electroweak physics, beta decay radiative corrections, CKM $V_{ud}$ unitarity, chiral EFT electroweak operators, and ab initio QMC calculations of electroweak responses.

Caveats

Calculations of $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_chi^2)$ 3N contributions were performed only for light nuclei (${}^{10}\text{C}$); their relative magnitude in medium-mass nuclei ($A \ge 14$), where two-body magnetic terms are suppressed, remains to be calculated.

Abstract

We derive three-nucleon potentials for radiative corrections at order $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_\chi^2)$ in chiral effective field theory where $\alpha$ is the electromagnetic coupling and $\epsilon_\chi$ is the chiral expansion parameter. These corrections contribute to the nuclear-structure-dependent term $\delta_\mathrm{NS}$ for determinations of the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix element $V_{ud}$ from superallowed nuclear $\beta$ decays. We compute matrix elements of three-nucleon contributions at orders $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ and $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_\chi^2)$ using quantum Monte Carlo methods. We find the matrix elements at order $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2)$ to be large and to have an effect on the extraction of $V_{ud}$ at the scale of $10^{-4}$. In the case of $^{10}$C, the matrix elements of $\mathcal{O}(\alpha \epsilon_\chi^2)$ three-nucleon operators provide a 2% correction to $\delta_{\text{NS}}$, and are thus beyond the current precision goals. The relative size of three-nucleon corrections to $\delta_{\text{NS}}$ in medium mass nuclei, where two-body contributions tend to be smaller, remains to be investigated. We then use these results to determine $V_{ud}$ from the superallowed transition ${}^{10}\mathrm{C}\rightarrow{}^{10}\mathrm{B}$.

Producing $\mu$Bq/m3-level low-226Ra ultrapure water for the Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory

Xi-Ting Xu, Cong Guo, Jin-Chang Liu, Yong-Peng Zhang, Xiao-Hua Liang, Xin-Jian Wen + 5 more

physics.ins-det hep-exarXiv:2611.23711 · cross-list

JUNO's 100 t/h ultrapure water system achieves a Ra-226 concentration of $<4\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$, exceeding the experiment's $<50\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$ background requirement.

Novel idea

Reports the design, engineering, and performance of JUNO's 100 t/h ultrapure water (UPW) system, which achieves an ultra-low ${}^{226}\text{Ra}$ concentration of $<4\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$. A custom assay system based on Mn-fiber adsorption and radon emanation was developed, attaining an unprecedented measurement sensitivity of $3.9\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$ for radium in water.

Method

The purification process combines multi-media filters, dedicated cationic softening resin, and two-stage reverse osmosis (RO). ${}^{226}\text{Ra}$ concentration is measured by concentrating radium on synthesized Mn-fibers, transferring emanated ${}^{222}\text{Rn}$ into an electropolished chamber, and counting alpha decays from ${}^{214}\text{Po}$ via electrostatic collection detectors over 24-hour acquisition windows.

Key results

  • Reduced water ${}^{226}\text{Ra}$ concentration from $\sim 4 \times 10^5\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$ in raw source water to $<4\ \mu\text{Bq/m}^3$ in product UPW ($>99.999\%$ overall reduction).
  • Determined that cationic softening resin and two-stage RO each independently achieve $>99\%$ ${}^{226}\text{Ra}$ removal efficiency.
  • Identified sea-salt regenerant sediment in softening tanks and unfiltered air ingress through tank breather valves as the main post-purification recontamination pathways.

Why it matters to you

Relevant as background baseline context for JUNO low-energy neutrino, geoneutrino, and supernova physics. However, the content is purely experimental facility engineering and detector instrumentation.

Caveats

High-resistivity water sampling data was incomplete due to sensor hardware limitations, and complete tank cleaning operations require future system shutdowns.

Abstract

The Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory (JUNO), a 20 kton low-background liquid-scintillator (LS) detector, was designed primarily to determine the neutrino mass ordering. JUNO requires ultrapure water (UPW) with a Ra concentration < 50~$\mu$Bq/m3 because of the direct liquid-liquid contact between LS and UPW during detector filling. To meet this stringent requirement, a high-sensitivity measurement system capable of detecting Ra in water down to 3.9 $\mu$Bq/m3 was developed, and the 100 t/h UPW production process was optimized. By integrating selective ion-exchange resin with membrane-based separation technologies, the system consistently produced UPW with a Ra concentration of <4$\mu$Bq/m3, exceeding the JUNO specification and, to our knowledge, representing one of the lowest reported levels achieved at this engineering scale. This paper describes the design and implementation of JUNO's UPW system and the high-sensitivity Ra measurement platform, together with a systematic evaluation of Ra removal efficiencies across the purification train and validation of the final water quality.

9worth readingTwist-4 QCD factorization

Twist-4 QCD Factorization for One-Point Correlators at Next-to-Leading Order in Electron-Ion Collisions

Omar Elgedawy, Zhong-Bo Kang, Hongxi Xing

hep-pharXiv:2611.23394

Establishes the first proof of NLO QCD twist-4 factorization for OPEC and OPCC angular widths in semi-inclusive DIS off nuclear targets.

Novel idea

The paper delivers the first formal proof of next-to-leading order (NLO) collinear QCD factorization for twist-4 power corrections to one-point energy and charge correlators (OPEC and OPCC) in electron-nucleus semi-inclusive DIS ($e+A$ SIDIS). It proves that medium-enhanced final-state soft and collinear singularities cancel completely, proving IRC safety at twist-4.

Method

The authors apply the higher-twist expansion framework to compute NLO real and virtual double-scattering diagrams in $e+A$ SIDIS. They calculate medium-induced transverse momentum broadening of the angular spread $\Delta \langle \sin^2 \theta \rangle$ and express the factorization in terms of nuclear matrix elements $T_{qg}(x,x_g)$ and fragmentation moments.

Key results

  • Proves explicit cancellation of all medium-associated final-state soft and collinear divergences at twist-4 NLO for OPEC and OPCC angular spreads.
  • Demonstrates that fragmentation function dependence reduces strictly to universal momentum ($M_q=1$) and charge ($M_q=Q_q$) moments.
  • Shows numerically that nuclear angular broadening grows linearly with nuclear size ($A^{1/3}$) and exhibits characteristic $1/Q^2$ power suppression.
  • Finds that OPCC broadening is smaller than OPEC due to the decoupling of charge-neutral gluons in charge-weighted correlators.

Why it matters to you

Directly relevant to lepton-nucleus/electron-scattering interactions, QCD factorization, nuclear medium effects, and cold nuclear matter studies for the upcoming Electron-Ion Collider (EIC).

Caveats

Numerical predictions incorporate only diagonal soft rescattering matrix elements; off-diagonal hard rescattering matrix elements remain unconstrained by existing nuclear data.

Abstract

Energy and charge correlators have emerged as powerful, infrared- and collinear-safe (IRC-safe) observables for probing QCD dynamics in the vacuum, established within the leading-twist factorization formalism. However, their IRC safety and factorization properties in a nuclear medium, where higher-twist contributions are critically enhanced, remain unproven. In this letter, we present the first proof of next-to-leading order QCD factorization for one-point energy and charge correlators (OPEC and OPCC) in electron-ion collisions at twist-4. Working within the higher-twist expansion framework, we calculate the twist-4 power corrections to the angular widths of the OPEC and OPCC, and explicitly demonstrate that the IRC singularities cancel and fragmentation-function dependence reduces to momentum and charge moments for final-state medium-enhanced observables in semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering. Supported by numerical evaluations, this framework establishes OPEC and OPCC angular widths as sensitive probes of higher-twist dynamics and fundamental properties of cold nuclear matter.

Gravitational Form Factors and Transverse Mechanical Structure of the Charged Kaon in a Two-Fock-Sector Light-Front Hamiltonian

Parashmani Thakuria, Madhurjya Lalung, Jayanta Kumar Sarma

hep-pharXiv:2611.23922

Charged-kaon electromagnetic and gravitational form factors are calculated in a light-front Hamiltonian with coupled $|u\bar{s}\rangle$ and $|u\bar{s}g\rangle$ Fock sectors, finding gluons carry only $2\%$ momentum but contribute $42\%$ to the $D$-term.

Novel idea

The work extends light-front Hamiltonian meson gravitational form factor calculations beyond the valence sector by including an explicit dynamical gluon Fock sector. This enables resolving the separate quark, antiquark, and gluon contributions to momentum and shear distributions using a projected Belinfante energy-momentum tensor that includes diagonal, sector-changing, and constrained-field contact terms.

Method

The coupled $|u\bar{s}\rangle \oplus |u\bar{s}g\rangle$ Hamiltonian is solved by Rayleigh-Ritz projection onto longitudinal Jacobi and full-plane transverse harmonic-oscillator bases, with coupling calibrated to the physical kaon mass. Momentum form factors are computed from $T^{++}$ overlaps in the Drell-Yan frame, while the mechanical $D$ form factor is extracted from the off-diagonal transverse shear $T^{12}$. Two rational UV continuations beyond $Q^2 = 2\text{ GeV}^2$ are matched to evaluate 2D Fourier-Bessel transverse pressure and shear profiles.

Key results

  • Forward momentum form factors at $\mu_0 = 0.58\text{ GeV}$ are $A_u(0) = 0.354$, $A_{\bar{s}}(0) = 0.625$, and $A_g(0) = 0.0205$, showing strange-quark momentum dominance.
  • Obtains $D_K(0) = -2.03610$, with the gluon carrying $42.4\%$ of the total mechanical term despite carrying only $2.05\%$ of the longitudinal momentum.
  • Calculated charge radius is $r_{\text{ch}} = 0.5178 \pm 0.0278\text{ fm}$ and transverse momentum radius is $r_{A,\perp} = 0.3394 \pm 0.0113\text{ fm}$.
  • Transverse mechanical radii are $0.4616 \pm 0.0130\text{ fm}$ (Ansatz 1) and $0.4768 \pm 0.0132\text{ fm}$ (Ansatz 2), demonstrating that central pressure is highly sensitive to the UV completion.
  • Both UV continuations yield positive internal pressure at small transverse distance and negative pressure at outer radii, fulfilling mechanical stability.

Why it matters to you

Although kaon mechanical structure is peripheral to neutrino-nucleus cross sections, the treatment of multi-sector light-front wavefunctions, operator overlap contractions, and EMT matrix elements provides reference methodology for hadronic matrix element calculations.

Caveats

The model omits dynamical sea-quark pairs ($|u\bar{s}q\bar{q}\rangle$), and the transverse pressure at small impact parameter depends significantly on the chosen rational UV continuation above $2\text{ GeV}^2$.

Abstract

We study the electromagnetic and gravitational form factors of the charged kaon in a light-front Hamiltonian model with coupled $|u\bar s\rangle$ and $|u\bar s g\rangle$ Fock sectors. We solve the coupled Hamiltonian in a longitudinal Jacobi and transverse harmonic-oscillator basis, with the interaction strength fixed by the kaon mass. The momentum form factors are extracted from $T^{++}$, while the stress form factors are obtained from the transverse shear projection of the QCD Belinfante energy-momentum tensor, including diagonal, sector-changing, and constrained-field contributions. The strange antiquark carries the largest fraction of the kaon's longitudinal momentum, whereas the gluon carries only $2.05\%$. Despite this small momentum fraction, the gluon contributes $42.4\%$ of $|D_K(0)|$, for which we obtain $D_K(0)=-2.03610$. The charge and transverse momentum radii are $0.5178\pm0.0278\,\mathrm{fm}$ and $0.3394\pm0.0113\,\mathrm{fm}$, respectively, with uncertainties estimated from mass and basis variations. We obtain the transverse mechanical distributions using two ultraviolet continuations of the directly calculated $D$ form factors beyond $Q^2=2\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$. Both continuations give positive pressure at small transverse distances and negative pressure at larger distances. The corresponding transverse mechanical radii are $0.4616\pm0.0130\,\mathrm{fm}$ and $0.4768\pm0.0132\,\mathrm{fm}$, both larger than the transverse momentum radius. The central pressure changes substantially between the two continuations, whereas the pressure zero and mechanical radii vary much less.

$\Lambda$-deuteron scattering below the $\Sigma$-threshold

A. Deltuva

nucl-th nucl-exarXiv:2611.24538

Multichannel three-body AGS equations are solved in momentum space for $\Lambda d$ scattering below the $\Sigma$ threshold, identifying observables sensitive to hyperon-nucleon forces and validating single-channel approximations at low energies.

Novel idea

This paper presents the first fully converged momentum-space calculation of $\Lambda d$ elastic scattering, breakup reactions, and spin observables below the $\Sigma$ threshold that fully retains the four-sector Hilbert space $(\Lambda pn \oplus \Sigma^+ nn \oplus \Sigma^0 pn \oplus \Sigma^- pp)$ across all relevant partial waves ($J \le 39/2$). It rigorously assesses the validity of the widely used single-channel approximation where $\Sigma$ channels are truncated in three-body equations.

Method

Alt-Grassberger-Sandhas (AGS) multichannel integral equations are solved directly in the momentum-space partial-wave basis for three-body transition operators. Nuclear dynamics are modeled using chiral N3LO and CD-Bonn $NN$ potentials alongside NLO chiral EFT $YN$ potentials (NLO19) with cutoffs varied from $500$ to $700\text{ MeV}$, including screened Coulomb interactions in the closed $\Sigma^- pp$ channel.

Key results

  • Doublet $\Lambda d$ scattering length is moderately model-independent ($18\text{--}21\text{ fm}$), whereas the quartet scattering length varies dramatically ($-9.5$ to $-30.7\text{ fm}$) due to a virtual-state pole near threshold.
  • The $\Lambda d$ breakup cross section exhibits a sharp increase approaching the $\Sigma$ threshold driven by a resonant $J^\Pi = 1/2^+$, isospin-1 state.
  • Semi-inclusive proton momentum and angular spectra feature prominent peaks from quasi-free $\Lambda n$ scattering around $E_p \approx 14\text{ MeV}$, providing a sensitive probe of unmeasured $\Lambda n$ forces.
  • Neglecting $\Sigma$ coupling in three-body equations is accurate within $10\text{--}20\%$ at low and intermediate energies, but breaks down severely near the $\Sigma$ threshold (up to $90\%$ deviations in elastic backward angles).

Why it matters to you

Provides rigorous few-body scattering techniques for extended Hilbert spaces; the exact multichannel AGS solutions and chiral EFT potential benchmarks are directly analogous to three-body continuum methods used in nuclear electroweak response theory.

Caveats

The calculations omit explicit three-body hypernuclear forces ($YNN$), and experimental $\Lambda d$ scattering data remain insufficient for direct potential calibration.

Abstract

$\Lambda d$ elastic scattering and breakup below the $\Sigma$ threshold is described fully including the virtual coupling to $\Sigma$ channels. Well-converged results are obtained solving the multichannel scattering equations for three-body transition operators in momentum-space. Scattering length, exclusive, semi-inclusive and total elastic and breakup cross sections, and spin observables are calculated, aiming to study their dependence on kinematic variables and dynamic ingredients. The uncertainty due to the hyperon-nucleon interaction model is most sizable. The observables and regimes that could help to constrain the hyperon-nucleon force models are identified. The single-channel approximation of the problem is validated at low and intermediate energies.

Radii of Sn Isotopes near the Proton Dripline from Nuclear Lattice Effective Field Theory

Fabian Hildenbrand, Serdar Elhatisari, Ulf-G. Meißner

nucl-th hep-lat hep-ph nucl-exarXiv:2611.24572

Nuclear lattice EFT with wavefunction matching and the partial-pinhole algorithm provides the first ab initio predictions of charge and matter radii for proton-dripline tin isotopes $^{96\text{--}104}\text{Sn}$.

Novel idea

This work achieves the first ab initio nuclear lattice EFT calculations of both charge and matter radii for heavy nuclei near the proton dripline ($A \sim 100$), specifically $^{96}\text{Sn}$ through $^{104}\text{Sn}$. It combines wavefunction matching with the partial-pinhole algorithm and systematic variations of partial-wave-resolved 3N interactions to establish radius-energy correlations in heavy systems.

Method

Simulations are performed using Euclidean-time lattice projection with spatial spacing $a = 1.32\text{ fm}$ in an $L=14$ box ($La = 18.48\text{ fm}$). The Hamiltonian incorporates an N3LO two-nucleon interaction with wavefunction matching to suppress sign oscillations, coupled to partial-wave-resolved 3N forces. Radii are extracted via two-body density correlations from a 12-particle partial pinhole, fully including Galilean invariance restoration (GIR) corrections.

Key results

  • Extracted charge radii for $^{96\text{--}104}\text{Sn}$ are systematically larger by $\approx 0.1\text{--}0.2\text{ fm}$ than Bogoliubov coupled-cluster results using EM(1.8/2.0) potentials, but consistent with self-consistent Green's functions with NNLOsat.
  • GIR corrections shift charge radii downward by $\sim 0.08\text{ fm}$ at $A \sim 100$, demonstrating an approximate linear scaling with $A$ that outpaces the naive phenomenological $A^{1/3}$ expectation.
  • Variations in 3N interactions that produce identical binding energies in light nuclei yield substantial spreads in tin radii, showing that radii in heavy nuclei provide independent constraints on 3N low-energy constants.
  • Extrapolated two-neutron separation energies $S_{2n}$ show a sharp step drop of $\approx 11\text{ MeV}$ across $N=50$, correctly capturing the shell closure.

Why it matters to you

Constraining 3N forces and charge radii via ab initio lattice EFT informs the nuclear ground-state distributions and nuclear-EFT inputs that underpin electroweak current matrix elements in medium and heavy nuclei.

Caveats

Calculations are conducted at a single lattice spacing without continuum extrapolation; sign oscillations restrict Euclidean projection time for $^{104}\text{Sn}$ to $\tau \le 0.8\text{ MeV}^{-1}$, leading to sizable extrapolation uncertainties.

Abstract

We present the first $\textit{ab initio}$ lattice calculations of charge and matter radii of tin isotopes $^{96}$Sn, $^{98}$Sn, $^{100}$Sn, $^{102}$Sn and $^{104}$Sn near the proton dripline using nuclear lattice effective field theory with high-fidelity two- and three-nucleon forces combined with the partial-pinhole algorithm. The resulting radii are larger then predicted from other ab-initio methods. We find that variations of the three-nucleon interaction reveal substantial radius differences even among interactions with similar binding energies. We intensively discuss of the role of different choices of the three-body forces.

Steerability in top quark decays at the LHC

Tao Han, Navin McGinnis, Sepehr Salamat, Shufang Su, Xingzhou Yu

hep-ph quant-pharXiv:2611.23368

Collider measurement of EPR steering is formulated for top-quark pairs at the LHC, establishing a convex criterion for fictitious states and projecting $7.7\sigma$ observation in the boosted semileptonic channel with current data.

Novel idea

The paper establishes the theoretical and experimental framework for measuring Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) steering at colliders. Crucially, it proves that the steerability integral is convex in the spin correlation matrix, guaranteeing that a steerable fictitious state reconstructed in the event-by-event helicity basis rigorously certifies genuine steerable quantum substates across phase space without requiring local hidden state assumptions.

Method

Leading-order $pp \to t\bar{t}$ events at $\sqrt{s}=13\text{ TeV}$ are simulated with MadGraph5, MadSpin, Pythia8, and Delphes3 in both semileptonic and dileptonic channels. The spin density matrix is reconstructed via decay angular asymmetries (unfolded with RooUnfold) and kinematic template methods. Closed-form analytic steering criteria are derived in terms of singular values of the spin correlation matrix, and fiducial phase space cuts are optimized for threshold and boosted regimes.

Key results

  • Derived exact lower and upper bounds on the steerability integral $\mathcal{S}$, yielding a sufficient steering condition $\sum_i \mu_i > 3/2$ and a necessary condition $\text{tr}(C^T C) > 3/4$.
